`status: exact` indicates that the claim has been mathematically validated under its stated assumptions. Every step of the derivation closes; the result holds as a matter of logical consequence from the stated premises. The qualifier "under stated assumptions" is load-bearing —
`exact` does not mean universally true, only that the formal argument is complete and correct given what the segment explicitly assumes.

The distinction from `axiomatic`: `exact` claims are *derived*; `axiomatic` claims are *stipulated*.
The distinction from `robust-qualitative`: `exact` claims have a specific form that is proved;
`robust-qualitative` claims have the right qualitative shape but their specific functional form may be approximate.

`exact` is the ceiling status for claims in the inevitability core: segments where the goal is
"given the prior objects, this is the only compatible form."
