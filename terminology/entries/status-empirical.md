`status: empirical` indicates that the claim is supported by data or simulation, but has not been derived from the framework's formal axioms. The claim is testable and the support is real, but it is fundamentally about how the world behaves rather than what the formalism forces.

The slug `status-empirical` distinguishes this as a frontmatter *status* value from
[`type-empirical`](type-empirical.md), which is a *segment type* value. A segment with
`type: empirical` describes a class of empirical claims; a segment with `status: empirical` asserts that this particular claim sits at the empirical epistemic tier.

`status: empirical` is not a demotion from the framework's perspective — empirical claims are where AAT becomes falsifiable and practically useful. The third ring (empirical, heuristic,
discussion) is where the theory interfaces with the world. The discipline is ensuring that claims labeled `empirical` are genuinely testable and that the available support is honestly stated.

FORMAT.md warns against using "Solid," "Confident," or "Plausible" as tier labels — these are not AAT terms. `empirical` is the correct term for data-supported-but-not-derived claims.
