`comprehensive` is the highest search log depth tier — reserved for searches that have been pushed
to human-researcher depth: multiple iterations, deliberate probing of corner cases and adjacent
literatures, and expert-level judgment about where relevant prior work might hide.

A genuinely comprehensive search would typically involve: running multiple passes with different
query strategies; following forward and backward citation chains from key priors; checking
proceedings of the relevant domain conferences for the relevant decade; and consulting with
researchers who have domain expertise. The `comprehensive` tier should not be claimed for
automated search tools alone (those are `nominally-comprehensive`).

A finding carrying `comprehensive` search depth and the `novelty` claim posture is the strongest
epistemic statement available in the Findings schema about the freshness of the contribution. Few
findings should claim this tier — the cost of claiming it dishonestly is high, and the bar for
honesty is proportionally demanding.
