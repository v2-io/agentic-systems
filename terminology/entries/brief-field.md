The Brief field is the first field in a `## Findings` entry and the most reader-facing: it is the field a curious non-specialist encounters first when browsing the curated FINDINGS.md catalog.

**The Feynman criterion** is the aspiration: *if you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it yet.* A genuinely good Brief reaches for the everyday physical or causal analog whose structure is *isomorphic* to the finding's load-bearing structure — not merely evocative. The test is whether a reader can predict the qualitative consequences of perturbing the analog and have those predictions hold against the formalism.

The canonical example is the bathtub gloss for the persistence condition: water level = gap between belief and reality; faucet flow = rate at which reality changes; drain rate = the agent's learning ability; bathtub size = how wrong the agent can be while still keeping up. Overflow when faucet outpaces drain at full. A reader who carries this picture away has the result in hand without ever meeting the symbols.

The Brief should stand on its own — it is not a translation of the Impact paragraph. Use technical language where there is no plain-language equivalent that preserves meaning, but anchor it on first use. The Brief is consistently the most-valuable field for external adoption: it decides whether an interested reader engages further.
