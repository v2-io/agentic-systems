`status: heuristic` indicates that a claim is useful operational guidance — it works in practice and can be explained intuitively — but a formal tier is not in hand. The quantitative form may not hold, and the claim should not be used where precision is required.

`heuristic` differs from `robust-qualitative` in that robust-qualitative claims have a cross-assumption justification for their qualitative shape; heuristics may not — they are adopted on the basis of usefulness and informal plausibility rather than demonstrated robustness.

`heuristic` is not a demotion: many of the most practically useful claims in a theoretical framework are heuristics. The status is honest about what kind of support the claim has, which is what allows readers to use it appropriately. A heuristic claim that has been formally justified under specific conditions should be upgraded to `conditional` or `exact` (under those conditions).
