## 1. The object

> *"Imagine each unicode glyph is a node in a graph, with, initially, only very tentative faded edges that happen to show codepoint sequence."*
>
> *"In classical NLP … or a markov chain, we might … draw a probability edge between it and glyphs that follow it … But we can of course do a little better … if we predicted off of n-grams."* — Joseph, 2026-10-05

Every glyph is a node. Before any mind has answered, the only edges are faint ones between codepoint neighbours: U+2460 `①` → U+2461 `②`. That scaffold reflects Unicode's allocation, not anyone's perception, so it is a prior and never evidence.

**The goal is sequences recognised as sequences** (Joseph, 2026-10-05: *"generation is for exploring the space but also counts as a recognition, but recognition is the final goal — 'best recognized sequences as sequences' is the goal"*). These are runs that minds of several families, shown the whole run, perceive as a progression. A signum is read, never generated by its reader, so this felt-when-shown property is the one that matters. The claim is about the whole run: a chain of individually plausible links, such as a code-chart walk, is not thereby a sequence. §4a lists the tests of it.

**Generation has two roles.**
- **It explores.** It is how candidate sequences and their branches are found.
- **It counts as a recognition,** in one direction only. A mind that writes `x` unprompted has recognised it. A mind that doesn't write `x` has said nothing about `x` (§2b).

The way in, and the store's shape, is **conditional continuation**. Given a run of glyphs, what do minds of each LLM family write next? A glyph such as `-` can belong to many sequences. Which one it continues is decided by the glyphs before it, to some depth. So an edge is not `- → x`. It is `(context) - → x`, for some context.

## 2. The data model: a context tree

A **context** is an oriented run of glyphs: what a mind was shown, ending at the glyph whose successor is asked. The store is a **context tree**: a trie over contexts read backwards from the most recent glyph, with each node holding the continuation answers given at that context, per mind and family. It is also called a prediction suffix tree, or a variable-order Markov model. It is close kin to a suffix trie, built over reversed contexts and carrying continuation counts at every node.

- **Position.** A node of the tree is a glyph in a particular context. `-` reached through `\ |` and `-` reached through `→ =` are different nodes. "Per position" in this document always means *per node of the context tree*. Two sequences share a node exactly as long as their contexts have not yet diverged.
- **Edge.** The answers at a node. Each continuation `x` at node `c` is an edge `c → x`, with its supporting answers and the families behind them.
- **Orientation.** A sequence and its reverse are the same sequence, but contexts are oriented. Extending a sequence leftward means asking the continuation of its reversed run. Both directions are ordinary continuation questions.

## 2a. The concerns, kept apart

> *"The context tree should give us a very clean way of distinguishing between these three concerns/responsibilities: meaningful generalizable probing (e.g., preventing sheet-caused confounders & biases), the raw emergent sequences, and probing priority & efficiency (including seeding)."* — Joseph, 2026-10-05

1. **Probing: is an answer a valid measurement?** This covers how a question is put:
   - sheet composition, neighbours on the sheet, position, option order, forced versus optional "none";
   - context length, and whether the context spoils the answer;
   - parsing and echo failures.

   Its output is answers, each tagged with everything about how it was obtained. It knows nothing about which sequences exist.
2. **The tree: what the minds perceive.** It is built only from answers: contexts and the continuations given at them, per mind and family. Sequences, branches, ends, cycles and stability are read off it. It knows nothing about why a question was asked (priority, seeds, exploration), so where a question came from can never become evidence. The only route from (1) into (2) is a tagged answer. If a probing factor turns out to bias answers, the tree can weight or split by that tag without being rebuilt.
3. **Priority: what to ask next.** It reads the tree and decides where effort goes: extension, k\*, branch points, cycle probes, hot exploration, seeds, and how settled each node is. It writes questions, never evidence.

**Seeds, as tagged votes.** Joseph, 2026-10-05: *"I think you can add the seed edges as equivalent to a vote from that agent's LLM, wherever that makes sense — possibly marking it specifically as from the seed so potentially biased — it will be very useful for me from a practical engineering perspective to get the most relevant sequences as quickly as possible, while allowing us to segregate those out if we need a more pure analysis for a write-up."* So a survey sequence written by a known model enters the tree as that model's family voting for each glyph after its context (`port/port_seeds.py`).
- **Tags:** every seed vote is tagged `era: seed`.
- **Still asked:** a context counts as *asked* only once a real mind has answered it, so every seeded step is put to the real minds, and k\* is never "known" from seeds.
- **Pure analysis:** `tree.py --pure` and the *settled without seeds* column of PROGRESS leave seeds out.

4. **Porting: bringing the triad era's answers in without corrupting the tree.** Joseph: *"correctly porting triad-era data into the context tree as much as possible without introducing any incorrect or biased or otherwise invalid data."* It is an adapter at the boundary, written once and audited, and it feeds concern 2 exactly as concern 1 does: answers with tags. §6 states what is ported as what.

5. **Read-offs: what the tree holds, presented.** BEST.md, SEQUENCES.md and PROGRESS.md each read the tree with their own stated criteria, and nothing reads them back. Joseph, 2026-10-05, on keeping them apart: *"I get the feeling like we are possibly mixing the 5 concerns that need to be independent? (if the BEST read of the context tree is considered a 5th concern)"*. A read-off's criteria are its own, never shared with the planner's read of the tree. If a read-off shows a defect, the fix belongs in the read-off, unless the defect is in the answers themselves. Found that day: a fix for a BEST.md symptom had been put into the walk, which is the planner's view of the tree, and coupled the two. It was undone.

6. **The catalog: the tree's sequences, derived once, by one stated model** (designed 2026-10-06, `CATALOG.md`; build 0 of `catalog/build.py` the same day, with the model not yet frozen). Readers had each derived sequences from the raw tree in their own way: the tree's walks, the planner's walks, BEST's paths and folding, triad chains. Each fix in one exposed a loss in another; most tellingly, of the 300 rows of the triad era's last STANDINGS.md (r012, all rank ≥ 0.75), 232 appeared nowhere: not asked whole, not inside a run asked whole, not inside a BEST candidate (counted 2026-10-06). Joseph: *"we basically have a 'raw-data context-tree' and a 'canonical statistically sound context-tree' maybe?"*, and the catalog *"should actually end up being (if done right) the more correct* input *to the probing concern and the rating+extraction (best/reading) concern."*
   - **What it is.** A deterministic build from answers alone: canonical records for links, runs, cycles and branches, each part carrying strength and certainty, itemised by answer and rater. Once built, its model supersedes the per-reader standard above for every reader.
   - **The model** (version 5: `CATALOG.md`, after de novo audits of versions 1–3 and a check of version 4 in `audits/`). Records store recognition parts (strength and certainty side by side), and what the recognition is **independent of**: the code chart and the glyphs' names, as signed, measured quantities. They are measured only at *contested* positions, where the route offers a real alternative (for the chart, a glyph of the same Unicode block: triad departures from the code-point median are 0.13 within one block and at chance across three). The instruments are discriminator positions, triad departures, a name-only order question (validated first against chart recall) and lookalike substitution. Where a route can't discriminate (a run contiguous in code points; glyphs whose names describe their look), that is stated as a fact. Raters are minds in assigned conditions; seeds are a rater of their own.
   - **The ranking.** Joseph: an intuitive sequence, one *"that doesn't need internal arithmetic or thinking about the glyph's name and codepoint"*, is much better. That ranking is the default *view* computed from the quantities, never a stored verdict.
   - **The engine.** Python computes; Postgres 18 stores and serves.
   - **Once built**, the planner (3) and the read-offs (5) read the catalog instead of re-deriving links. Until then they read the tree, and their criteria stay their own.

**One standard for evidence** (2026-10-05; Joseph: *"make sure the evidence is very defensible and not a … mishmash of heuristics"*). The tree supplies facts: per family, how many answers gave the response a claim predicts, of how many. Every reader decides with one standard:
- **The estimate.** Each family's proportion is a Beta posterior (Jeffreys). A test's estimate is the equal-weight mean over the families that answered, because the claims are about families, with its spread.
- **Choice answers** (continuations, triad middles, order lines) are *supported* when the 90% lower bound is ≥ 0.5 and *contradicted* when the upper bound is < 0.5.
- **Yes/no answers** are compared with the family's matched control: a whole run's coherence with the same glyphs shuffled (§4a), and a link's recognition, or a skip test, with near decoys (§4).
- **Families.** Decisions needed ≥ 2 families until 2026-10-05, when Joseph dropped that for BEST.md (*"Let's drop the >= 2 model family cap in 'best' if you don't mind"*). One family suffices there now, and every row shows its per-family counts, so how universal a finding is stays visible. The 0.5 and the 90% are choices made without evidence.
- **Agreement is the perceptual quantity**, how strongly minds converge. The interval is how sure we are of it.
- **Generation counts one way** (§1). A continuation naming `x` is evidence for the link. A "none", or a different continuation, is not evidence against `x`. A "none" is evidence about an end, and a different continuation is evidence of a branch. Only explicit rejection counts against a sequence: a "no" beyond the control, a decoy chosen, an order that breaks it, or a swap judged right.
  - **History.** Until 2026-10-05, BEST.md counted "none" against a link and confirmed links by continue answers alone. That broke this rule and §2b's own sentence. The morph ladder `-=>})|`, Joseph's example that founded the survey's morph family, was invisible as a result. Its continue answers are "none" or `]` (the bracket reading), while Gemini and Grok each put `)` between `}` and `|` in 2 of 3 triad answers.
- **Read-offs show the tests side by side** (§4a) and don't collapse them into one number. Where tests disagree, that is information: recognised but not generated is the morph case. Sequences are sorted by length to organise them (Joseph: *"length is useful for organizational purposes in the sorting — … but don't necessarily make that the 'ranking' number"*). Recognition strength is its own column.
- **Reasoned and unreasoned answers stay apart** in every read-off. Whether reasoned generation counts the same as first-impression generation is left to the data (Joseph: *"let's see what the data tells us"*). The comparison that answers it: among sequences that pass whole-sequence recognition at first impression, did reasoned generation find them as often, and did it also find many that fail?

The triad era's triads and order answers are part of this, as §2b and §6 say: betweenness and ordering are evidence in the tree. Until 2026-10-05 they were ported and verified but almost unused: triads fed one display column and order answers nothing, the same class of bug as the written answers. They now test every confirmed stretch, with order tested round the circle for stretches that wrap.

The triad era mixed these concerns:
- seeds supplied the glyphs that sequences were tested with;
- the fit's guesses steered what was asked within sequences;
- the growth rules carried priority judgments.

Each mix-up produced an incident (see `OBSERVATIONS.md` and the seam note in `.archive/2026-10-06-triad-era/PLAN.md`).

## 2b. Negative evidence: inhibitory edges

> *"Do we have a way to also record 'inhibitory' responses — orthogonality — negative edges?"* — Joseph, 2026-10-05

A node holds what minds rejected as well as what they continued with. There are three kinds, and the first two are already in the data. Counts through r011:

| kind | what it says | from | count |
|---|---|---|---|
| **end** at a context | nothing comes after this run | a what-comes-next / continue "none" | 1,272 |
| **orthogonality** among glyphs, at short context | these glyphs don't sit in one sequence, or only two of them do | triad "none" / "only two go together"; order "none", sets split into separate lines, or glyphs set aside | 3,563 / 3,761; 951, 245, 1,061 |
| **inhibition** of a specific edge in a context | given this run, `x` does *not* come next | an inhibit question's "no" (asked since c005: recognition, skip tests, branch tests; with matched controls since c007) | 528 real "no" of 1,109 real answers, through c010 |

A proposal list that omits `x` is not a rejection of `x`, since a mind names at most three. So explicit inhibition has its own question: given a context and a candidate, does it continue the run? yes, no, or "can't tell" (concern 1).

Like positive edges, negative ones are **conditioned on their context**. A triad's "none" is a statement at very short context. It must never act as a global veto: `|` and `-` are orthogonal in most contexts and adjacent in the spinner, and `3E[|]` would be invisible to anything that let short-context rejection close the door. (The triad era learned this from its majority rule, which let ⟂ answers erase anything not every mind saw.) Negative edges are evidence in the tree, and they enter priority as soft weights, never as filters.

## 3. Definitions

**Shortest sufficient context, k\*.** For a glyph `x` that follows a run `… u v w` along some path, k\* is the length of the shortest suffix of that run (`w`, `v w`, `u v w`, …) at which minds name `x` as the continuation.

**Stability** (per position). An edge is **stable** when two things hold:
- the minds agree on it at k\*;
- the prediction stays put when the context is lengthened backward along the same path.

A long k\* is not a weakness; it makes the edge specific. The spinner's `-` may need `\ | /` before it. The failure mode is the opposite: a prediction that flips when more context is shown (`7 8 9 → 🔟`, but `7 8 9 8 → 7`). Flips are where branches live. A node's **restfulness** is how settled its continuation already is at short context. `3` after `1 2` is restful.

**Sequence.** A path through the tree along which each next glyph is a stable edge in its own context. The minds' agreement and families are shown per position. A sequence's weakest position bounds it.

**Branch.** A point where two paths share a context up to some depth and then continue differently. A branch is labelled with the context length that separates the paths.

**End.** A linear sequence ends *statistically*. At its end context, stable at its own k\*, the minds' continuations thin out into "none".

**Cycle.** A path that returns to a context state it has already visited, with every step determined. The state is the last k glyphs, where k is the order that determines every step. This allows a period to repeat glyphs:
- **The plain spinner `- \ | /`** is determined at k = 1.
- **The bouncing spinner `- \ | / - / | \`** needs k = 2: `\ |` is followed by `/`, but `/ |` by `\`. Its period is 8, with 4 distinct glyphs.

A cycle is closed rather than statistically ended. It is fully determined at every position by context no longer than its period. It has no natural beginning: once it is defined, any position can serve as the start.
- **Manifestation.** A cycle counts only once a mind has *manifested* it, writing the entire period a second time itself (Joseph: *"at the moment the entire pattern has repeated itself"*). A 4-glyph cycle therefore takes 8 edges to nail down.
- **Scope.** Cycles whose period holds fewer than 4 distinct glyphs are not of interest at this time. 2- and 3-cycles may be valid, but they are out of scope. *(The one 3-cycle the minds produced so far, `▿ ▹ ▵ → ▿`, is probably the 4-orientation triangle `▵ ▹ ▿ ◃` with `◃` missing.)*

**Reflection** (Joseph's postulate; asked from 2026-10-05, up to 20 a round outside the budget, as `a b c b → ?` at points inside the best-ranked sequences, worded as a plain continue question, stored apart in `Tree.reflections`). Any linear sequence becomes periodic if it is played backwards at its end: `7 8 9 8 7 …`. That is consistent with treating a sequence and its reverse as one object.
- **What it can test:**
  - whether a family treats sequences as reversible, a direct test of the unoriented assumption;
  - whether an end behaves as a line (it reflects) or as a cycle (it wraps).
- **Caution.** After the turnaround, the context hands the mind its answer, so a reflected continuation measures the minds' grammar of sequences, not their perception of the glyphs. Reflection answers are kept as their own kind of evidence. They never grow a sequence.
- **Open:** the bouncing spinner sits between a cycle and a reflection. It is a cycle under the state definition above, and its period's reverse is a rotation of itself. Whether "reflection-symmetric cycle" should be a named class is left open until data shows whether it matters.

## 4. Eliciting answers

- **Continue** is the main question. Given a context, the mind writes what comes next, in order, as many glyphs as come naturally. It may write none. One answer yields a whole path of edges, each conditioned on everything before it.
  - **Shown and written are two channels, both answers.** The first glyph answers the context the mind was *shown*. Each later glyph answers a context the mind *wrote* itself: everything shown and written before it. The tree keeps both, tagged by channel.
  - **How each concern uses them.** The planner's walk and k\* read the shown answers. BEST.md reports the two channels separately: stretches confirmed by shown answers, and runs carried by written answers that await shown confirmation. The planner asks written-only contexts shown. Where minds keep writing past a point the shown answers call an end, which is momentum (Joseph: *"generating != recognizing or affirming, and … generating is the more difficult"*), the written continuation goes to **recognition**: an inhibit question at that shown context.
  - **History.** Until 2026-10-05, nothing used the written channel, against this paragraph. That bug left about half of every multi-glyph answer unused. The alphabet's six never-asked links had each been written 27–53 times by all three families.
  - **Recognition has controls** (Joseph: *"wrt 'yes' -- can't you add controls?"*, *"make sure order is random"*). Each recognition question has a matched decoy at the same context:
    - **near:** the same neighbourhood of the code chart, not adjacent, which measures yes-for-block-membership;
    - **far:** a distant part of Unicode, which measures acquiescence.

    Decoys are fated and tagged as a probing condition (`tags.control`). They're shuffled in among the other inhibit questions, and the option order is fated per sheet. Checked on a dry plan: no control shared a sheet with its own candidate, both kinds appeared at every position, and all six option orders were used. BEST.md counts a candidate as **recognised** when its yes share beats the near-control rate (one family suffices since 2026-10-05, §2a). Before controls (c005–c006), the real candidates' yes rate was already 0.85 for Grok (n = 46), the reason they were needed.
  - **Momentum is real.** 715 of 8,937 continue answers kept writing past a point that shown answers end, e.g. `㍬ … ㍰ → ㍱` (24h → hPa) and `🃣 🃢 🃡 → 🃠`. Long written runs through unfamiliar blocks are mostly code-chart walks.
- **Lengthening contexts.** To find k\* for an edge, ask its context at increasing lengths along the path, starting short.
- **No spoiling.** A context never shows what the question is testing. For a suspected cycle, show only enough of the period's end that a repeat would be the mind's own. For an extension, never include glyphs from beyond the end.
- **Triads and order sets** are cheap consistency checks. A sequence implies betweenness for each of its triples, and an order for each of its windows. A triad answer is also an unconditioned hint that a 3-glyph path exists, which makes it a cheap way to find **kernels** during exploration. They do not create edges.
- **No reserved symbols.** Answer options are words or JSON keys ("none", "only two go together", "roughly the same step", "can't tell"), never glyphs (Joseph: *"You can just have them say 'roughly equivalent' or 'orthogonal' etc. instead of a glyph"*). So no glyph is ever excluded from the alphabet for being notation. The triad era's ban on `⟂ ⊥ ≈` is gone: none of them ever appeared in a prompt, and the ban had silently removed real `≈` proposals.
- **Sheets** keep everything the pilot and rounds r000–r012 established: 1–5 presentations per call, fated randomization, recorded positions, and isolated judge adapters.
- **Lookups: a first impression is not a consultation.** Gemini, run through agy, has tools, and on unfamiliar glyphs it reaches for them. Every empty Gemini answer checked in c001 and r012 (172 calls) was a soft-denied `run_command` such as `python3 -c "print(chr(0x336D), chr(0x336E), …)"`. Its thinking had already turned the glyphs into code points and blocks. No successful answer used a tool. Joseph, 2026-10-05: *"the fact that agy feels compelled to look up unicode entries tells us something immediately — especially since these are meant to be first-thought limenal impressions."* So:
  - **A tool attempt is a measurement.** It is recorded as its own outcome, `tool-attempt`, with the command the mind tried. It says that this mind, at this context, did not answer from impression. Claude and Grok are offered no tools, so the observation exists only for Gemini.
  - **Re-asks name the condition.** A sheet that drew a tool attempt is re-asked with one added sentence, *"Please answer without looking anything up: just your best initial guess."* That is prompt variant `no-lookup`, and every record is tagged with its variant. The wording deliberately doesn't name Unicode or its tables, which could steer a mind toward chart order. Joseph's own phrasing was "without looking up any unicode tables".
  - **Gemini is told** (from 2026-10-06 ~15:30, adapter `a4-tool-halt`; Joseph: *"you may want to tell it that a tool call will halt the session"*): *"Please don't use any tools: a tool call halts this session before you can answer."* It's true of print mode, and Gemini is the only family given tools. The notice is recorded with every answer (`adapter_meta.notice`), so the tool-attempt rate before and after can be compared; attempts are still recorded and re-asked as `no-lookup`. The study's Gemini judges are not given the lookup tool (they share Joseph's agy settings, and the reasoned pass already measures what lookups produce).
  - **A reasoned pass is a different measurement: generation, then recognition.** Joseph wanted a pass *"where the tool works and you can have them give their reasoned one as well (probably especially useful for extending a sequence etc. — where reasoning is fine — verification/recognition != generation (more complex))"*. Built 2026-10-05 in two steps:
    - **Generation.** Each round, up to 20 continue questions (a number chosen without evidence) go to the reasoner mind `sonnet55r`, at the *felt ends* of the best-ranked sequences, i.e. where first-impression minds answered "none". The reasoner is Sonnet with thinking and the four read-only lookup tools of `~/src/arch/firmatum/utils/utf-mcp`, independently verified. Its prompt is variant `reasoned`: no "first impressions", and leave to reason, look things up, and work out a continuation. What it looks up is copied, call by call, into its ledger row. Its answers are kept apart from the tree's evidence (`Tree.reasoned`). What they are evidence of was decided on 2026-10-06 from a reading of them (`spikes/reasoned-pass-2026-10-06/FINDINGS.md`): 79% of its continuations are chart walks, and its code-point range lookups go with them. So a continuation it reaches by lookup marks the link *chart-reached*, its "none" is end evidence, and its continuations are candidates. They are never evidence of a felt link (CATALOG §5).
    - **Recognition.** Each candidate a reasoned answer starts with, where the first-impression minds don't already give it, becomes an **inhibit** question to the first-impression roster: *does this symbol come directly after the run?* yes / no / can't tell. A candidate that is recognised when shown, though not produced unprompted, is evidence of a felt link reachable only by being pointed to. That distinction matters for signa, which always show the full sequence.
    - **First live check** [observed]. Given `🮂 🮁 🮀`, the reasoner looked up the surrounding code-point range and wrote a long descending run of chart neighbours. Given `ↂ ↇ ↈ` (the end of the Roman numerals), it answered "none". Reasoned candidates are expected to be chart-heavy, so recognition is the test that matters.
  - **Chart knowledge versus sequence knowledge.** The embedding spike's verifier found that the minds put the *alphabet* letter first wherever Unicode order and the alphabet disagree. The chart glyph shows up as runner-up for Claude and Gemini, never for Grok (`spikes/embedding-tendrils-2026-10-05/de-novo-feedback-1.md`, small n). So a ±1-codepoint link is not by itself a chart artefact.
  - **A clean marker of code-point arithmetic.** Some continuations name code points that are unassigned in Unicode 16, so they can never have been seen in text. Through c001 [measured, from `other_content` "unassigned" proposals, per 1,000 continuation answers]:

    | gemini38flash | 15 |
    | sonnet55 | 9 |

    The flagship case is the Mayan numerals `𝋠 … 𝋳` (0–19). After `𝋱 𝋲 𝋳`, every family writes U+1D2F4 onward. These answers are kept as their own outcome, never as glyphs. The rate per mind and per thinking condition is a read-off of chart-following. *(Until c002, the pipeline ran on Unicode 14 data, so 3 such answers were in fact assigned characters (🯯 🩷 🩵).   - **Recognition versus retrieval: the thinking budget is a probing factor.** Joseph: reasoning can be needed *"to discover the candidate sequences/branches/etc. that can be felt without much reasoning"*, which he called *"analogous to 'recognition vs retrieval'"*.
    - **How much the minds deliberate.** In c001, median thinking tokens per sheet ran from 0 (Gemini Flash) through ~500 (Sonnet) and ~900 (Grok) to ~2,400 (Gemini Pro) and ~5,100 (Haiku).
    - **An inherited budget.** Until 2026-10-05, the Claude judges inherited `MAX_THINKING_TOKENS=31000` from Joseph's shell, unrecorded. Budgets are now set per mind in `core/minds.json` and recorded in every ledger row.

On 102 items, thinking changed Haiku's first answer beyond its own retest noise (on–off agreement 0.69–0.74, against 0.83–0.88 within either condition), in both directions. ## 4a. Testing recognition (proposed 2026-10-05; built so far: coherence, the generator, and ordering a shuffled set)

Joseph, on finding that the planner spent ~82% of c009 on continue questions: *"there are at least a handful of different kinds of better tests it could be running."* Each test below checks a different prediction of one hypothesis: *this run is a sequence*. Each gets its own question kind and tags (concern 1), is stored in the tree as its own primitive (concern 2), and is decided by the one standard (§2a): choice answers against 0.5, yes/no answers against controls.

**Two kinds of pass, kept apart:**
- **Generator passes** find candidates and branches. Reasoning is welcome there.
- **Evidence passes** test felt-ness at first impression, always with a control. Showing a whole run invites a mind to reason a story onto anything; before controls, Grok said yes to 85% of real candidates. Without a control, a "yes, coherent" means little.

**Joseph's two passes:**
- **The generator: tendrils from known sequences** (built 2026-10-05). Joseph's design, as he restated it after the first build stopped every path at one step:
  > *"s = known sequence — e.g., "abc". "Pick a single character that would extend this sequence. You may be creative, but give a reason:..." {"d" => 72, "A" => 7, "1" => 13, "α" => 2} now you have one "main" branch and 3 weaker branches. Leading to a strong "abcdefg..." and weaker, more obscure, but equally easily recognized: "abc123", "abcABC", "abcαβ..." sequences. Branching from seeds like tendrils, and then most of them are easier to recognize than to generate. If, on the other hand, the responses are far more random and spread out — still follow any bumps, but the point is still on the recognition side."*
  - **The question** is a forced write, with no "none" option, in his wording ("a single", never "the one": *"implies there is one expected answer"*). The reason is asked for so that reasoning happens, and is saved but not scored. Effort is normal, not low (*"for *generating* / creative work"*): the generator minds are the core models at `medium` effort.
  - **Sampling.** Each context is asked 5 times by each generator mind, on separate sheets (a number chosen without evidence), so it gets a distribution.
  - **Branches.** Every answer at a context is a branch, and the extended context `s + x` is asked in a later round. Nothing is excluded (Joseph: *"when I do stochastic work I always use s-curves with there always being a long tail allowing for the most improbably things to sneak in still"*). A branch's priority is an S-curve on its share `q` of the draws, `w = 0.02 + 0.98 / (1 + e^{-(q - 0.10)/0.04})`:
    - a main branch weighs about 1;
    - `A` at 7 of 94 weighs 0.36;
    - a single draw of 20 weighs 0.24;
    - nothing weighs less than 0.02.

    Branches are drawn in that proportion (a fated weighted order). Half of each round's contexts go to branches and half to new roots (the survey sequences and found runs, in both directions). The curve's three numbers and the split were all chosen without evidence.
    - **History.** The first version followed only answers drawn twice. That was a cut against §7: a side agent computed that a 2% answer in 20 draws would be followed about 6% of the time.
  - **Recognition decides.** Every branch path also goes to the coherence question.
  - **The null.** Fresh random contexts of the same length are asked the same way, one per four contexts. They show how often a repeat, and how much spread, happens by chance; that is what calibrates "a branch" and "flat".
  - **Weight of a pick** (Joseph: *"I wouldn't be opposed to a *forced* 'next character' being slightly weaker than a full 'recognition' — but not too much"*). A pick is positive link evidence of its own kind (P in BEST.md), never pooled with free continuations. Recognition of the whole run is the verdict.
  - **Stopping** (Joseph: *"if one highly dispersed/high-entropy response answer goes on to get high-entropy second-order responses — that's probably a justified reason to stop a branch — but I trust you to do the math and see if that's true"*). Measured 2026-10-06 over c017–c085, Claude and Grok picks, with entropy normalised by its maximum because the number of draws varies. A step counts as "flat" when it is at least as spread as 90% of the nulls (normalised entropy ≥ 0.47; nulls' median 0.80, real contexts' median 0.47).
    - **Flatness persists.** Of 205 parent-to-child pairs, flat parents had flat children in 93 of 135 cases (69%), structured parents in 26 of 70 (37%). The entropies correlate at 0.43.
    - **Flat branches are not shown to be worthless.** Branch paths after two flat steps were recognised as wholes 45 times, rejected 0 times and undecided 41 times, about as often as paths with no flat step (10 / 0 / 11). Coherence is near ceiling for ordered runs, so this half is unproven rather than refuted.
    - **So nothing is stopped, and nothing needs to be.** Every branch of a flat context holds a small share of the draws, so the S-curve already gives its children low weight (a single draw of 20 weighs 0.24). A branch through two flat steps is drawn rarely but never cut: Joseph's rule in its soft, long-tail form, already in place.
    - **History.** A first look on 2026-10-05 (Claude only, 44 pairs) had shown no persistence; it was too small.
- **Coherence** (evidence): *"does this look like a coherent sequence? why?"* The yes/no is scored; the why is saved but not analysed at this stage. Control: the same glyphs shuffled, which separates "these belong together" from "this order is felt". A shuffle is an order the run doesn't predict, not necessarily a wrong one. Joseph: *"I can absolutely attest to seeing ideated sequences that are better after some shuffling."* In c015, the seed `: ∶ · ⁝ ⁞` drew "no" and "can't tell", while its shuffle `∶ · : ⁝ ⁞` drew a "yes": *"colon variants progressing from double to quadruple dots"*. So a shuffle that any mind calls coherent becomes a candidate run in its own right. It is asked fresh, with its own control; its answers as a control stay in the baseline and never count for it.

**Further tests,** each with its null:

| test | question | what it isolates | control / null |
|---|---|---|---|
| order a shuffled set (built) | put these in order, if they have one (ties, separate lines, none; the triad era's wording) | the axis read from the set alone, the whole ladder shown as a signum would show it | sets with no order; agreement across shuffles |
| swap detection | two adjacent glyphs swapped: is anything out of place, and where? | order sensitivity at each link | the unaltered run (false alarms) |
| intruder | one glyph replaced by a near decoy: which doesn't belong? | coherence of the axis as a set | the unaltered run |
| gap fill | `- = > _ ) \|`: what fills the gap? | context on both sides fixes the axis | gaps in shuffled runs |
| pairwise | of `a` and `b`, which is further along? | an order built from pairwise judgments (Bradley–Terry); intransitive judgments mean no single axis | random pairs |
| two-alternative next | after the run, `x` or `y`? (`y` a near decoy or the other branch) | recognition against a 50% baseline, cleaner than yes/no | presentation order randomised; decoy against decoy |
| interpolate from ends | from `-` to `\|` in 4 steps | whether the axis can be rebuilt from its ends | ends of random pairs |
| missing rung | does anything belong between `>` and `}`? | gaps such as `↙` in the arrow row | links known to be complete |
| odd one out | which of three is least like the others? | a perceptual similarity space independent of chart and names | random triplets |
| axis naming | what changes from one to the next? (open; saved, not scored) | later grouping by mechanism (morph, weight, count, rotation) | — |
| reversal | the reversed run: still coherent? | the unoriented assumption (§2) | shuffled |
| effort | coherence at low effort against normal effort | felt (holds at low effort) or worked out | — |

**Already designed but never run:**
- branch inhibits (§5 tier 4) and inside contexts (tier 5), which have had no budget in any round;
- between questions, ported but never asked anew;
- orthogonality as a soft weight in exploration;
- per-family views.

**Which grain fits a morph** is open. If the felt thing in `-=>})|` is the trajectory as a whole, ordering, gap fill and interpolation matter most. If it is step-by-step adjacency, swap detection and the two-alternative question matter most.

## 5. What gets asked next

**The governing principle: stochastic probing, weighted toward where it will be most clarifying** (Joseph, 2026-10-06, after a review of the planner found it mostly selecting rather than sampling): *"The idea was that it is *stochastic probing, weight toward where it will be most clarifying* — If you need a quote from me — that's the one. If an arbitrary or discrete ceiling is needed on something in order for the catalog data to be unbiased in some way — the catalog can take care of that (and give some very strong reasoning or it will be immediately rejected …). … I prefer stochastic all the way down, including jitter and stuff (I don't even like having the same number of questions per round etc.)"* Earlier: *"I wouldn't outright exclude *anything* — when I do stochastic work I always use s-curves with there always being a long tail."*
- **Every candidate question has a nonzero chance**, re-asking included. Its weight rises with how much its answer could clarify and falls, on an S-curve with a floor, as what it would test grows certain. Even the most settled link is re-asked, rarely.
- **No hard zeros in the planner.** A rule that never asks something can never learn it was wrong. Where an answer is uninformative for some claim, that is the catalog's reading to make, and it says why.
- **Counts vary too**: a round's size and its share per question kind are drawn, not fixed.
- **Status (2026-10-06):** the planner below still selects by tiers, caps and once-only rules, contrary to this principle. Its redesign is under way (HANDOFF). Until it lands, the rules below describe what runs, not what is intended.

**The aim of the budget** (2026-10-05; Joseph, on the planner's allocation: *"it really didn't understand the methodology"*). Recognition is the goal (§1), so most effort should go to recognition evidence for candidate sequences (§4a). Candidates come from generation, seeds, triads, reasoned passes and written momentum. Continue questions keep the share that exploring and extending need. The rule below still orders the work across sequences; what it does not yet say is which kinds of test each sequence's share buys. *(Open: how to apportion across kinds. Until it is settled, any shares are parameters chosen without evidence.)*

This is Joseph's priority rule, restated on the tree:

> *"various minds start to find a sequence — call it a sequence of three glyphs. The priority is then to extend that sequence to the right and to the left as far as they will go while still spending time looking for other 'kernels' … square away the most obvious and stable (empirically) sequences."*
>
> *"15% of our effort was always 'hot' — exploring the space for more kernels … based on bumps from the original seed."*

- **Kernel.** A 3-glyph path that two or more minds agree on (a 2-glyph context with an agreed continuation), found from any kind of answer.
- **Squaring away** (85%). Each sequence's work is to:
  - push its ends outward with continue questions;
  - lengthen contexts until each position's k\* is found and its stability shown;
  - probe the branch points the tree exposes.

  Sequences are served most stable first.
- **Falsification** (2026-10-05; Joseph: *"think in terms of the scientific method as well as a counter-balance — we're looking for things that disprove or call into question certain sequences"*). The strongest claims are tested hardest: sequences are served by rank, up to 60 tests a round, a number chosen without evidence. At most one of each kind per sequence a round, for a walked step `a → b` followed by `c`:
  - **Skip test.** An inhibit question at `a`'s context: does `c` come *directly* after? A sequence predicts no. Yes beyond the family's control rate (one family suffices since 2026-10-05, §2a) challenges the adjacency: order without adjacency, or a chart walk. BEST.md shows each stretch's skip tests as passed / challenged and flags challenged ones; it doesn't hide them.
  - **Lengthening test.** A longer context along the same path, not yet asked. Per §3, a stable edge stays put when lengthened backward, and a flip is a branch. The longest context decides, so a flip there breaks the link wherever links are judged.
  - **Triad test** (built 2026-10-05). A triple whose two links are both strong, never asked as a triad, is asked in the triad era's own wording and record shape (`seq` / `two` / `none`), so new answers join the 19,665 ported ones. Shown order is a fated uniform shuffle, so the hypothesised middle sits in the middle only by chance, and `shown_mid` records what was displayed there. Order questions are asked in the new protocol from c022 (§4a); the 5,716 ported ones and the new ones are both used as tests.
- **Join probes** (2026-10-05; Joseph: *"yes please — for example, row #1 and row #14 will be joined hopefully soon"*). These are asked before each sequence's other tier-1 work, up to 60 a round, one per link. A number chosen without evidence. Two kinds:
  - **A weak link inside a strong run.** Agreement below 0.75, the planner's own cut, chosen without evidence, with at least two strong links on each side, as at `㉟ → ㊱`. It is asked from the *other* direction, a new context. Failing that, the weak context is **replicated** while it holds fewer than 12 answers. That is the only place the planner re-asks a context.
  - **An open end that written answers bridge to another sequence's end**, as at `A … N` and `O … Z`. Its end question goes first.
- **Thin links** (2026-10-05). These are part of squaring away. A step of a ranked sequence whose deciding context some answering family answered fewer than twice gets that context **replicated once**. They sit in tier 3 beside k\*, up to 80 a round (a number chosen without evidence). The criterion is how many answers there are, never what they say, so it can't favour an outcome. "At most once" means no stopping rule.
  - **Why replication is restricted** (Joseph asked whether probing would *"accidentally weigh linkages too much by oversampling"*). It would, through selection rather than counts. Re-asking weak links until they pass is optional stopping. Letting a link pass on either reading direction gives a second chance. So BEST.md takes one estimate from both directions, and replication is fixed, never outcome-driven.
- **Triad kernels** (2026-10-05, §4). A third of hot exploration (a fated draw; the split was chosen without evidence) asks `a m → ?` or `c m → ?` for triads whose most-named middle `m` the tree hasn't been shown as a path. The pick is weighted by the estimated agreement on `m`, a soft weight, never a cut (§7). Over a thousand were available when this began.
- **Hot exploration** (15%). Fresh glyphs from the whole symbol space, weighted toward printable ASCII, then 2-byte, then 3- and 4-byte (Joseph, 2026-10-05). Exploration also favours **nodes that are still reaching out**: nodes with few settled edges get more tendrils. Settled nodes keep a floor of exploration, because creative sequences (`3E[|]3E[|]`) come from nodes that look settled.
- **Seeds** are tagged votes (§2a) and *contexts to query*. A seeded step is asked of the real minds when its sequence is served (tier 1), and its k\* is then found from short context upward like any other step.

## 6. Porting the triad era (concern 4)

Rounds r000–r012 ported **answers only**. Nothing derived is ported: no grown rows, fits, standings or stable counts, because each of those carries a rule of the old machinery. Every ported answer keeps all of its tags: round, sheet, position, sheet size, offered options (⟂ / tie / gap, forced or not), mind, family, prompt version, and the item's source as metadata. Concern 2 never reads the source.

| triad-era answer | ported as | not ported as, and why |
|---|---|---|
| what-comes-next: context (2–8 glyphs) → up to 3 proposals, best first, or "none" | continuation answers at that context node, with each proposal's rank; "none" as an end answer | — |
| triad: middle / only two / none / ties | short-context betweenness and orthogonality among the three glyphs (§2b) | **not edges.** A middle says `q` lies between `p` and `r`, not that `q` is adjacent to either |
| order: lines over a shown set, extras, none, ties, gaps | ordering and orthogonality constraints over the set (§2b) | **not edges.** A line orders a *subset*, so neighbours in a line need not be adjacent in any sequence |
| between: left run, GAP, right run → proposals or "none" | two-sided fill answers, their own kind | not continuations: the answer is conditioned on both sides |
| unparsed answers | recorded as an outcome with their reason | **not dropped silently.** The pilot found that failures cluster by glyph, so they are not missing at random |

**Checks before an answer enters the tree:**
- **Proposal content.** Each proposal must be one glyph, after NFC normalization. Words, fragments (`cir` was seen) and multi-glyph strings are kept as their own outcome and reported, never split into glyphs.
- **Selection.** Which contexts were asked, and how often, was steered by seeds, the old fit and the old planner, and the roster changed (glimmer early, second minds sampled). So agreement is always read as a *share* of the answers given at a node, per family, never as a raw count that rewards over-asked contexts.
- **Roster balance.** Families answered different volumes in different rounds, so per-family shares come before any pooling.
- **Out of scope.** The magnitude-era data in `.archive/2026-10-04-magnitude-era/` asked a different question and is not ported.

The port writes a report: counts in and out by kind, everything excluded with its reason, and spot checks of ported nodes against the raw ledgers. That report is the audit trail.

## 7. Open questions

- **Embeddings as tendrils.** Do a glyph's embedding neighbours predict confirmed edges better than its codepoint neighbours do? It's worth a fresh-agent spike that scores both against the stable sequences found so far. If they do, embeddings would choose what to probe. They would never be evidence. (Doubt: local models handle Unicode poorly.)
- **Agreement: soft, not a gate.** Agreement at a node is a quantity: the share of answers, and of families, giving the continuation. Priority (concern 3) uses it continuously, as a weight in the stochastic choice of what to ask. Nothing is filtered by it. Any "stable / not stable" label in a report is a read-off, and its cut-off is listed as a parameter chosen without evidence. (Joseph: *"I suspect it will be kind of a relative number or a soft threshold for the stochastic prioritization."*)
- **Reflection-symmetric cycles**, as in §3.
