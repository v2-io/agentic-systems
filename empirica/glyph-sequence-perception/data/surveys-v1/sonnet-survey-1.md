# Perception survey — sequences.md (append-only)

Fresh first-instinct pass. Not reading prior surveyors' work or other unicode
corpus material until after this pass, per the brief. Strength = my honest
confidence that a pairwise "which is more?" would recover this order. I'm
also flagging **immediacy**: does the magnitude arrive in the percept itself
(a fill level, a size, a count you can subitize) or does it require a symbol
lookup (a digit glyph, a letter, a learned convention)? Fill/size/count reads
feel pre-attentive; digit/letter reads feel like they route through
"recognize the symbol, then recall what it denotes" even when fast.

---

## Geometric Shapes (U+25A0–U+25FF)

### Circle fill ramp — ○ ◔ ◑ ◕ ●
`○ → ◔ → ◑ → ◕ → ●`
Empty circle, quarter-black (upper-right quadrant), half-black, three-quarter
black, full black. This is the strongest single find so far. It reads like a
literal pie-chart / battery-level gauge. **Immediacy: very high** — I don't
decode a symbol, I just *see* how much of the disc is filled, the same way
I'd read a loading spinner. Strength: very high, this would survive almost
any pairwise ordering task.
Note ◐ (left-half) and ◑ (right-half) are the *same* magnitude (50%) with
different orientation — I don't feel an ordering between them, they're a tie,
which itself is interesting data: my magnitude sense is insensitive to which
side is filled, only how much.

### Circle-outline nesting — ◌ ○ ◎ ◉ ●
`◌ → ○ → ◎ → ◉ → ●`
Dotted circle (barely-there) → plain outline → bullseye (outline + filled
core) → fisheye (mostly filled, thin ring) → solid black. Feels like
"density/solidity increasing," weaker and more composite a feel than the
fill-ramp above (it's blending "how much ink" with "how continuous the ink
is"). Strength: medium-high. Immediacy: high but slightly more effortful
than the fill ramp — I'm integrating over ink-density rather than reading a
single clean gauge.

### Square size ladder — ▫ ◽ ◻ □ / ▪ ◾ ◼ ■
`▫ → ◽ → ◻ → □` (white, small → small → medium → full-size)
`▪ → ◾ → ◼ → ■` (black, same size ladder)
Pure size/area magnitude, extremely immediate — literally "which glyph looks
bigger." Strength: high. This is about as close to a physical-magnitude
percept as text gets.

### Triangle size ladder — ▵ △ / ▴ ▲
`▵ → △` (white small-up-triangle → white up-triangle)
`▴ → ▲` (black small → black)
Same size-magnitude logic as squares, just two rungs instead of four.
Strength: high but shorter ladder = less impressive as a sequence. Immediacy:
high.

### Square fill-pattern series — □ ▤ ▥ ▦ ▧▨ ▩ ■
This one I'm *not* confident about as a magnitude ladder. Horizontal-fill,
vertical-fill, crosshatch, diagonal-fills, diagonal-crosshatch all sit at
"partially inked" but I don't feel a strong monotonic "more than" between
e.g. ▤ (horizontal lines) and ▦ (crosshatch) — crosshatch clearly reads as
"more ink than a single hatch," but the two single-direction hatches (▤
vs ▥) don't order against each other, they're a tie (orientation, not
magnitude, differs). So the ladder I'll actually claim is:
`□ → ▤ (or ▥, tied) → ▦ → ◫(bisected, ~50%, tie-ish with ▦) → ▩ → ■`
Strength: medium-low, this is a composite/soft feel, more "ink density"
than a clean percept. Flagging low confidence rather than omitting.

## Miscellaneous Symbols (U+2600–U+26FF)

### Die faces — ⚀ ⚁ ⚂ ⚃ ⚄ ⚅
`⚀ → ⚁ → ⚂ → ⚃ → ⚄ → ⚅`
Exactly the one Joseph predicted I'd predict :-) Count magnitude via
subitizing pips, textbook-strong. **Immediacy: very high** — I see the dot
count directly, no lookup. Strength: very high, essentially unimpeachable.
Worth noting *why* it's so strong relative to, say, digits '1'-'6': the dots
are literally countable objects in the glyph, not an arbitrary shape that
denotes a number by convention. That's the immediacy distinction Joseph
asked me to watch for — die faces sit at the "fill ramp" end, digits sit at
the "symbol lookup" end, even though both "encode" the same integers.

### Moon phase pair — ☽ ☾ (first/last quarter)
Only two present in this block so it's a weak ladder here (I'll look for the
fuller moon-phase set later, if any — I recall a proper waxing/waning set
sits elsewhere in the corpus, possibly in Miscellaneous Symbols and
Pictographs, but I'm deliberately not chasing it across blocks yet). As a
pair alone: strength low (not really an "ordering," just two shapes).

### Circle-with-dots progression — ⚆ ⚇ ⚈ ⚉
`⚆ (white, 1 dot) → ⚇ (white, 2 dots)` and separately
`⚈ (black, 1 dot) → ⚉ (black, 2 dots)`
Dot-count ordering within each color pair feels real and immediate
(subitizing again), but the black/white split breaks a single 4-way chain —
I don't get a clean feel for whether ⚇ (white, 2 dots) is "more" or "less"
than ⚈ (black, 1 dot); fill-color and dot-count are pulling in ways that
don't resolve to one axis. Strength: medium for the two 2-item sub-ladders,
low/absent for a unified 4-item chain.

### Yin-yang digrams — ⚊ ⚋ / ⚌ ⚍ ⚎ ⚏
Monogram yang/yin are just single unbroken/broken lines — a binary, not a
magnitude. The digrams (greater yang, lesser yin, lesser yang, greater yin)
encode a genuine philosophical intensity ordering (greater > lesser) but
**that ordering does not arrive in the percept** — I have to know the
semantic convention (which stacked-line pattern is "greater"). Immediacy:
low, this is a pure symbol-lookup case, a useful contrast item precisely
*because* it fails the immediacy test despite having a real denoted
magnitude. Strength (of the semantic ordering, if you already know the
convention): medium; strength of *perceptual* ordering without that
knowledge: near zero — I'd guess this is a case Joseph specifically wants
flagged as a negative/contrast example.

### Chess piece rank — ♙ ♘/♗ ♖ ♕ ♔ (white); ♟ ♞/♝ ♜ ♛ ♚ (black)
Pawn < knight≈bishop < rook < queen < king by conventional piece value. Real
semantic ladder, but knight/bishop tie in value (~3) and are visually
unrelated shapes, so it's not a clean single-file chain — it's more a
partial order. Immediacy: low, entirely dependent on knowing chess piece
values, nothing in the glyph shapes themselves suggests relative worth (a
king glyph isn't visually "bigger" than a queen's in any systematic way
across fonts). Strength: medium for someone who knows chess, ~zero
otherwise — flagging as another good immediacy-contrast case.

### Circle size/fill — ⚪ ⚫ / ⚬
Medium white circle, medium black circle (fill-only distinction, no
magnitude order I can feel — tie), medium-*small* white circle. If I had to
force an order by size alone: `⚬ → ⚪/⚫(tie)`. Weak, only two real size
rungs.

## Block Elements (U+2580–U+259F)

### Sparkline bar heights — ▁ ▂ ▃ ▄ ▅ ▆ ▇ █
`▁ → ▂ → ▃ → ▄ → ▅ → ▆ → ▇ → █`
Eighths of a block, bottom-anchored. This is the single best find of the
whole pass so far — it's literally the glyph set terminal sparkline
libraries use, and for good reason: it's a bar-height percept, the most
direct "more" available in text. **Immediacy: maximal** — there's no
decoding step whatsoever, it reads exactly like a tiny bar chart because it
functionally is one. Strength: about as close to certain as anything in
this survey; I'd expect this to survive not just pairwise but n-way sorting
by a fresh viewer with zero explanation.
Mirror set exists on the left-anchor axis (fill direction rather than
height): `▏ → ▎ → ▍ → ▌ → ▋ → ▊ → ▉ → █` — same strength, same
immediacy, just filling left-to-right instead of bottom-to-top. Interesting
that these two ladders share the endpoint █ (full block) — the "8/8" rung of
one axis is identical to the "8/8" rung of the other; they aren't in tension
with each other perceptually.

### Shade fill ramp — ░ ▒ ▓ █
`░ → ▒ → ▓ → █` (light shade → medium shade → dark shade → full)
A second, coarser fill-density ramp, this time via stipple density rather
than geometric fraction. Immediacy: very high, reads like a grayscale/opacity
gradient. Strength: very high, only four rungs so less impressive as a
sequence but I have no hesitation about the order.

### Quadrant popcount — ▘ / ▖▝ (tie) / ▚▞ (tie, diag) / ▛▙▜▟ / ▙... 
Genuinely tried this one and it doesn't cohere into a single ladder — the
quadrant blocks encode a 4-bit combination (which of 4 corners is filled),
not a scalar. Ones with 1 filled corner (▘▖▝▗) feel like a tied group,
ones with 2 opposite corners (▚▞, the "diagonal" pair) are ambiguous
against ones with 2 adjacent corners (there are none of those alone in this
set, only as parts of 3-corner glyphs), and ones with 3 filled corners
(▙▛▜▟) feel like a tied "almost full" group. So: `1-corner(tie) → 3-corner
(tie)` is the only ladder-like feel I get, by popcount, and even that's
soft because I have to consciously count corners rather than perceiving
"more" pre-attentively — closer to symbol-lookup than the linear bars above.
Strength: low-medium. Noting it mainly as a contrast case: same visual
*family* as the extremely strong bar/shade ramps above, but this one fails
to be a clean magnitude because the underlying structure is combinatorial
(position) rather than scalar (amount).

## Superscripts and Subscripts (U+2070–U+209F)

### Superscript/subscript digits — ⁰ ⁴ ⁵ ⁶ ⁷ ⁸ ⁹ / ₀ ₁ ₂ ... ₉
Semantic ordering is obviously total and correct (0 < 4 < 5 < ... < 9, and
the subscript run is complete 0-9). But I want to flag this explicitly as
the clean **negative/contrast case** for immediacy: there is nothing in the
*shape* of '⁷' that is bigger, fuller, or more-of-anything than '⁴' — I only
get the ordering because I've memorized which glyph denotes which integer.
It is symbol-lookup, full stop, same cognitive move as reading normal-sized
digits, just rendered smaller/raised. Strength of the recovered order: very
high (nobody would get 4 and 7 backwards). Immediacy: low. This is a good
paired contrast against the die faces / bar-block ramps above — same
strength of recoverable order, opposite mechanism.

## Number Forms (U+2150–U+218F)

### Roman numeral tally break — Ⅰ Ⅱ Ⅲ | Ⅳ Ⅴ Ⅵ Ⅶ Ⅷ Ⅸ Ⅹ Ⅺ Ⅻ
`Ⅰ → Ⅱ → Ⅲ` feels *partially* immediate — I'm essentially subitizing
vertical strokes (1, 2, 3 tally marks), similar mechanism to the die faces,
just less iconic. But the instant it hits `Ⅳ` the mechanism completely
changes: I have to know the subtractive convention (IV = 5-1) — the glyph
itself has *two* strokes, fewer than Ⅲ's three, yet denotes a bigger number.
So this sequence is a genuinely interesting **immediacy cliff within a
single family**: strong stroke-counting percept for the first three items,
then a hard transition to pure symbol/convention lookup for everything
after, and the post-cliff portion would actively mislead a naive stroke-
counter (Ⅳ has fewer marks than Ⅲ despite being "more"). Strength of the
overall semantic order I→XII: high (if you know Roman numerals). Immediacy:
high for I-III, then drops off a cliff — flagging the cliff itself as the
interesting datum, more than either segment alone.

### Vulgar fraction magnitude — ⅒ ⅑ ⅐ ⅙ ⅕ ⅛(wait, reorder) 
Actually let me state the fraction *values* properly rather than codepoint
order: as magnitudes, 1/10 < 1/9 < 1/8 < 1/7 < 1/6 < 1/5 < 1/4(not present)
< 1/3 < 2/5 < 1/2(not present) < 3/5 < 2/3 < 3/4 < 4/5 < 5/6 < 7/8. Knowing
which fraction is bigger is *purely* semantic/arithmetic — nothing in ⅐ vs
⅙ visually signals magnitude (if anything the *denominator glyph* is what
you'd have to read and then invert, an extra cognitive step beyond even
plain digit-reading). Immediacy: very low, arguably the least immediate
item in this survey — you must read a two-part symbol AND perform an
inversion. Interesting as an extreme contrast case. Strength (given full
arithmetic competence): high but effortful, not "felt."

## Arrows (U+2190–U+21FF)

### Arrowhead-count intensity — → ⇉ ⇶
`→ (1 arrow) → ⇉ (2 paired rightwards) → ⇶ (3 rightwards arrows)`
A genuine count/multiplicity ladder that reads immediately as "more
insistence / more flow" — like a repeated-icon intensity cue (think '!!!' or
stacked chevrons on a fast-forward button). Immediacy: high, I subitize the
arrow count without needing to think about what it denotes. Strength: high
for 1→2→3, though this specific triple only exists for rightwards in this
block (I didn't spot a leftwards/up/down triple, so it's a short, direction-
locked ladder rather than a full family).

### Single vs double vs triple shaft arrows — → ⇒ ⇛
`→ (single line arrow) → ⇒ (double line/"double arrow") → ⇛ (triple arrow)`
Distinct from the count-of-icons ladder above — here it's stroke-multiplicity
*within one arrow glyph* (single shaft, double shaft, triple shaft), which
conventionally reads as "stronger implication" in logic notation (→ vs ⇒)
but that convention is learned, not seen — a naive viewer might read ⇒ as
just "a fancier arrow," not necessarily "more" of anything. Immediacy:
medium (the visual "thickening/doubling" does suggest emphasis fairly
directly, more than pure symbol lookup, but less than a fill or count
ramp). Strength: medium, and mostly familiar to people with a math/logic
background (⇒ = implies, stronger commitment than →).

### Arrow negation-stroke count — ↚ ↛ vs ⇷⇸ (1 stroke) vs ⇺⇻ (2 strokes)
Strokes-through-the-shaft counting 1 vs 2 as "more blocked / more strongly
negated." I can feel a weak ordering (2 strokes reads as "more forbidden"
than 1) but it's a stretch — most viewers encounter ↛ as simply "not," a
boolean, and wouldn't naturally expect a *graded* negation. Strength: low,
flagging as attempted-and-mostly-failed rather than omitting per the
"strength of feel, even if low" spirit of the brief.

## Enclosed Alphanumerics (U+2460–U+24FF)

### Circled digits — ① ② ③ ... ⑳
Codepoint order and semantic order coincide perfectly here, and the
"circle" decoration adds nothing perceptual to the ordering — it's just
digit-recognition-in-a-badge. Same immediacy verdict as plain digits: low
(symbol lookup), strength: high (order is unambiguous once you can read
digits).

## Summary of this pass — strongest finds, ranked by my confidence

1. **Sparkline bars ▁▂▃▄▅▆▇█** — strongest single item in the whole survey.
   Maximal immediacy, maximal strength.
2. **Shade ramp ░▒▓█** — same family, very strong, shorter.
3. **Circle fill ramp ○◔◑◕●** — strongest "shape as gauge" find, very high
   immediacy.
4. **Die faces ⚀⚁⚂⚃⚄⚅** — the one everyone (Joseph included) expected;
   confirmed as maximally strong and maximally immediate, via subitizing
   rather than symbol convention.
5. Square/triangle size ladders (▫◽◻□, ▵△, ▴▲) — strong, simple, short.
6. Circle-outline density (◌○◎◉●) — good but composite/softer than #3.

**Best immediacy-cliff / contrast finds** (real semantic magnitude exists
but does NOT arrive perceptually, or arrives only partway):
- Roman numerals Ⅰ Ⅱ Ⅲ | Ⅳ...Ⅻ — stroke-counting works for I-III, then a
  hard cliff into pure convention.
- Superscript/subscript digits — pure convention throughout, no shape cue.
- Vulgar fractions (⅐⅙⅓ etc.) — least immediate item found; requires
  reading a two-part symbol and performing an inversion.
- Yin-yang digrams (greater/lesser yang/yin) — real semantic magnitude,
  zero perceptual cue without the convention.
- Chess piece rank — real semantic magnitude, zero perceptual cue, plus a
  genuine tie (knight≈bishop) that breaks single-file ordering.

**What surprised me most:** how cleanly the "immediacy cliff" showed up
*within a single family* (Roman numerals I-III vs IV+) rather than only
between families — I expected immediacy to be a property of a symbol *set*,
but it's really a property of the *specific comparison*, and some sets
straddle both regimes.

I've covered Geometric Shapes, Block Elements, Miscellaneous Symbols,
Arrows, Superscripts/Subscripts, Number Forms, and Enclosed Alphanumerics in
this pass. Mathematical Operators, Misc Technical, Dingbats, Spacing

Stopping here to get first-instinct findings in front of Joseph, rather
