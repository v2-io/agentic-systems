# Audit: the v1.0 campaign and its analysis (2026-10-03)

*An independent critical pass over the registered v1.0 campaign: `protocol/PROTOCOL-v1.0.md`, `PREDICTIONS-v1.0.md`, the harness in `harness/runner/`, the ledgers in `data/runs-v1/`, the generated `analysis/v1.0-results.md`, and the synthesis `analysis/2026-10-03-v1.0-findings.md` (hereafter "the findings"). The auditor is a Claude Opus 5.5 agent with no part in building or running the campaign. I read everything and wrote only inside `analysis/verification/`.*

*`audit_v1.py` in this directory re-derives the numbers with its own ledger loader, its own answer parser, and its own statistics. It imports the harness parser in exactly one place (§1, to diff the two parsers answer by answer). Run it from anywhere with `python3 analysis/verification/audit_v1.py [section …]`. It reads the ledgers as they are on disk at run time. Local-model runs were still being appended while I worked, so the phi4mini numbers below come from a later snapshot (about 22:30 UTC) than `v1.0-results.md` does. No API-model number depends on the snapshot.*

## What matters most

1. **qwen3-4b never answered a single item. Every qwen number in the results and the findings is a parser artifact.** All 4,085 qwen calls return exactly 48 output tokens of truncated reasoning that begins "First, the user is asking about two symbols: ⬟ and ▲. I need to respond based on my first instinct only…". Two settings combine to produce this. The adapter passes `think: false`, but qwen3 writes its reasoning into the visible content anyway. And `num_predict` is 48, so the text is cut off before any answer appears. Parser p1.2 then reads the leading word "First," as the fallback answer *first* (`instruments.py` L106). So every presentation scores as a win for the first-shown glyph.
   - That one artifact produces:
     - the "100% first-position answers and 99% cycles" that the findings call "the limiting case" of position bias;
     - qwen's PASS on P2 (⟂ share 0.00) and on P5s (cycle rate 0.99);
     - "qwen3-4b has no consistent edges at all", because "first" in both orders always flips;
     - qwen's 1:1 cell on every conflict item.
   - The gestalt parser does something similar. It pulls glyphs out of qwen's restatement of the prompt ("symbols in scrambled order: ⚀ ⚁ ⚄ …"), so the qwen gestalt column scores the stimulus's own shuffle against the reference.
   - qwen3-4b should be reported as *no valid data (adapter failure)* and removed from every verdict. A rerun needs either a larger `num_predict` with the reasoning stripped or the model's no-think control applied the way it actually takes effect. The shakedown had no qwen row, so this adapter was never exercised before the campaign.

2. **In this design the registered cycle rate measures position-uniformity, not intransitivity.** Orientation set 0 presents (A,B), (B,C), (C,A). Its eight possible answer patterns produce a 3-cycle exactly when all three answers pick the first-shown glyph or all three pick the second-shown glyph. The other six patterns are transitive. So every cycle P5 counts is a position-uniform set by construction. The findings' explanation for Sonnet-5 is correct as far as it goes: all 31 of its cycles are all-second sets (§5). But the same holds for every cycle of every judge. The statistic cannot tell a mind that is intransitive from one that has a position habit.
   - The separable test uses bias-immune edges, meaning pairs that come out consistent-directed across both orientation sets (§13). Among triads whose three pairs are all bias-immune, the cycle rate is:
     - 0 of 32–87 for every frontier run;
     - **1 of 46 for llama3.2-3b** and 0 of 8 for gemma3-4b.
   - Small models are therefore not intransitive where they are consistent. llama's raw cycle rate (0.13) is also *below* the 0.25 that random orientation would give, so it does not indicate position bias either.
   - What separates the tiers is how often a judge is consistent at all:
     - cross-orientation agreement is 0.40–0.61 for small models against 0.66–0.90 for frontier runs;
     - small models use ⟂ far less.
   - So claim 4 as rewritten in the findings ("capability buys discipline *and* the axes") holds for ⟂ use, for consistency and for the two correlates. It does not hold for transitivity. P5 should be re-specified, or reported next to the bias-immune version.

3. **The sheet-completeness gate never re-asked its worst sheets, and the analysis scores missing answers as substantive ones.**
   - `run.py` L109 and `inventory.py` treat a row as done when `not r.get("sheet_incomplete")`. A sheet with zero parseable items is flagged `0.0`, and `0.0` is falsy. So exactly the fully truncated sheets pass as complete.
   - 20 rows are flagged 0.0 and none was ever re-asked (§2):
     - grok46: format-perp 3 sheets / 120 presentations; forced 2 / 80; tie 1 / 40; triads 9 sheets (198 presentations still unanswered); top40b 1 / 40;
     - gemini38flash: top40b 4 / 160.
   - The raw texts are grok streams that stop mid-JSON with `stop: end_turn`. INVENTORY.md and the results table report these runs as "0 failed" (format-perp-sheet-grok46: 16 calls, 16 ok).
   - Downstream, two statistics impute the missing answers, although the protocol says "unparsed is reported, never imputed":
     - **P1:** a tie-directed pair whose perp presentation is missing counts as *not dissolved* (`analyze_v1.py` L180–181). grok's 0.26 becomes **31/78 = 0.40 [0.30, 0.51]** once unknown pairs are excluded (§3). P1 still fails for grok. But grok is no longer the low end of the range the findings quote ("26–80%"). claude-sonnet-5-5 single (0.27) is.
     - **Cross-orientation agreement:** an unknown pair counts as disagreement (L239–241). grok moves from 0.62 to 0.79 when only known pairs are counted.
   - Every other API run changes by 0.01 or less under either correction. Among local runs, llama's agreement moves from 0.58 to 0.61.

4. **The registered predictions are universal claims, but the results score them cell by cell, and the findings leave out two of the failures.**
   - The registered thresholds say "for every frontier judge" or "for every small local model". Scored as written, with qwen removed (item 1):
     - **pass:** P2, P3, P4, P7, P9;
     - **fail:** P1, P5, P6, P8, P11, P13, P14, P15;
     - **depends on reading:** P10 and P12 (next bullet).
   - The findings report cell counts instead ("P5 passes 8 of 9", "P14 passes 3 of 8"). Nowhere does a reader see that 8 of the 15 registered predictions failed as written.
   - **P6 failed** (haiku45 uniform-stratum ⟂ = 0.73) and **P5s failed** for llama3.2-3b (0.13 < 0.15). Neither is mentioned in the findings. The only other prediction the findings omit is P2, which passed.
   - **P10–P12 are worded differently from P9.** P9 says "for every frontier judge". P10–P12 say "of committed frontier answers" or "of frontier presentations", which reads naturally as pooled across the frontier. Pooled (§6):
     - P10a: 43/43;
     - P10b: 95/117 = 0.81, PASS;
     - P11: 36/101 = 0.36, FAIL;
     - **P12: 73/108 = 0.68, PASS**, where the findings say "passes only 4 of 9";
     - P13's noise half: 60/95 = 0.63, FAIL either way.

   The scoring code (`analyze_v1.py`) was first committed in cdcc1626, about 30 minutes into data collection. Writing analysis code after registration is normal. It is also where the unregistered choices above entered: missing answers imputed as not dissolved, per-run rather than pooled scoring, and an `n<min` rule. Each deserves a sentence in the findings.

5. **Two of the findings' strongest specimens rest on details that the aggregates hide.**
   - **₈ vs 3.** The findings list "₈ > 3" among the items where compiled decode wins "for every frontier judge". At the item level the Sonnet family chooses the full-size 3:
     - claude-sonnet-5-5 single 0 of 6;
     - claude-sonnet-5-5 sheet 2 of 5;
     - claude-sonnet-5 1 of 5.

     claude-sonnet-5 also loses ⁹ vs 2 (2 of 5). P9 passes only because it pools 13 items. For the Sonnet models, size beats value on subscripts. That bears directly on "Size breaks value ties" and on "Where a compiled numeric reading exists, it arbitrates".
   - **Opus on unfold.** "Opus reconstructs it perfectly (τ 1.00)" was the same answer on all three shuffles: `|)}>` with `EXTRA -=`. Opus ordered four of the six glyphs, in reverse, and excluded `-` and `=` from the order. τ is computed only over the glyphs in the answer, so the score is 1.00. It is a stable, non-chance answer: a random 4-arrangement scores |τ| = 1 with probability 0.08. But it reconstructs a sub-order, not unfold, and Opus's P14 PASS on unfold rests on it. gemini-3.8-flash's third shuffle has the same shape. Gestalt rows should report coverage (k of n) beside τ.

6. **"Commitments improve under ⟂" (P4) is a selection effect.** On pairs that a judge committed to under *both* forced and perp, the value-correlate is identical in 6 of 8 frontier runs (§10). For example, gemini-3.1-pro scores 20/20 vs 20/20 and claude-sonnet-5 20/20 vs 20/20. Only claude-opus-5-5 (+2 pairs) and claude-sonnet-5-5 single (+1) change. The rise comes from the pairs committed only under forced, whose value accuracy is 0.40–0.92: under perp the judges decline exactly those pairs with ⟂. P4 passes as registered, since it asked for direction only. But "Committed numeric edges get *more* accurate under ⟂" should say that ⟂ removes the less accurate commitments, not that the same commitments become more accurate. The selection story is, if anything, stronger evidence for claim 3.

7. **The pilot comparison is cleaner on a different measure, and the pilot-condition arm does not reproduce the pilot's tie condition.**
   - **The pilot on the same 160 pairs** (§4). Its dissolution was **59/77 = 0.77**, not the 0.80 measured over all 533 pairs. On these pairs the pilot's walk2 judges used ≈ on **81 of 160 (51%)**. Every v1.0 Claude run used ≈ on 0.00 of the same pairs under *tie*, and so did the pilot-condition arm. So the v1.0 tie format is a different instrument from walk2. Its ≈ wording ("comparable and equal on a shared axis") is walk5b's, and the walk2 brief itself is not preserved in `harness/pilot-scripts/`. The P1 denominators are therefore different populations.
   - **The like-for-like comparison** is the ⟂ share of perp presentations on the pilot-replication stratum:
     - pilot walk3: 0.88;
     - pilot-condition arm: 0.76;
     - claude-sonnet-5: 0.56; claude-sonnet-5-5 sheet: 0.47; claude-sonnet-5-5 single: 0.41.

     The findings' conclusion (the magnitude does not replicate) survives on this measure, more cleanly than on P1.
   - **Bundled factors in the pilot-condition arm.** The findings compare the arm's 0.76 with "⟂ shares of 0.37 and 0.47". Those two figures cover all strata. On the arm's own stratum they are 0.41 and 0.47. The arm's spec also records that the subagents ran with the user's global CLAUDE.md loaded and at agent-default effort, not `--effort low`. The findings list "harness, wording and sheet size" as the bundled factors. The global context, which is heavy on truth-honoring and refusing to overclaim, and the effort level belong on that list. The arm's tie format is also walk5b wording without ⟂, not the pilot's walk2 wording.

## Other findings

**Design and interpretation**

- **The seed-coverage defect is bigger than the PROTOCOL note makes it sound.**
  - grok-1, the survey left out, holds 293 of the 849 sequence records. Together with sonnet-survey-3 and sonnet-survey-4 the defect drops 350 records (41%). The seed stratum's surveyors are fable-1, sonnet5-1 and sonnet-survey-1/2: all Anthropic-family authors, and grok-1 was the only non-Anthropic surveyor.
  - "Graduation by retest works" therefore means that sequences written by Claude-family models are confirmed by Claude, Grok, GPT and Gemini judges. That cross-family agreement is real (gemini-3.1-pro 82/82). But no non-Claude surveyor's sequences were retested.
  - The same applies to P3's "within-locus" pairs and to the seed-local triads: their loci are Claude-family perceptions.
- **‱ vs % does not separate element count from decode.** ‱ is per *ten thousand*. A judge that decodes the sign can still call ‱ "more" because 10,000 > 100. The Arabic ؊ carries the same ambiguity. So "‱ beats % only for the Sonnet models" may be a difference in which decoded quantity a judge attends to (denominator or fraction), not a visual reading. On this battery, P11 cannot support "weak decode splits toward the visual".
- **The yang/yin items carry a semantic reading.** ☰ (heaven, the creative) and solid yang lines have a compiled cultural "more". P10b's "yang/ink side wins" is therefore confounded with semantic decode, and "line count first, then fill" is one of at least two readings.
- **P15's τ ≥ 0.60 is weak for 4-rung ladders.** A single adjacent swap gives 0.67. A random 4-arrangement reaches 0.67 or more with probability 1/3 per shuffle. Among 5-arrangements only 2% reach τ = 1. On elab-n, seven of eight frontier runs produce the same order `nɳɲŋ` on at least two shuffles. So the judges agree with each other on an order that differs from Grok's written reference by one swap. That says more about the reference than about the judges.
- **Seed-retest chance baselines** (§9). If each committed pair were oriented at random, a triad would "agree with the written order" with probability:
  - 0.29–0.37 for the frontier runs' triad mix;
  - 0.42–0.47 for the small models'.

  The frontier figures (0.90–1.00) are far above chance. The small models' (llama 0.60 [0.46, 0.74], gemma 0.67) are slightly above it, which fits "near chance" loosely. The baseline belongs in the table.
- **"First instinct" was not equalized across judges.** At `--effort low`:
  - claude-haiku-4-5 spends a mean of 403 thinking tokens on every call, in 100% of calls;
  - claude-sonnet-5 spends 0; claude-opus-5-5 29; claude-sonnet-5-5 60.

  claude-opus-5-5 also writes visible decoding before its answer in about 2% of calls ("These are Kharoshthi numerals: 𐩆 is 100, 𐩇 is 1000"). Three consequences:
  - The value-correlate partly measures named-codepoint knowledge.
  - Haiku's misses on P6 and P8 come from a judge that deliberated on every item.
  - The sonnet55 mode control confounds mode with thinking: single mode averages 60 thinking tokens per call, sheet mode 0.

  "Thinking-token counts vs ⟂ use" was registered as exploratory, and the findings do not report it.
- **The Wilson intervals treat correlated units as independent.** Conflict cells pool 2 orders × 3 reps of each item, so P9's n = 78 rests on 13 items. Triad edges contain repeated glyph pairs. Read the intervals as lower bounds on the uncertainty.

**Parsing**

- **The leading-glyph rule misreads answers that start with an explanation.** p1.2 takes the first whitespace token as the answer if it is a stimulus glyph. Examples:
  - "㏔ is "MB"-like (square mb). ᱟ is Ol Chiki letter.⏎⏎⟂" parses as `dir ㏔`;
  - "㎪ is kPa, ㎫ is MPa. MPa is 1000x larger.⏎⏎㎫, vastly" parses as `dir ㎪`, the reverse of the answer.

  Comparing the harness parse with a parse of the last line:
  - about 45 claude-opus-5-5 and about 10 claude-sonnet-5-5 single-mode answers disagree. That is about 1%, and none of the verdicts I recomputed moved.
  - **phi4mini is another matter: 35% of its triad answers are long, and 208 disagree.** hermes3: 19. If phi4mini enters a future analysis, as its now-complete ledgers invite, its parse needs repair first.
- **`has_a and has_b → first mentioned`** resolves two-answer replies toward the first-shown glyph, as in mistral's "㎜, somewhat / 𝝌, ≈". It affects a few dozen mistral answers.

**Records and statements that do not match the data**

- **gpt-oss 20B produced no data.** `triads-perp-single-gptoss20b/` holds only `spec.json`, and `local-queue.sh` schedules it last, for the full queue. Yet both the findings ("gpt-oss ran triads, conflict and gestalt only") and the append-only PROTOCOL note state in the past tense that it ran.
- **"Thirteen judges from seven model families"** does not match the panel listed directly below it: fifteen models from nine vendors. Fifteen labels have data (fourteen models plus the pilot-condition sonnet-agent), and one of those (qwen) has none that is valid.
- **"Every judge was stripped of … memory"** overstates the isolation:
  - For agy (Gemini) `judges-v1.json` says "HOME not isolated (agy's knowledge/brain stores exist)".
  - Claude judges see the account email.
  - The per-call evidence that is recorded holds up: every Claude call reports `tools: []`, every agy call `num_turns: 1`, and no a2 grok call shows a `tool_call` event.
- **The grok search query cannot be checked from the record.** "searched 'unicode musical symbols forte piano'" does not appear in any ledger. The adapter discards tool-call content and keeps only event types. The aborted run shows 7 `tool_call` events, each next to a `plan` event, of unrecorded kind. The observation is plausible, but its quoted query has no source in the repository.
- **SIGNA.** "Every frontier judge places ⚬ below ╍" is false for claude-haiku-4-5, whose order is `·╶╌╍○⚬━⬤◎◉═`.
- **Size vs count.** "Sonnet-5 and GPT split on ⁖ vs ●" is inaccurate for claude-sonnet-5, which prefers ⁖ 4:2 (count over size).
- **Top-40.** The findings say "ranks 64 survey sequences … 31 of 64 have stability ≥ 0.90". The committed `top40.md` ranks 117, of which 32 reach 0.90 (28 of the first 64).
  - The step score takes each judge's dominant direction after the fact, so a judge that reads a ladder backwards still scores full.
  - The 160 gemini-3.8-flash top40b presentations lost to the gate bug count as unconfirmed rungs.
- **Held-out leakage.** The builder's pilot census skipped workflow output files. But `wr2n0is6k.json` holds the pilot's *salted* sequences, which pilot judges did see. Eight of its salt glyphs are in the v1.0 seed stratum (🀰 ◐ 🌖 🌘 ‵ ‶ ‷ ‾). Four of them (◐ ‵ ‶ ‷) occur in 20 triad presentations (1.1%). This is numerically negligible, but it breaks the protocol's "never shown in any pilot stimulus" rule.
- **The PROTOCOL post-freeze note for p1.2** says the bug was found "while inspecting interim gestalt arrangements". The data were seen before that parser change. The change fixes a real bug, and its candour is appropriate. But it is a data-informed analysis change, not a pre-data one.

## What re-derived

My code is independent of the harness except in §1, so its agreement with `v1.0-results.md` counts as evidence.

- **Registration timing.** The PREDICTIONS commit 026cfb56 is 20:11:37 UTC and the first non-shakedown ledger row is 20:11:59 UTC. This rests on a local author timestamp, not an external anchor. The registered text is unchanged since: only the errata and post-freeze notes were appended.
- **Stimulus construction.**
  - No sheet in any run contains both orders of a pair, or both orientation sets of a triad.
  - The fated option order varies per item (six permutations in use).
  - No banned glyph (≈ ⟂ ⊥) appears in any v1.0 stimulus.
  - The seed-coverage defect is exactly as the PROTOCOL note describes it (`type` vs `record_type`).
- **Correlates and cycles for every API judge** match the results table to within one or two edges. The small differences come from the ambiguous answers my parser leaves unclassified:
  - value-correlates 0.95–1.00 and ink-correlates 0.64–0.83;
  - P7f 9/9; the ink misses as reported.
- **The claude-sonnet-5 explanation.** Its first-shown share is 0.33, and all 31 of its cycles are all-second sets.
- **P3 is robust.** It also passes when "survival" is read as conditional on being tie-directed: within-locus minus mixed is +0.42 to +0.79 for all 8 runs.
- **P9 pooled per judge** reproduces (0.81–1.00), and every P9 item is a genuine value-vs-ink conflict by measured ink. The seven-segment glyphs are astral and unmeasured. The fraction items' ink differences are small (0.011–0.035).
- **Dissolution and ⟂ shares** reproduce for every run except where item 3 applies.

## Scope and limits of this audit

What I did not do:

- **Stimulus rebuild.** I did not re-run `stimuli.py`, because it writes into `data/`. The byte-identical rebuild claim is unchecked.
- **ollama and the judges.** I did not touch ollama or call any judge.
- **Ink pipeline.** I did not audit the ink measurements themselves; the prior audit covers their cross-face caveats.
- **Transcripts.** I did not read the pilot-condition transcripts in `~/.cache/gmp-judges/` (they embed private context).
- **Raw sheet answers.** I did not inspect gemini or gpt sheet answers beyond what the parser diff surfaced.
- **Top-40.** I did not audit top-40 beyond its counts and scoring rule.

My parser is stricter than the harness's: an answer that carries two signals stays unclassified. So where my counts differ by an edge or two, the difference is that choice, not a defect. This report's finding list is its content; areas it does not mention were not necessarily checked.

On the work itself: the campaign is unusually well recorded. Every raw answer is kept verbatim, every change is logged append-only, and the registration happened before the data. That record is what made each finding above checkable in minutes. The two defects that matter most share a shape. In both, a statistic looked like the thing it was named for: "first" read as an answer, and "cycle" read as intransitivity. In neither case was the statistic checked against raw answers or against the design's own combinatorics before conclusions were built on it.
