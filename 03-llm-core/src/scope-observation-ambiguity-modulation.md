# Scope: Goal-Distortion of a Belief, and What Observation Sharpness Does to It

How much a goal-coupled update can move a belief *off the truth* is the quantity Part II's approximation error for Class 3 (Coupled) agents depends on, and it is a different quantity from how much the belief *depends on the goal*. The first is distortion: the reported belief's departure from the conditional law of the world given the agent's information — the reliability term of #der-directed-separation's Scope Condition. The second is goal-dependence: the displacement of the goal-conditional belief from the goal-average belief, which #deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound bounds in transferred goal information and which calibrated allocation also produces. This segment is about the first. Two things are landed. An exact, model-free statement: a distorted belief's divergence from the calibrated one is bounded by the range of their log-density ratio, so the only thing that limits distortion without a model of the processor is how large a reweighting the processor applies. And a hypothesis about Class 3 processors — that the distortion is a *fixed-gain tilt* along the goal's desirability of world-states — under which the divergence factors into a processor gain times the residual spread of the goal-relevant quantity under the calibrated posterior, and the engineering intuition this segment has always carried becomes a derived consequence: a test failure leaves no room for motivated reading because it pins the quantity the goal cares about; "the code is messy" leaves a lot. Whether real coupled architectures tilt with a fixed gain is the hypothesis, and it has a regression test.

## Formal Expression

*[Definition (calibrated-base-and-distortion)]*

Fix an event $e_\tau$, the prior epistemic state $M_{\tau^-}$ and the realized action. A goal-coupled update reports $M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}$, a probability measure on world-states that may vary with the goal $G$. Write

$$\pi \;:=\; P_G\big(\Omega_\tau \in \cdot \mid M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}\big)$$

for the **calibrated belief**: the recalibration of the report, the conditional law of the world given the report's own value under goal $G$'s law ( #der-directed-separation, Scope Condition). When the report is one-to-one in the information $\mathcal H$ the update conditions on — the external event including the action completion, with whatever the update encoded from it — this is $P(\Omega_\tau \in \cdot \mid \mathcal H)$, the belief a goal-blind update reports; and under (NWI) it is the same law for every goal. The report's **distortion** is its divergence from $\pi$, $\mathrm{KL}(\pi \,\Vert\, M_{\tau^+}^{(G)})$ or $\mathrm{TV}(M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}, \pi)$; it is the per-event form of the reliability term, zero for every $G$ exactly when the report is calibrated under each goal's law — which is when the agent's goal-dependence is allocation only. A report that gives up encoded information (reports the conditional law given part of what it holds) has $\pi$ equal to itself and distortion zero; a tilt of a calibrated belief has that belief as its recalibration, so for a tilt $\pi$ is the belief the tilt acted on.

*[Derived (log-ratio-range-bound, model-free)]*

Let $M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}$ and $\pi$ be mutually absolutely continuous, with density ratio $r_G = dM_{\tau^+}^{(G)} / d\pi$, and let $\log r_G$ take values in an interval of length $L_G$ on the support of $\pi$. Then

$$\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi \,\Vert\, M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}\big) \;=\; \log \mathbb{E}_\pi[\tilde r_G] - \mathbb{E}_\pi[\log \tilde r_G] \quad\text{for any } \tilde r_G \propto r_G,$$

the Jensen gap of $\log \tilde r_G$ under $\pi$ (with $r_G$ itself normalized this reads $-\mathbb{E}_\pi \log r_G$), and

$$\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi \,\Vert\, M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}\big) \;\leq\; \tfrac{L_G^2}{8}, \qquad \mathrm{KL}\big(M_{\tau^+}^{(G)} \,\Vert\, \pi\big) \;\leq\; \tfrac{L_G^2}{8}, \qquad \mathrm{TV}\big(M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}, \pi\big) \;\leq\; \tfrac{L_G}{4}.$$

*Derivation.* Any $M_{\tau^+}^{(G)} \ll \pi$ with $\pi \ll M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}$ is the multiplicative reweighting $M_{\tau^+}^{(G)} = \pi \cdot \tilde r_G / \mathbb{E}_\pi \tilde r_G$ for any positive $\tilde r_G$ proportional to the density ratio; then $\log(d\pi / dM_{\tau^+}^{(G)}) = \log \mathbb{E}_\pi \tilde r_G - \log \tilde r_G$, and integrating against $\pi$ gives the identity. The first bound is Hoeffding's lemma, $\log \mathbb{E} e^{X} \leq \mathbb{E} X + (b - a)^2 / 8$ for $a \leq X \leq b$, applied to $X = \log \tilde r_G$ under $\pi$. For the reverse direction, $\mathbb{E}_{M^{(G)}} e^{-X} = \mathbb{E}_\pi[e^{X} e^{-X}] / \mathbb{E}_\pi e^X = 1 / \mathbb{E}_\pi e^{X}$, so Hoeffding under $M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}$ for $-X$ gives $-\log \mathbb{E}_\pi e^X \leq -\mathbb{E}_{M^{(G)}} X + L_G^2 / 8$, which is $\mathrm{KL}(M_{\tau^+}^{(G)} \Vert \pi) = \mathbb{E}_{M^{(G)}} X - \log \mathbb{E}_\pi e^X \leq L_G^2 / 8$; Pinsker gives the total-variation bound. The reverse direction uses that the two supports coincide. $\square$

The statement is model-free, and what it says is exactly as much as a model-free statement can: the distortion is bounded by the *size* of the reweighting the processor applies, and by nothing about the evidence. It does not say that sharp observations leave no room — a model-free distortion can move a sharp posterior arbitrarily far: $\pi = (0.99, 0.01)$ reported as $(0.5, 0.5)$ has $\mathrm{KL}(\pi \Vert M^+) = 0.637$ nats, and $L$ is correspondingly large. Any dependence of distortion on how sharp the evidence is has to come from a model of how the processor's reweighting scales with the evidence.

*[Hypothesis (fixed-gain-goal-tilt)]*

A Class 3 (Coupled) update distorts by a **fixed-gain tilt along the goal's desirability of world-states**:

$$M_{\tau^+}^{(G)} \;=\; \frac{\pi \cdot e^{\lambda\, u_G}}{\mathbb{E}_\pi[e^{\lambda\, u_G}]},$$

where $u_G(\omega)$ is the goal's desirability of world-state $\omega$ and $\lambda \geq 0$ is a **distortion gain**, a property of the processor that does not depend on how sharp the evidence is. This is Bayes' rule with a goal-pseudo-likelihood; it is the "process-form, multiplicative" cell of #disc-partial-coupling-pathways (precision weighting, attention gain, motivated reweighting) with the exponent fixed to the goal's utility. Under the hypothesis,

$$\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi \,\Vert\, M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}\big) \;=\; \Lambda_\pi(\lambda) - \lambda\, \mathbb{E}_\pi[u_G] \;=\; \tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\, \mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G) + O(\lambda^3),$$

with $\Lambda_\pi$ the cumulant generating function of $u_G$ under $\pi$ (the identity above with $\log r_G = \lambda u_G$; the expansion is the cumulant expansion), and with $L_G = \lambda \cdot \mathrm{range}_\pi(u_G)$ in the range bound. The exact quantity is the cumulant generating function, which depends on the whole law of $u_G$ under $\pi$; $\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G)$ is its leading order and not a bound (at $\lambda = 1$, $\pi(\Omega = 1) = 0.1$ the divergence is $0.0586$ against a leading term of $0.045$). The sharpness dependence is nevertheless *exact*, by Bennett's lemma. Write $X = u_G - \mathbb E_\pi u_G$ and $b = \sup_{\mathrm{supp}\,\pi} u_G - \mathbb E_\pi u_G$; since $(e^{\lambda x} - 1 - \lambda x) / x^2$ is nondecreasing in $x$, $e^{\lambda X} \leq 1 + \lambda X + X^2\, \psi(\lambda b) / b^2$ with $\psi(x) = e^x - 1 - x$, and taking $\mathbb E_\pi$ and $\log(1 + y) \leq y$,

$$\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi \,\Vert\, M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}\big) \;=\; \log \mathbb E_\pi e^{\lambda X} \;\leq\; \mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G)\,\frac{e^{\lambda b} - 1 - \lambda b}{b^2}, \qquad \lambda \geq 0$$

(for a pessimistic tilt, $\lambda \lt 0$, the same lemma applied to $-X$ with $b$ the lower deviation; the reverse divergence keeps the range bound). The bound holds for every gain, is linear in $\mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G)$, is zero when the observation pins $u_G$, reduces to $\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G)$ as $\lambda b \to 0$, and is tight (the ratio of divergence to bound reaches $1.0000$ over $20000$ random instances; at $\lambda = 1$ the four values below are bounded by $0.149$, $0.062$, $0.0071$, $0.00072$). The distortion therefore factors: a **processor** factor, $\lambda$ at low gain and $\psi(\lambda b) / b^2$ in general — which depends on the *support* of $\pi$ and grows exponentially in the gain — and an **observation-times-goal** factor $\mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G)$ (or $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$, or $\mathrm{range}_\pi(u_G)$ in the range bound), the residual spread of the goal-relevant quantity under the calibrated posterior. The second factor is computed from the calibrated posterior and the goal's desirability function, with no processor in it; it is zero when the observation pins down the goal-relevant quantity even if $\Omega_\tau$ stays uncertain in goal-irrelevant directions, which is sharper than any entropy of $\Omega_\tau$ and matches the examples exactly — a test result pins $u_G$, "the code is messy" does not. And it is zero for allocation-only agents ($\lambda = 0$) by construction. The support factor adds an engineering statement the leading order misses: at $\lambda = 10$ an observation leaving $0.1\%$ of mass on the desirable state ($\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G) = 0.03$) still allows $3.13$ nats of distortion, so for a high-gain processor "the observation pins $u_G$" has to mean the residual mass on high-$u_G$ states is *zero*, not small.

*What the hypothesis is, and how it fails.* The sharpness dependence is entirely the hypothesis that the gain is fixed. With $\lambda = 1$ and $u = (0, 1)$ on a binary world, $\mathrm{KL}(\pi \Vert M^+)$ is $0.120$, $0.059$, $0.0070$ and $0.00072$ nats as $\pi(\Omega = 1)$ goes $0.5$, $0.1$, $0.01$, $0.001$ — sharp evidence leaves less room. A gain that grows as the evidence sharpens defeats it: with $\lambda = 0.5 / \mathrm{sd}_\pi(u)$ the divergence rises to $8.9$ nats at $\pi(\Omega = 1) = 0.001$. Two further things the hypothesis commits to. The direction: that the distortion points along the goal's *utility* — wishful thinking; fear-driven pessimism, plan-consistency pressure and sycophancy toward an interlocutor's goal need not, and for them $u_G$ is not the goal's desirability but some other functional, which the regression test below would show as a poor fit or a different regressor. And identifiability: given only $M_{\tau^+}^{(G)}$ and $\pi$, the pair $(\lambda, u_G)$ is identified only up to the product $\lambda u_G$, so the factorization has content only when $u_G$ is fixed independently, as the goal's utility over world-states. **Regression test:** across events of varying sharpness and a goal family with known $u_G$, regress the goal-conditioned log-odds shift $\log r_G(\omega) - \log r_G(\omega')$ on $u_G(\omega) - u_G(\omega')$; a slope $\lambda$ that is constant across sharpness supports the hypothesis, a slope that rises with $1 / \mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$ refutes it, and a poor fit says the distortion points elsewhere than the goal's utility.

*[Scope Condition (domain-classification, conditional on the hypothesis)]*

Under the fixed-gain hypothesis, domains classify by the typical residual spread of the goal-relevant functional their observations leave, which is what governs how much Part II machinery a Class 3 (Coupled) agent inherits there:

| Domain class | Typical $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$ after an observation | Part II applicability under the hypothesis | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low residual spread** | observations pin the goal-relevant quantity | Part II approximately exact even for Class 3 (Coupled) | Software with comprehensive tests, manufacturing with precise sensors, formal verification |
| **Mixed** | varies by observation type | Part II reliable for verifiable observations, degraded for interpretive ones | Software development (tests pin $u_G$, code reviews do not), clinical medicine (lab results vs. symptom interpretation) |
| **High residual spread** | observations leave the goal-relevant quantity open | Part II results significantly degraded for Class 3 (Coupled) | Organizational strategy, intelligence analysis, creative evaluation, philosophical reasoning |

*No-go: no observation-stream ambiguity factor.* The quantity that sorts the domains is the residual spread of the goal-relevant functional, and no mutual information between the goal and the world can stand in for it. The natural candidate, $I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$, is the goal's evidential value about the world after the event, and it is zero in exactly the regime where goal-dependence is a defect: with the realized action in the event ( #der-directed-separation, Scope Condition) and a policy injective in the goal, the goal is a function of the conditioning set and the quantity is identically zero for *every* agent, whether or not the goal is informative about the world (a principal-set goal informative about a flaw: $0.1855$ nats without the action in the conditioning set, $0$ with it). Without the action in the conditioning set the quantity is positive even for an uninformative goal, through the collider the observation forms between goal and world. What the goal's informativeness does measure is the (NWI) premise of #der-directed-separation, as $I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid M_{\tau^-})$: the per-goal reliability a goal-blind report loses by not using a goal that is evidence, which is the price of ignoring an informative goal and not interpretive latitude ( #der-directed-separation, "What the goal may carry"). Composing any such quantity multiplicatively with $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ is also unavailable, since $\kappa$ saturates at any noiseless coupling (Measurement below). The displacement bound of #deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound bounds a different quantity, the belief's goal-dependence, in transferred goal information, and is stated there in that form.

### Measurement

*[Discussion (measuring-the-two-factors)]*

Neither factor is estimable from goal-probing alone. Presenting the same event to a goal-coupled interpreter under several goal primings and measuring how its readings disperse measures that interpreter's own goal-dependence — $\lambda_{\text{interp}} \cdot \mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$ under the hypothesis, with the interpreter's gain in it — and cannot separate the processor factor from the observation-times-goal factor; no between-goal dispersion of a reference interpreter's readings measures a property of the observation. Measuring $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$ needs the calibrated posterior $\pi$ for the event — from outcome ground truth or from a reference posterior that does not itself tilt — and the goal's desirability function $u_G$. Measuring $\lambda$ for a processor needs, in addition, that processor's goal-conditioned beliefs, and is the regression above. Both are standard requirements for separating allocation from distortion: without outcomes or a reference posterior the two are indistinguishable from goal-probing data (a calibrated coarsening and a miscalibrated reweighting can produce the same family of reports), though a martingale screen — under each goal's law, the event-averaged reported posterior should equal the prior — can *refute* a class of distortions without outcomes under well-specification and access, and cannot certify allocation; the screen has to be run per goal, since a goal-symmetric tilt ("ship" up, "correct" down) passes a goal-averaged one. $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ ( #der-directed-separation) is a third quantity and measures neither factor: under the tilt model it is $1$ under the probe law for every $\lambda \gt 0$ with distinct $u_G$ across goals, because mutual information measures the goal's recoverability from the belief, not the belief's displacement.

## Epistemic Status

*Conditional.* Four claims at different levels.

**The log-ratio-range bound** is *exact* and model-free: Jensen's gap, Hoeffding's lemma in both directions, and Pinsker, for any reported belief mutually absolutely continuous with the calibrated one. Its content is honest and limited: distortion is bounded by the size of the reweighting, and by nothing about the evidence.

**The fixed-gain tilt** is a *hypothesis* about Class 3 (Coupled) processors — descriptive, untested, with the regression test named. Under it the Bennett bound on the distortion is *exact* for every gain, and the $\lambda \cdot \mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$ factorization is its leading order. The hypothesis carries two commitments that the test also checks: that the gain does not scale with the evidence, and that the distortion points along the goal's utility.

**The domain classification** is *robust qualitative* conditional on the hypothesis; the quantity that sorts the domains is zero exactly when the observation pins the goal-relevant quantity, with the support factor's qualification for high-gain processors.

**The no-go on an ambiguity factor** is *exact*: that $I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ vanishes for every injective-policy agent once the action is in the event is a consequence of the definitions; that it is the collider's contribution without the action is the instance in #der-directed-separation; and that the goal's informativeness $I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid M_{\tau^-})$ equals the goal-averaged per-goal reliability of the goal-blind prior report is the tower property. The displacement bound of #deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound, with the information term $I_\rho(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}) = \kappa_{\text{processing}} \cdot H_\rho(G)$ under the probe law, is a correct transport inequality on the belief's *goal-dependence*; it is not a bound on distortion, and this segment does not use it as one.

Max attainable: the hypothesis could become a result for a named architecture class if a model of how goal-conditioning enters the likelihood (attention-weighted inference with a fixed temperature, say) were shown to give a fixed gain; a bound for additive-content distortions (a goal-dependent shift of a mean that is a tilt in a different coordinate) is the same identity with a different $\log r_G$ and is covered by the model-free statement, so what is open is only the gain's dependence on the evidence for real processors.

## Discussion

**Why the goal's informativeness is the wrong quantity, in two instances.** Both are exact enumerations. An ambiguous observation leaves the calibrated posterior at $(0.5, 0.5)$; a tilting processor reports goal-conditioned beliefs $0.76$ apart in total variation; and $I(G; \Omega \mid e, M^-) = 0$ because the goal is independent of the world — the informativeness reads *no ambiguity* on the case the domain table is built for. Conversely, when goals are assigned by a principal who knows more ($P(\text{flaw} \mid e, G = \text{correct}) = 0.8$ against $0.2$ under $G = \text{ship}$), the normalized informativeness is $0.278$ and the agent that uses the goal moves $0.30$ in total variation from the goal-blind belief and *scores better* ($0.5004$ against $0.6931$ nats): a bound on that displacement would bound the correct use of an informative goal. The quantity the domain table needs is the goal-relative *relevance* of the residual uncertainty — what $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$ measures — not the goal's *informativeness*, which is zero exactly when "the goal tells you nothing about the world that the observation didn't already determine."

**Why sharpness matters more than coupling — under the hypothesis.** For LLM-based agents the processor factor is architectural: a Class 3 substrate cannot be made to stop tilting by prompting, only by wrapping ( #der-class-coercion-via-wrapping). The observation-times-goal factor is a property of the domain and of observation design, which is under the designer's control: more tests, more precise metrics, more structured outputs, fewer interpretive judgments — each pins the quantity the goal cares about and shrinks $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$. This is AAT's **anti-collapse** discipline ( #disc-anti-collapse) surfacing in the logogenic volume: a cause (goal-distorted belief) tempts the wrong knob (the architectural gain) when the designer-controllable knob is the residual spread. The prescription holds under a condition — a gain that does not grow as the evidence sharpens — and the regression test checks it; for a high-gain processor the prescription reads "leave zero residual mass on the desirable states," not "leave little."

**The software domain advantage.** Software development is a mixed domain with an unusually high proportion of observations that pin the goal-relevant quantity: tests pass or fail, linters report specific violations, type checkers give precise errors, deployments succeed or fail, metrics are numerical. Goal-distortion is then confined to the interpretive observations (code review, architectural assessment, requirement interpretation), and the pinned observations anchor the agent's epistemic state in goal-independent reality.

**Motivated reasoning, stated in the new terms.** Motivated reasoning is a tilt with $\lambda \gt 0$ along the goal's utility, acting on an observation that leaves the goal-relevant quantity open. An LLM agent reading code with goal "ship the feature today" that downweights evidence of bugs is tilting toward high $u_G$ on an observation with large $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$. It vanishes when the observation pins the quantity — a test failure is a test failure regardless of the deadline — and, if the gain is fixed, it vanishes *for that reason*, which is what the hypothesis says and the test checks.

**Relationship to Part I quantities.** The mismatch signal $\delta_t$ ( #def-mismatch-signal) is defined on observable prediction error. Where observations pin the goal-relevant quantity, $\delta_t$ is goal-independent. Where they leave it open, the *prediction* itself may be goal-tilted, introducing bias into $\delta_t$; Part I's quantities stay well-defined and become effectively biased, in proportion to the distortion this segment bounds.

## Findings

### Goal-Distortion of a Belief Is Bounded by the Size of the Reweighting; Under a Fixed-Gain Tilt It Factors into Processor Gain Times Residual Spread of the Goal-Relevant Quantity

**Brief:** When an agent's processing cannot keep its goals out of its belief update — as in a single-forward-pass transformer — the belief it reports can be pushed off the truth. Without any model of the processor, the only thing that limits how far is how hard it pushes: the divergence from the calibrated belief is bounded by the range of the log-reweighting it applies, and a sharp belief can be pushed as far as a vague one. If the push is a fixed-strength tilt toward what the goal wants (the hypothesis this segment lands, with its test), then the divergence factors into the strength of the push times how much room the evidence leaves on the quantity the goal cares about — so a test result, which pins that quantity, leaves no room, and "the code is messy" leaves a lot. That second factor is a property of the observation and the goal, not of the agent, and it is the one a designer can engineer.

**Impact:** Gives the Class 3 (Coupled) approximation error of Part II its honest carrier: a model-free range bound on distortion, and a fixed-gain hypothesis under which the distortion is bounded exactly (Bennett) by the processor's gain and the residual spread of the goal-relevant quantity — so the engineering guidance for Class 3 architectures, engineer the observations rather than the architecture, holds when the processor's gain does not grow as the evidence sharpens, which is testable, and for high-gain processors means zero residual mass rather than small. It records the no-go on an observation-stream ambiguity factor: the goal's evidential value about the world is zero for every injective-policy agent once the action is in the event, and where positive it measures the price of ignoring an informative goal. It separates three quantities the volume had run together: distortion (this segment; the reliability term), goal-dependence of the belief (the displacement bound of #deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound, in transferred information), and the goal's recoverability from the belief ($\kappa_{\text{processing}}$, #der-directed-separation), and says which of them each claim is about.

**Novelty Claim:** *No novelty* on the identity and range bounds (Jensen's gap; Hoeffding's lemma; Pinsker) or on the resolution/reliability framing they instantiate (Murphy 1973; Bröcker 2009). *Claim differentiation* on the fixed-gain tilt as the hypothesis under which "sharp observations leave no room for goal-dependence" is a theorem rather than an intuition, with the residual spread of the goal's utility under the calibrated posterior as the observation-times-goal factor, and on the regression test that separates a fixed gain from one that scales with the evidence. The empirical phenomena the hypothesis would organize are documented in the LLM-agent literature below; the hypothesis itself is untested.

## Working Notes

- **Regression guard.** Do not restore $\mathcal A(e_\tau) = I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}) / H(\Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ as an observation-stream measure of interpretive latitude, nor $\kappa_{\text{processing}} \cdot \mathcal A$ as a bias law, nor the between-goal-variance estimator $\hat{\mathcal A}$ built on a goal-coupled reference interpreter: the numerator is the goal's evidential value (identically zero with the action in the event for injective policies; the collider's contribution without it; the (NWI) violation as $I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid M_{\tau^-})$), the composition with $\kappa$ was by assertion, and the estimator measures the interpreter's own goal-dependence. Do not state "positive exactly when the goal is evidence about the world" for the event-conditioned quantity; that holds only for the quantity conditioned on the prior state alone. Do not call the $\lambda \cdot \mathrm{sd}$ factorization exact; the Bennett bound is. The displacement bound in #deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound is about goal-dependence, with $\kappa$ entering by definition under the probe law; it is not this segment's quantity. The exact enumerations behind the instances in this segment are registered as `empirica:part-ii-coupling-checks`.
- **Open.** Whether real coupled architectures tilt with a fixed gain (the regression test); whether the tilt points along the goal's utility or some other functional; and a name and symbol for $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$, which is close to the literature's *decision-relevant posterior uncertainty* and should take a standard name if one fits (INTENT F.4) — the symbol $\mathcal A$ is retired with its definition and should not be reused for it.
- **Reference goal family.** The domain classification averages over a family of goals; the earlier text's observation that the family is a specification choice (uniform over a task-relevant taxonomy; the deployed-goal distribution; an adversarial family concentrated on goals that maximize $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$) carries over to the new factor unchanged.
- The domain classification is coarse. A finer one would characterize the *distribution* of $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$ across the observation stream: what fraction of events pin the goal-relevant quantity and what fraction leave it open. In a software domain the open ones (code review, architecture decisions) are often the most *strategically important*, a worst-case correlation between residual spread and strategic significance.

An Upper Bound on Goal-Coupling Displacement", `~/src/neurips/03-llm-hallucinate-bound/`) defines its bias as the displacement of the goal-conditional post-update law from the goal-marginal one and bounds it, correctly, by transferred goal information $I(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ (its §5 Lemma 5.1 chain rule is on the post-update law; §4 Theorem 4.1). Its architectural *corollary* factors the transferred information through $\kappa := I(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid \cdot) / I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid \cdot)$ under a bounded-attenuation commitment; under goal-independence the denominator is zero for every agent, so the factorization applies only where the goal is evidence, and its Class 1 criterion "$G \perp M_{\tau^+} \mid (\Omega_\tau, e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$" is violated by the goal-as-evidence Bayesian ($I(G; M^+ \mid \Omega, e) = H(G \mid \Omega, e) \gt 0$ in the N7β instance) — points for that paper's authors, recorded here because this segment used to lean on the corollary.
