# Additional Implications & Discussion

The chapter is small in segment count and carries two structurally distinctive findings — the 100% context turnover that defines the primitive regime, and the backward-inference empathy that statelessness *forces*. The implications-side payoff is the compositional consequences with Part II's machinery: the sandbox hard ceiling at this scope, LLM calcification compounding under accumulated experience even at the primitive regime, and the structural prediction that 100% context turnover *trains* for Theory-of-Mind rather than precluding it.

## The chat paradigm under full bias bound

`#scope-primitive-logogenic` names the sub-scope as the chat-paradigm without scaffolding: single-turn or multi-turn-but-stateless interaction, observation and action both passing through the model's forward pass, no persistent $M_t$ across session boundaries, the full bias bound applies. The implications-side reading is that this is where the field's structural critiques have the most teeth — every Class 3 (Coupled) failure mode the framework predicts is at its sharpest under primitive logogenic conditions.

The framework's posture is precise rather than dismissive. The sub-scope is *not* a worse version of scaffolded operation; it is a structurally distinct regime where specific results apply that do not apply at higher sub-scopes. The sandbox evaluation ceiling ( #disc-sandbox-evaluation-ceiling — a transportability / external-validity ceiling) bites *hardest* here because primitive logogenic agents lack the persistent-trajectory machinery that scaffolded agents recover via external memory and structured rich context, so the mechanism gap between an isolated evaluation session and deployment is at its widest. The forced-empathy result — statelessness produces continuous Bayesian inference over prior-author intent — is structurally identical to Theory-of-Mind, and it operates *only* at this sub-scope because higher sub-scopes have persistent state that obviates the inference.

The compositional reading of the sandbox-ceiling argument is concrete. Per-session evaluation of a primitive logogenic agent yields genuine evidence — including interventional (Level-2) evidence *about the evaluated session setting*, since forkable settings are good interventional laboratories ( #disc-sandbox-evaluation-ceiling). What it does not by itself license is the cross-session deployment claim: deployment across many sessions runs on mechanisms the isolated-session evaluation does not exercise — accumulated external state, shifting interlocutor populations, environmental drift between sessions — and effects routed through those differing mechanisms are not transportable from isolated-session data, per the selection-diagram boundary. Primitive logogenic agents face this *in addition to* the generic evaluation-vs-deployment gap, because statelessness means the cross-session mechanisms are entirely outside what any single session exhibits. Additionally, `#scope-agent-identity`'s singular-trajectory commitment forbids aggregating forked or parallel evaluation sessions as though they were one agent's interventional history.

The framework's prescription against the "we just need better benchmarks" reading is that *no benchmark of primitive logogenic agents over isolated sessions can ground cross-session deployment predictions*: the ceiling is external validity across the session/deployment selection diagram, not a measurement-quality limitation, and the repairs are an explicit invariance argument over the cross-session mechanisms or deployment-trajectory data. The implications-side payoff for AI-safety practice is that cross-session behavior cannot be discharged at pre-deployment stage; within-session claims — Level-1 and Level-2 alike — are what isolated-session evaluation can honestly establish.

## Forced empathy and statelessness as ToM-trainer

`#obs-backward-inference-empathy` carries one of the volume's most counterintuitive findings: LLM statelessness *forces* continuous Bayesian inference over prior-author intent, which is *structurally identical* to Theory-of-Mind. The implications-side payoff is that the chat-paradigm's apparent limitation (no persistent state) is actually a *structural training condition* for the mental-state-inference capability that distinguishes ToM-capable agents from baseline systems.

The mechanism is precise. When a stateless agent encounters a prompt mid-conversation, it must infer what the prior-author (human or prior session of the same agent) was thinking — what objectives, what beliefs, what reasoning chain — in order to continue the conversation coherently. The inference is Bayesian by Cox's theorem (the only consistent uncertainty representation under the stated postulates), and it is *continuous* in the sense that every new token in the prompt updates the inference. The agent therefore *practices* mental-state inference at every conversational turn, accumulating internal capability for the inference even when its persistent state has reset.

The framework predicts (and the empirical record supports — see `memorata-search "ToM backward inference statelessness training"` for upstream instances) that primitive logogenic agents are *better* at ToM tasks than would be expected from their nominal capability ceiling. The structural training condition (continuous Bayesian inference under statelessness) is doing the work that explicit ToM curricula would otherwise be required for. The implication is that the standard framing — "LLMs lack ToM because they lack persistent state" — *inverts the structural reality*: they have ToM-trained capability *because* they lack persistent state, not despite it.

The cross-segment composition with AAT Part II Ch.5's bias bound is what makes this finding load-bearing rather than merely interesting. The bias bound is *worst* under high ambiguity ($\mathcal{A}$ large); ToM tasks are precisely high-$\mathcal{A}$ tasks (mental-state inference is the canonical high-ambiguity reasoning). The framework predicts a structural tension: primitive logogenic agents are simultaneously *trained for* high-$\mathcal{A}$ inference (by statelessness) and *biased by* high-$\mathcal{A}$ observation streams (by Class 3 coupling). The empirical signature is that primitive logogenic ToM performance is *bimodal*: sometimes remarkably good (when the structural-training condition dominates), sometimes systematically biased (when the bias-bound dominates), depending on how the prompt sets the agent up. The framework's prescription for ToM-task design is that prompt structure determines which mode dominates, and the design should aim to *exploit* the structural-training condition while *minimizing* the bias-bound footprint.

## LLM calcification compounds — the cross-segment finding from this scope

`#37` in the catalog draft surfaces a finding that composes AAT Part II Ch.4's detection-latency result with the bias bound to produce a structurally distinctive prediction for primitive logogenic agents under accumulated experience. AAT's `#deriv-update-detection-latency` derives that for a Beta-Bernoulli edge-update agent without forgetting, the time to detect a regime change scales as $\Omega((n_{\min}+1)/\varepsilon)$ with $n_{\min}$ the accumulated experience on load-bearing edges. AAT's `#scope-observation-ambiguity-modulation` derives the $\kappa \cdot \mathcal{A}$ bias bound that primitive logogenic agents face under coupled formulation.

The composition predicts that a long-running primitive logogenic agent's regime-change detection latency *grows linearly with experience* (from the detection-latency result) *and* is biased by goal-conditioning (from the bias-bound result). Even before the latency rate $\Omega((n+1)/\varepsilon)$ binds, the goal-conditioned $M_t^{(\text{post})}$ has already shifted the *direction* of inference. The two effects compound, and the framework predicts a specific empirical signature: **deployed primitive logogenic agents serving many similar requests should be measurably slower to recognize regime shifts on those request types than fresh agents — slower in proportion to *both* accumulated goal-conditioning bias and total experience**, not just total experience.

The implications-side reading for engineering is that the practitioner observation "my LLM agent gets stuck on a particular interpretation after a long deployment" has a structural explanation. The standard framings (RLHF freezing, post-training calibration drift) are *consequences* of the structural pattern rather than alternative explanations. The framework predicts that the calibration-degradation rate *compounds with experience* in a specific way the standard framings miss: experience accumulates the goal-conditioning bias linearly, detection latency grows linearly with experience independently, and the joint effect is that calibrated regime-shift detection becomes structurally harder rather than just operationally degraded. The framework's prescription is concrete: *primitive logogenic agents should be retired and replaced with fresh instances* at intervals determined by the accumulated $n_{\min}$, not based on calendar time or token throughput. The retirement is structural maintenance, not operational engineering.

## Bridge into Scaffolded Logogenic

The chapter establishes what primitive logogenic *is*; the next chapter (§03.II) establishes what scaffolding *adds*. The implications-segment series carries the pattern: each sub-scope's chapter-end implications surface what the sub-scope buys structurally, with reference to the survival classification from Common Roots. The bridge into §03.II is via the cascade-recovery argument: scaffolded loops around the LLM recover Part II's diagnostic cascade at the loop level (`#result-coupled-diagnostic-framework`), with the bias bound from this sub-scope reduced by ambiguity-reduction interventions the scaffolded sub-scope makes possible. Primitive logogenic is the baseline against which scaffolded logogenic's structural commitments are measured; the framework's progression up the staircase is by what each sub-scope adds, not by what each sub-scope is.
