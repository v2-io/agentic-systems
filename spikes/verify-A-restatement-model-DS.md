## 1. What §4 actually claims, read literally

§4 of the confound spike (lines 55–80) sets up the update $x_{t+1}=\Phi(x_t)+d_t$ with $\Phi$ incrementally sector-bounded (DA2′-inc), a contraction with modulus $\lambda = 1-\alpha/\nu \lt 1$, defines the accumulation operator

$$\mathcal A[d]_m \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{\,m-1-k}\, d_k$$

("the discrete variation-of-constants / resolvent of the closed loop"), and states the "restatement" that the template *is exactly*

$$\big\lVert \mathcal A \big\rVert_{\,\partial\to\Sigma} \;=\; \sup_{\lVert d\rVert_\infty \le 1}\ \limsup_m \lVert \mathcal A[d]_m\rVert \;=\; \frac{1}{1-\lambda} \;=\; \frac{\nu}{\alpha},$$

with epistemic status "**exact** ... the template-as-$\mathcal A$-boundedness identity is an algebraic rewrite of `#result-sector-persistence-template`, not a new claim."

Two things are immediately true and worth conceding to §4 up front, because the strengthen-first posture requires steel-manning it:

- **For Model D, §4 is correct and genuinely exact.** Discretize Prop A.1's $\dot V \le -\lVert\delta\rVert(\alpha\lVert\delta\rVert-\rho)$ to the affine contraction $\lVert\delta_{m+1}\rVert \le \lambda\lVert\delta_m\rVert + \lVert d_m\rVert$ with $\lambda = 1-\alpha/\nu$ (this is exactly `#deriv-discrete-sector-condition`'s $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}$, the contraction factor `#form-composition-closure` line 141 uses, $\lambda = 1-\alpha_c/\nu_c$). Unrolling gives $\lVert\delta_m\rVert \le \lambda^m\lVert\delta_0\rVert + \sum_{k}\lambda^{m-1-k}\lVert d_k\rVert$, and with $\lVert d_k\rVert\le\rho$ the geometric tail is $\rho/(1-\lambda) = \rho\nu/\alpha = \rho/\alpha$ in continuous-rate units. That **is** $R^\ast = \rho/\alpha$ (Prop A.1), and it **is** an operator-norm reading of the linear resolvent in the $\ell^\infty\!\to\!\ell^\infty$ induced norm. No objection here — §4's "exact, algebraic rewrite, not a new claim" is correct *for Model D*.

- The bridge-lemma instantiation (`#form-composition-closure` lines 135–141) is *also* a Model-D-flavored linear-resolvent reading: state $e_m$, disturbance $\varepsilon^\ast\nu_c$, contraction $\lambda=1-\alpha_c/\nu_c$, $\limsup\lVert e_m\rVert\le\varepsilon^\ast\nu_c/\alpha_c$. So §4's $\mathcal A$ is *exactly* the right description of the bridge-lemma attachee. The land-order claim is *locally* true for the $\varepsilon^\ast(N)$ / regime-I-II / bridge family — those genuinely all attach to one linear $\mathcal A$.

The problem is *only* the universal-quantifier jump from "the Model-D instantiation is $\mathcal A$-boundedness" to "**the template** is $\mathcal A$-boundedness," when the template explicitly carries Model S as a co-equal disturbance model (template (T3): "**Either** Model D ... **or** Model S"), and the heaviest planned attachee lives in Model S.

## 2. The Model-S side, read literally — the operator is different in kind

`#result-sector-persistence-template` Model S (lines 35, 47–51) and its proof in `#deriv-sector-condition` Prop A.1S (lines 178–256) give $d\delta = -F(\delta)\,dt + \sigma_w\,dW_t$, hence $\tfrac{d}{dt}\mathbb E[V] \le -2\alpha\,\mathbb E[V] + \tfrac{n}{2}\sigma_w^2$ and $\mathbb E[\lVert\delta\rVert^2]_{ss} = n\sigma_w^2/(2\alpha)$. Run the "is this $\mathcal A$ in another norm?" test honestly:

- **The disturbance is not in $\partial$ in §4's sense.** §4's $d_k$ is a *deterministic per-step element* with $\lVert d\rVert_\infty\le 1$. Model S's forcing is a *Wiener increment*: zero-mean, but $\mathbb E[\lVert dW\rVert^2]\sim n\,dt$. The object that controls the Model-S bound, $\tfrac{n}{2}\sigma_w^2$, is the **Itô / quadratic-variation term**. There is no quadratic-variation term anywhere in §4's algebra, because a deterministic geometric sum has no second-order term. The Model-S constant is *manufactured by a piece of structure the §4 operator does not contain* (`#deriv-sector-condition` lines 220–222 is explicit: the $\tfrac12\sigma_w^2 n\,dt$ "is the Itô correction"). An operator-in-another-norm cannot acquire a term that is absent from the operator.

- **Homogeneity argument (the decisive one).** Suppose, for contradiction, Model D and Model S *were* one bounded operator $T$ read in two norms $\lVert\cdot\rVert_a$, $\lVert\cdot\rVert_b$, with the disturbance the same object measured two ways. Then the two ultimate-bound constants would be $\lVert T\rVert_a$ and $\lVert T\rVert_b$, and for a *fixed* $T$ the dependence on the contraction parameter $\alpha$ enters only through $T$ itself (the same $\lambda=1-\alpha/\nu$ in both norms). Both constants would therefore have the **same** $\alpha$-homogeneity. They do not: $R^\ast_D \propto \alpha^{-1}$, $R^\ast_S \propto \alpha^{-1/2}$ (template line 51; `#deriv-sector-condition` line 254: "Doubling the correction efficiency halves the deterministic steady-state mismatch but only reduces the stochastic steady-state by a factor of $\sqrt 2$"). A change in the *exponent* of the governing parameter cannot be produced by changing the norm on a fixed operator (equivalent norms differ by an $\alpha$-independent bounded factor). Therefore the Model-D resolvent and the Model-S object are **not one operator in two norms**. This is a clean structural no-go for the one-operator reading, not a hunch.

- **Cor A.1S.1 makes the split categorical, not gradual.** `#deriv-sector-condition` Cor A.1S.1 (lines 258–268): the first-exit probability $P(\tau_R\lt\infty)$ is **exactly the two-point set $\{0,1\}$** — $0$ under Model D (positive invariance), $1$ under Model S (a.s. exit of a non-degenerate diffusion), **$\alpha$-invariant**. "Pathwise containment of $\mathcal B_R$ is categorically a bounded-disturbance property." A single bounded operator cannot have its central qualitative output jump categorically between $0$ and $1$ as a function of *which disturbance model* you read it under, with no interpolation. The canon itself already names this a *kind*-of-guarantee difference, not a rate difference (`#deriv-sector-condition` Discussion "Kind of guarantee, not just rate," lines 322–323). §4's "one operator, contraction-regime" picture is *structurally a Model-D picture*; the canon's own Cor A.1S.1 is the proof that Model S is a different kind of object.

## 3. The continuity Lindley operator, read literally — a third, nonlinear object

Continuity `02`§3 (line 52) and `03`§1 give the **Lindley recursion**

$$g_{k+1}=\big(g_k+\rho_k-\eta_k\big)_+,\qquad \xi_k:=\rho_k-\eta_k,\qquad g_k\ge 0;$$

its stationary law is the **Loynes representation**

$$g_\infty \;\stackrel{d}{=}\; \sup_{n\ge0}S_n,\qquad S_n=\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^n\xi_{-j}$$

(continuity `03` line 36, Loynes 1962). Contrast with §4's linear geometric resolvent $\mathcal A$ above:

| | §4 $\mathcal A$ (Model D) | continuity Lindley/Loynes |
|---|---|---|
| linearity | **linear** in $d$ | **nonlinear** (reflection $(\cdot)_+$) |
| kernel | geometric $\lambda^{m-1-k}$, $\lambda\lt1$ | **no geometric decay** — weights are $1$, not $\lambda^{j}$ |
| governing thm | geometric-series sum / Banach resolvent | **Loynes 1962** sup-representation + **Atkinson 1976** at $\mu=0$ |
| boundedness | $\lVert\mathcal A\rVert=\nu/\alpha\lt\infty$ iff $\lambda\lt1$ | $g_\infty\lt\infty$ **iff $\mathbb E[\xi]\lt0$ strictly**; at $\mu=0$ no finite stationary law |
| regime that matters | $\lambda\lt1$ (contraction) | the **boundary** $\mu=0$ where §4's $\lambda\to1$ and $\lVert\mathcal A\rVert\to\infty$ |

The continuity operator is not "§4's $\mathcal A$ in a mean-square norm." It is a **different operator**: a reflected (Lindley) random walk whose governing theorem is Loynes' sup-representation, *not* a geometric resolvent. Three structural reasons it cannot be folded into §4's $\mathcal A$:

- **(O1) the reflection.** $(\cdot)_+$ is nonlinear; §4's $\mathcal A$ is linear. §4's object has no reflection because Model-D positive invariance ($\alpha R\gt\rho$) makes it never bind. Continuity *requires* the reflection ($g_k\ge0$; you cannot carry more than all the identity-MI, continuity `02` line 54). Removing it changes the stationary law.

- **(O2) no geometric kernel.** §4's boundedness *is* the geometric series $\sum\lambda^j=1/(1-\lambda)$. The Lindley walk in the regime of interest is the **driftless / near-driftless** walk — there is no $\lambda\lt1$. Its finiteness is not "geometric tail summable" but "Loynes sup is a.s. finite iff the increment has strictly negative mean." Different finiteness mechanism, different theorem.

- **(O3) the boundary is §4's singularity.** The sharp content C-DMP extracts ($\mu=0$ ⇒ no finite stationary law, strict-$\lt$ required, Atkinson) lives *exactly at* $\lambda\to1$, where §4's $\lVert\mathcal A\rVert=\nu/\alpha=1/(1-\lambda)\to\infty$. §4's operator is, by its own epistemic-status sentence, defined to be bounded *iff DA2′-inc holds* (i.e. iff $\lambda\lt1$ strictly). The continuity result's load-bearing case is the one §4's object explicitly excludes. This is the confound spike's own $\mathcal R$-layer (§6.6, §6(g) self-instance): the continuity $\mu=0$ error *is* §6.6's verified $\mathcal R$-erasure instance. The continuity spike named the operator (Lindley) and *still* the boundary bit — because the regime $\mathcal R$ (contraction vs critical) is a third coordinate neither "$\partial/\Sigma$ marked" nor "$\mathcal A$ named" carries. **§4's identity sentence is itself an $\mathcal R$=contraction special case** — which is exactly what confound §6.6 (lines 133–134) *says* ("The two confound directions named in §4 ... are both $\mathcal R$ = contraction special cases"). So the confound spike *already contains the refutation of its own §4 universal claim* — it is just located in §6.6 and not propagated back to the §4 "exact / not a new claim / the template *is*" wording or to the §9 "single shared upstream object" land-order.

## 4. The strengthen-first attempt: can the one-object unification be saved?

Per the posture, I tried to *make the unification true* before concluding it splits. Three honest attempts, each fails for a stated reason — recording the failures so they are not re-attempted:

**Attempt A — "one operator, two induced norms" ($\ell^\infty$ for D, $L^2$ / mean-square for S).** Fails on the **homogeneity argument (O2)**: equivalent or even non-equivalent induced norms on a *fixed* linear operator cannot change the exponent of $\alpha$ in the bound; $1/\alpha$ vs $1/\sqrt\alpha$ is a change of homogeneity degree, which proves the underlying object differs. This is not "hard to close" — it is a clean impossibility. Recorded as a no-go for the two-norms reading.

**Attempt B — "the monotone-operator / resolvent literature unifies them" (the brief's suggested search).** The template's own "External mathematical lineage" section (lines 68–74) and `#deriv-sector-condition` lines 84–94 already do this homework: the Lyapunov-Grönwall (Model D) and Itô-Lyapunov (Model S) arguments are *both* "standard specializations of the monotone-operator perturbation theorem (Bauschke-Combettes Thm 5.14–5.16)" — but the template itself flags, as **AAT-distinctive content (ii)**, precisely that "monotone-operator theory has perturbation theorems but **no systematic bounded-adversarial vs stochastic-zero-mean split** propagating to adversarial-exponent regimes at $b=2$ vs $b=3/2$." So the literature the brief points at *explicitly does not* collapse the D/S split — the canon already verified this and recorded it as a place the external unification *stops*. The resolvent literature unifies the *contraction machinery* (the $\mathcal A$ for Model D, the cocoercive-operator reading); it does not unify Model D with Model S into one operator. Attempt B fails, and the canon already knew it failed — cited here so the next agent does not re-run the search expecting a different answer.

**Attempt C — "absorb the reflection: §4's $\mathcal A$ restricted to the positive cone *is* the Lindley operator."** This is the most serious attempt because it is locally plausible. It fails on **(O2)/(O3) jointly**: even granting the cone restriction, the Lindley operator's governing regime is $\mu\le0$ with the *driftless boundary* load-bearing, which is exactly $\lambda\to1$ where §4's geometric-resolvent boundedness *does not exist*. You cannot restrict a bounded linear operator to a cone and obtain an object whose entire content lives at the parameter value where the original's norm is $+\infty$. The reflected walk at $\mu=0$ is governed by Atkinson's recurrence theorem for mean-zero ergodic cocycles — a theorem about **non-contracting** walks. §4's $\mathcal A$ is, by construction, the **contracting** case. They meet only in the limit, and the limit is exactly where one of them ceases to be the object §4 defined. Attempt C fails for a structural reason, not for lack of cleverness.

**What *does* survive the strengthen-first attempt.** The genuinely shared, genuinely exact, genuinely upstream object is **the template's conditional**: "(T1) + (T2) + (T3-D **or** T3-S) ⇒ persistence, with ultimate-bound constant $\rho/\alpha$ (D) or $\sigma\sqrt{n/(2\alpha)}$ (S), and the Cor-A.1S.1 containment dichotomy." That conditional *is* what both spikes independently re-derived and *is* a legitimate single shared upstream — the continuity spike instantiates it honestly (continuity `03`§3 lines 50–52 maps $\alpha R\gt\rho \rightsquigarrow \mathbb E[\eta]\gt\mathbb E[\rho]$ and $R^\ast=\rho/\alpha\rightsquigarrow R^\ast_{\mathrm{id}}=\bar\rho/\alpha_{\mathrm{id}}$ — that is the *template*, correctly used, **including** that continuity's Theorem 2′ is the **Model-D / (C-D) bounded** analog while Theorem 2.1/2.3 is the **Model-S-flavored / (C-S) stochastic** analog: continuity *itself* carries the D/S split as its (C-D) vs (C-S) regimes). The convergence the two spikes celebrate is real **at the template level**. It is over-stated **at the operator level**.

So the strengthen-first verdict: the unification *can* be saved, but only by relocating it from "$\mathcal A$ is one bounded operator" to "the template's two-model conditional is one shared upstream." That relocation is not a softening — it is the *correct* shared object, and it is strictly more faithful to what the canon already says (the template already leads with "Either Model D ... or Model S" and already carries both constants and the dichotomy). The thing that must be deleted is the *operator-level universal*, not the land-order.

## 5. The precise split, named

For the next pass and any landing, the nameable split is:

- **$\mathcal A_D$ — the linear geometric resolvent.** $\mathcal A_D[d]_m=\sum_k\lambda^{m-1-k}d_k$, $\lambda=1-\alpha/\nu\lt1$, $\lVert\mathcal A_D\rVert_{\ell^\infty\to\ell^\infty}=\nu/\alpha$. - **$\mathcal A_S$ — the mean-square / stationary-second-moment functional.** Produced by Itô-Lyapunov; $R^\ast_S=\sigma\sqrt{n/(2\alpha)}$, $1/\sqrt\alpha$ homogeneity, governed by the Grönwall solution of $\tfrac{d}{dt}\mathbb E[V]\le-2\alpha\mathbb E[V]+\tfrac n2\sigma^2$ plus Cor-A.1S.1's categorical $P(\tau_R\lt\infty)=1$. **Not** $\mathcal A_D$ in another norm (homogeneity proof O2). - **$\mathcal A_{\mathrm{refl}}$ — the reflected Lindley operator (Loynes / Atkinson).** $g_{k+1}=(g_k+\xi_k)_+$, stationary law $\sup_{n}S_n$, finite **iff $\mathbb E[\xi]\lt0$ strictly**, *no finite stationary law at the boundary*. Nonlinear; governing theorem Loynes 1962 + Atkinson 1976. Governs continuity-persistence T1/T2/T2.3 and the C-DMP strict-$\lt$ correction. Its load-bearing regime ($\mu=0$) is $\mathcal A_D$'s singularity ($\lambda\to1$). This is a **Model-S-family** object (driftless/stochastic, distributional guarantee, boundary-fragile) *with* a reflection Model S in the template does not itself carry — continuity `03`'s (M-FREE) is precisely the disclosure that the reflection's upper end is idealized, and the QSD unification (continuity `03` line 20, `98`§4) is *internal to $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{refl}}$*, not a bridge to $\mathcal A_D$.

These three are related — they are all "the template instantiated" — but they are **not one operator**. $\mathcal A_D$ and $\mathcal A_S$ already differ by the canon's own Model-D/Model-S split (different homogeneity, Cor-A.1S.1 categorical). $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{refl}}$ is a fourth structural element (nonlinear; Loynes-governed) that is *Model-S-family but not $\mathcal A_S$ either* — continuity's Theorem 2′ is its Model-D-bounded face, so even the continuity operator internally re-instantiates the D/S split. The shared upstream is the **template conditional**, full stop.

## 7. Why this is landing (2)→(3), not (1) — stated plainly

The brief's three landings: (1) clean one-object; (2) principled nameable split; (3) problematic elision (spine cracks).

- It is **not (1)**: the homogeneity proof (O2) is a clean impossibility for "one operator, two norms"; the reflection (O1) and the boundary-singularity (O3) independently block it. Three independent structural obstructions, any one sufficient.

- It **is (2)**: the split is principled and nameable ($\mathcal A_D$ / $\mathcal A_S$ / $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{refl}}$), and the *land-order survives* under the correct shared object (the template conditional). This is the dominant characterization.

- It **shades into (3) for the specific sentence §4 writes**: "the template *is* the theorem that $\mathcal A$ is bounded ... exact ... not a new claim," and the §1 "the sector-persistence template is *precisely* the theorem that $\mathcal A$ is bounded," are over-unifications at the operator level — true for Model D, false-as-stated for the template (which carries Model S) and for the heaviest attachee (continuity, reflected/Loynes). The crack is real but **narrow and self-contained**: the confound spike already proved the refutation in its own §6.6 ($\mathcal R$=contraction is a special case); it simply did not propagate it back to the §4/§1 universal wording or the §9 land-order. The spine is not shattered — it is *miscut at one joint*: re-state the shared upstream as the two-model template conditional and the structure stands, the convergence stays real, the land-order stays useful. Left unfixed, landing "the $\mathcal A$-restatement" as a single-operator paragraph in canon would carry a Model-D-only over-reach into `#result-sector-persistence-template` — which is exactly the integration-is-replacement failure (a sharp-but-too-narrow claim replacing a correctly-scoped one). That is the concrete reason this matters operationally and is worth the flagged-note channel rather than silent agreement.

## Appendix: the one-line test that settles it

A bounded linear operator $T$ read in any two norms yields two constants with the **same homogeneity** in any internal parameter of $T$. Model D gives $R^\ast\propto\alpha^{-1}$; Model S gives $R^\ast\propto\alpha^{-1/2}$ (`#result-sector-persistence-template` line 51; `#deriv-sector-condition` line 254). Different homogeneity ⇒ different object. Therefore "the template is the statement that *the* operator $\mathcal A$ is bounded" cannot be a single-operator statement; it is at best the *Model-D* operator's boundedness, and the template — and the continuity attachee — carry strictly more structure (Model S; the reflected Loynes walk) that the single-operator framing elides.
