# Allocation versus distortion: where the line between legitimate and illegitimate goal-dependence falls

*KR C.2. Numbers cited as (N1)–(N12) are from `sims/check.py`, output in `sims/check-output.txt`. The companion `02-adversarial-instances.md` carries one deliberately out-of-family instance per universally quantified claim below.*

## 0. What is examined

The target (owner review, integrator item C3, verbatim): *"Goal-dependence in what is encoded preserves calibration if the update is selection-aware; goal-dependence in how the agent updates on what it encoded strictly worsens accuracy under every proper scoring rule, assuming the goal carries no world information. $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ cannot separate the two; `der-directed-separation.md:54` vs `:56` draw the line inconsistently. Resolves the IB-vs-directed-separation crack at `form-information-bottleneck.md:93`. Owed: a second reader on break-point 4 (a goal-allocated encoder starves cascade step 5a); whether the $\kappa \cdot \mathcal A$ bias bound counts allocation as hallucination."*

I take each clause in turn (§§2–4, §7, §8), the two owed items (§5, §6), and then the question underneath the clauses, which turns out to be the one that matters: *what is the invariant that "goal-blind" was reaching for?* (§4, §9). The short form of what I found: the explorer's three claims hold, two of them only under hypotheses that have to be named; the measure canon uses cannot see the distinction and in one definite sense cannot see magnitude at all; the bias bound's ambiguity factor is identically zero in the regime where the norm it serves applies; and there is a clean replacement for both the criterion and the bound.

## 1. Setup and the conditioning set

Let $\Omega$ be the outcome-relevant latent world-state (canon's $\Omega_\tau$), $M^-$ the agent's pre-update epistemic state, $G$ its purposeful state, $a$ the realized action, $e$ the realized event. I treat $M^-$ as (a function of) the chronica, so the realized action $a$ is recoverable from $M^-$ once taken; I write it explicitly because it matters below. Write $\mathcal F := \sigma(M^-, a, e, G)$ for everything the agent could in principle condition on after the event, and $\pi := P(\Omega \mid M^-, a, e)$ for the *goal-blind full-information posterior*.

**Hypothesis (NWI) — the goal carries no world information beyond the prior state and the realized action:** $G \perp \Omega \mid (M^-, a)$.

Two remarks on (NWI), both load-bearing later.

*(1.1) The realized action belongs in the conditioning set.* The explorer wrote the hypothesis as $G \perp W \mid (M_{\tau^-}, a, e_\tau)$ and canon's $\mathcal A$ conditions on $(e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$. If $a = \pi_{\text{pol}}(M^-, G)$ depends on the goal and $e$ depends on $(\Omega, a)$, then $e$ is a collider between $G$ and $\Omega$, and conditioning on $e$ without $a$ can *create* dependence between $G$ and $\Omega$ even when $G \perp \Omega \mid M^-$ (binary example: $e = \Omega \oplus a$ makes $G$ and $\Omega$ perfectly dependent given $e$). With $a$ in the conditioning set the independence propagates: $P(\Omega \mid M^-, a, e, G) = P(\Omega \mid M^-, a, e)$ follows from $G \perp \Omega \mid (M^-, a)$ and $e \perp G \mid (\Omega, a, M^-)$. This is the precise content of canon's "goal-blindness conditional on the realized event": the *realized event includes the realized action*. (F, exact; a standard d-separation fact.)

*(1.2) When (NWI) holds, and how to restore it when it does not.* (NWI) holds when the goal is set exogenously and the event of its being set is recorded in $M^-$, or when the goal is a function of $M^-$ (self-set from the agent's own beliefs). It fails when the goal was set using information the agent does not hold — the explorer's break-point 3 (a commander's objective encoding intelligence the unit lacks). In that case the *fact that this goal was set* is an observation about the world, and updating $M$ on it is ordinary goal-blind processing of an event; once that update is in $M^-$, (NWI) holds for the residual. Under canon's own update forms the failure is generic rather than exotic: `der-directed-separation.md:38` has $f_G(G^-, M^+, e)$ take the event directly while $M = \phi(\mathcal C)$ is lossy, so $G$ can retain world information that $M$ discarded. If $M$ is predictively sufficient for the history (`#def-model-sufficiency`, $S(M) = 1$ with $\Omega$ among the predicted quantities) and $G$ is a function of the history and exogenous goal-setting events recorded in $M$, then $G \perp \Omega \mid M$ and (NWI) holds. So (NWI) is not a free assumption; it is *model sufficiency plus recorded goal-setting*, and it has a canon anchor. (F, exact as a conditional; the sufficiency route is the explorer's §7.1 point 2 seen from the other side.) §8 returns to the consequences.

## 2. Two kinds of goal-dependence, and the criterion that separates them

The explorer factors the update into an encoder $z = \phi_G(e)$ and an updater $g(M^-, z, G)$. That factorization is a representational choice (it is exactly the P1-vs-P2–P4 split of `#disc-partial-coupling-pathways`), and §4 shows the selection/processing line inherits its arbitrariness. The criterion below does not depend on it.

**Definition 2.1 (calibrated belief).** A reported belief $M^+$ (a random probability measure on $\Omega$'s space, measurable with respect to $\mathcal F$) is *calibrated* if $M^+ = P(\Omega \in \cdot \mid \sigma(M^+))$ almost surely: the belief equals the conditional law of the world given the belief's own value.

**Lemma 2.2 (calibration is conditional-expectation form).** $M^+$ is calibrated if and only if there is a sub-$\sigma$-field $\mathcal H \subseteq \mathcal F$ with $M^+ = P(\Omega \in \cdot \mid \mathcal H)$ a.s. *Proof.* If $M^+ = P(\Omega \mid \mathcal H)$ then $\sigma(M^+) \subseteq \mathcal H$ and the tower property gives $P(\Omega \mid \sigma(M^+)) = \mathbb E[P(\Omega \mid \mathcal H) \mid \sigma(M^+)] = \mathbb E[M^+ \mid \sigma(M^+)] = M^+$. Conversely take $\mathcal H = \sigma(M^+)$. $\square$ (F, exact.)

So *every* calibrated belief is "the Bayes posterior given some information the agent holds," and the only freedom is *which* information. That is the whole distinction:

**Definition 2.3.** Let $\{M^+_G\}$ be the family of beliefs the agent reports under each goal $G$ at fixed $(M^-, a, e)$. The family exhibits **allocation coupling** if it is goal-dependent and every member is calibrated — equivalently, by Lemma 2.2, $M^+_G = P(\Omega \mid \mathcal H_G)$ for goal-indexed sub-$\sigma$-fields $\mathcal H_G \subseteq \mathcal F$. It exhibits **distortion coupling** if some member is not calibrated.

In the explorer's encoder/updater picture, allocation is $\mathcal H_G = \sigma(M^-, a, z_G, [\phi_G])$ with $g$ the Bayes posterior given that information including the encoder's identity; distortion is any $g$ that departs from it. But Definition 2.3 is stated without the picture.

### 2.4 Claim (A): allocation preserves calibration — with the condition named correctly

**Proposition A.** If $M^+_G = P(\Omega \mid \mathcal H_G)$ for a sub-$\sigma$-field $\mathcal H_G$ of the agent's information, then $M^+_G$ is calibrated (Lemma 2.2) and is a martingale in the information: $\mathbb E[M^+_G \mid \mathcal H'] = P(\Omega \mid \mathcal H')$ for every $\mathcal H' \subseteq \mathcal H_G$, so in particular no choice of $\mathcal H_G$ — including a choice made to favor the goal — moves the belief in expectation away from what the coarser information supports. (F, exact; tower property. The martingale clause is the content of Kadane–Schervish–Seidenfeld's "reasoning to a foregone conclusion" result and of Good's total-evidence theorem; see `lit-scan.md` for what the literature scan found and verified.)

**What "selection-aware" has to mean.** The explorer's condition is that the update "condition on *which* encoder was used." That is necessary but it is not the sharp condition. The sharp condition is Rubin's ignorability of the encoding mechanism, transposed to attention. Write the encoding as a pattern (which features of $e$ were retained) plus retained values. If the pattern depends only on quantities the updater also conditions on — $G$, $M^-$, $a$, and retained content — then the mechanism is *ignorable* (missing-at-random in Rubin's sense): the likelihood of the retained values with the pattern treated as given is correct, and no model of the attention rule is needed. If the pattern depends on *dropped* content — the agent skipped a feature *because of what it showed*, and did not retain what it showed — the mechanism is non-ignorable (missing-not-at-random), the pattern is itself evidence about $\Omega$, and an updater that treats the pattern as uninformative is **not** calibrated even though "only allocation" happened. (N5): a rule that retains a confirming feature and drops a disconfirming one, followed by a naive update, has reliability $0.0148$ nats; the same rule with the pattern modeled has reliability $0$; a rule whose drop decision depends only on a *retained* feature has reliability $0$ under the naive update. So:

**Proposition A′ (ignorability is the condition).** Allocation preserves calibration iff the encoding mechanism is ignorable with respect to the dropped content, or the updater models the mechanism. Peeking-then-dropping is allocation in form and distortion in effect. (F, exact; it is Rubin 1976 applied to the agent's own attention.) This sharpens the explorer's break-point 2: "humans and LLMs generally do not condition on their own attention policy" becomes the testable statement "their drop decisions depend on content they do not retain."

### 2.5 Claim (B): distortion strictly costs — subjectively always, objectively under well-specification

**Proposition B.** Let $S$ be a strictly proper scoring rule (negatively oriented). For any belief $M^+$ measurable with respect to the agent's information,

$$\mathbb E[S(M^+, \Omega)] = \mathbb E[S(P(\Omega \mid \sigma(M^+)), \Omega)] + \mathbb E\big[D_S\big(P(\Omega \mid \sigma(M^+)),\, M^+\big)\big],$$

where $D_S \ge 0$ is the Bregman divergence generated by $S$ and the second term is zero iff $M^+$ is calibrated. Hence a distorted belief is strictly worse than its own recalibration under every strictly proper rule, and the Bayes posterior given any fixed information is the unique minimizer among beliefs measurable with respect to it. (F, exact; standard — the reliability term of the Murphy/Bröcker decomposition; Gneiting–Raftery for the Bregman form.)

**Scope that must be stated.** The expectation is under *some* law for $(\Omega, \mathcal F)$. Under the agent's own model it is the agent's subjective expected score, and Proposition B holds unconditionally: no agent can expect, by its own lights, to gain from distorting. Under the *true* law it holds iff the agent's model is well-specified. For a misspecified agent a goal-tilt can lower true expected loss. (N4): true $P(\Omega = 1) = 0.8$, agent prior $0.3$, weak observation; the objective log score is $1.0015$ at tilt $\lambda = 0$ and $0.4927$ at $\lambda = 2.5$, minimized near $\lambda \approx 2.23 \gt 0$; the subjective score is minimized at $\lambda = 0$. The explorer's "strictly worsens accuracy about the world … for every goal" is therefore true as a statement about the agent's own epistemic standards and about well-specified agents, and false as a universal about objective accuracy. The honest form of the degradation norm's "tracks reality strictly better" is: *distortion strictly worsens the reliability term of any proper score, under the agent's model unconditionally and under the world's law when the model is right.* (F, exact, with the scope now explicit.) "For every goal" also needs "for every goal under which the update actually departs from the conditional" — a goal under which $M^+_G$ happens to be calibrated costs nothing.

### 2.6 Claim (C): allocation is a trade-off

Across sub-$\sigma$-fields, expected proper score is monotone in Blackwell's order (finer information never scores worse in expectation — this is the resolution term, $\mathbb E[D_S(P(\Omega \mid \mathcal H), P(\Omega))]$, being nondecreasing in $\mathcal H$), and incomparable sub-$\sigma$-fields of equal capacity are not ordered. At fixed capacity the information-bottleneck encoder of `#form-information-bottleneck` maximizes goal-neutral predictive resolution by its definition; a goal-chosen encoder maximizes the goal's decision value (rational inattention, value-equivalent models). Neither dominates. (N3): allocating feature $x_1$ gives resolution $0.3552$, feature $x_2$ gives $0.0791$, both with reliability $0$; which is better depends on which feature the goal needs. (F, exact for the ordering; the identification of the IB encoder as the goal-neutral optimum is by definition.)

## 3. The instrument that separates the two, and why $\kappa$ cannot

**Theorem 3.1 (the split is the reliability/resolution split).** For any strictly proper $S$ and any goal-indexed family of beliefs,

$$\mathbb E[S(M^+_G, \Omega)] = \underbrace{\mathbb E[S(P(\Omega), \Omega)]}_{\text{uncertainty}} - \underbrace{\mathbb E[D_S(P(\Omega \mid \sigma(M^+_G)), P(\Omega))]}_{\mathrm{RES}(G)} + \underbrace{\mathbb E[D_S(P(\Omega \mid \sigma(M^+_G)), M^+_G)]}_{\mathrm{REL}(G)}.$$

Allocation coupling varies $\mathrm{RES}(G)$ with $\mathrm{REL}(G) \equiv 0$; distortion coupling has $\mathrm{REL}(G) \gt 0$ for some $G$. The two kinds are separated by a quantity that is standard, scoring-rule-generic, and estimable from the same probe design as $\hat\kappa$ (same event, different goals) *plus outcome ground truth*. (F, exact; (N3) exhibits the three columns: full information $\mathrm{RES} = 0.3849$, $\mathrm{REL} = 0$; a tilt toward $\Omega = 1$ leaves $\mathrm{RES}$ at $0.3849$ and adds $\mathrm{REL} = 0.0420$; a tilt of the $x_1$-posterior adds $\mathrm{REL} = 0.0218$ at $\mathrm{RES} = 0.3552$.)

**Proposition 3.2 ($\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ cannot separate them).** $I(G; M^+ \mid e, M^-) \gt 0$ whenever $M^+_G$ varies with $G$ at fixed $(e, M^-)$, which allocation does by definition. (N8): the allocation agent of (N3) has beliefs that differ across the two goals on every feature pattern, so $I(G; M^+ \mid e, M^-) = H(G)$ and $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 1$ there, with reliability $0$. Confirmed, as the explorer said. (F, exact.)

**Proposition 3.3 ($\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ does not measure magnitude either).** If $G$ is discrete and $M^+_G$ is a deterministic injective function of $G$ at fixed $(e, M^-)$, then $I(G; M^+ \mid e, M^-) = H(G \mid e, M^-)$ and $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 1$ regardless of how little the beliefs differ. (N2): a tilt of strength $\lambda = 10^{-6}$ moves the belief by total variation $10^{-6}$ and gives $\kappa = 1$, the same as $\lambda = 1$ (TV $0.76$). Mutual information measures *recoverability* of the goal from the belief, not *displacement*. With update noise the qualification is exact: for a binary goal and reported log-odds $\pm\lambda$ plus Gaussian noise of standard deviation $s$, the mutual information is a function of $\lambda / s$ alone — (N9): $\kappa = 0.486$ at $(\lambda, s) = (1, 1)$ and $\kappa = 1.000$ at $(10^{-3}, 10^{-4})$ — so $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ is the *detectability* of the goal in the belief against the update's own noise, a signal-to-noise ratio, and a noiseless update of any nonzero magnitude saturates it. Canon's prose ("$\kappa \approx 1$: nearly all goal information is available to the epistemic update," `der-directed-separation.md:90`) is literally right and is not a statement about how much the belief moved. The behavioral estimator $\hat\kappa$ at `:97`, a normalized distance, estimates displacement, not the mutual-information definition; the definition and its estimator are different quantities. (F, exact; the point about $\hat\kappa$ is by inspection of `:97`.)

Paper 3's $\kappa$ (the ratio $I(G; M^+ \mid e, M^-) / I(G; \Omega \mid e, M^-)$) is a third quantity; see §5.

**Remark 3.4 (an identifiability floor, stated carefully).** From goal-probing data alone — $\{M^+_G(e)\}_G$ with no outcomes and no reference posterior — allocation and distortion are indistinguishable: a calibrated coarsening and a miscalibrated reweighting can produce the same family of reports. Theorem 3.1 needs either outcomes (to form $P(\Omega \mid \sigma(M^+_G))$) or an oracle posterior. This has the shape of canon's identifiability-floor pattern (M1), with two escapes (ground truth; a reference posterior), but I have not run the five-element test of `#disc-identifiability-floor` and do not claim it as an instance. (F for the indistinguishability; the floor-instance question is open.)

## 4. Calibration is the boundary-invariant criterion; selection/processing is not

Canon's line is drawn between *selection* (goal shapes which event arrives; permitted) and *processing* (goal shapes how the arrived event is processed; forbidden), with the boundary at the input of $f_M$. Consider an agent whose encoder $\phi_G$ coarsens $e$ to $z_G$ and whose updater is the Bayes posterior given $(M^-, a, z_G, [\phi_G])$.

- Draw the boundary at the input of $\phi_G$: the goal enters $f_M$; $\kappa_{\text{processing}} \gt 0$; clause 2 of the scope condition (`:54`) is violated. Classification: coupled.
- Draw the boundary at the output of $\phi_G$, treating $z_G$ as "the event that arrived": the goal shaped which event arrived, through an internal action; $f_M$ processes $z_G$ goal-blindly; $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$ with respect to $z_G$. Classification: separated, by `:56`.

Nothing about the agent changed. Its beliefs are the same, its reliability is $0$ in both descriptions, its resolution is the same. **Proposition 4.1.** The selection/processing classification and the value of $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ depend on where the $f_M$ boundary is drawn; calibration (Definition 2.1) and the reliability/resolution terms do not, because they are defined on the reported belief and the world, not on the decomposition of the update. (F, exact.) The inconsistency between `:54` and `:56` is therefore not a slip to be fixed by picking a side; it is the symptom of drawing a normative line at a representational boundary. Attention *is* selection applied to the agent's internal channel — "the event" the updater sees is what attention let through, and the same reason selection is harmless (the agent knows what it did, and conditions on it) makes allocation harmless under exactly the same condition (Proposition A′). The three-way split that survives is: selection (external channel), allocation (internal channel), distortion (departure from conditional expectation); the first two are one phenomenon at two boundaries, and only the third is a defect.

The content-axis boundary of `der-directed-separation.md:170` / `#disc-structural-imagination` is a separate, fourth distinction (within-model vs model-space reasoning); everything in this spike is within-model.

## 5. What the $\kappa \cdot \mathcal A$ bias bound measures

The owed question was whether the bound counts allocation as hallucination. It does (Proposition 3.2 and (N8): a calibrated allocator is at $\kappa = 1$ in canon's $\kappa$ and at $I(G; M^+ \mid e, M^-) = H(G)$ in Paper 3's information term). But the examination surfaced something prior to that.

### 5.1 The ambiguity factor is zero in the regime the norm is about

Canon defines $\mathcal A(e_\tau) = I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}) / H(\Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ "at the Bayesian-optimal level," the numerator "computed under $P_{\text{ref}}(G)$ and the Bayesian-optimal posterior $P(\Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}, G)$" (`scope-observation-ambiguity-modulation.md:22`–`:26`). For this mutual information to be positive, the *true* conditional law of the world given the event and prior state must depend on the goal: the goal must be evidence about the world. Under (NWI) — with the realized action in the conditioning set per (1.1), which is the only reading under which "Bayesian-optimal" is unambiguous — $P(\Omega \mid e, M^-, G) = P(\Omega \mid e, M^-)$ and $I(G; \Omega \mid e, M^-) = 0$ identically, for every reference goal prior. (N7α): an ambiguous observation with goal-blind posterior $(0.5, 0.5)$ and $G \perp \Omega$ gives $\mathcal A = 0$, while a tilting agent displays goal-conditioned beliefs $0.76$ apart in total variation. The segment's own text concedes the point from the estimator side: a $\kappa = 0$ reference interpreter "would produce no between-goal variance" (`:77`), i.e. the Bayes-optimal interpreter reports $\hat{\mathcal A} = 0$, and the estimator is required to use a goal-coupled interpreter and measures that interpreter's coupling.

Conversely, when $I(G; \Omega \mid e, M^-) \gt 0$, the goal *is* evidence, and the goal-conditional Bayes posterior is the correct belief. The displacement between it and the goal-blind posterior is then the correct amount of updating on an informative variable. (N7β): with goals assigned by a principal who knows more ($P(\text{flaw} \mid e, G = \text{correct}) = 0.8$, $0.2$ under $G = \text{ship}$), $\mathcal A = 0.278$, the goal-using Bayesian moves $0.30$ in TV from the goal-blind belief, and scores *better* ($0.5004$ vs $0.6931$ nats). What canon calls bias at `:43` is, under its own definitions, the Bayes-correct use of a world-informative goal.

So the factor $\mathcal A$ as defined does not measure "interpretive latitude." It measures *how much the goal is evidence about the world after the observation*, which is zero exactly when goal-dependence is a defect and positive exactly when goal-dependence is correct. (F, exact, from the definitions; it is a consequence of (1.1)–(1.2), not of any model.)

### 5.2 Where canon's derivation makes the identification

`deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound.md:52` (Track 1, Step 1) applies the chain rule $\mathbb E_G[\mathrm{KL}(P_{\Omega \mid e, M, G} \Vert P_{\Omega \mid e, M})] = I(G; \Omega \mid e, M)$. That identity holds for the true conditional laws. The worked example at `:143` then sets $P_{\Omega \mid e, M, G} = N(\mu_M + \beta(G), \sigma^2 I)$ with $\beta(G)$ "a goal-dependent shift" — the *agent's* goal-conditioned belief. The two coincide only for a Bayesian agent using $G$ as evidence; for a distorting agent under (NWI) the true law has no $G$-dependence and the agent's belief does, and the chain-rule step relates two different objects. The bound's left side, $\Delta M_{\text{bias}} := f_X^M(X^-, e) - f_M(M^-, e)$ (`:24`), is displacement from the goal-blind truth; its right side, through Step 1, is the information the goal carries about the world. Under (NWI) the right side is $0$ and the left side is whatever the distortion is. The segment's Working Note (`:252`) already senses the seam ("the $\mathcal A$ factor may be absorbable into the $I$ term").

### 5.3 How Paper 3 resolves the seam, and what that costs

Paper 3 (`~/src/neurips/03-llm-hallucinate-bound/src/re/03-setup.md`, `04-main-results.md`, `05-mechanism.md`) makes three moves, each verified against its text:

1. The bias quantity is redefined as the displacement of the goal-conditional post-update law from the goal-*marginal* post-update law, $d(P_{M^+ \mid e, M^-, G}, P_{M^+ \mid e, M^-})$ — the agent's belief under $G$ versus the agent's belief averaged over goals, with no reference to the world.
2. The umbrella theorem is $\mathbb E\lVert \Delta M_{\text{bias}} \rVert \le C \sqrt{I(G; M^+ \mid e, M^-)}$, in the *post-update* information (Lemma 5.1's chain rule is on the post-update law). This is a correct transport inequality on the agent's own goal-dependence; it says nothing about the world, and by Proposition 3.3 its right side saturates at $C\sqrt{H(G)}$ for any injective coupling however small.
3. $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ is redefined as the ratio $I(G; M^+ \mid e, M^-) / I(G; \Omega \mid e, M^-)$, under a named commitment $(H_\kappa)$: $\kappa \le 1$. The paper notes that the "parrot" ($M^+ := G$ with $G \perp \Omega$ given $(e, M^-)$) has $\kappa = \infty$ and is "explicitly outside scope," and adopts the convention $0/0 := 0$.

Under (NWI) the denominator of Paper 3's $\kappa$ is $0$ for every agent, so $(H_\kappa)$ can hold only if the numerator is $0$ too: the architectural factorization $\kappa \cdot I(G; \Omega \mid e, M^-)$ applies, in the no-world-information regime, only to agents whose beliefs do not depend on the goal at all. Motivated reasoning with an uninformative goal — the phenomenon the $\kappa \times \mathcal A$ law is advertised as formalizing (`scope-observation-ambiguity-modulation.md:101`) — is the parrot's regime, scoped out. Paper 3's Class 1 is defined as "$G \perp M^+ \mid \Omega$ given $(e, M^-)$ — $G$ affects $M^+$ only through the latent world-state," which read literally is *the goal may enter beliefs only as evidence about the world*. That is the correct normative criterion, and it is the one §2 reaches; but with the factorization scoped to $(H_\kappa)$, the paper's architectural corollary describes agents that use the goal as evidence, not agents that distort.

### 5.4 Answer to the owed item, and the larger finding

The bound counts allocation as hallucination (Proposition 3.2, (N8)), in both canon's and Paper 3's forms, because both measure displacement across goals rather than departure from calibration. The larger finding is that the ambiguity factor $\mathcal A$, as defined, is not an observation-stream measure of interpretive latitude; it is the goal's evidential value about the world, identically zero under (NWI). The product form $\kappa \cdot \mathcal A$, which canon already marks as "not derived" (`:87`), has no well-posed content in the regime where the degradation norm applies. What survives of the bound: Paper 3's umbrella theorem (a transport inequality on the agent's goal-dependence, correct and world-silent), and the qualitative intuition that sharp observations leave no room for goal-dependence, which §6 recovers with a different carrier. (F, exact for the identically-zero claim and the scoping; the reading of Paper 3 is against its own stated definitions and commitments.)

## 6. A well-formed replacement: the multiplicative-tilt law

The informal claims the segment wanted to carry are right: a test failure leaves no room for motivated reading; "the code is messy" leaves a lot. The carrier was wrong. Here is one that works.

**Setting.** Let $\pi = P(\Omega \mid \mathcal H)$ be the agent's calibrated belief given whatever information $\mathcal H$ it holds (allocation already applied). Let the agent's reported belief be a *multiplicative reweighting* of it, $M^+_G = \pi \cdot r_G / \mathbb E_\pi[r_G]$ with $r_G \gt 0$ a goal-indexed function on world-states. This is Bayes' rule with a goal-pseudo-likelihood; it is the "process-form, multiplicative" cell of `#disc-partial-coupling-pathways` (precision weighting, attention gain, motivated reweighting), and the exponential case $r_G = e^{\lambda u_G}$ with $u_G$ the goal's desirability of each world-state is its canonical member. Allocation-only agents are $r_G \equiv 1$.

**Theorem 6.1 (Jensen-gap identity and range bound).** For $M^+_G = \pi r_G / \mathbb E_\pi r_G$,

$$\mathrm{KL}(\pi \Vert M^+_G) = \log \mathbb E_\pi[r_G] - \mathbb E_\pi[\log r_G],$$

the Jensen gap of $\log r_G$ under $\pi$. Consequently: (i) $\mathrm{KL}(\pi \Vert M^+_G) = 0$ iff $\log r_G$ is $\pi$-a.s. constant — iff the goal's tilt does not vary over the support of the calibrated posterior; (ii) if $\log r_G$ takes values in an interval of length $L_\pi(G)$ on $\mathrm{supp}\,\pi$, then $\mathrm{KL}(\pi \Vert M^+_G) \le L_\pi(G)^2 / 8$, $\mathrm{KL}(M^+_G \Vert \pi) \le L_\pi(G)^2 / 8$, and $\mathrm{TV}(M^+_G, \pi) \le L_\pi(G) / 4$; (iii) in the exponential case, $\mathrm{KL}(\pi \Vert M^+_G) = \Lambda_\pi(\lambda) - \lambda \mathbb E_\pi u_G = \tfrac{\lambda^2}{2} \mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G) + O(\lambda^3)$, where $\Lambda_\pi$ is the cumulant generating function of $u_G$ under $\pi$.

*Proof.* $\log(\pi / M^+_G) = \log \mathbb E_\pi r_G - \log r_G$; integrate against $\pi$. (i) is Jensen's equality case for the strictly concave $\log$. (ii) is Hoeffding's lemma, $\log \mathbb E e^{X} \le \mathbb E X + (b - a)^2 / 8$ for $a \le X \le b$, applied to $X = \log r_G$ under $\pi$ (and to $-X$ under $M^+_G$ for the reverse direction, using $\mathbb E_{M^+_G} e^{-X} = 1 / \mathbb E_\pi r_G$), then Pinsker. (iii) is the cumulant expansion. $\square$ (N1): the identity holds on 2000 random cases; the bounds hold with observed ratio to the bound approaching $1$; a tilt that varies only off the support gives $\mathrm{KL} = 0$. (F, exact within the multiplicative class.)

**What the two factors are.** In the exponential case the displacement is governed by $\lambda$, a property of the processor (how hard the goal pushes — the distortion gain, replacing $\kappa$), times $\mathrm{sd}_\pi(u_G)$ or $L_\pi(G)$, the residual spread of the *goal-relevant functional* under the calibrated posterior (replacing $\mathcal A$). The second factor is a genuine observation-stream-times-goal property: it is computed from the goal-blind posterior and the goal's desirability function, with no agent in it; it does not require the goal to carry world information; it is zero when the observation pins down the goal-relevant quantity even if $\Omega$ stays uncertain in goal-irrelevant directions, which is sharper than $H(\Omega \mid e, M^-)$ and matches the segment's examples exactly (a test result pins $u_G$; "the code is messy" does not). And the law is zero for allocation-only agents by construction, which disposes of the owed item on its own terms: this bound does not count allocation as anything.

**Scope.** This is a theorem within a *model class* (multiplicative reweighting of a calibrated base), not a model-free bound. Additive-content-form distortions (a goal-dependent shift of a mean that is not a reweighting) are not covered, and a different argument is needed for them. The exponential tilt is the natural first-order model and, per `#disc-partial-coupling-pathways:66`, the common process form; whether real coupled architectures are well described by it is a D question with the obvious test (regress goal-conditioned log-odds shifts on $u_G$). Note also that the displacement bounded here is from the *calibrated* belief, i.e. it is a reliability-type quantity, not a displacement from a goal-average; that is the right object for the degradation norm.

**Relation to $\kappa$.** $\lambda$ is not $\kappa$. Under the tilt model $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ is $1$ for every $\lambda \gt 0$ with distinct $u_G$ across goals (Proposition 3.3). The processor property that controls bias is a gain, not an information ratio. (F, exact.)

## 7. Break-point 4: when a goal-allocated encoder starves step 5a

The explorer's claim: allocation keeps beliefs calibrated but makes the information set goal-dependent, so evidence that the goal is infeasible is what a goal-allocated encoder is least likely to encode, and cascade step 5a ("check whether $M_t$ correction changes the feasibility assessment," `der-orient-cascade.md:60`) is starved. Second reader's verdict: right for a specific and common allocator, wrong as stated for allocation per se, and the condition is exact.

**Proposition 7.1 (value-of-information form).** Let an allocator choose what to encode, at fixed capacity, to maximize $\mathbb E[\max_{d \in D} \mathbb E[U(d, \Omega) \mid \mathcal H]]$ over admissible $\mathcal H$, where $D$ is the decision set over which value is computed. Let $f_{\text{feas}}$ be a feature of $\Omega$ whose only effect on payoffs is to make every goal-pursuing action's payoff equal (to the infeasible value) when the goal is infeasible. Then: (a) if $D$ contains only goal-pursuing actions, the value of information of $f_{\text{feas}}$ is exactly zero, so a capacity-limited VOI-optimal allocator never encodes it and 5a is starved; (b) if $D$ contains an action whose payoff does not depend on feasibility (abandon, revise the objective), the value of information of $f_{\text{feas}}$ is positive whenever that action is optimal under infeasibility and not under feasibility, and the same allocator encodes it. *Proof.* (a) Under infeasibility all actions in $D$ tie, so learning $f_{\text{feas}}$ never changes the argmax in a way that changes value; the inner maximum is linear in $P(\text{feasible})$ and VOI of a variable that only rescales a linear objective is zero. (b) The inner maximum becomes piecewise linear with a kink at the switch point; VOI is the expected gain over the kink. $\square$ (N6): with pursue-only $D = \{a_1, a_2\}$, $\mathrm{VOI}(f_{\text{feas}}) = 0$ while $\mathrm{VOI}(f_1) = 0.30$; adding an abandon action paying $0.25$ gives $\mathrm{VOI}(f_{\text{feas}}) = 0.10$. (F, exact.)

So the starvation is not a property of goal-allocation; it is a property of allocating by the value of a decision problem that *excludes the option the cascade's step 5d exercises*. This connects two things canon holds separately. First, the cascade's own structure: 5a–5c precede 5d, but an allocator that computes value only over pursuing actions has already priced 5d at zero; the cascade's late step is invisible to the early allocator. Second, `#deriv-self-actuation-grounding`: for the revise option to carry value the allocator needs a value for "goal revised" that is not computed from the goal's own functional — which is that segment's grounding condition seen from the attention side. Three remedies follow, in the register of design claims (N): (i) a goal-independent encoding reserve (the explorer's $\varepsilon$-attention); (ii) include objective revision in the allocator's decision set, so infeasibility evidence has positive VOI — the principled remedy, and the one that needs the grounding invariant; (iii) an external observer whose attention is not shaped by this agent's goal — the attention-level instance of `#hyp-solicitable-escape`'s other-observer route. The test is the explorer's: agents with and without each remedy, in worlds where the goal becomes infeasible through a change in a goal-irrelevant feature.

The explorer's analogy to `#der-observability-dominance`'s absorbing regions holds with one sharpening: the frozen edges there are *imposed* ($\sigma_v \approx 0$) and the agent cannot tell case (c) from case (a) of the zero-aporia trichotomy; here the freeze is *chosen*, and a calibrated allocator knows exactly which features it has left at the prior — its uncertainty about them is honest. What it lacks is not knowledge of its ignorance but a reason to spend capacity on it. That is why remedy (ii) is the structural one.

## 8. Break-point 3: goals that carry information

Per (1.2), a world-informative goal is handled by treating its arrival as an event. Two consequences for canon. First, Paper 3's Class 1 criterion ("$G$ affects $M^+$ only through $\Omega$") is, read as a norm, exactly right: the goal may enter beliefs only as evidence, and (NWI)-restoration by recording the goal-setting event is how an agent complies. Second, the two scoping facts in §1 (collider; sufficiency) say when canon's conditional-independence statement at `der-directed-separation.md:204` and the (NWI) hypothesis actually agree: when the realized action is in the conditioning set and $M$ is sufficient for what $G$ retained. An agent with a lossy $M$ and a $G$ that remembers more than $M$ does — which canon's $f_G(G^-, M^+, e)$ permits — will show $\kappa_{\text{processing}} \gt 0$ *for correct Bayesian reasons*. This is a third benign source of positive $\kappa$ alongside allocation, and another reason the measure cannot carry the norm. (F, exact as conditionals.)

## 9. What this does to the classification and the norm

$\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ measures whether and how recoverably goal information reaches the belief. It classifies architectures by *ports* — where goal information can flow — and that is a legitimate and certifiable architectural fact (`der-directed-separation.md:73`'s certifiability reading survives intact). It does not measure whether beliefs are corrupted. Three benign sources make it positive: calibrated allocation (§2), correct use of a world-informative goal (§8), and a lossy $M$ with a goal that retained what $M$ dropped (§8). One malign source makes it positive: distortion. The degradation norm "less coupling tracks reality strictly better" is true of the reliability term under the agent's model and under a well-specified world (§2.5), false of resolution (§2.6), and silent about the benign sources. Stated at the strength the mathematics earns:

> *Goal-dependence of the belief update is a defect exactly when it moves the reported belief off the conditional law of the world given the agent's information (Definition 2.1). Goal-dependence of the information itself — through external selection or internal allocation — is not a defect, provided the attention mechanism is ignorable or modeled (Proposition A′), and is a resolution trade-off, not a reliability loss (Theorem 3.1). The architectural classification by $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ certifies, at $\kappa = 0$, that no goal-attributable reliability loss is possible; at $\kappa \gt 0$ it says nothing about reliability, and the reliability term itself, estimated with outcomes, is the diagnostic.*

What Class 1 by construction still buys is the certificate: with no goal port, goal-attributable reliability loss is impossible by inspection. An allocation-only Class 2 agent is equally honest but must be *shown* so with outcomes — the certifiability/behavior asymmetry canon already draws at Boundary 1 (`:101`), now with the right object (reliability, not $\kappa$) on the behavioral side.

## 10. The IB crack

`form-information-bottleneck.md:50`'s policy-relativity makes the optimal encoder goal-dependent. That is allocation coupling: the IB-optimal $\phi^\ast_\pi$ is a choice of $\mathcal H$, and $M = P(\Omega \mid \phi^\ast_\pi(\mathcal C))$ is calibrated for every $\pi$ (Proposition A, with the mechanism ignorable because the encoder is chosen from the policy, not from the content it drops). Directed separation as a *calibration* invariant is untouched by policy-relative compression; directed separation as the *conditional-independence* statement at `:204` is violated by it, and canon's resolution (fix a default $\pi_{\text{cont}}$ to build $M_t$) buys the conditional-independence form back at the price of goal-relevant efficiency. That is one legitimate allocation; the goal-following allocation with a reserve (§7 remedy i) or with revision in the decision set (remedy ii) is another. The crack is real for the CI form and absent for the calibration form. (F, exact given §2.)

## 11. The wrapper level, briefly

In the W₁ regime of `#der-class-coercion-via-wrapping`, the wrapper's belief-update map takes the query made as an argument by type signature, so the wrapper is *selection-aware by construction*; under (C3) the component's response given the query carries no further goal-dependence. The residual W₁ "leakage" $\kappa_{W_1}^{\text{sel}} = I(A(q_M); G^{\text{op}}) \le I(q_M; G^{\text{op}})$ is therefore, under (C2′)+(C3), goal-dependent query selection with a calibration-preserving update — allocation, with zero reliability cost. What *does* cost reliability at the wrapper level is a (C3) failure (the component infers the operator goal from the query and tilts its answer), which is Theorem 2's KL-leakage, and it is bounded separately. The structural content of the W₁ result is right; the word "leakage" for the selection-channel term labels a non-defect as a defect, and the quality–separation trade-off the segment describes is a resolution trade-off, exactly §2.6. I offer this as a reading, not a verified defect; the verifier should check whether the segment anywhere treats $\kappa_{W_1}^{\text{sel}}$ as a bias on $M_W$. (D/F, reading.)
