# Debrief — what turned out to be true about goals getting into beliefs

Joseph — this is written to you from the corrected files, not from memory of the work. Where I'm unsure what you hold I've put a prerequisite in parentheses rather than explaining it.

## The question I was handed

Part II's keystone says the belief update must not look at the goal: $M_{\tau^+} = f_M(M_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$, no $G$ argument. The Part II explorer this morning noticed that the way the goal gets in matters. A goal can decide *what you look at* (you read the error log, not the style guide), or it can decide *what you conclude from what you looked at* (you read the error log and decide it isn't really an error because you want to ship). He conjectured the first is harmless and the second is the actual pathology, that canon's measure $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ cannot tell them apart, and that `der-directed-separation` already contradicts itself about where attention sits. He left two things owed: whether a goal that chooses what to look at starves the orient cascade's "would a model correction change feasibility?" step, and whether the Volume 3 bias bound ($\kappa \cdot \mathcal A$) counts the harmless kind as hallucination.

## What is true, in the order I'd want you to have it

**1. The split is real and the criterion that separates it is older and simpler than the explorer's phrasing.** A belief is *calibrated* when it equals the probability of the world given the belief itself (assumes the tower property). That single condition is equivalent to "the belief is the Bayes posterior given *some* information the agent actually holds." So the only freedom a calibrated agent has is *which* information; *that* is what a goal may legitimately decide. Goal-dependence that keeps beliefs calibrated is a choice of information (allocation); goal-dependence that breaks calibration is a departure from Bayes on whatever information was used (distortion). Everything else in the spike is consequences of this.

**2. "Harmless" has a precise condition, and it is Rubin's, not ours.** Looking only at some of the evidence is harmless in the sense that the belief stays calibrated *if* the rule for what you skipped did not depend on what the skipped part said (or, if it did, you model that). Skipping the disconfirming paragraph *because it looked disconfirming*, then updating as if you'd never seen it, is miscalibrated even though "all you did was allocate attention." This is exactly ignorability / missing-at-random (Rubin 1976) applied to the agent's own attention. The explorer's "selection-aware" was a name for this; the condition is sharper than the name. In the worked example the peeking rule costs $0.015$ nats of calibration error; the same rule with the skip modeled costs $0$.

**3. Distortion strictly costs — by the agent's own lights always; by the world's only if the agent's model is right.** Under every strictly proper scoring rule, a miscalibrated belief scores worse than its own recalibration, and the Bayes posterior is the unique best belief on any fixed information. That is standard (the reliability term of the Murphy/Bröcker decomposition). But the explorer's "worsens accuracy about the world, for every goal" is false as a universal: a *misspecified* agent whose prior is too pessimistic can be made objectively more accurate by a goal-tilt toward optimism. I built one: true probability $0.8$, agent prior $0.3$, and the objective log loss falls from $1.00$ at no tilt to $0.49$ at a strong tilt, while the agent's *subjective* loss is minimized at no tilt. So canon's norm "less coupling tracks reality strictly better" is true of the calibration term under the agent's model unconditionally and under the world's law when the model is well-specified, and that scope has to be stated.

**4. The instrument that separates the two kinds is the standard reliability/resolution decomposition, and it needs outcomes.** Expected proper score = uncertainty − resolution + reliability. Allocation moves resolution only (which features you keep changes how much the world is resolved); distortion adds reliability loss. From goal-probing alone (same event, different goals, no ground truth) the two are indistinguishable — a calibrated coarsening and a miscalibrated reweighting can produce the same family of reports. E.1's experiment needs outcomes (or an oracle posterior) alongside $\hat\kappa$, or it will read a calibrated W₁ wrapper and a sycophant the same way.

**5. $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ cannot separate them, and does not measure magnitude either.** It is a conditional mutual information. A perfectly calibrated agent whose two goals make it encode two different features sits at $\kappa = 1$. Worse: mutual information measures how *recoverable* the goal is from the belief, not how far the belief moved. A noiseless goal-dependence of total variation $10^{-6}$ gives $\kappa = 1$, the same as one of $0.76$; with update noise it becomes a signal-to-noise ratio of the goal against the update's own noise. Canon's prose at `:90` ("nearly all goal information is available to the epistemic update") is literally right and is not a statement about displacement. The behavioral estimator at `:97` is a normalized distance — it estimates displacement, a different quantity from the definition it is said to estimate.

**6. The `:54` vs `:56` inconsistency is not a slip; it is what happens when a normative line is drawn at a representational boundary.** Take an agent whose attention coarsens the event and whose update is Bayes on what it kept. Draw the boundary of $f_M$ *before* attention: the goal enters $f_M$, $\kappa \gt 0$, clause 2 violated — coupled. Draw it *after* attention, calling the coarsened thing "the event that arrived": goal-blind processing of a goal-selected event — separated, by `:56`. Same agent, same beliefs, same calibration. Attention *is* selection applied to the internal channel, and the reason selection is fine (you know what you did and condition on it) is the reason allocation is fine under the same condition. Calibration and the reliability term do not move when you redraw the boundary; the verbal classification does. Canon's $\kappa$ itself does not move either — it conditions on the external event and stays positive for this agent under both drawings — so canon's own two instruments disagree with each other about one agent, and the invariant is calibration.

**7. The bias bound's ambiguity factor is identically zero in the regime it is meant for.** This is the finding I'd most want checked by someone who hasn't read my file. Canon defines $\mathcal A(e_\tau) = I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}) / H(\Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ at the Bayesian-optimal level. For that mutual information to be positive, the *true* law of the world given the observation must depend on the goal — the goal must be *evidence about the world*. When the goal carries no world information (the explorer's hypothesis, which is also the only regime in which goal-dependence is a defect), it is zero, for every reference goal prior. The segment half-knows this: it concedes at `:77` that a goal-blind reference interpreter "would produce no between-goal variance," i.e. the Bayes-optimal interpreter reports $\hat{\mathcal A} = 0$, so the estimator must use a goal-coupled interpreter and measures that interpreter's coupling. Conversely when $\mathcal A \gt 0$ the goal *is* evidence and the "bias" the bound measures is the correct Bayesian update on an informative variable — in my two-state example the goal-using agent moves $0.30$ from the goal-blind belief and scores *better*. So $\mathcal A$ does not measure interpretive latitude; it measures the goal's evidential value, which is zero exactly when goal-dependence is wrong and positive exactly when it is right. The product $\kappa \cdot \mathcal A$ — which canon already marks "not derived" — has no well-posed content in the regime the norm is about. One subtlety a verifier must not skip: the realized action has to be in the conditioning set, or conditioning on the observation alone manufactures a spurious goal–world dependence (the observation is a collider). "Conditional on the realized event" has to mean the event including the action taken.

Paper 3 (`~/src/neurips/03-llm-hallucinate-bound/`) resolved this seam differently and, I think, consistently but at a cost: its bias is displacement from the goal-*averaged* belief (no world in it), its theorem is a transport inequality in the *post-update* mutual information (correct, and silent about the world), and its $\kappa$ is the ratio of post-update to world mutual information under a commitment $\kappa \le 1$ that scopes out the "parrot" — which, under no-world-information, is every agent whose beliefs depend on the goal at all. The architectural factorization there describes agents that use the goal as evidence, not agents that distort. I read the paper's definitions; I did not re-derive its theorem.

**8. There is a well-formed replacement that carries what the bound wanted to say.** The intuition was right: a test result leaves no room for motivated reading; "the code is messy" leaves plenty. The right carrier (assumes the exponential-family / tilting idea): model distortion as multiplying the calibrated posterior by a goal-dependent weight — Bayes with a goal-pseudo-likelihood, which is the multiplicative process-form cell of `#disc-partial-coupling-pathways`. Then the displacement from the calibrated belief is *exactly* the Jensen gap of the log-weight under the calibrated posterior: zero iff the goal's tilt is constant on the posterior's support, bounded by one-eighth of the squared range of the log-tilt on that support, and to second order equal to $\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda^2\,\mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G)$ — distortion gain squared times the residual variance of the *goal-relevant* quantity under the goal-blind posterior. Both factors are well-defined: $\lambda$ is a processor property (how hard the goal pushes); $\mathrm{Var}_\pi(u_G)$ is an observation-times-goal property with no agent in it, zero when the observation pins down what the goal cares about even if the world stays uncertain elsewhere, and it does not need the goal to carry world information. It is zero for allocation-only agents by construction, which answers the owed question on its own terms. Canon's own Gaussian worked example found numerically the $\sigma^2$ dependence this identity gives exactly. The scope: exact within the multiplicative class; an impossible state asserted with positive probability is outside the class (that is the level-confusion mechanism of `#disc-structural-imagination`, not drift).

**9. The 5a-starvation worry is right for a specific allocator and the condition is exact.** An allocator that spends its capacity by value of information *over goal-pursuing actions only* assigns zero value to learning the goal is infeasible — if the goal is infeasible, every pursuing action ties, so knowing it changes nothing it can do. It never looks. Add an "abandon / revise the objective" action that is optimal under infeasibility and not otherwise, and the same allocator gives feasibility evidence positive value and looks. The cascade puts objective revision last (5d), but an allocator that values only pursuit has priced 5d at zero before 5a runs. Three remedies, in design register: a goal-independent attention reserve (the explorer's); put revision in the allocator's decision set — the principled one, and it needs the grounding invariant of `#deriv-self-actuation-grounding` for "goal revised" to have a value not computed from the goal's own functional; or an external observer whose attention isn't shaped by this agent's goal (the attention-level instance of `#hyp-solicitable-escape`). The sharpening on the explorer's analogy to frozen edges: here the freeze is chosen and the agent's uncertainty about the unattended features is honest; what it lacks is a reason to spend on them, not knowledge of its ignorance.

**10. The IB crack is in the conditional-independence form, not the calibration form.** Policy-relative compression is allocation: the IB-optimal encoder for a policy is a choice of information, and the posterior on it is calibrated for every policy. Canon's fix (freeze a default $\pi_{\text{cont}}$ to build $M_t$) buys back the conditional-independence statement at the price of goal-relevant efficiency; it is one legitimate choice, not the only one.

## What this means for the classification

$\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ classifies architectures by *ports* — where goal information can reach the belief — and that remains a real, certifiable fact about wiring; Class 1 by construction still buys the certificate that no goal-attributable calibration loss is possible, by inspection. What it does not do is measure corruption. Three benign things make it positive (calibrated allocation; correct use of a goal that is evidence; a lossy $M$ with a goal that remembers what $M$ dropped — which canon's own $f_G(G^-, M^+, e)$ permits) and one malign thing does. The norm, at the strength the mathematics earns: *goal-dependence of the belief update is a defect exactly when it moves the reported belief off the probability of the world given the agent's information; goal-dependence of the information is not, provided the attention mechanism is ignorable or modeled, and is a resolution trade-off rather than a reliability loss.*
