# Neighborhood map — what canon already holds around the allocation/distortion question

*KR C.2 under the top intent "Complete 'in-place' refactor of ASF — meta, all artifacts, and theory itself." This file is the first deliverable of the spike: the map I built before deriving, so that an ill-posed brief is caught here rather than inherited by the derivation. Line numbers refer to HEAD `620ac98c` (2026-10-30).*

## 1. The question as handed to me, in one paragraph

The owner review's Part II explorer (`msc/inside-out-2026-10/owner-review/explorers/part-ii.md` §7.2) proposed that goal-dependence in the belief update comes in two kinds: *allocation* (the goal shapes what is encoded) and *distortion* (the goal shapes how the agent updates on what it encoded). It claimed allocation preserves calibration if the update is selection-aware, distortion strictly worsens accuracy under every proper scoring rule when the goal carries no world information, $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ cannot separate the two, and `der-directed-separation.md` draws the line inconsistently at `:54` versus `:56`. It left two items owed: a second reader on break-point 4 (a goal-allocated encoder starves cascade step 5a), and whether the $\kappa \cdot \mathcal A$ bias bound counts allocation as hallucination.

## 2. What canon establishes that bears on this

**The condition itself.** `01-aat-core/src/der-directed-separation.md:36` states directed separation as $M_{\tau^+} = f_M(M_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$ with no $G_t$ argument; `:204` gives the conditional-independence form $M_{\tau^+} \perp G_t \mid (M_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$. `:16` and `:56` scope it as goal-blindness *conditional on the realized event*: selection of events through $G \to \pi \to a \to e$ is permitted; processing of the arrived event must not reference goals. `:53`–`:54` list the two clauses of the scope condition; clause 2 forbids "goal-dependent attention thresholds … baked into $f_M$." `:56` then lists "goal-directed sensing, attention allocation, query selection" as permitted selection. The two sentences use the word *attention* on opposite sides of the line. The explorer's inconsistency claim is verified.

**The measure.** `der-directed-separation.md:85` defines $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = I(G_t; M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}) / H(G_t \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$. `:95`–`:99` give a behavioral estimator $\hat\kappa$ as a normalized distance between goal-conditioned epistemic responses. `:237` (audit gold) already flags the zero-denominator problem. The Working Note at `:215` records an open two-senses-of-competence thread in which implicit goal inference from query patterns raises the wrapper-level leak.

**The three classes and what they certify.** `:73` says the classes are positions of one coupling distinguished by what is *certifiable* about it; `:109`–`:113` define *causal discipline* as $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$ and state the degradation norm: "less coupling tracks reality strictly better, all else equal," carried by an $\varepsilon(\kappa)$ bound. That norm is the thing the allocation/distortion split tests.

**The sub-typology.** `01-aat-core/src/disc-partial-coupling-pathways.md:15` already says in words that the scalar "cannot distinguish the agent that uses goals to *attend* from the one that uses goals to *interpret*." Its (stage × source × form) parameterization puts a goal argument in P1 featurization $x = \phi(e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ as coupling (`:35`, `:40`), while its phenomena table (`:144`) files "Goal-directed attention" as "(selection — pre-$f_M$) … formally allowed." So the same segment treats goal-dependent featurization as coupling and goal-directed attention as not, with no criterion for which an instance is. The form axis there (content vs process) classifies distortions by separability; it has no cell for a goal-dependent *coarsening* of the input, which is what allocation is.

**The bias bound and the ambiguity factor.** `03-llm-core/src/scope-observation-ambiguity-modulation.md:24` defines $\mathcal A(e_\tau) = I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}) / H(\Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ "at the Bayesian-optimal level," with the numerator computed under "the Bayesian-optimal posterior $P(\Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-}, G)$"; `:43` states the bound $\lVert \Delta M_{\text{bias}} \rVert \le C \kappa_{\text{processing}} I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$; `:77` concedes that a $\kappa = 0$ reference interpreter "would produce no between-goal variance." `01-aat-core/src/deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound.md:24` defines $\Delta M_{\text{bias}} := f_X^M(X_{\tau^-}, e_\tau) - f_M(M_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$ (displacement from the goal-blind update); its Track 1 Step 1 (`:52`) applies the chain rule to $P_{\Omega \mid e, M, G}$ as a *true* conditional law; its Gaussian example (`:143`) makes that same object the *agent's* goal-shifted belief $N(\mu_M + \beta(G), \sigma^2 I)$. Those two readings coincide only for a Bayesian agent that treats the goal as evidence about the world. The segment's own Working Note (`:252`) flags that "the $\mathcal A$ factor may be absorbable into the $I$ term," which is the seam. Paper 3 (`~/src/neurips/03-llm-hallucinate-bound/src/re/03-setup.md`) resolves the seam differently: its bias quantity is the displacement of the goal-conditional post-update law from the goal-*marginal* law (not from the goal-blind truth), its umbrella theorem is stated in $I(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ (the post-update mutual information, Lemma 5.1), and its $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ is the *ratio* $I(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid e, M^-) / I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e, M^-)$ under a named commitment $(H_\kappa)$: $\kappa \le 1$, with the "parrot" $M_{\tau^+} := G$ on goal-orthogonal evidence ($\kappa = \infty$) explicitly outside scope and a $0/0 := 0$ convention. Canon's and Paper 3's $\kappa$ are different quantities with the same name.

**The orient cascade.** `01-aat-core/src/der-orient-cascade.md:60` is step 5a ("check whether $M_t$ correction changes the feasibility assessment"). The explorer's break-point 4 says a goal-allocated encoder never gathers the information 5a needs. `der-observability-dominance.md:16` is the absorbing-region mechanism the explorer invokes ("cannot learn, and cannot recognize that it cannot learn"); `hyp-solicitable-escape` (Part III) is canon's result that the other-observer route is the only solicitable escape from such regions.

**The IB crack.** `01-aat-core/src/form-information-bottleneck.md:50` makes the IB objective policy-relative; `:93` (audit gold) records a fresh reader calling this "the very first crack in the armor of Directed Separation" and routes it to `der-directed-separation` and the cascade, where it is not addressed (verified: no mention of policy-relative compression in either body).

**The frozen-coordinate result.** `01-aat-core/src/deriv-decomposition-uniqueness.md` Proposition 1(b): a frozen goal coordinate has the information-flow signature of a belief; the partition is carved by the goal-intervention family, not discoverable from a single agent's update algebra. Consequence for this spike: design-time goal-specialized compression is invisible to $\kappa$ (denominator $H(G) = 0$); only runtime goal-*varying* allocation registers anywhere.

**The third kind of goal-dependence canon already names.** `der-directed-separation.md:170` and `disc-structural-imagination.md:45` carve a *content-axis* boundary: goal-blind processing is correct for within-model evidence; goal-*directed* exploration is correct for model-space reasoning. Allocation and distortion are both *within-model*; this spike does not touch the model-space side.

**Wrapping.** `01-aat-core/src/der-class-coercion-via-wrapping.md` W₁ regime: the wrapper's belief-update map $f_M(M_W, o_W, q_M, A(q_M))$ takes the *query made* as an argument by type signature, and the residual W₁ leak is the selection-channel quantity $\kappa_{W_1}^{\text{sel}} = I(A(q_M); G^{\text{op}}) \le I(q_M; G^{\text{op}})$ (DPI along $G^{\text{op}} \to q_M \to A(q_M)$ under (C2′)). Under (C3) the component's response given the query is goal-independent, so this residual is goal-dependent *query selection* with a selection-aware update, which is the shape the explorer calls allocation. The spike's `.integrated/spike-directed-separation-foundation-proposal-2026-05-31.md:131` records that the earlier W₁ bound was "circular/vacuous" and was replaced by this selection-channel form.

**Prior spikes (regression axis, spikes.sop §2a).** `spikes/.integrated/spike-kappa-hb-operationalization.md:59`–`:67` introduced the $\kappa_{\text{selection}} / \kappa_{\text{processing}}$ split and said selection "is fine; the processing coupling is the problem." `spike-kappa-session-residual.md:48`–`:52` named a third kind, "filtering coupling (which parts of an event to attend to) — somewhere between 1 and 2," and left it there. Pickaxe on `*/src/*` for `rational inattention`, `allocation coupling`, `selection-aware`, `proper scoring` returns nothing. No formal allocation/distortion result was ever landed or corrected away; this is a *never-landed* question, not a *corrected-away* one.

## 3. Where the live tensions are

1. The selection/processing line is drawn at a representational boundary (where $f_M$ begins). Attention sits on both sides of it in the same segment and in the sub-typology. No criterion in canon says which side a given goal-dependent coarsening falls on.
2. $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ is defined as a conditional mutual information. A mutual information measures how recoverable $G$ is from $M_{\tau^+}$, not how far $M_{\tau^+}$ moved; it is positive for any goal-dependent coarsening, however benign.
3. The bias bound's information term is written as $I(G; \Omega_\tau \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ in canon and as $I(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ in Paper 3. The first is a property of the world–goal joint; the second is a property of the agent. The explorer's hypothesis "the goal carries no world information" makes the first identically zero.
4. Canon's degradation norm ("less coupling tracks reality strictly better") is stated for one coupling. The explorer's split says it is true of one kind and false of the other.

## 4. What the target genuinely adds, if it holds

A *normative* criterion for goal-dependence (does the update stay a conditional expectation of the world given the agent's information?) that is independent of where the $f_M$ boundary is drawn; a standard decomposition (reliability/resolution) that separates the two kinds from the same probing data plus outcomes; a scoped version of the degradation norm; a resolution of the IB crack; a precise condition for break-point 4; and a determination of what the bias bound measures. The derivation is `01-derivation.md`; the adversarial instances and numbers are `02-adversarial-instances.md` with `sims/check.py`.

## 5. Terms I use that are not canon's (do-not-inherit candidates)

*allocation*, *distortion*, *calibrated update*, *reliability*/*resolution*, *(NWI)* for the no-world-information hypothesis, *tilt law*. All are either standard outside ASF (reliability/resolution, Rubin's ignorability, proper scoring rules) or local to this spike; none should enter canon without the naming cycle asking whether a standard name exists (INTENT F.4).
