## 1. Finding — VERIFIED — M1's actor-positioning self-description does not match two of its own four instances

`#disc-identifiability-floor` uses actor-positioning as a **load-bearing discriminator**: it is the stated reason two near-miss results are routed *out* of the catalog to sister meta-segments. The exclusion of reward-channel learning reads:

> in Instances 1–4 the actor frustrated by the floor is the *agent itself* (the agent cannot detect causal insufficiency from on-policy data; cannot identify L1' mixtures from single-channel observations; cannot certify composite contraction from component data; cannot identify architecture from on-policy summary statistics) … In the reward-channel learning case the actor frustrated by the floor is the *principal* … agent-exploiting / principal-frustrated rather than agent-frustrated / agent-escaping.

Checked against the instances' own segments, the parenthetical is accurate for Instances 1 and 2 and **not** accurate for Instances 3 and 4.

**Instance 1** ( `#der-causal-insufficiency-detection`) is squarely agent-frustrated and agent-escaping. Its own text: *"The agent generates test data through the standard exploration mechanism (SA3)"*; *"the agent probes its own model's structural assumptions by violating (S3) deliberately"*; route (b) *"uses only machinery the theory already requires."* The frustrated party and the escaping party are the same agent, reasoning about its own model.

**Instance 4** ( `#der-architecture-noidentifiability`) is **observer**-frustrated throughout its own text — the word is the segment's, used consistently and structurally, not incidentally: *"An **observer** with that access cannot distinguish the two agents"*; *"the **observer** has on-policy, in-regime, summary-only access"*; whether the architectural content *"can be recovered from the **observer's** accessible data"*; and the escapes are individuated by *"what the **observer** must have (white-box access versus interventional access versus moment-level access)."* The agent whose architecture is unidentifiable is not frustrated by this floor at all — it is the *object* of the failed inference, not its subject.

**Instance 3** ( `#deriv-critical-mass-composition` / `#result-contraction-template`) is the same shape, and Instance 4's own cross-reference says so: it names the three deployment modes of interventional access as agent-self-intervention (Instance 1), **"observer-on-sub-agent intervention"** (Instance 3), and **"observer-on-agent-input intervention"** (Instance 4). Two of the three modes are an observer's.

**The narrow, defensible form of the finding.** The discriminator has two halves and only one is inconsistent. *"The escape elevates agent-side machinery"* holds for all four — loop-interventional access is AAT machinery regardless of who deploys it. *"The actor frustrated by the floor is the agent itself"* holds for two of four. So this is not a claim that Instances 3 and 4 are misfiled; it is that **the stated criterion does not pick out the set it is claimed to pick out**, and the criterion is doing real work at the catalog's boundary.

**Why this is worth fixing rather than noting.** The reward-channel exclusion turns specifically on the frustrated-actor half (*"the actor frustrated … is the principal"*). If observer-frustrated results are already inside the catalog, then "the frustrated actor is not the agent" cannot by itself carry that exclusion — a principal inferring about an agent from reward data occupies a structurally similar position to an observer inferring about an agent from summary statistics. The exclusion may well be right on other grounds (the escape menu really is principal-side commitment rather than data augmentation, which is the *machinery* half and survives intact). The repair is therefore likely a **sharpening of the stated criterion to the half that actually discriminates**, not a re-filing of anything.

Candidate sharpening, offered as a starting point rather than a recommendation: the axis that separates the three clusters cleanly may be *what kind of move the escape is* — data/information augmentation (M1) vs off-interface anchoring (value-functional-grounding) vs design-constraint relaxation (implementation-impossibility) — with the frustrated actor demoted to descriptive context. That reading admits Instances 1–4 uniformly, keeps both existing exclusions, and needs no new cell.

## 3. Hypothesis — UNVERIFIED — peer-standpoint escape as a structural category

In all four current M1 instances the frustrated party has at least one escape it can deploy from its own position. In `#der-compensation-channel-uniqueness` it provably does not: under (FW) the unique channel is a cohort member whose $p_{k+1}$ is correlated with $Y$ *because that member conditioned on $E$'s actual trajectory*. The entity can **solicit** the escape; it cannot **supply** it.

**The strongest evidence against this, stated up front.** `#disc-identifiability-floor` already lists the rank-augmentation routes as *"interventional data via the loop, a side channel, **a witness**"* — the witness is contemplated. And `#disc-value-functional-grounding-floor`'s Working Notes record an auditor (384279) attempting exactly a peer-based third route against its exhaustion claim and finding it collapses: *"inter-agent grounding (a peer agent's value functional anchoring this agent's goal-stability — just shifts the narrowness one level out, since the peer's value functional is itself narrow)."*

**Why I think the collapse argument does not reach this case, and the distinction to attack.** The refuted route asks a peer to supply **the same faculty** the agent lacks; it collapses because the peer's faculty is narrow in the same way, so the deficiency relocates rather than resolves. `#der-compensation-channel-uniqueness` has a peer supply **a standpoint**, not a faculty: what carries the information is the peer's *positional correlation with $E$'s trajectory*, and the peer's own identity-persistence difficulties are irrelevant to whether it can attest to $E$'s. There is nothing to shift one level out, and the DPI argument is a proof of that rather than an intuition about it.

Compressed: **faculty-supply collapses; standpoint-supply does not.** If that asymmetry is real it is worth a sentence in canon somewhere, because it is the difference between two moves that currently look alike.

**Scope discipline — what this is not.** The exhaustion claim it appears to bear on is scoped to *value-functional / goal-stability anchoring*. `#der-compensation-channel-uniqueness` is a different floor (*identity persistence*). It is therefore **not** a counterexample to two-routes-exhausts, and reading it as one would be a scope error. What it is: a landed derivation exhibiting peer-standpoint escape, which the recorded collapse argument does not address.

**Live, well-scoped work this suggests.** `#disc-value-functional-grounding-floor` has a **Tier-1 adversarial spike queued** in `spikes/PROPOSED.md` against its two-routes-exhausts claim, with the auditor's own admission that they *"did not push hard."* All three recorded attempts are faculty-supply-shaped. ## 4a. Finding — VERIFIED, and it convicts §4's segment — the parent derives gain-collapse, not signal-absence

Surfaced by the unprimed read (§7.1) and verified here at the primary. `#der-observability-dominance`'s `## Formal Expression` derives exactly this: $\text{obs}(P) = \min_{v \in P} \sigma_v$; and when $\sigma_v \approx 0$ for any $v$ on the path, then by `#emp-update-gain` $\eta_{\text{edge}} = U_{\text{edge}}/(U_{\text{edge}} + U_{\text{obs}}) \to 0$ as $U_{\text{obs}} \to \infty$, so the edges are *"frozen at their prior."*

That is **update-gain collapse**. It is *not* the claim that the agent has no signal. The segment's prose — *"the agent cannot learn and cannot recognize that it cannot learn"* — adds the second, stronger conjunct, and that conjunct is **asserted, not derived**.

**This convicts `#hyp-solicitable-escape` as landed this morning.** Its targeting argument reads *"the absorbing condition is precisely that the agent has no signal distinguishing a frozen belief from a settled one."* Gain collapse does not supply that. Worse, the derived mechanism runs the other way: $\eta \to 0$ *because* $U_{\text{obs}} \to \infty$, so an agent with a calibrated $U_{\text{obs}}$ **knows exactly which node is poorly observed** — which makes the region targetable, self-instrumentation aimable, and the segment's central claim false in that case. I inherited a prose claim as though it were the derivation, in a segment I had just read at the primary specifically to check someone else's overreach.

**The honest scope condition** (the soften): the targeting premise holds only where the agent's own $\sigma_v$ / $U_{\text{obs}}$ estimate is unavailable or miscalibrated. Where $U_{\text{obs}}$ is known-large, the blind spot is addressable and the segment does not apply.

**The strengthening to attempt first, per strengthen-before-soften.** Rather than scoping, try to derive that a calibrated $U_{\text{obs}}$ is *itself* unavailable inside the region: calibrating $\sigma_v$ requires ground truth on whether $v$ was achieved, which is exactly what $\sigma_v \approx 0$ withholds — so a self-locking region should be provably one where the agent cannot know its own observation quality. If that lands it supplies the missing **element 2** (a channel cannot certify its own informativeness without ground truth it does not carry), converts the targeting premise from asserted to derived, and discharges the promotion step §4 names. If it fails, the scope condition above is the fallback and the segment narrows.

**A second, independent problem with the same segment**, also from the unprimed read: my argument treats the parent's three-escape list as a **closed enumeration**, and the parent did not write it as one (it reads as illustrative prose, not an exhaustiveness claim). A partition argument over a set nobody proved complete is not a partition argument. Either the closure gets established or the claim weakens to "of the three routes named."
