## 1. The RGM reading (Friston-Heins-Verbelen-Da Costa et al. 2025, read first-hand 2026-06-10)

Read: pp. 3–12 (generative models, active inference/learning/selection, "Renormalizing generative models", image-compression worked example) + the discussion's scale-invariance passage (p. ~34) + targeted text search for stability/convergence/scale-invariance claims across the full text.

**What the paper actually provides:**

- **The RG step is a blocking transformation over state space and time.** Groups of states at level $n$ are generated by a single state at level $n{+}1$ (spatial blocking via spin-block tessellation + SVD + quantization); temporally, "successively higher levels encode sequences of sequences," with a higher-level state generating a *fixed number* of lower-level transitions. Quote (p. 7): *"These scale transformations entail a coarse graining that generally induces a separation of temporal scales, such that the dynamics—here, belief updating—slow down as one ascends levels or scales."*
- **Timescale separation is therefore structural, not parametric**: one slow tick per block of $K$ fast ticks, by construction. This is the discrete-time analog of an enforced $\epsilon_k/\epsilon_{k+1} = 1/K \ll 1$ — and is exactly the architecture-enforced separation AAT's `#def-auxilia-hierarchy` (H5) macro-clock and `#form-composition-closure` $K_c \gg 1$ already carry.
- **Convergence guarantees are variational, per level**: message passing "inherits the same convergence proofs of coordinate descent" (p. 6). I.e., each level has a stable attractor given its context — the per-level Tikhonov hypothesis in variational clothing.
- **The scale-invariance claims are architectural/philosophical, not stability theorems.** The discussion grounds scale-invariance in Haken's slaving principle "under the rubric of the renormalization group": *"if the world behaves according to Haken, then any generative model appropriate for that world will feature the same scale invariance."* There are no multi-timescale composite-stability proofs in the paper; the paper explicitly positions itself as a pragmatic application ("This is not a machine learning paper… we refrain from benchmarking").

**Fork resolution (the spike scope's anticipated decision):** the state-space difference (RGM categorical/Dirichlet vs. AAT's continuous mismatch coordinates) is *not* what blocks adoption-as-machinery — what blocks it is that RGM contains no stability theorem to adopt. The formal machinery must come from singular-perturbation theory (Tikhonov 1952; Khalil 2002 Ch. 11; composite-Lyapunov treatment per Saberi-Khalil 1984), with Chen-Goldenfeld-Oono 1996 as the bridge showing RG and singular-perturbation asymptotics are one method. RGM lands in the segment as (a) the constructive discrete-time instance of structurally-enforced separation, (b) the Haken slaving-principle lineage, (c) a Working-Notes pointer for the open "which structural-adaptation mechanisms satisfy the premises" question. This is the prior-art-integration discipline working as intended: adopt the concept, cite the source, don't pretend it proves what it doesn't.

## 2. The derivation — two-timescale stacked persistence (Model D)

### Setup

Fast state $x_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_1}$, slow state $x_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{n_2}$:

$$\dot x_1 = -F_1(x_1; x_2) + w_1(t), \qquad \dot x_2 = \epsilon\left[-F_2(x_2; x_1) + w_2(t)\right]$$

with $\epsilon \gt 0$ the timescale ratio (in AAT vocabulary $\epsilon = \nu_2/\nu_1$, the adjacent-level event-rate ratio of `#der-temporal-nesting`).

### Premises

- **(S1) Lipschitz quasi-steady-state manifold.** There is $h : \mathcal{X}_2 \to \mathbb{R}^{n_1}$ with $F_1(h(x_2);x_2) = 0$ for all $x_2$ in the slow scope region, and $\lVert Dh(x_2)\rVert \le L_h$.
- **(S2) Per-level sector conditions** (the template's (T2), per level, the fast one *uniformly parametrized* by the slow state):
  - Fast: $(x_1 - h(x_2))^\top F_1(x_1;x_2) \ge \alpha_1 \lVert x_1 - h(x_2)\rVert^2$ for $\lVert x_1 - h(x_2)\rVert \le R_1$, uniformly over the slow scope region.
  - Slow (reduced dynamics): with $\bar F_2(x_2) := F_2(x_2; h(x_2))$, there is $x_2^\ast$ with $\bar F_2(x_2^\ast) = 0$ and $(x_2 - x_2^\ast)^\top \bar F_2(x_2) \ge \alpha_2 \lVert x_2 - x_2^\ast\rVert^2$ for $\lVert x_2 - x_2^\ast\rVert \le R_2$.
- **(S3) Bounded interconnection.** $\lVert F_2(x_2;x_1) - F_2(x_2;h(x_2))\rVert \le L_{21}\lVert x_1 - h(x_2)\rVert$ on the joint scope region; and $M_2 := \sup_{\lVert x_2 - x_2^\ast\rVert \le R_2} \lVert\bar F_2(x_2)\rVert \lt \infty$ (if $\bar F_2$ is $L_2$-Lipschitz, $M_2 \le L_2 R_2$).
- **(S4) Bounded disturbances (Model D).** $\lVert w_1(t)\rVert \le \rho_1$, $\lVert w_2(t)\rVert \le \rho_2$.

Error coordinates: $y_1 := x_1 - h(x_2)$, $y_2 := x_2 - x_2^\ast$. Scope region $\mathcal{B} := \{\lVert y_1\rVert \le R_1\} \times \{\lVert y_2\rVert \le R_2\}$.

### Step 1 — slow velocity bound (a-priori, on $\mathcal{B}$)

On $\mathcal{B}$: $\lVert F_2(x_2;x_1)\rVert \le \lVert \bar F_2(x_2)\rVert + L_{21}\lVert y_1\rVert \le M_2 + L_{21}R_1$, so

$$\lVert \dot x_2 \rVert \le \epsilon\, V_2^{\max}, \qquad V_2^{\max} := M_2 + L_{21}R_1 + \rho_2.$$

This uses only scope constants — no circular dependence on the ultimate bounds derived below.

### Step 2 — the fast level instantiates the template with manifold-drift disturbance

$$\dot y_1 = \dot x_1 - Dh(x_2)\,\dot x_2 = -F_1(y_1 + h(x_2); x_2) + \underbrace{w_1 - Dh(x_2)\,\dot x_2}_{w_1^{\mathrm{eff}}}.$$

On $\mathcal{B}$, by (S1) and Step 1: $\lVert w_1^{\mathrm{eff}}\rVert \le \rho_1^{\mathrm{eff}} := \rho_1 + \epsilon\, L_h V_2^{\max}$. With $V_1 = \tfrac12\lVert y_1\rVert^2$ and (S2-fast):

$$\dot V_1 \le -\alpha_1 \lVert y_1\rVert^2 + \lVert y_1\rVert\, \rho_1^{\mathrm{eff}}.$$

The fast level is a (T1)–(T3) instance of `#result-sector-persistence-template` with $(\xi, F, \rho_\xi, R) = (y_1, F_1(\cdot+h;x_2), \rho_1^{\mathrm{eff}}, R_1)$: the slow level's motion enters the fast level as *additional Model-D disturbance at rate $\epsilon L_h V_2^{\max}$* — the moving-target term. Structural persistence of the fast level requires $\alpha_1 R_1 \gt \rho_1^{\mathrm{eff}}$, i.e.

$$\epsilon \lt \epsilon^\ast := \frac{\alpha_1 R_1 - \rho_1}{L_h\, V_2^{\max}} = \frac{\Delta\rho_1^\ast}{L_h\,(M_2 + L_{21}R_1 + \rho_2)}. \tag{C1}$$

The numerator is the fast level's adaptive reserve $\Delta\rho_1^\ast$ (already a named template quantity). **The qualitative convergence constraint $\nu_2 \ll \nu_1$ is now a closed-form threshold: the admissible timescale ratio is the fast level's spare reserve divided by the rate at which the slow level drags the fast target.**

### Step 3 — the slow level instantiates the template with renormalized disturbance

$$\dot y_2 = \epsilon\left[-\bar F_2(x_2) - \big(F_2(x_2;x_1) - \bar F_2(x_2)\big) + w_2\right].$$

By (S3), the bracket's middle term is bounded by $L_{21}\lVert y_1\rVert$. With $V_2 = \tfrac12\lVert y_2\rVert^2$ and (S2-slow):

$$\dot V_2 \le \epsilon\left[-\alpha_2\lVert y_2\rVert^2 + \lVert y_2\rVert\big(\rho_2 + L_{21}\lVert y_1\rVert\big)\right].$$

In slow time $\tau = \epsilon t$ the $\epsilon$ factors out: the slow level is a (T1)–(T3) template instance with effective disturbance $\rho_2 + L_{21}\lVert y_1\rVert$ — *the fast level, integrated out, leaves a residue equal to its ultimate bound times the interconnection sensitivity.*

### Step 4 — joint forward-invariance (cold-start) and ultimate bounds

**Invariance.** Suppose (C1) holds and

$$\alpha_2 R_2 \gt \rho_2 + L_{21} R_1. \tag{C2-cold}$$

On the boundary shell $\lVert y_1\rVert = R_1$ (with $\lVert y_2 \rVert \le R_2$): $\dot V_1 \le R_1(-\alpha_1 R_1 + \rho_1^{\mathrm{eff}}) \lt 0$ by (C1). On the shell $\lVert y_2\rVert = R_2$ (with $\lVert y_1\rVert \le R_1$): $\dot V_2 \le \epsilon R_2(-\alpha_2 R_2 + \rho_2 + L_{21}R_1) \lt 0$ by (C2-cold). Each $V_i$ is strictly decreasing on its own face of $\partial\mathcal{B}$, so $\mathcal{B}$ is forward-invariant: trajectories starting in $\mathcal{B}$ remain in $\mathcal{B}$ for all $t \ge 0$, and all bounds above remain valid for all time.

**Ultimate bounds (cascade limsup argument).** Within $\mathcal{B}$, the fast Lyapunov inequality gives, by the template's Grönwall argument, $\limsup_{t\to\infty} \lVert y_1(t)\rVert \le r_1 := \rho_1^{\mathrm{eff}}/\alpha_1$. Fix any $\delta \gt 0$; after finite time $\lVert y_1\rVert \le r_1 + \delta$, so the slow effective disturbance is eventually bounded by $\rho_2 + L_{21}(r_1+\delta)$, giving $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lVert y_2(t)\rVert \le (\rho_2 + L_{21}(r_1+\delta))/\alpha_2$ for every $\delta \gt 0$, hence

$$\limsup_{t\to\infty}\lVert y_1(t)\rVert \le r_1 = \frac{\rho_1 + \epsilon L_h V_2^{\max}}{\alpha_1}, \qquad \limsup_{t\to\infty}\lVert y_2(t)\rVert \le r_2 := \frac{\rho_2 + L_{21}\, r_1}{\alpha_2}.$$

**Warm start.** If the fast level is already settled at composition time ($\lVert y_1(0)\rVert \le r_1 + \delta$), then the smaller ball $\{\lVert y_1\rVert \le r_1+\delta\}$ is itself forward-invariant (same shell argument with (C1) strict), and (C2-cold) weakens to

$$\alpha_2 R_2 \gt \rho_2 + L_{21}(r_1 + \delta). \tag{C2-warm}$$

**The warm/cold gap $L_{21}(R_1 - r_1)$ is the quantitative price of acting on transients**: a slow level that engages before the fast level has converged must carry extra reserve proportional to the fast level's *worst-case* transient excursion rather than its *settled* residue. This is the sketch's central qualitative claim ("a slower process must not act before the faster process beneath it has converged") made exact — and it is graded, not binary: early action does not void the guarantee, it raises the reserve requirement.

### Step 5 — the Tikhonov prerequisite, supplied rather than caveated

The 2026-05-31 gold-lift off-ramp finding (Gemini, AUDIT-WORKING-193847) observed that Tikhonov's theorem requires the frozen fast system to have a *unique isolated* root $x_1^\ast(x_2)$, which non-convex fast dynamics can violate. Under (S2-fast) the prerequisite holds *by construction within scope*: if $F_1(y + h(x_2); x_2) = 0$ with $0 \lt \lVert y\rVert \le R_1$, then $0 = y^\top F_1 \ge \alpha_1 \lVert y\rVert^2 \gt 0$ — contradiction. So $h(x_2)$ is the unique equilibrium of the frozen fast dynamics in the radius-$R_1$ ball, and (S2-fast) additionally gives exponential attraction toward it (strictly more than Tikhonov asks). Outside the sector ball the guarantee is void — multiple fast equilibria or limit cycles (non-convex losses per `#deriv-gain-sector`; cyclic multi-agent dynamics per `#deriv-strategic-composition` R2-regime) sit outside (S2-fast)'s scope, and the theorem honestly does not cover them. The finding lands as a *named scope boundary of a strengthened theorem*, not as a caveat on a sketch.

### Step 6 — $N$-level form (nearest-neighbor coupling), by induction

Levels $k = 1, \dots, N$ (fastest to slowest), rates $\epsilon_1 = 1 \gg \epsilon_2 \gg \cdots \gg \epsilon_N$, each level's QSS manifold $h_k(x_{k+1})$ a function of the level immediately above (the nesting idealization; the general case adds a bounded sum over all slower levels, dominated by the adjacent term given the rate ordering). Premises (S1)–(S4) per level. Each level $k$ then sees three disturbance sources: its own $\rho_k$; the residue of the level below, $L_{k,k-1}\lVert y_{k-1}\rVert$; and the manifold drift from the level above, $(\epsilon_{k+1}/\epsilon_k) L_{h_k} V_{k+1}^{\max}$ (boundary terms zero for $k=1$ and $k=N$). The per-level cold-start persistence conditions

$$\alpha_k R_k \gt \rho_k + L_{k,k-1} R_{k-1} + \frac{\epsilon_{k+1}}{\epsilon_k}\, L_{h_k} V_{k+1}^{\max} \qquad (k = 1, \dots, N) \tag{C-N}$$

jointly give forward-invariance of $\prod_k \{\lVert y_k\rVert \le R_k\}$ by the same per-face shell argument, and the cascade limsup argument propagates upward level by level, giving the renormalization recursion on ultimate bounds:

$$r_k = \frac{\rho_k + L_{k,k-1}\, r_{k-1} + (\epsilon_{k+1}/\epsilon_k) L_{h_k} V_{k+1}^{\max}}{\alpha_k}, \qquad r_0 := 0.$$

The from-above terms use a-priori scope constants and the from-below terms use already-derived $r_{k-1}$, so the recursion is well-founded in both directions (no circularity). For *general* (non-nearest-neighbor) coupling the per-face argument is replaced by the standard vector-Lyapunov / M-matrix condition for interconnected systems (Khalil 2002 §9.5; Michel-Miller 1977) — imported machinery, named as the general gate without re-derivation.

### Step 7 — the renormalization reading (the RG flow on template parameters)

Step 3's structure is a renormalization step in the Chen-Goldenfeld-Oono sense (CGO 1996 showed RG and singular-perturbation asymptotics are one method): *integrating out the fast level maps the slow level onto the same template form with renormalized disturbance* $\rho_{k+1} \mapsto \rho_{k+1} + L_{k+1,k}\, r_k$. The sector-persistence template is form-invariant under this coarse-graining — each level of the hierarchy presents the identical $(\alpha, R, \rho^{\mathrm{eff}})$ persistence problem with parameters renormalized by the levels below and above. This is the precise sense in which the persistence architecture is scale-free, and it is the point of contact with RGM: Friston et al.'s blocking construction enforces the structural preconditions of exactly this recursion in the discrete categorical setting (one slow tick per $K$ fast ticks ⇒ $\epsilon_k/\epsilon_{k+1} = 1/K$ enforced architecturally; per-level coordinate-descent convergence ⇒ the per-level stable attractor). What RGM asserts architecturally, (C1)/(C-N) state as a quantitative threshold.

## 3. Model S (assessed, not fully derived — honest tier: conditional)

With Wiener disturbance at the slow level, $y_1 = x_1 - h(x_2)$ picks up an Itô correction $-\tfrac{\epsilon^2}{2}\operatorname{tr}\big(\sigma_2^\top (\nabla^2 h)\, \sigma_2\big)$ (componentwise in $h$) requiring (S1′) $h \in C^2$ with bounded Hessian; the manifold-drift term becomes a state-dependent diffusion contribution of order $\epsilon\, L_h \sigma_2$. The stacked result should then transfer via the Itô-Lyapunov argument of `#deriv-sector-condition` Prop A.1S with the usual $1/\sqrt{\alpha}$ scaling and the region-aware (stopped) form, with the additional curvature term entering $\rho^{\mathrm{eff}}$; but the stopped-process bookkeeping across *two* coupled levels (the invariance argument of Step 4 becomes a joint exit-time problem) is genuinely additional work, not a transcription. Tiered *conditional / not yet derived* in the segment; recorded as a follow-up item. The Model D / Model S asymmetry is honest: drift-type slow-level motion is exactly what Model D covers, and the dominant AAT use cases for the stacking (structural adaptation, consolidation schedules, macro-clocks) are drift-like.

- **F1 (template-instantiation precision).** The template as stated has autonomous $F$ and constant disturbance bound; the stacked instances are non-autonomous (fast $F$ time-varying through $x_2(t)$) with eventually-bounded effective disturbance. - **F4 (claim-type honesty).** The promotion *conditionalizes* the sketch's open problem rather than closing it: the theorem covers any deeper-level dynamics satisfying the premises; whether AAT's actual structural-adaptation mechanisms (discrete/jump updates) satisfy them stays open — stated in the segment's Epistemic Status, not buried in Working Notes.
