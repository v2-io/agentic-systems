## 1. Mandate and candidate statement

**Candidate (as seeded in `03-DYNAMIC-REGIME-AXIS.md` §H.5).** From sub-agent marginal data alone — per-sub-agent trajectories / mismatch observations / update rules, with no observation of the composite-level coupling topology — an external observer cannot in general identify the composite's *dynamic regime* $\mathcal R(X^c) \in \{\text{R0 contraction}, \text{R1 equilibrium}, \text{R2 cyclic-distributional}\}$. The "structural escape" is composite-level coupling-topology observation (interventional, or direct read of the game-structure object).

**Working sharper form.** The candidate, written so the no-go-or-not is decidable: there exists a *specific bit pair* — call it the **regime-bit pair** $(\sigma, p)$, with $\sigma$ the coupling-sign-bit (cooperative vs adversarial) and $p$ the potential-existence bit (potential/monotone vs none) — whose value flips $\mathcal R$ but which has zero on-distribution Fisher information in the joint per-sub-agent marginal observation law. By the Liberzon 2003 / Dayawansa-Martin 1999 common-Lyapunov-nonexistence machinery (the external theorem Instance 3 already invokes), there exist pairs of coupled composites with **identical sub-agent marginal observation distributions** that realize **distinct regime values**, so no statistic of those marginals, no Bayesian comparison over them, no online estimator from them can succeed.

**M1 five-element test (the standard of `#disc-identifiability-floor` § The Pattern, sharpened by the 2026-05-20 spike's §2):** (E1) setting → (E2) external theorem independent of AAT → (E3) categorical no-go → (E4) ≥2 structurally distinct boundary escapes mapped to AAT machinery → (E5) strengthened consequence elevating named machinery to "unique broadly-available escape." Plus the mechanism taxonomy from `#disc-stability-certificate` §"The three obstructions are distinct" (Sylvester / Mori-Zwanzig / Helmholtz): a clean instance fits one family or proposes a fourth derivably.

## 3. The derivation

### 3.1 Setting (E1)

A composite agent $(X^c, \pi^c)$ satisfying `#scope-composite-agent` via some route C-i through C-iv, with $N \geq 2$ sub-agents indexed $i \in \{1, \ldots, N\}$. The composite's *dynamic regime* $\mathcal R(X^c) \in \{\text{R0}, \text{R1}, \text{R2}\}$ is structurally determined by the joint best-response field $F$ and the sub-agent objectives $\{O^{(i)}\}$ per `03-DYNAMIC-REGIME-AXIS.md` §B (R0: unique attracting fixed point with global Lyapunov; R1: fixed-point set with local Lyapunov-on-deviation under potential or monotone structure; R2: no pure-strategy fixed point or saddle-only with empirical-distribution CCE convergence at $O(1/\sqrt T)$).

An *external observer* $B$ accesses only **per-sub-agent marginal data**: for each $i$, the marginal trajectory $\{X_t^{(i)}\}$ (or stationary marginal distribution $\mu_\infty^{(i)}$), the local mismatch process $\{\delta_t^{(i)}\}$, and the per-sub-agent update rule $f_M^{(i)}$ as a structural object (white-box on each sub-agent, but observed in isolation — no observation of how sub-agent $i$'s output enters sub-agent $j$'s input under the composite's routing $R$). The observer does *not* see: the composite-level coupling topology (the sign and structure of cross-agent influence), the joint trajectory $\{X_t^c\}$ (only the projection onto per-sub-agent marginals), the game-structure object $\{O^{(j)}\}_{j \neq i}$ from sub-agent $i$'s frame, or composite-level convergence-rate-class diagnostics.

The inferential question: from the per-sub-agent marginal data, can $B$ identify $\mathcal R(X^c)$?

### 3.2 External theorem (E2)

**Primary anchor (inherited from Instance 3).** Liberzon, D. (2003), *Switching in Systems and Control*, Birkhäuser, Theorem 2.1: there exist pairs of stable LTI systems $(A_1, A_2)$ such that no common quadratic Lyapunov function exists certifying joint stability of every switching sequence. Equivalently, the coupling-sign-bit between two systems is unidentifiable from each system's *own* trajectory in isolation; only the *joint* trajectory or direct observation of the coupling topology reveals it. The explicit $2 \times 2$ counterexample is Dayawansa, W. P. & Martin, C. F. (1999), "A converse Lyapunov theorem for a class of dynamical systems which undergo switching," *IEEE Trans. Automat. Control* 44:751–760. The systematic review is Shorten, R., Wirth, F., Mason, O., Wulff, K. & King, C. (2007), "Stability criteria for switched and hybrid systems," *SIAM Review* 49(4):545–592 (§3 specifically on common-quadratic-Lyapunov-function (CQLF) existence as the unidentifiable bit). All three are *formal antecedents* held locally as `ref/` mentions in the Instance-3 derivation in `#deriv-critical-mass-composition` Related Work + `#disc-identifiability-floor` Instance 3 Boundary Characterization.

**Why the same anchor extends.** Instance 3's no-go construction is a switched-system counter-example where two coupling-sign realizations produce identical *per-system* (= per-sub-agent) marginal observation distributions. The extension to regime identification uses the same mechanism with the topology coordinate widened from the *sign bit* alone to the *topology-class bit*: cooperative-shared-objective coupling produces R0; antisymmetric-best-response coupling at a Nash basin produces R1 (with locally identical second-moment marginals at appropriate parameter choices); cycle-inducing best-response coupling produces R2. The per-sub-agent marginals are projections of the joint state under the sub-agent's local update; the topology-class bit lives on the *coupling* coordinate which is orthogonal to that projection, exactly as Instance 3's sign bit does.

No new external theorem is required. The Liberzon / Dayawansa-Martin / Shorten machinery is precisely the anchor for "topology unidentifiable from marginals"; whether the unidentified bit is *sign-of-coupling* (Instance 3) or *topology-class-determining-regime* (this candidate) is a question of *which subset of the topology coordinate is varied in the counter-construction*, not of which external theorem is invoked.

### 3.3 The no-go (E3)

**Claim.** For matched-symmetric-Tier-1-scalar sub-agents under standard coupling models (Model D for disturbance, (C1) for coupling, (C2) for coordination cost — the same models `#deriv-critical-mass-composition` uses, where the Instance-3 no-go is established), there exist pairs of composites $(\Sigma_A, \Sigma_B)$ with **identical sub-agent marginal stationary distributions** $\mu_{\infty, A}^{(i)} = \mu_{\infty, B}^{(i)}$ for every $i$ but distinct regime values $\mathcal R(\Sigma_A) \neq \mathcal R(\Sigma_B)$.

**Construction (cross-regime witness).** Take $N=2$ scalar sub-agents with state $q_i \in \mathbb R$, observation noise $w_i \sim \mathcal N(0, \sigma^2)$, base correction rate $\alpha \gt 0$, base disturbance bound $\rho$, tempo $\mathcal T$.

*Composite A (R0 — shared-objective contraction).* Sub-agent objective $O_A^{(i)} = -\tfrac{1}{2}(q_1 + q_2 - T)^2$ for shared target $T$. Per-agent best-response under gradient flow: $\dot q_i = -\alpha(q_i + q_j - T) + w_i$. Stationary marginal: $q_i \to T/2$ with marginal variance $\sigma^2/(2\alpha)$ from the linearized dynamics. Composite is R0: unique attracting fixed point at $(T/2, T/2)$, joint Lyapunov $V = \tfrac{1}{2}\lVert q - (T/2, T/2)\rVert^2$, exponential convergence at rate $\alpha$.

*Composite B (R1 — strategic potential-game equilibrium).* Sub-agent objective $O_B^{(i)} = q_i(a_0 - b(q_1 + q_2) - c)$ — quadratic-profit Cournot in canonical form. The potential is $\Phi_B = a_0(q_1+q_2) - b(q_1+q_2)^2 + (b - b/2)(q_1^2 + q_2^2) - c(q_1+q_2)$ (Monderer-Shapley 1996; Cournot is a potential game). Best-response gradient flow: $\dot q_i = \nabla_i \Phi_B + w_i = a_0 - 2b q_j - 2b q_i \cdot (\text{adjustment}) - c + w_i$. With $a_0, b, c$ tuned, the unique Nash $q_{B, \text{Nash}}^\ast$ is locally attracting and the marginal stationary distribution is $\mathcal N(q_{B, \text{Nash}}^\ast, \sigma^2 / (2\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^2 \Phi_B)))$.

*Parameter-matching.* Choose $T_A = 2 q_{B, \text{Nash}}^\ast$ so the marginal means coincide: $T_A/2 = q_{B, \text{Nash}}^\ast$. Choose $b$ in Composite B such that $2\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^2 \Phi_B) = 2\alpha$ (the marginal second moments match). Both composites then produce identical per-sub-agent stationary marginals: $\mu_{\infty, A}^{(i)} = \mu_{\infty, B}^{(i)} = \mathcal N(q^\ast, \sigma^2/(2\alpha))$. The marginal *transient* second moments also match under the matched local-curvature condition (both are exponentially-converging Ornstein-Uhlenbeck-type processes per sub-agent in the linearization).

*The witness.* Composite A is R0 (unique global attractor + global Lyapunov). Composite B is R1 (unique-Nash equilibrium with local Lyapunov-on-deviation, but Nash structure rather than shared-target structure). The observer with only marginal data sees identical $\mu^{(i)}$ in both; cannot distinguish $\mathcal R = \text{R0}$ from $\mathcal R = \text{R1}$.

*Sharpening to R0-vs-R2.* For an R0-vs-R2 witness, take Composite C as the matching-pennies $2 \times 2$ payoff (no pure-strategy Nash; mixed-Nash at $(1/2, 1/2)$; no-regret play cycles in distribution) with continuous-action smoothing so the marginal stationary distribution under no-regret dynamics is a fixed continuous distribution with mean $1/2$ and bounded variance. Match Composite A's $T_A$ and $\alpha$ to align mean and variance of the per-sub-agent stationary marginal. The two then have identical per-sub-agent marginals but $\mathcal R(\Sigma_A) = \text{R0}$ while $\mathcal R(\Sigma_C) = \text{R2}$. (This sharpening is genuinely cleaner than the R0-vs-R1 witness because the *joint* dynamic regimes differ qualitatively — convergent vs cyclic-in-joint-distribution — while the per-sub-agent marginals can be made stationary in both cases by appropriate observation-aggregation, since "cyclic in the joint distribution" projects to "stationary in the marginal distribution" when the cycle is over the joint coordinate that the marginal averages out.)

*Mechanism (Sylvester at one remove, same as Instances 1/2/4).* The composite-level topology coordinate (the bit $(\sigma, p)$ distinguishing R0/R1/R2) enters the per-sub-agent marginal observation law $p(q_i \mid \theta_{\text{topology}})$ only through the *fixed-point* and *local-curvature* parameters that the construction matches across composites. Computing the Fisher information of the marginal observation law with respect to $\theta_{\text{topology}}$ gives a rank-deficient matrix: the topology coordinate has a null direction along the manifold $\{\theta : (\text{mean}, \text{variance})(\theta) = (q^\ast, \sigma^2/(2\alpha))\}$. By Sylvester's law of inertia (`#disc-identifiability-floor` Sylvester-recognition Finding), no reparameterization of the marginal-observation coordinate system removes this null direction; the irreducibility is invariant under the observer's representational freedom. The escape requires *rank-augmentation* — observation of a coordinate the marginal projection has annihilated — exactly the same mechanism as Instances 1, 2, and 4. **Same rank-collapse subclass as Instance 3, with the rank-collapse occurring on a topology coordinate of higher dimension than Instance 3's single sign-bit.**

### 3.4 Boundary characterization (E4)

**The four Instance-3 escapes inherit verbatim.** The four escapes named in `#disc-identifiability-floor` Instance 3 — (a) observable coupling topology via composite-extended `#der-loop-interventional-access`; (b) matched Tier at the composite level admitting a joint quadratic Lyapunov; (c) passivity / storage-function certificate on the coupling channel; (d) common contraction metric via Lohmiller-Slotine — apply *identically* here, because the no-go construction is the same Liberzon-2003 machinery with a broader unidentified bit. Each escape mechanism that breaks the sign-bit no-go (Instance 3) also breaks the broader topology-class no-go (this candidate): observable coupling topology reveals not just the sign but the full game-structure object; matched Tier admits a joint Lyapunov that distinguishes potential-game R1 from cooperative R0 (the *form* of the Lyapunov differs — squared-distance from target vs potential-from-Nash — and the observability of *which form* the certificate takes is the disambiguating bit); the passivity certificate is constructed differently depending on whether the coupling is cooperative or strategic; the contraction-metric construction in `#result-contraction-template` is sensitive to game-structure via the (CC-feedback) preconditions.

**One new escape — (e) composite-level convergence-rate-class observation.** From `03-DYNAMIC-REGIME-AXIS.md` §H.4: R0 and R1 give exponential convergence; R2 gives polynomial $O(1/\sqrt T)$. A composite-level observation of the *rate class* of joint convergence (exponential vs polynomial) is a regime invariant that distinguishes R2 from {R0, R1}. This escape does *not* require interventional access — it requires only observation of the *joint* trajectory at sufficient duration, which is composite-level (not per-sub-agent-marginal). It is mechanism-genuinely-different from escapes (a)-(d): it does not need to observe the *topology* directly, only the *consequence* of the topology at the rate-class level. By the same logic that elevates Instance 3's escapes from "useful" to "structurally required," escape (e) becomes the unique broadly-available *passive* escape (no intervention needed) for the R2-vs-{R0, R1} sub-distinction.

The R0-vs-R1 sub-distinction (cooperative shared-target contraction vs strategic-equilibrium contraction) is *not* solved by escape (e) — both give exponential convergence at potentially-matched rates — and requires one of (a)–(d). This is honest scope-marking: escape (e) is *partial* for the regime axis, addressing the convergence-rate-class bit but not the game-structure bit.

**The escape menu therefore has five entries, four inherited and one new.** This satisfies E4 (≥2 structurally distinct escapes) with substantial overshoot — and the inheritance of the four Instance-3 escapes is itself the structural signature that this candidate *is* a scope-extension of Instance 3 rather than an independent instance.

**Tier.** *Exact* for the symmetric-matched-Tier-1-scalar cross-regime witness construction in §3.3 (the R0-vs-R1 and R0-vs-R2 marginal-matching is explicit, parameter-by-parameter, the same way Instance 3's symmetric-matched-Tier-1-scalar coupling-sign-bit construction is exact). *Robust qualitative* for general heterogeneous composites, inheriting Liberzon / Dayawansa-Martin / Shorten common-Lyapunov-nonexistence without a closed-form AAT-level counterexample (the same tier statement as Instance 3).

## 4. Why this is *not* an independent Instance 6 — the integrity test

This is the strengthen-first second pass, applied to the §3 derivation. Even though §3 closes — there *is* a derivable no-go on regime identification from sub-agent marginals — the question for *landing* is whether it lands as a distinct sixth instance or as a scope-extension of the third.

The 2026-05-20 spike's §2 made the "is this a clean new instance" test rigorous. Applying it here:

**Test 1 — Independent external theorem (E2 distinctness).** Fails. The external theorem is the same Liberzon-2003 / Dayawansa-Martin / Shorten common-Lyapunov-nonexistence machinery Instance 3 invokes. No new external theorem; only a broader application of the same one. Instances 1, 2, 4 each invoke *different* external theorems (CHT / Cramér-Rao / Kalman-Ho-dual-with-CHT) — a candidate that re-uses Instance 3's theorem is structurally a *scope extension* of Instance 3, not a sixth instance.

**Test 2 — Independent mechanism family (mechanism-taxonomy distinctness).** Fails. The mechanism is rank-collapse / Sylvester-at-one-remove, exactly the family Instances 1, 2, 4 occupy. (Instance 3 itself is the *composition / projection-closure* family — Schur-complement / memory-kernel — but the *no-go construction* used in Instance 3 is the coupling-sign-bit-via-Liberzon, which is more naturally read as the rank-collapse projection of the topology coordinate onto the marginal-observation manifold. The mechanism-taxonomy assignment of Instance 3 to "composition" reflects its *role* (certifying composition); the *construction* is rank-collapse on the marginal-Fisher. This candidate is the same mechanism with broader topology coordinate.) A candidate that does not propose a fourth mechanism family beyond Sylvester/Mori-Zwanzig/Helmholtz is, in the M1 economy, not adding new mechanism content.

**Test 3 — Independent escape menu (E4 distinctness).** Fails substantially. Four of five escapes inherit verbatim from Instance 3; only escape (e) is new. A candidate where 80% of the escape menu is the *same* escape menu (just-with-broader-payload) is not exhibiting the independent-instance pattern that Instances 1–4 exhibit (each Instance has its *own* escape menu mapped to its *own* AAT machinery).

**Test 4 — Independent AAT-machinery elevation (E5 distinctness).** Partial. Two of three elevations inherit-with-broadening from Instance 3; only the `#deriv-strategic-composition` sub-scope elevation is genuinely new. A candidate with one new elevation and two inherited elevations is a *scope-extension* of the source instance.

**Verdict.** Three of four distinctness tests fail; the fourth is partial. **Integration-is-replacement check (per `~/.claude/memory/epistemic-discipline/integration-is-replacement.md`).** The strengthen-first landing is integration *as replacement*, not coexistence. The seeded candidate from `03-DYNAMIC-REGIME-AXIS.md` §H.5 ("regime-identification-from-marginals is a candidate Identifiability-floor Instance 6") is **deleted** by this spike's verdict; what replaces it is the strengthened Instance 3 (broader unidentified bit, escape menu broadened by one new escape (e), one new AAT-machinery elevation of `#deriv-strategic-composition` sub-scope $\alpha'/\beta'$).
