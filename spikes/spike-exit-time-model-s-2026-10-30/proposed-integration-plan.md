## What to verify first, ordered by damage-if-wrong

1. **Theorem 1's one inequality.** $\mathcal L v \geq \mathcal L_{\mathrm{OU}}\Phi$ rests on two signs: $-\Phi' \geq 0$ (Lemma 1's integrating-factor identity) and $\hat\delta^\top F \geq \alpha r$ (A2'). If either sign is wrong the whole spike inverts. Re-derive $\Phi'(r) = -\tfrac{2}{\sigma_w^2}G(r)/m(r)$ from the radial ODE independently; then re-derive the radial Laplacian $\Delta(\Phi\circ\lVert\cdot\rVert) = \Phi'' + \tfrac{n-1}{r}\Phi'$ and check the sign bookkeeping in §3's displayed line without reading mine.
2. **The Dynkin limit.** The passage $t \to \infty$ uses $v$ bounded on the ball and $v(\delta_{\tau_R}) = 0$. Check that nothing is assumed about $\mathbb{E}\tau_R \lt \infty$ in the lower-bound direction (it should not be; the case $P(\tau_R = \infty) \gt 0$ is dispatched trivially).
3. **Lemma 1 itself** — that the double-integral formula is the OU mean exit time from a ball in $n$ dimensions, including the behavior at $r = 0$ (the $m\psi \to 0$ boundary condition) and the regularity of $v = \Phi\circ\lVert\cdot\rVert$ at the origin. The $n=1$ case is the explorer's checked formula; the $n \geq 2$ case is new to this corpus and is checked only numerically (`checks.py` §(2)).
4. **Lemma 1′ (asymptotic).** The Laplace endpoint evaluation and the $\Gamma(n/2)$ prefactor. A wrong prefactor would not break Theorem 1 (which uses $\Phi$, not its asymptotic) but would break Corollary 3's and 5's displayed asymptotic forms.
5. **Corollary 3's reading of $v$ on $\{\tau_R \gt L\}$.** The bound $v(\delta_L) \leq \Phi(0)$ uses that $\Phi$ is maximized at the origin. Trivial given $\Phi' \leq 0$, but it is the step a reader could misread as using the stationary law.
6. **Theorem 2's polarity fact** for $n \geq 2$ (non-degenerate diffusion with locally bounded drift does not hit a point) and the Tanaka/reflected-OU argument for $n = 1$. These are the only places the proof goes beyond Itô + Dynkin. If a verifier is uncomfortable with either, Theorem 2 can be landed at `conditional` or dropped in favor of Corollary 2.1 (Laplace order), which needs neither; Theorem 1 and Corollaries 3–5 do not depend on Theorem 2.
7. **Corollary 4's numerical claim** ("$\alpha\Phi(0) \lt 1$ at $\kappa = n/2$ for all tested $n$, decreasing in $n$") — a table in `checks-output.txt`, not a derivation. If it is to be stated in canon it should be stated as what it is: a computed property of the explicit benchmark at the threshold, with the reason (the stationary radial law concentrates on the sphere of radius $R^\ast_S$ as $n$ grows) given as explanation, not as proof.
8. **The literature scan** (`02-literature.md`, by a Sonnet agent): whether the comparison theorem is already a lemma somewhere. If it is, canon cites it and derives only the AAT-specific consequences (Corollaries 3–5 and the reading). Do not let a "new result" label survive an unread literature check.

## Suggestions beyond this result (reasoning given; none is a request)

- **The two kinds of "exit time" in the corpus.** The NeurIPS back-integration spike (`spikes/neurips-back-integration-2026-05-08.md` item 4) carries a *Model D below-threshold* worst-case finite exit-time bound. With this spike there are now two exit-time objects with opposite roles: Model D below threshold — escape *guaranteed* within a computable time; Model S above the mean-square threshold — escape *delayed* by an exponentially long expected time. If both land, a two-row table in `#impl-persistence-and-limits` would prevent the confusion.
- **The three-condition picture** (`#impl-persistence-and-limits` WN "Forward"): transient adequacy ($1/\alpha \lt T_{\text{tolerance}}$) is about recovery time; Corollary 5 is about tenure. They are different clauses with a lifetime in them, and if the structural / task / transient triple is ever built, tenure is a fourth, Model-S-only clause. Naming it there would keep the taxonomy honest.
- **Information cost of tenure.** Holding exponent $\kappa$ at fixed $R, \sigma_w$ means $\alpha = \kappa\sigma_w^2/R^2$, and `#deriv-persistence-cost` prices $\alpha$ at $n\alpha/2$ nats per unit time. So expected tenure grows exponentially in a quantity whose information cost grows linearly — tenure is exponentially cheap in information rate. This composes two conditional results and is discussion-grade; it is also a sentence Joseph's "price of contact" thesis would want. Not derived further here.
- **The glyph the front door needs.** `#impl-persistence-and-limits` records that two readers called threshold-plus-dichotomy the chapter's climax. If the hub-and-spoke figure is built, the Model S spoke should carry two labels, not one.
