# Neighborhood map — what canon already holds around the exit-time question

Written first, before any derivation, from a whole read of the segments named below at HEAD `7b429bcc`. Its job is to catch an ill-posed brief before the derivation inherits it, and to name every Working Note the result has to be reconciled with at landing.

## The question, in canon's own objects

Mismatch $\delta(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ under Model S (GA-2S): $d\delta = -F(\mathcal{T},\delta)\,dt + \sigma_w\,dW_t$, $W$ a standard $n$-dimensional Wiener process, with (A1) $F(0)=0$ and (A2') $\delta^\top F \geq \alpha\lVert\delta\rVert^2$ on $\mathcal{B}_R$ (`#form-sector-condition`). $\tau_R = \inf\{t : \lVert\delta(t)\rVert \gt R\}$ (`#deriv-sector-condition` Prop A.1S). Canon establishes $P(\tau_R \lt \infty) = 1$ for every $\alpha$, every $\sigma_w \gt 0$, every $F$ under A2' (Cor A.1S.1; the no-go demonstration in `#deriv-stochastic-non-exit`). Canon does not compute, bound, or mention $\mathbb{E}[\tau_R]$ or the law of $\tau_R$ anywhere (grep: Kramers / Freidlin / Wentzell / first-passage / quasi-potential appear in no `src/` segment; "exit time" only as the definition of $\tau_R$ and as an open item in `#der-multi-timescale-stability`).

The owner review's explorer (`msc/inside-out-2026-10/owner-review/explorers/part-i.md` §8) proposes: $\liminf_{\sigma_w\to0}\sigma_w^2\log\mathbb{E}[\tau_R] \geq \alpha R^2$ via Freidlin–Wentzell plus a comparison argument on the quasi-potential, equality for linear isotropic $F$. Owed by its own account: the FW hypotheses as applied (Lipschitz $F$, which A2' does not supply); the prefactor for general $F$; a literature check; anisotropic noise; discrete time; heavy tails.

## Live tensions the result touches

1. **Whether vs when.** Canon's Model S story is complete on *whether* (certain exit) and silent on *when*. The explorer's point stands: the agent-relevant quantity is the time, and canon's one finite-horizon bound (iv) is weak. The question as posed is well-formed; it does not dissolve.
2. **The mean-square condition's content.** $\alpha \gt n\sigma_w^2/(2R^2)$ is $\kappa \gt n/2$, where $\kappa$ will turn out to be the exit exponent. At that threshold the region is left in about one correction time. The condition certifies the typical scale, as canon already says; the exit-time result lets canon say precisely what tenure it does *not* certify, and what condition would.
3. **The front door.** "No correction strength buys that" is true and the natural reading of it ("correction strength is irrelevant to staying in the region") is false. The repair is an added clause, not a swap: correction strength cannot buy forever; it buys expected tenure exponentially in $\alpha R^2/\sigma_w^2$.
4. **Tightness.** Lemma A.1N makes the Model D threshold agent-level iff exactly under $\bar\alpha = \alpha$. If the exit-time bound is exact under the same condition, one hypothesis governs both results; if it is not, that asymmetry is itself a finding.

## What the target genuinely adds, if it holds

A quantitative object ($\mathbb{E}[\tau_R]$, or better the law of $\tau_R$) on the Model S side of the dichotomy, with $\alpha$, $R$, $\sigma_w$, $n$ as its arguments; a finite-lifetime containment statement that improves exponentially in $\kappa$ (replacing the linear-in-$\sigma_w^2$ (iv) as the operative Model S containment bound); and an agent-level reading of headline 2 that is true at every finite horizon, not only at infinity.

## Scope decisions taken here

- Isotropic additive noise $\sigma_w I$, continuous time, A2' on the closed ball. Anisotropic $\Sigma_w$, discrete time (DA2'), state-dependent noise and heavy tails are out of scope; each is named with what is known in the derivation file.
- "Regularity": canon's A2' is a pointwise inequality and carries no smoothness. The derivation states exactly which regularity each step uses (existence of a weak solution; Itô for $\lVert\delta\rVert^2$; polarity of the origin for the pathwise route in $n\geq2$) and avoids global Lipschitz where it can.
