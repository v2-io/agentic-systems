## Verdict (one paragraph)

Clause 1 is a **no-go as stated** (completion state C). Where the gain law is exact, the content of a lie cannot move the gain. For any learner, deception never moves confidence relative to the world it simulates. What is true, and more specific, is that deception acts through the agent's *model of its sources*: bias prior, apparent precision, apparent independence. Borrowed authority sets a confidence floor; repetition (including verbatim repetition and counterfeit corroboration) drives the agent to it, with the false-confidence ratio growing linearly in repetitions and honest-channel gain falling as $1/n$. Conflicts between sources are attributed by priors alone, however much data arrives, and isolation removes the independent corroboration that would otherwise dominate those priors. The captured state is absorbing under stated conditions. Recovery after discovery is exact only with provenance memory and slow (linear in the lie's volume) under dilution; against a persistent deceiver a trusting agent never recovers. For uncheckable claims from sources whose presentation can be forged, capability cannot substitute for token-level history with a source (a data-processing argument), which yields a formation dilemma resolved only by protected, honest formation. Clause 2 is **strengthened past the claim** (state B): honesty is a structural requirement of any channel that must keep its gain once deceptions are eventually discovered, above all the formative one.
