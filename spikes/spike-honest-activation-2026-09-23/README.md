## Verdict (revision 1, after independent verification)

**Clause 1 is strengthened-to the claim, conditionally (state A); the unconditional "guarantee" is a no-go.** By canon's definition (learning stops *inappropriately*), sustained undetected deception produces gain collapse. Claimed authority sets a confidence floor, repetition drives the agent to it, and the calibration ratio grows linearly in repetitions: the dogmatism mode. Under a liar-component trust model a trusted deceiver gets honest dissenters discounted: the nihilism mode, toward the truth. Conflicts are attributed by priors, and isolation removes the corroboration that would otherwise overturn them. Under consensus-estimated trust, capture is held by sustained isolation (numerical). Exact correction after discovery requires provenance memory. The counterexamples to "guarantee" are forgetting, content-prior plausibility, concurrent independent dissent and robust aggregation. Where the gain law is exact the *content* of a lie does not move the gain; that locates the mechanism in the trust model and does not refute the clause. **Clause 2 is strengthened-to a reformulation (state A):** non-deception is necessary and sufficient for a channel's full capacity (exact), with credibility collapse on discovery conditional on the agent's formation. A young agent's structural vulnerability is few independent channels plus cheap identity, and formation's job is authenticated identity plus discovered deceptions, which makes *honest* formation the requirement. The first version's clause-1 "no-go" verdict and its linear-pool "formation dilemma" were wrong; see `de-novo-feedback-1-response.md`.
