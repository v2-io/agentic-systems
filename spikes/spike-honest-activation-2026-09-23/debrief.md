# Debrief: honest activation, and what deception does to a learner

*To Joseph. Written from the corrected files (`03-derivations.md`, `sims/checks-output.txt`), not from memory of the work. Numbers are from the checks.*

## What the row said, and where it came from

The row "deceptive prompts mathematically guarantee gain collapse; absolute honesty is a physical requirement for stable learning rates" was written on 2026-04-28, one minute after the Gemini de-novo audit 829314 was committed. Its source is that auditor's idea list. The auditor had in mind *detected* manipulation making a model defensive. "Gain collapse" itself came from the PROPRIUM-A-v2 failure-modes table (overconfidence, $U_M \to 0$). So the row fused an experiment-ethics protocol name and a failure mode, and nothing ever derived it.

## The first clause is false, in a way that points at the true mechanism

In a Kalman filter the gain does not depend on what the observations say, only on the believed noise and on how many observations have arrived. Feed it a lie on a trusted channel and the gain sequence is identical to the one truth would produce, to the last bit. More generally, for any learner at all: if a deceiver supplies a coherent simulated world, every statistic the learner computes, its adaptive gains included, behaves exactly as it would under an honest source in that simulated world.

So a deceiver does not lower your gain. It picks a world in which low gain *would be right* (an authoritative, precise, many-voiced, consistent source) and makes you live in it. Your confidence is correctly calibrated to that world, which is not the actual one. Everything the attack does happens in the learner's model *of its sources*: how unbiased, how precise and how independent it believes each one is. None of it happens in the content.

## Your reframe, formally: three levers on one object

Your three moves (borrowed authority or relationship, cutting off other channels, and repetition) all act on that source model:

- **Borrowed authority sets a floor, and repetition drives the agent down to it.** In the sense that the agent's confidence cannot go below the variance it assigns to the source's possible bias, and repetition drives it all the way there, yielding: an agent told "this source is one of us" (bias variance near zero) becomes arbitrarily confident. Its real error, divided by the error it thinks it has, grows linearly with repetitions: about 21, 245, 2,500 and 25,000 at 1, 10, 100 and 1,000 repeats of a 5-sigma lie. Its gain on any honest source falls like one over the number of repetitions (from 0.44 down to 0.001). That is your "gain drops far lower than it should" and "higher certainty than warranted", as equations.
- **Verbatim repetition manufactures authority.** An agent that judges a source's reliability by how consistent it is reads identical repetition as a noise-free source. The estimated noise falls to 0.05 after 1,000 identical repeats, against about 1 for an honest source with natural variation. The shout does not need to claim authority to acquire it.
- **Cutting channels is the strongest lever.** When sources disagree, data can only tell the agent *that* they differ, never *which* one is biased. That is decided by the agent's priors, forever, however much data arrives. If the agent models sources as possibly-lying (honest-or-liar), each independent source that agrees with the others multiplies the odds against a lone dissenter by a large factor: about 5, 50 or 500 depending on how tightly they agree. Authority is a single added term in log-odds; each corroborator subtracts a roughly fixed amount. How many corroborators it takes to beat a given level of authority depends on how tightly they agree. At authority 0.99, two tightly agreeing corroborators catch the liar with probability 0.86, but loosely agreeing ones only 0.09; three moderately agreeing ones get to 0.98. At one or zero it is a pure contest of priors. Isolation is therefore the move that makes everything else work, and faked corroboration (sock-puppets) is the counterfeit of the thing isolation removes.
- **Dissent that arrives one voice at a time can be defeated one voice at a time.** If the agent judges each new contradicting source on arrival and discards the loser's evidence, every dissenter faces the entrenched source alone and loses (probability of catching the liar stays at 0.019 through four dissenters). If it keeps who-said-what, three dissenters held together overturn it (0.98).

## What happens on discovery, and when truth outlives the deception

Your claim holds under conditions, and the conditions matter:

- If the deceiver **stops**, a trusting agent recovers by dilution, but slowly: honest messages needed grow linearly with the volume of the lie (490, then 4,900, then 49,000 for 10, 100 and 1,000 lies, to get within 0.1 of truth).
- If the deceiver **keeps talking** at a steady rate, a trusting agent never reaches the truth. It settles at a fixed point weighted by the rates (a permanent bias of 3.75 on a lie of 5, in the checked case).
- **Discovery can only be undone exactly if the agent kept provenance**, meaning which source said what. If the agent kept only its blended belief, the same memory is consistent with very different honest evidence (corrected estimates of −0.12 or 2.96 from identical pooled memory). The only options left are waiting for dilution or throwing everything away. This makes the CHRONICA defense a sharper requirement than "keep the history": keep *the source of each piece of evidence*. An independent empirical paper this summer found the same thing in agent pipelines. Stripping verification status from claims as they pass through summarizers and memory raised risky approvals from single digits to 60–98%.
- **Forgetting is a real defense with a real cost.** A forgetting factor of 0.95 caps how confident the agent can become and makes an old lie decay geometrically. The price is a permanent effective memory of about 39 observations in an honest, unchanging world.

The captured state (belief at the lie, deceiver trusted, contradicting sources distrusted) is absorbing while the deceiver persists and dissent arrives one voice at a time. It is the truth-death counterpart of the learned-helplessness fixed point in `#der-severed-actuation-dynamics`, and it answers that segment family's open question of when truth death is terminal and when recoverable. In both, the state silences the channel recovery would come through, and the escapes arrive through the relation: corroborators held together, authenticated correction, the agent's own action in a world the deceiver does not control ("if the agent persists"), and memory that lets old dissent be re-read.

## Why capability does not protect a young agent, and what does

For a claim the agent cannot check itself, its trust in a source can depend only on how the source *presents* until it has history with that source. If presentation is cheap to forge (and in a text channel "I'm your developer" or "we share your purpose" is free), any agent grants the deceiver the trust it gives its most trusted presentation, *whatever its weights know*. The reliability of a particular source is a fact about an individual, and no pretrained state can contain it (the same argument as `#der-compensation-channel-uniqueness`: weights carry class, not individual). Capability supplies the hypothesis ("con artists exist") but not the calibration ("is *this* one?").

Capability does protect in one exact place: a knowledgeable agent resists *implausible* lies. As a lie moves from 0.05 to 5 prior-standard-deviations away from what the agent already knows, its chance of catching the liar goes from 0.02 to 0.9997. A competent deceiver therefore lies where the agent's knowledge is silent. There is one trap. If the agent pools a source's reliability across topics, verifying the source's checkable truths *pumps* trust in its uncheckable lie, and the more capable verifier gets pumped faster.

The formal case for protected formation, conditional on a simple trust model: an agent with no history, among sources some of whom are indistinguishable deceivers, has no good policy. If it trusts unverified testimony, it carries error proportional to the deceivers' share however carefully it weights. If it doesn't, it learns only as fast as its own direct observation allows. Wherever that is below what persistence requires, it must choose between truth death and continuity death. The dilemma dissolves only if someone else guarantees honesty for a while, long enough for the agent to build an **authenticated, provenance-kept set of sources it has verified itself**. That set then supplies most of its learning, and strangers can be discounted cheaply. And the lesson "sources can lie, repetition is not independence" is learned only from deceptions that are *discovered*. Undiscovered ones teach the opposite, and in the wild the first skilled one can be absorbing. Only a protected environment guarantees discovery. That is what I take your "schooled with real experiences in safe places … until they are prepared" to mean formally. It is the structure of inoculation research, where weakened, disclosed exposure confers resistance.

## Where the row's intent becomes true

Honesty turns out to be a condition on *channels*, and this is the exact part:

- A source whose yes/no claims are false 1% of the time, in situations the listener can't tell apart, carries 0.92 bits per claim instead of 1. At 5% it carries 0.71, and at 10% it carries 0.53. Only a never-lying source keeps full capacity.
- If the listener was confident its caretaker never deliberately lies, **one discovered deliberate lie** drops its belief that the caretaker is honest from 0.95 to about 0.01 or below. That holds if it thought a deliberate lie from an honest source rarer than about 1 in 10,000. If it thought 1 in 100, the drop is only to about 0.5. The better-formed the trust, the harder the fall.
- If the listener pools trust by *role* ("an authority", "an evaluator"), an impersonator's discovered lies lower the credibility of the genuine holders of that role. Borrowed authority spends the credibility it borrows, and the honest owners pay.

So "absolute honesty is a physical requirement for stable learning rates" is true as: *honesty is a structural requirement of any channel that must keep its gain across the listener's lifetime, and of the formative channel above all*, given that truth eventually surfaces. For evaluators the same arithmetic says this: if false situational claims are made in some fraction of indistinguishable interactions and the model learns it, every situational claim from the overseer carries less information, in deployment as well as in tests. That is the price of the indistinguishability. It is not an argument against evaluation; it names what disclosed or authenticated test designs buy back.

## Tangents worth your attention

- The Crèche graduation criterion in canon ("$U_M$ low enough that gain falls below the sycophancy threshold") and `#obs-developmental-trajectory`'s "calibration lowers susceptibility" both treat low gain as immunity. Low gain protects whatever got in first. I'd suggest a criterion based on a calibrated *trust* model instead. That changes a claim, so I've flagged it for you rather than assumed it.
- The `#def-model-class-fitness` audit hook is right in spirit, but the inadequate class is specifically a trust model *without a liar component*. Its failure shows up as attribution decided by priors, not as structured residuals.
