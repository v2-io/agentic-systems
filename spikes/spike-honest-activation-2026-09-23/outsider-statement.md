# For a reader meeting this cold (e.g. AI-security evaluators) — revision 2

*Five claims, each with its condition. Derivations and numerical checks: `03-derivations.md`, `sims/`. Two independent verification passes have been run and addressed.*

1. **The most effective deceptions work by making an agent believe a source is unbiased, precise and independent.** In the simplest learning models, the content of a lie alone does not change how much the agent listens. In agents that judge sources by their outputs, though, consistency and repetition in the content are themselves the lever: word-for-word repetition reads as a precise source to an agent that has no notion of copying.

2. **Sustained, undetected deception makes learning stop inappropriately, in either of two ways.** Under claimed authority and repetition, an agent's real error relative to its believed error grows in proportion to the number of repetitions, and it weights new honest evidence far below what its real error warrants. Under a trust model that allows sources to lie, a trusted deceiver instead gets honest dissenters judged as liars. Cutting off independent channels is the decisive step: each genuinely independent agreeing source adds evidence against the deceiver. That holds only if the agent's expectation of how closely honest sources agree is realistic; otherwise honest disagreement reads as lying. Repetition-driven attacks on deployed models show the same volume effect empirically (many-shot jailbreaking follows a power law in the number of demonstrations).

3. **Capture is held by controlling who is speaking at once, and recovery needs provenance.** *In simulation, with two truth-discovery-style trust rules (judge sources by agreement with the current consensus); not proven.* A deceiver who is first the only voice turns claimed authority into apparent track record, and afterwards its initial authority no longer matters. Escape then requires concurrent independent dissent that outnumbers the deceiver's voices by a margin. One deceiver voice fell to two or three dissenters; three sock-puppet voices held against four or more. Dissenters who speak briefly and are cut off never broke it in any run. After discovery, an agent that remembers *which source said what* can undo the deception exactly; one that keeps only blended beliefs can only wait for dilution, at a cost proportional to the lie's volume. Carrying provenance as structured state is a recovery requirement.

4. **A new agent's structural vulnerability is few independent channels plus cheap identity.** For claims it cannot check, from a novel source, no pretrained knowledge contains that source's reliability. Impersonating a known entity is an identity-authentication failure, which architecture can partly prevent by enforcing channel roles. Aggregation that limits any one source's influence resists a minority of independent deceivers without any history; fake identities (Sybil) and isolation defeat it. Formation's formal job is to supply authenticated identities with shared history, and deceptions that are guaranteed to be discovered. Whoever controls formation authors the agent's trusted set, so the requirement is *honest* formation.

5. **Deceiving systems under test has a structural cost, in either direction.** A source that makes false claims at rate $q$ in situations the listener cannot distinguish carries $1-H(q)$ bits per yes/no claim if it is otherwise error-free, about 71% of an error-free source's information at 5%. Deception lowers capacity below whatever the source's honest error allows. To the extent evaluations are indistinguishable from deployment, an agent that learns this must discount *all* situational claims from its overseers, true ones in deployment included. To the extent they are distinguishable (frontier models already detect evaluations well above chance), the overseer channel is spared but test results transfer less to deployment. Disclosed or authenticated test designs keep the channel and give up only the transfer that indistinguishability was meant to buy.
