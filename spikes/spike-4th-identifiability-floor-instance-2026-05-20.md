## 2. My read of M1's three-property structure (independent characterization)

A first-class M1 instance must, restated in operational terms:

- **E1–E3 (setting / external theorem / no-go).** The external theorem must be (i) independent of AAT axiom-choice (Instances 1–3 invoke CHT, Cramér-Rao, Liberzon — each fully external; the KNOWN-DEFECTIVE Instance-4 fails on this very point, because its Čencov anchor is the uniqueness machinery for its escape, not the *forbidder* of the no-go). And (ii) of *structural* form: the no-go partitions the input regime into "this inferential task cannot succeed" categorically, not "the task succeeds slowly / approximately". Quantitative finite-sample bounds on a task that is *otherwise attainable in the limit* do not fit; the no-go must be the categorical impossibility, not the rate.

- **E4 ($\geq 2$ distinct escapes mapped to AAT machinery).** The KNOWN-DEFECTIVE Instance-4 fails specifically here — one forced escape via a uniqueness theorem (Čencov) is *not enough* and is provably incompatible with the floor structure (a uniqueness theorem on the escape side mathematically forbids a second structurally distinct escape; see §2 Step 2 of the 2026-05-18 resolution spike). Each escape must correspond to *a different thing the agent must do* (intervene, instrument, observe-jointly, get a witness), and the AAT machinery involved must be *elevated by* the floor — not consumed by it.

- **E5 (strengthened consequence).** The floor must promote AAT machinery from "useful" to "structurally required" — i.e., name a piece of theory whose load-bearing status *follows from the floor*, not "this is consistent with existing load-bearing machinery."

- **Mechanism.** Rank-collapse (Sylvester via Fisher congruence) / composition projection (Mori–Zwanzig / Schur) / forced-identity-failure (Helmholtz non-integrability) — these are canon's three irreducibility mechanisms (`#disc-stability-certificate` lines 56–62). A clean instance fits one, *or* proposes a derivable fourth family explicitly with the same diagnostic clarity.

The five-element + mechanism test, applied honestly, is *strict* — and that is the point. M1's load-bearing role is precisely scope-honesty; admitting an under-fit instance weakens the meta-segment rather than strengthens it (the KNOWN-DEFECTIVE Instance-4 is the live evidence).

## 3. Fano's inequality on observer-side prediction — attempt: Outcome C

**The proposal (471203 Fresh-5 + `#der-agent-opacity` Working Notes 156).** External theorem: Fano's inequality, $P_e \geq (H(X \mid Y) - 1)/\log\lvert\mathcal X\rvert$. Applied to observer $B$ predicting agent $A$'s future action $a_{A,t+\tau}$ from filtration $\mathcal F_B^t$, with $H(X \mid Y) = H_b^{A \mid B}(t,\tau)$ — exactly the quantity `#der-agent-opacity` formalizes (observer-, horizon-, trajectory-indexed predictive entropy, adopted from Hafez 2026). No-go: $B$ cannot drive prediction error to zero while $H_b \gt 0$. AAT-side escape: reduce $H_b$ via richer filtration (IDT pattern), Class-1 architecture narrowing the support, cooperative-coupling commitments.

**Provisional first-pass verdict: A. Strengthen-before-soften check against my own provisional, and the prior-art (§4 of the 2026-05-18 spike): the provisional fails on three independent E-tests.**

**Fail-test (E1–E3) — the no-go is not categorical, it is a finite-sample rate.** Fano's inequality is a quantitative lower bound on $P_e$ given $H_b$. It does not categorically forbid any inferential task; it bounds the *rate* at which prediction can be perfected. Instance 1 (CHT) categorically separates Level-1 and Level-2 *content*; Instance 2 (Cramér-Rao) categorically forbids unbiased finite-variance estimation in a *null direction of the Fisher matrix*; Instance 3 (Liberzon) categorically forbids a *common* Lyapunov function. Each is structurally a partition of the inference space into "the task is impossible in this regime" and "the task is possible in this regime"; the regime boundary is the floor. Fano on $H_b$ gives a *continuous* bound parameterized by $H_b$ itself: for any $H_b \gt 0$, $P_e$ is bounded below by a function of $H_b$, with $P_e = 0$ available only at $H_b = 0$. This is the textbook bound on prediction given entropy, not a structural no-go. The closest thing to a categorical reading — "the observer cannot drive $P_e$ to zero while $H_b \gt 0$" — is trivially true and not a *regime characterization* in the M1 sense.

**Fail-test (E4) — the named escapes are not *AAT machinery elevated by the floor*.** Re-examining my provisional escape list:

- *"Reduce $H_b$ via richer filtration / IDT pattern"* — this is the right-hand-side reading of Fano itself: the bound goes down as the observer adds information. It is not AAT-specific machinery; it is the textbook reading of the bound. The IDT pattern in `#der-agent-opacity` is *named as load-bearing* by the segment *because of the OODA framing*, not because of a floor. The floor does not elevate IDT; it would re-explain it via a textbook bound that any observer-with-prediction-need already takes.

- *"Class-1 (Separated) architecture narrowing the support"* — this changes the agent's *generative law*, not the observer's inferential capability under a fixed regime. It is a structural commitment of the agent, not an instrument the observer wields against an otherwise-floor.

- *"Cooperative-coupling commitments"* — same: agent-side policy commitment, not observer-side identifiability escape.

None of these are the rank-augmentation / instrument-the-latent / intervene-on-the-loop kind of escape Instances 1–3 carve out. They are either trivial restatements of Fano's RHS or commitments on the *agent side* under a *cooperation* framing — different problem, different role. This is the same E4 failure the KNOWN-DEFECTIVE Instance-4 carries, in disguise: the proposed AAT machinery is consumed-by-the-no-go rather than elevated-by-it.

**Fail-test (mechanism) — Fano on a discrete-action conditional entropy does not fit the Sylvester/Mori-Zwanzig/Helmholtz taxonomy.** Fano is a direct inequality on a single conditional entropy, not a rank statement on an information operator. It is not a congruence-orbit invariant (Sylvester-family), not a non-invertible-projection memory commutator (Mori-Zwanzig), and not a non-integrability of a vector field (Helmholtz). A floor-family fourth-mechanism is not impossible in principle, but proposing one needs a derivable diagnostic; "Fano gives a non-Sylvester non-Schur non-Helmholtz floor mechanism" is, at best, a research-seed.

**Independent confirmation — the 2026-05-18 spike already tested Fano-as-anchor for the closely-related agent-internal identifiability question (Object B) and rejected it on cleaner mathematical grounds** (`spike-identifiability-floor-instance4-resolution-2026-05-18.md` §4): "In the similarity-orbit construction, $I(A; \text{obs}) = 0$ *exactly* — the observation law is *identical*, not merely close — so Fano degenerates to the trivial bound 'error probability $\geq$ prior' — true but vacuous, and strictly weaker than the *exact* Kalman-Ho 'they are indistinguishable, full stop.' **Fano is the right tool for the *approximate* / finite-sample version ... it is the wrong tool for the *exact* population no-go.**" That spike's verdict is on a different inferential task (predicting *architecture*, not *action*); my read above is on the action-prediction task. **The reasons differ, but the conclusion is convergent — Fano-as-floor-anchor does not close cleanly in either reading.** The reasons-convergence is itself diagnostic of the shape of the failure: Fano is the right tool for the *finite-sample quantitative refinement of a task that is otherwise structurally OK*, not for the *categorical structural no-go on a regime* that M1 instances require. *(That said, this convergence-evidence note is screening, not arbiter, per `doc/spike-routing.md` §0 — the §3 re-derivation against the five-element test is what carries the verdict.)*

**Outcome for Fano: C (no-go on M1-fit, demonstrated).** Fano is a true, load-bearing observer-side bound on action prediction — but it is a finite-sample / quantitative-rate bound, not a categorical structural floor, the proposed escapes are not AAT-machinery-elevated-by-the-floor in the M1 sense, and the mechanism is not in the canonical taxonomy. The right home for Fano-on-$H_b$ is *adjacent to* M1 (as `#der-agent-opacity` already places it, in §"Meta-pattern positioning", discussion-grade) — not in the floor count itself. Strict tier: *exact* that Fano does not satisfy the E1–E3 categorical-no-go test (the non-categoricality is structural); *robust qualitative* that the proposed escapes fail E4 (the argument depends on what counts as "AAT machinery elevated by the floor," a judgment that inherits the meta-segment's own discussion-grade tier).

## 4. Gibbard-Satterthwaite — attempt: Outcome C

**The proposal (849201 Fresh-5 + `#deriv-strategic-composition` §Discussion line 179–180).** External theorem: GS 1973–75 — every non-dictatorial Pareto-efficient social-choice rule on $\geq 3$ alternatives is manipulable. AAT-side escape: sub-scope $\alpha'$ potential-game conditions in `#deriv-strategic-composition` (or Bayes-Nash relaxation, randomized mechanisms, subsidy injection from the standard mechanism-design literature) "operationalize the escape."

**Verdict: C, on two independent grounds.**

**Fail-test (E5 with category coherence) — the named AAT-side escape does not address what GS forbids.** GS is a no-go on *strategy-proofness* of the mechanism (truthful revelation as a dominant-strategy equilibrium). Sub-scope $\alpha'$ potential-game machinery in `#deriv-strategic-composition` addresses a *different* question: *convergence* of best-response dynamics to a Nash equilibrium, given a mechanism. Nash equilibria of potential games *are still manipulable in the GS sense* (an agent may have a profitable unilateral deviation under a different preference revelation, even when the joint dynamics converge under the current revelation). Sub-scope $\alpha'$ is adjacent territory, not the GS escape. Bayes-Nash IC, preference-domain restriction (single-peaked), randomization, and subsidies *are* the standard GS escapes — but *none of them is currently AAT machinery*. AAT's directed-separation, sub-scope $\alpha'$, signed-coupling, and Class-1/2/3 apparatus do not select preference restrictions, do not authorize randomized allocations, do not budget subsidies. The proposed E5 is **vacuous** — there is no AAT machinery to elevate, because the named candidate machinery does not engage GS's no-go in the first place.

**Fail-test (E1, structural fit) — GS is a no-go on what a *designer* can *implement*, not on what an *agent* can *identify* from a *regime*.** The three established M1 instances (and the genuine candidate Object B from the 2026-05-18 spike) share an inferential-task shape: detect a property of the world / identify a parameter / distinguish two model classes. Boundary-route escapes characterize *what additional information* the agent needs. GS forbids *implementation under incentive-compatibility constraints*; its escapes (BIC / preference restriction / randomization / subsidies) are *design choices over the mechanism*, not *information augmentations of an inferential regime*. This is not a mere philosophical distinction — it changes the diagnostic shape of the floor. M1's "the agent needs $X$ capability to escape the no-go" becomes, for GS, "the designer needs $X$ constraint relaxation to escape the no-go." Same no-go-form, different *actor* under the no-go, different *kind* of remedy. Either GS belongs in a sibling meta-segment (call it *Implementation Impossibility*, separate from M1's *Identifiability Floor*), or the M1 segment needs an explicit broadening of its frame ("inferential task" $\to$ "structural achievability task") — a meta-segment-structural decision Joseph-reserved, not a floor-instance landing.

**Mechanism.** GS's obstruction is *combinatorial* — a counting argument over preference orderings and mechanism outputs, fundamentally discrete/topological (the proof in Reny 2001's geometric form makes the topological content explicit). It is neither Sylvester-rank-collapse nor Mori-Zwanzig-projection nor Helmholtz-non-integrability. Like Fano, this is not disqualifying — but proposing GS as a floor-family fourth-mechanism needs the same derivable-diagnostic discipline.

**Strengthen-first check — is there a route to make GS-as-M1-instance true that I am missing?** Three reframings tested and discarded:

1. *"GS forbids identifying truthful preferences from observed strategic play"* — this is closer to the M1 shape (identifiability), but the no-go is then **revelation principle** + the manipulation lemma, not GS itself; and the inferential task is now sub-agent-preference inference from on-policy strategic play, which is a *different* candidate-instance that may have merit (it is closer in shape to Object B — an architectural-identifiability floor on the preference layer) but is *not what the audit proposed*. Surfacing this as a *fifth* candidate below.

2. *"GS as Section III multi-agent meta-no-go on `#scope-composite-agent` (C-iv) routes"* — sub-scope $\alpha'$ is precisely the route designed to bypass strategic manipulation by *not requiring* strategy-proofness (best-response dynamics under known potential give a convergence guarantee without needing strategy-proofness; potential-game agents "manipulate" but the dynamics converge anyway). GS is then *orthogonal* to AAT's strategic-composition machinery — neither blocks AAT, nor is blocked by it. Adjacent literature, not a floor.

3. *"GS as an obstruction to alignment-design — the designer cannot make the sub-agents' truthful objective the dominant-strategy equilibrium"* — this is the most promising reframing and what `#deriv-strategic-composition`'s "Mechanism-design impossibility" Discussion line 179 actually says. But the AAT machinery in scope here (the (C-iv) scope route) is not the *escape* from GS; it is the *acknowledgement* that AAT's strategic-composition results live in the $\alpha'/\beta'$ regime where GS is not the bottleneck. This positions GS as a *boundary marker on AAT's scope*, not a *floor instance the scope escapes*. The right home is `#disc-separability-pattern`'s *general-open* tier of the strategic-composition ladder, not M1.

**Outcome for GS: C (no-go on M1-fit, demonstrated; with a derived honest re-home as a scope-marker for `#disc-separability-pattern`'s strategic-composition ladder, not a floor instance).** Tier: *exact* that the named E5-candidate machinery (sub-scope $\alpha'$) does not engage the GS no-go (this is a category check, immediate); *robust qualitative* that no alternative AAT-machinery escape is currently available (the three reframings cover the natural rephrasings; deeper search may surface a route I haven't seen).
