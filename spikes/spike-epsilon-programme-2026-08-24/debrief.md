Stated in canon's own scoping vocabulary (`#der-directed-separation`'s scoping rule, which names *both* mis-scoping directions as defects — over-narrowing a zero-scoped result to Class 1, and under-claiming a bounded-coupling result to the exact zero): the week's work already stood on *both* sides of the zero, exactly. The canonicity theorem is scoped to **causal discipline** — the condition κ_processing = 0, held by construction or in effect; its hypothesis is the zero, not the Class-1 certificate. Its Corollary 5 and both sibling spikes sit explicitly on the **coupled** side — separation violated, the belief object shrinking, splintering, or vanishing — and those are exact statements about coupled agents, not approximations. What no result yet occupied was the territory *between*: κ small but nonzero, where every real Class-2 architecture actually lives, and where canon's own degradation norm asks each zero-scoped result to carry an ε(κ) bound. This spike was commissioned to build that quantitative bridge — how much of the zero-side structure survives at small coupling, with what error, and whether anything breaks discontinuously at the zero — and that is what it delivered.

## The answer, in one paragraph

Nothing breaks at zero — provided you ask the approximate question. The scary results from earlier this week (that for a typical goal-coupled agent an *exact* belief object doesn't exist at all, and that where it does exist it can splinter into incompatible versions) turn out to be facts about demanding *perfect* self-containedness of belief. If instead you ask "what does this agent believe, tracked to within the size of its own leak?", the answer is as clean as the disciplined case: the belief/goal boundary is exactly the same carve as at zero (it never shifts — it's a discrete object and stays put), and the belief dynamics are pinned down up to an error exactly equal to the coupling. As the coupling shrinks to zero, everything converges smoothly to the exact theory. The discontinuity people might have feared lives only in the exact-tolerance limit.

## The five things learned

**1. The boundary is rigid; only the dynamics blur.** I expected small coupling might tilt the optimal belief/goal boundary slightly — a little goal mixed into belief being "approximately best." It cannot. The conditions that pin the carve (nothing intervenable in belief; everything non-intervenable in it) are yes/no conditions on a discrete object, so the carve either holds exactly or breaks by a whole discrete jump. All the approximation lands on the *dynamics*: how the belief-state evolves.

**2. What coupling costs is a prior.** At zero coupling, the belief dynamics are one canonical thing. Off zero, defining the belief dynamics requires averaging over the goal you're ignoring — and the answer depends on what distribution over goals you average with. The independent verifier caught my first wording overclaiming this ("pinned down up to exactly the coupling") without saying which candidate kernels count; the repaired statement is layered and, in its strong layer, restored in full: among belief kernels the lumped process can exhibit as its next-step conditional law under some starting condition (realizable ones — a one-step notion; no single kernel need fit all times, which is exactly the still-open weak-lumpability question), the freedom is *exactly* the choice of prior over goals, and all choices agree to within δ, the leak size. A slightly wider class of merely-serviceable kernels agrees to within 2δ. Either way: "belief becomes prior-relative under coupling" isn't a modeling escape-hatch — it is what coupling costs, priced at the coupling itself.

**3. The degradation bound for the carving results now exists — joining, not founding, canon's ε-machinery.** `#der-directed-separation` carries a norm: results proved at κ = 0 should carry an ε(κ) bound where possible — exact at the zero, decaying controllably. My first draft claimed this spike was the first to discharge it; a canon sweep (prompted by your correction through the coordinator, twice) showed that was wrong — canon already holds a landed per-step worked model (the observation-ambiguity bias bound, factor-κ, recognized as such in 2026-05-31 Working Notes), the ε* closure-defect apparatus, the survival classification, and the whole March κ-spike lineage. What this spike genuinely adds on top: the ε-treatment of the *carve itself* (which the existing bounds take as given), and a *compounding-over-time* bound — predicting the belief-trajectory with the approximate dynamics is wrong by at most min(t·δ, δ/(1−mixing)) after t steps — which is exactly the "propagated-residual bound" shape `#impl-orient-cascade` flags as an open strengthening. It also answers the March 2026 κ-spikes' central open question (sharp regime boundary vs smooth degradation?) at the carve level: both — existence-of-exact-structure is regime-like and jumps at zero; tracking error is smooth and linear. The two intuitions that spike couldn't decide between were about different objects.

**4. The week's pathologies are one trade-off, made visible.** The "no belief object exists" and "belief splinters into incompatible carvings" results all come from insisting belief be *exactly* self-contained, and quietly paying for that by throwing away completeness — merging belief-distinctions until the leak cancels. The ε-view shows the two currencies: keep completeness and pay δ in predictive exactness, or keep exactness and pay in big discrete lumps of lost belief-content (with a non-canonical choice of which lumps — that's the splintering). Both are legitimate; they answer different questions. For every practical purpose in the framework — including the FAST paper — the first is the relevant one, and there the picture is canonical-with-error-bars. The splintering phenomena stay real and important, but scale-relative: they are the structure of belief *below* the agent's own coupling scale, which for LLM-class (heavily coupled) agents is admittedly most of the range — so they stay operative for 03-llm-core, while for near-disciplined agents they live in a thin window.

**5. A bonus, from the adversarial-instance drill.** The genericity spike guessed a "complementarity" construction (an agent whose possible belief-carvings can each learn one feature of the world but no carving can learn two) would need 6 belief-values. It exists at 4: an agent whose two possible belief-carvings track "first bit" and "second bit" respectively, each blind to the other, with no carving seeing both. The adversarial checking of this construction produced a double-correction worth confessing: I first wrongly "discovered" a third (parity-tracking) carving by checking only half the required conditions, then caught my own catch on the final re-read. The near-miss was informative anyway: the *unleaked* version of the agent has exactly three carvings — first bit, second bit, parity, the three characters of the group {0,1}² — and the goal-leak, which is itself parity-shaped, kills exactly the parity carve and spares the two orthogonal to it. That suggests the splintering structure of modular/tied architectures (the realistic kind, per the genericity spike's own argument) may be classifiable by symmetry/Fourier analysis. Flagged as an open line, not a result.

## Honest edges

The core theorems are finite-state; the carrying-over to continuous state spaces looks routine for the main bounds but is unchecked. One sharpness claim (that the linear-growth rate is achieved by the *marginal* form of the bound, not just the trajectory form) is plausible-unverified. "Soft" (probabilistic) belief-carvings escape the rigidity theorem by design and are the natural next object — untouched. The literature scan found a 2024 paper (Michel & Siegle, error bounds for state-space reduction) that may already contain the degradation bound's mathematical shape — it must be read before any novelty claim; the framework-facing content (the rigidity/gauge split, the trade pricing, the window resolution of this week's pathologies) I have no reason to believe exists elsewhere, but that is an impression, not a claim.
