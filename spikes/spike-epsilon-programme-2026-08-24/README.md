## 0. Verdict in five lines

1. **There is no discontinuity at zero in what exists — the discontinuity is confined to the exact-tolerance limit.** For any coupled kernel, the goal-orbit partition $\Omega$ itself is an $\varepsilon$-approximate belief carving with defect exactly the coupling scale $\delta(P)$, and the whole disciplined picture — canonical carve, quotient belief kernel, degradation bound — is restored at any tolerance $\varepsilon \geq \delta(P)$ (§3). The genericity spike's annihilation result and the splintering phenomena are real but live entirely inside the sub-coupling window $\varepsilon \lt \delta(P)$: they are what you see when you demand belief-precision *finer than the coupling itself* (§5).
2. **The carve is rigid; only the dynamics degrade.** Under the per-state forms of approximate purity/completeness, any $\varepsilon$-admissible decomposition with $\varepsilon$ below an explicit threshold has *exactly* the canonical partition — approximation never moves the carve, because (R2)+(R3) alone (no closure needed) pin the partition set-theoretically (§2, Theorem E1). The entire ε-freedom lands on the belief *kernel*, which — among *realizable* kernels (one-step conditional laws the lumped process can actually exhibit; §3's admissibility layering) — is canonical up to a **prior gauge** of TV-diameter $\leq \delta(P)$: the choice of an averaging prior over the goal fiber (§3). At $\delta \to 0$ the gauge closes and Theorem 2 / Theorem D are recovered. The ε-theory's new gauge being *a prior over goals* independently motivates the weak-lumpability open line: prior-relativity of belief is not an escape hatch, it is what coupling *costs*, priced at $\delta$.
3. **The degradation bound has the exact shape canon's norm asks for** (§4, Theorem E3): the lumped belief process is $\delta$-approximately Markov, with marginal prediction error $\leq \min(t\,\delta,\; \delta/(1-\beta))$ after $t$ steps ($\beta$ = Dobrushin coefficient of the belief kernel) — exact at the zero, linear in the coupling off it; sharpness holds for the ceiling and the trajectory-form linear rate, with the marginal-form linear rate robust-qualitative (the honest split is §4.3's). A Pinsker-route lemma connects $\delta$ to canon's $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ numerator: $\delta \leq \sqrt{2 I / w_{\min}}$ (§4.4), so the bound genuinely is an $\varepsilon(\kappa)$ bound.
4. **The three exact-regime pathologies are one trade, now priced.** Annihilation and splintering arise from insisting on *exact* epistemic closure (D2/K2) and paying for it in completeness (R3) — the carvings $A$, $B$ of the sibling spikes satisfy purity but violate completeness. The ε-theory prices the trade: keeping full completeness (the carve = $\Omega$) costs exactly $\delta$ of closure; keeping exact closure costs completeness in discrete lumps. Exactness forces you to choose; approximation lets you buy both, at price $\delta$ (§5.1).
5. **Bonus, from the adversarial drill on the sibling's open line:** the two-feature complementarity construction the genericity spike judged constructible at $\lvert\mathcal M\rvert = 6$ exists already at $\lvert\mathcal M\rvert = 4$ (8 states), by a product construction — two incomparable minimal carvings, each with a spectral-gapped quotient learnable on one feature and exactly blind to the other, with no carving seeing both (§6). Its ε-reading: the complementarity dissolves at tolerance $\geq \delta(P)$, where $\Omega$ re-enters and sees both features.

## 1. Setup and the coupling defect

Finite $\mathcal X = \mathcal M \times \mathcal G$ throughout (the finite case is where the sibling spikes' pathologies were exhibited, so it is where the ε-repair must be shown; the infinite case is flagged open in §7). Per-event kernels $P_e$; goal-orbit partition $\Omega$ with blocks $O_m = \{m\} \times \mathcal G$. All definitions are stated for full intervention access; §2.3 carries the restricted-access form (orbits replaced by $\{m\} \times R(\gamma)$ classes) — the arguments are identical and I state where they are not.

For a state $x$ and partition $\pi$, write $\mathrm{row}^\pi_e(x) \in \Delta(\pi)$ for the $\pi$-pushforward of $P_e(x, \cdot)$. The **lumpability defect** of a coarsening $\pi$ of $\Omega$ is

$$\Delta_e(\pi) := \max_{B \in \pi} \; \max_{x, x' \in B} \; \mathrm{TV}\big(\mathrm{row}^\pi_e(x),\, \mathrm{row}^\pi_e(x')\big), \qquad \Delta(\pi) := \max_e \Delta_e(\pi).$$

$\Delta(\pi) = 0$ is exactly Kemeny–Snell strong lumpability of every $P_e$ w.r.t. $\pi$ — the (K2) of the distributional sibling. A **$\varepsilon$-carving** is a coarsening of $\Omega$ with $\Delta(\pi) \leq \varepsilon$.

The **coupling defect** of the kernel family is the defect of the orbit partition itself:

$$\delta(P) := \Delta(\Omega) = \max_e \max_m \max_{g, g'} \mathrm{TV}\big(\mathrm{row}^\Omega_e(m, g),\, \mathrm{row}^\Omega_e(m, g')\big)$$

— the largest amount, in total variation on next-step *belief-orbit* content, by which the goal state moves the epistemic marginal. $\delta(P) = 0$ iff the $\mathcal M$-marginal kernel has no $\mathcal G$-argument, i.e. iff the agent is causally disciplined under every input law ($\kappa_{\text{processing}} \equiv 0$, per the distributional sibling's recognition, re-derived here in §4.4 without relying on it). $\delta$ is the sup-norm, distribution-free companion of $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$: it measures the *worst-case realized* leak rather than an information-theoretic average. §4.4 gives the quantitative bridge.

## 2. Rigidity: the carve does not bend — it holds exactly or breaks discretely

The exact theorem's admissibility hypotheses were (R1) product-completeness, (R2) purity ($\mu \circ \iota_h = \mu$), (R3) completeness ($\ker \mu \subseteq\; \sim_{\mathrm{cf}}$), plus the closure/cascade conditions (D2)/(D3). The first observation of the ε-programme is a bookkeeping fact with structural consequences:

**Observation.** (R2) and (R3) alone — with no closure hypothesis of any kind — already pin the partition of $\mu$ exactly. Under full access, (R2) forces $\mu$ constant on each orbit (constant interventions connect every pair in an orbit), so $\ker\mu$-blocks are unions of orbits; (R3) forces $\ker\mu$-blocks inside orbits. Jointly: the blocks *are* the orbits, $\mu \cong q$ as a partition map. The same two lines run verbatim for restricted access with $R$-classes in place of full orbits. (In the exact theorem, closure (D2) did the work of making the quotient a *dynamical* factor; it never constrained the partition further. This is why the exact uniqueness proof routed through the universal property rather than the congruence lattice — the distributional sibling's §2 observation, seen from the other side.)

Consequently, the ε-relaxation splits cleanly into two questions with opposite answers: what happens to the *carve* when (R2)/(R3) are relaxed (§2.1: nothing, below a threshold — rigidity), and what happens to the *dynamics* when (D2)/(K2) is relaxed (§3–4: a gauge opens and a degradation bound appears — the actual ε-content).

### 2.1 Theorem E1 (rigidity of the carve)

Fix a deterministic decomposition map $\mu : \mathcal X \to \mathcal M'$ ($\mathcal M'$ discrete). Three inequivalent formalizations of "$\mu$ is $\varepsilon$-approximately pure," in decreasing strength:

- **(P-sup)** per-state, uniform: $\mu(\iota_h x) = \mu(x)$ fails for no $(x, h)$ — relaxed form: the fraction of failing $(x,h)$ pairs is... there is nothing to relax: for deterministic $\mu$ into a discrete space, each equality is 0/1. **Any $\varepsilon \lt 1$ in a sup-metric formulation is equivalent to $\varepsilon = 0$.** Same for (R3). So under (P-sup): every $\varepsilon$-admissible decomposition, $\varepsilon \lt 1$, has exactly the canonical carve — Theorem 2's partition-uniqueness holds *verbatim*, not approximately, at every coupling level. $\square$
- **(P-mass)** law-weighted, per-state coupling form: for a reference law $\nu$ on $\mathcal X$, $\sup_h \nu\{x : \mu(\iota_h x) \neq \mu(x)\} \leq \varepsilon$. Then, under full access, **$\mu$ agrees with an exactly pure map except on $\nu$-mass $\leq \varepsilon$**: fix any $g_0$ and set $\mu'(m, g) := \mu(m, g_0)$; $\mu'$ is constant on orbits, and $\nu\{\mu \neq \mu'\} = \nu\{x : \mu(x) \neq \mu(\iota_{h_{g_0}} x)\} \leq \varepsilon$ by the hypothesis at $h = h_{g_0}$. For the completeness half, take the mass-form of (R3) to be: $\nu\big\{x : \mu\text{-block of } x \text{ contains a state outside } x\text{'s orbit, drawn from } \operatorname{supp}\nu\big\}$ — more precisely, writing $E_{R3} := \{x : \exists x' \in \operatorname{supp}\nu,\, \mu(x') = \mu(x),\, q(x') \neq q(x)\}$, assume $\nu(E_{R3}) \leq \varepsilon$. Then on $\operatorname{supp}\nu$ minus $E_{R3} \cup \{\mu \neq \mu'\}$ (the intersection with $\operatorname{supp}\nu$ is needed because the contradiction argument requires the colliding partner as an in-support witness — verification addendum; off-support collisions are $\nu$-null, so the mass conclusion is unchanged; total mass $\geq 1 - 2\varepsilon$), $\mu'$ is orbit-constant *and* orbit-separating, i.e. agrees with the canonical quotient map up to injective relabeling of the labels it uses there. So: **the carve equals the canonical one off $\nu$-mass $\leq 2\varepsilon$** — a *mass* statement, not a partition statement: a whole orbit-pair of total mass $\leq 2\varepsilon$ can still be merged outright, and any landing segment should say so (verification F3's refinement, adopted). $\square$
- **(P-push)** law-weighted, pushforward form: $\sup_h \mathrm{TV}\big(\mu_* \nu,\, \mu_* (\iota_h)_* \nu\big) \leq \varepsilon$. **This form is genuinely weaker and does *not* control the carve** — adversarial witness (constructed for the standing drill: an instance deliberately outside the motivating family): $\mathcal M = \{1\}$, $\mathcal G = \{g_1, g_2\}$, $\mu(1, g_1) = a \neq b = \mu(1, g_2)$, $\nu$ uniform, $h$ = the swap. The pushforward is $\tfrac12 a + \tfrac12 b$ before and after — TV defect $0$ — while $\mu$ is maximally impure: label *swaps* are invisible to marginals. (P-push) is therefore the wrong operationalization for any downstream use; the ε-theory should carry (P-sup) or (P-mass). $\square$

**What rigidity means.** The carve is a discrete object and behaves like one: it cannot drift continuously off the canonical partition as coupling grows. Approximation in the ε-theory never relocates the belief/goal boundary; it degrades what the boundary *delivers*. (This also answers a natural worry about the exact theorem's practical meaning: one might fear that at $\kappa \gt 0$ the "right" carve tilts slightly toward mixing a little goal into belief. It does not — under any per-state reading of admissibility the optimum is pinned, and the tilt is expressible only as the discrete lump-jumps of §5.)

### 2.2 What was *not* relaxed, honestly

Rigidity as stated relaxes (R2)/(R3) for *deterministic* carvings. A **stochastic** carving ($\mu$ a kernel $\mathcal X \to \Delta(\mathcal M')$ — soft belief assignment) has genuinely continuous purity defect, and the rigidity argument does not apply; soft carvings are the one place a continuously-tilting belief boundary could live. I did not develop the soft theory beyond noticing (§5.3) that near a splintered kernel the convex interpolations of two exact carvings are natural soft carvings. Flagged open, with a shape-guess labeled as such in §7.

### 2.3 Restricted access

All of §2 runs with $H$ arbitrary and orbits replaced by $\{m\} \times R(\gamma)$: (R2) gives constancy on $R$-classes, (R3) bounds blocks inside classes, and the carve is pinned to the $R$-class partition — exactly, under (P-sup). For (P-mass) the first draft claimed a chain bound $L\varepsilon$ over generator chains of length $L$; **that step was unjustified as written** (verification F2): the hypothesis controls a single intervention applied *from $\nu$*, while step $k$ of a chain needs the one-step event under the pushforward $(\iota_{h_{k-1}} \cdots \iota_{h_1})_* \nu$. Two honest repairs: **(a)** if $H$ is composition-closed (contains its own compositions — true for the constant-map family and for any group-like $H$), each chain collapses to a single intervention and the bound is $\varepsilon$ outright (the $L$ was an under-claim); **(b)** for general $H$, assume $\nu$ quasi-invariant under the $\iota_h$ with Radon–Nikodym bound $\sup_h \lVert \mathrm d(\iota_h)_*\nu / \mathrm d\nu \rVert_\infty \leq \Lambda$; then step $k$'s event has $\nu$-mass $\leq \Lambda^{k-1}\varepsilon$ and the chain bound is $\varepsilon(\Lambda^L - 1)/(\Lambda - 1)$ — reducing to $L\varepsilon$ only at $\Lambda = 1$ (measure-preserving interventions). Without one of (a)/(b) the (P-mass) restricted-access bound is open. The compatibility condition (C) acquires a defect version in §4.5.

## 3. The ε-existence theorem and the prior gauge

The exact-regime disaster off the zero was existence: generically *no* nontrivial coarsening of $\Omega$ is exactly lumpable (genericity sibling, Prop G1/Cor G2 — re-derived in miniature here: lumpability is finitely many affine equalities on the kernel polytope, generically all violated). The ε-repair is immediate once stated, and its content is that the *right* candidate was always $\Omega$ itself:

**Theorem E2 (ε-existence and ε-canonicity).** Let $P$ have coupling defect $\delta = \delta(P)$. Then:

**(i) (Existence.)** $\Omega$ is a $\delta$-carving — by definition of $\delta$. Moreover $\Omega$ is the *finest* coarsening of $\Omega$ trivially, and it satisfies (R2)+(R3) exactly (not approximately) as a partition. So at every tolerance $\varepsilon \geq \delta$, an $\varepsilon$-carving satisfying full exact admissibility-as-a-partition exists. **Approximate belief always exists, with defect exactly the coupling.** Annihilation is a statement about $\varepsilon = 0$ only.

**(ii) (Canonicity among admissible ε-carvings.)** Any $\varepsilon$-carving whose map satisfies (R2)+(R3) in a per-state form ((P-sup), or (P-mass) up to the stated mass) *is* $\Omega$ (up to the stated mass) — by Theorem E1. Uniqueness at the partition level is exact, for every $\varepsilon \lt 1$ and every coupling level. The carvings $A, B$ of the sibling witnesses are not counterexamples: they violate (R3) outright (each merges distinct orbits), which is visible only once (R3) is carried into the coupled regime rather than dropped there — see §5.1.

**(iii) (The prior gauge on the belief kernel.)** With the carve pinned at $\Omega$, what remains is the belief *kernel*, and the honest statement needs an explicit admissibility notion (the first draft asserted "exactly the fiber-averaging freedom" without one — verification finding F1; the repair below strengthens rather than softens). Call a kernel $Q$ on $\mathcal M$ **realizable** if it is the one-step conditional next-block law of the true lumped process under some initial law $\nu$ with $\nu(q^{-1}(m)) \gt 0$ for every $m$ (the support clause is part of the definition, not a footnote — without it the conditional is undefined on null blocks and the equivalence below fails exactly there; it is costless since $\nu$ is existentially quantified and every prior family has a full-support realizer — verification addendum): $Q(m, \cdot) = \mathbb E[\mathrm{row}^\Omega_e(X) \mid q(X) = m]$. The realizable kernels are *exactly* the fiber-averages: conditioning gives a fiber-mixture with weights $\nu(g \mid m)$, and conversely any prior family $w$ is realized by any full-support $\nu$ with those fiber-conditionals — and one such $\nu$ realizes the whole per-event family $\{Q^w_e\}_e$ simultaneously with the same $w$, which is what makes the realizable class match the gauge orbit across events rather than event-by-event. Realizability is a *one-step, initial-law* notion: the process's fiber-conditional drifts at later times, so no single realizable $Q$ need be the conditional law time-uniformly — that time-uniform question is precisely the weak-lumpability interior question flagged below. A strictly wider set is admissible in the weaker companion-(a) sense (every fiber row within $\delta$ of the corresponding $Q$-row — the property Theorem E3's proof actually consumes): that set contains the realizable ones, can leave their convex hull, and has TV-diameter $\leq 2\delta$; E3 holds verbatim for any kernel in it. For the *realizable* family $w = (w_m)_m$:

$$Q^w_e(m, \cdot) := \sum_{g} w_m(g)\, \mathrm{row}^\Omega_e(m, g).$$

Every $Q^w_e$ row lies in the convex hull of the fiber rows, any two of which are within $\delta$ in TV; hence **any two realizable choices satisfy $\max_m \mathrm{TV}\big(Q^w_e(m,\cdot), Q^{w'}_e(m,\cdot)\big) \leq \delta$** — the realizable set has TV-diameter $\leq \delta$ and closes to a point as $\delta \to 0$, recovering the exact canonical $Q_e$ of Theorem D / the $f_M$ of Theorem 2. *Derivation of the diameter bound:* $Q^w - Q^{w'}$ is a difference of two convex combinations of points of pairwise TV-distance $\leq \delta$; TV is a norm-ball constraint, and the convex hull of a set of diameter $\delta$ has diameter $\leq \delta$. So the layered statement, each layer derived: realizable = fiber-averages (exactly the prior freedom, diameter $\leq \delta$); companion-(a)-admissible $\supseteq$ realizable (diameter $\leq 2\delta$; E3-sufficient). "Underdetermined by exactly the prior" is true of realizable kernels, and that is the operationally right class — a belief kernel nobody's lumped process can exhibit is not a candidate belief. $\square$

**The gauge is a prior — the structural point.** In the exact theory the residual freedom (Prop 3) was a relabeling of the *goal* fiber; the epistemic side was gauge-free. Off the zero, a second, genuinely epistemic gauge opens: the belief kernel depends on *what you assume about the goal you are averaging out*, to exactly $\delta$. This is the same mathematical object as weak lumpability's initial-law-relativity (the distributional sibling's §6 escape-hatch guess), but the ε-theory changes its status: prior-relativity is not an optional alternative formalization — it is the *forced and priced* residue of coupling. At $\delta = 0$ belief is prior-free; at $\delta \gt 0$ it is prior-relative to within $\delta$, no more. (Weak-lumpability exactness — whether special priors make the lumped process *exactly* Markov — remains open and is now a question about the interior of this gauge orbit.)

## 4. The degradation theorem — canon's ε(κ) norm, discharged for this cluster

### 4.1 Theorem E3 (approximate Markovianity of the belief process)

Let $X_t$ be the true chain (any event sequence $e_1, e_2, \ldots$; any initial law $\nu_0$), $B_t := q(X_t) \in \mathcal M$ the belief-orbit trajectory, and fix any gauge choice $Q^w$. Let $\beta := \max_e \beta(Q^w_e)$ be the worst Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient. Then for every $t$:

$$\mathrm{TV}\Big( \mathcal L(B_t),\; \nu_0^\Omega\, Q^w_{e_1} \cdots Q^w_{e_t} \Big) \;\leq\; \sum_{s=1}^{t} \beta^{\,t-s}\, \delta \;\leq\; \min\Big( t\,\delta,\; \frac{\delta}{1 - \beta} \Big).$$

*Derivation.* One-step: $\mathcal L(B_{s+1}) = \mathbb E\big[\mathrm{row}^\Omega_{e_{s+1}}(X_s)\big]$. Conditional on $B_s = m$, the row $\mathrm{row}^\Omega_{e_{s+1}}(X_s)$ is one of the fiber rows of orbit $m$, each within $\delta$ of $Q^w_{e_{s+1}}(m, \cdot)$ (a convex combination of points within $\delta$ of each fiber row — §3(iii)'s hull argument again). Averaging over the conditional fiber distribution and then over $B_s$: $\mathrm{TV}\big(\mathcal L(B_{s+1}),\, \mathcal L(B_s)\, Q^w_{e_{s+1}}\big) \leq \delta$. Then telescope: with $\epsilon_s := \mathrm{TV}(\mathcal L(B_s), \nu_0^\Omega Q^w_{e_1} \cdots Q^w_{e_s})$, the triangle inequality plus contraction of TV under $Q^w_{e_{s+1}}$ by factor $\beta$ gives $\epsilon_{s+1} \leq \delta + \beta\, \epsilon_s$; unroll. $\square$

Two companions, same method: **(a)** the *conditional one-step prediction* error is pointwise $\leq \delta$: given any realized past, the next-block law is within $\delta$ of the $Q^w$ prediction from the current block — this is the per-decision form an implementer wants; **(b)** the *whole-trajectory* law satisfies $\mathrm{TV}\big(\mathcal L(B_{1:t}),\, \text{Markov}(Q^w)\big) \leq t\,\delta$ (couple step-by-step; each step fails with probability $\leq \delta$; union bound along the trajectory). The trajectory bound cannot be improved to a mixing-saturated form in general — trajectory-level discrepancies do not contract.

### 4.3 Sharpness (shape, not constants)

Both regimes of the $\min$ are achieved up to constants. *Saturation regime:* a two-orbit kernel whose $g_1$-slice biases belief-drift by $+\delta/2$ and $g_2$-slice by $-\delta/2$, goal frozen at $g_1$, uniform gauge $w$: the true marginal converges to the $g_1$-slice stationary law, the $Q^w$-chain to the averaged one, at TV-distance $\Theta(\delta)$ for fixed spectral gap — so the $\delta/(1-\beta)$ ceiling is the truth, not an artifact. *Linear regime:* slow the chain (holding probability $1 - \eta$): discrepancy accumulates at rate $\Theta(\eta\delta)$ per step against mixing time $\Theta(1/\eta)$, tracking $t\,\delta$-type growth below the mixing time... **correction, caught in the standing wrap-up drill:** with holding probability the per-step leak is also scaled by $\eta$, so the growth is $\Theta(\eta \delta t)$ against ceiling $\Theta(\delta)$ — this exhibits linear *growth* but not the unsaturated $t\delta$ *rate*. The clean witness for the linear rate is the trajectory bound (b), and it can be named concretely (added per verification F6, which rightly graded the original one-sentence mechanism below its "derived" label): the saturation witness with goal frozen at $g_1$ — the true block process is the i.i.d.-in-time $g_1$-slice dynamics, biased $+\delta/2$ toward one block, while the uniform-gauge $Q^w$ chain is unbiased; per step, the two next-block laws differ by $\delta/2$ on a *named* block, so the step-$s$ block values can be coupled to disagree with probability $\geq \delta/2$ independently across steps, and distinguishability of the disagreement event (it lands on the identified block) prevents cancellation: trajectory-TV $\geq 1 - (1 - \delta/2)^t \approx t\,\delta/2$ for $t \ll 1/\delta$, exactly linear. Marginal-TV sharpness of the *unsaturated* $t\delta$ regime I checked only in the $\beta \to 1$ limit informally; graded **robust-qualitative**, not derived — the honest statement is: upper bounds derived (Theorem E3); ceiling sharpness derived; linear-rate sharpness derived for the trajectory form, plausible-unverified for the marginal form.

### 4.4 The bridge to κ_processing

Canon's $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ is information-theoretic; $\delta$ is TV. The zeros coincide: $\delta = 0$ iff the $\mathcal M$-marginal has no goal argument iff $I(G; M^+ \mid e, M^-) = 0$ under every input law (both directions elementary: no argument ⟹ conditional independence; conversely $I = 0$ under the law putting mass on a violating fiber pair forces equal rows). Quantitatively, one direction is clean:

**Lemma E4 (Pinsker route).** Fix $(e, m)$ and a fiber prior $w$ with $w_{\min} := \min_g w_m(g) \gt 0$. With $I := I(G;\, M^+ \mid e, m)$ in nats under this prior, every fiber row satisfies $\mathrm{TV}(\mathrm{row}_g, \overline{\mathrm{row}}) \leq \sqrt{ \mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{row}_g \Vert \overline{\mathrm{row}}) / 2 } \leq \sqrt{ I / (2 w_m(g)) }$, hence

$$\delta_{e,m} \leq 2 \max_g \mathrm{TV}(\mathrm{row}_g, \overline{\mathrm{row}}) \leq \sqrt{ 2 I / w_{\min} }.$$

*Derivation:* $I = \sum_g w_m(g)\, \mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{row}_g \Vert \overline{\mathrm{row}})$ (mutual information as prior-weighted KL to the mixture), so each summand is $\leq I / w_m(g)$; Pinsker ($\mathrm{KL} \geq 2\,\mathrm{TV}^2$, nats); triangle inequality. $\square$

So Theorem E3's bound *is* an $\varepsilon(\kappa)$ bound: error $\leq \min\big(t, \tfrac{1}{1-\beta}\big) \cdot \sqrt{2 I^\ast / w_{\min}}$, with $I^\ast := \max_{e,m} I$ (the lemma is per-$(e,m)$; the composition needs the max — verification F8) and $I$ the numerator of $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$. The square-root loss is Pinsker's and real. The reverse direction ($I \lesssim \delta$) needs a density lower bound on the rows (reverse-Pinsker-type inequalities; working-knowledge attribution: Verdú 2014 and successors, **unverified**, queued for the lit pass) — without support control, $I$ can be small while $\delta$ is large only in low-prior-mass fibers, which is exactly the regime where the sup-norm $\delta$ and the averaged $\kappa$ *should* disagree; the disagreement is a feature of measuring worst-case vs typical leak, not a defect of either.

## 5. The sub-coupling window: where the exact pathologies live, and what they become

### 5.1 The completeness/closure trade, priced

Restate the sibling spikes' objects in admissibility language. The exact belief carvings of a coupled kernel ($A$, $B$ of the witnesses; the trivial carve of generic annihilation) all satisfy purity (R2) and exact closure (D2/K2) — and all **violate completeness (R3)**, each merging orbit-distinctions that goal-intervention *can* move. The exact theory off the zero thus silently traded (R3) away to keep (D2). The ε-theory makes the trade explicit and priced:

- Keep (R3) (and the canonical carve $\Omega$): closure holds to within exactly $\delta(P)$, never exactly (for a genuinely coupled kernel). Cost: $\delta$, paid in prediction error (Theorem E3).
- Keep (D2) exactly: (R3) must be abandoned in *discrete lumps* — merge whole orbit-families until the leak cancels (the witnesses' $A$/$B$) or everything merges (generic annihilation). Cost: unboundedly coarse belief, and non-canonical choice of which completeness to sacrifice (splintering).

Neither is wrong; they answer different questions ("what does this agent believe, tracked to accuracy $\delta$" vs "what *exactly* self-contained summaries does this agent's dynamics support"). The framework's practical question — canon's degradation norm, the FAST paper's Class-2 story — is the first one. **The trichotomy pathologies are exact-closure phenomena, invisible at any tolerance $\geq \delta(P)$.**

### 5.2 The window structure

For tolerance $\varepsilon$ against coupling $\delta = \delta(P)$:

- **$\varepsilon \geq \delta$:** $\Omega$ qualifies; it refines every carving; the $\varepsilon$-carving poset has a unique minimum ($\Omega$), the prior gauge has diameter $\leq \delta \leq \varepsilon$, and every coarser carving's quotient is a further lumping of $\Omega$'s — the disciplined picture with error bars. In the witnesses: $\Omega$ (4 belief-states) sees everything $A$ and $B$ each half-see; the "carve-relative learnability" paradox of the genericity spike's §4 *dissolves* — both features are learnable from the $\Omega$-quotient, and the paradox is revealed as an artifact of exact-closure over-coarsening.
- **$\varepsilon \lt \delta$:** $\Omega$ is excluded, and the landscape is governed by which *exact* carvings exist nearby: generically only the trivial one (annihilation); on symmetric strata, gauge-equivalent splintered minima; on tuned strata, inequivalent splintered minima. Exact carvings are stable as ε-carvings: if $\pi$ is exactly lumpable for $P'$ and $\lVert P - P' \rVert_{\mathrm{TV,rows}} \leq \eta$ then $\Delta_P(\pi) \leq 2\eta$ (triangle inequality through $P'$) — so each exact-regime stratum broadens into an $\varepsilon$-basin, and the sibling spikes' taxonomy persists as the classification of *sub-coupling-tolerance* belief. (Basin geometry beyond this containment statement: not developed.)
- **The zero-limit question, answered precisely:** along any path $P_t \to P_0$ of coupled kernels approaching the disciplined stratum ($\delta(P_t) \to 0$), the *exact* finest-carving correspondence is discontinuous at $t = 0$ (e.g. the witness family: $\{A, B\}$ for every $t \gt 0$, jumping to $\Omega$ at $t = 0$), while for any fixed tolerance $\varepsilon \gt 0$ the ε-correspondence is eventually constant at $\Omega$ (as soon as $\delta(P_t) \lt \varepsilon$) and the belief kernel converges (gauge diameter $\to 0$). **The exact theory is discontinuous at zero; the ε-theory is continuous — the carve is locally constant, and the realizable belief kernels converge to the canonical one because their diameter-bound $\delta(P_t)$ vanishes.** (A first draft added "the kernel is 1-Lipschitz in the coupling"; cut per verification F4.2 — the derivable Lipschitz fact is in the *row-perturbation* metric ($\Delta_P(\pi)$ moves by $\leq 2\eta$ under row-TV perturbation $\eta$, the §5.2 stability bound), and "Lipschitz in $\delta(P)$" is not well-posed: equal-$\delta$ kernels can have arbitrarily different quotients.) This is the direct answer to the commissioned question.

### 5.3 What splintering means after the ε-theory

The genericity spike closed by asking whether real relevance survives the null-stratum confinement. The answer this spike supports: splintering is real but *scale-relative* — it is the structure of the belief question **below the coupling scale**, i.e. it matters exactly when one insists on belief-summaries more self-contained than the agent's own leak. For `03-llm-core/` purposes (Class 3, $\delta$ large), the window $\varepsilon \lt \delta$ is *most* of the range, so splintering remains operative there; for Class 2 near the zero ($\delta$ small), the window is thin and the ε-canonical picture dominates. One honest bridge object noticed but not developed: near a splintered kernel, the convex interpolations $\lambda A + (1{-}\lambda) B$ of the two carve-projections are *soft* carvings (§2.2) with continuously varying defect — the natural home for a future soft-ε-theory unifying the window's two sides.

## 6. The complementarity construction — |𝓜| = 4 suffices (sibling's open line, closed beyond its estimate)

The genericity spike (§6) conjectured "disconnected learnability in both directions" constructible at $\lvert\mathcal M\rvert = 6$. It exists at $\lvert\mathcal M\rvert = 4$, $\lvert\mathcal X\rvert = 8$, by a product construction. Take $\mathcal M = \{0,1\}^2$ with coordinates ("features") $f_1, f_2$; $\mathcal G = \{g_1, g_2\}$, goal frozen; one event. Base belief-dynamics: independent product of two 2-state chains, $K_1 = \begin{pmatrix} .6 & .4 \\ .4 & .6 \end{pmatrix}$ on $f_1$ (gap eigenvalue $0.2$), $K_2 = \begin{pmatrix} .65 & .35 \\ .35 & .65 \end{pmatrix}$ on $f_2$ (gap $0.3$): orbit-mass vectors $q_m(O_{m'}) = K_1(f_1, f_1') K_2(f_2, f_2')$. Leak: only orbit $O_{(0,0)}$'s two fiber rows are perturbed, to $q_{(0,0)} \pm d$ with $d = t\,(1, -1, -1, 1)$ in the orbit-order $\big(O_{(0,0)}, O_{(0,1)}, O_{(1,0)}, O_{(1,1)}\big)$, $t = 1/16$.

*Checks (each a one-line sum; arranged for re-derivation by inspection).* **Positivity:** smallest base entry $.4 \times .35 = .14 \gt t$. ✓ **Coupled:** $O_{(0,0)}$'s fiber rows differ ($t \neq 0$). ✓ **Carve $A$ (by $f_1$: blocks $\{O_{(0,0)}, O_{(0,1)}\}, \{O_{(1,0)}, O_{(1,1)}\}$) is lumpable:** leak sums per $A$-block: $t - t = 0$, $-t + t = 0$; base mass into $A$-block-1 is $K_1(f_1, 0)$ — depends on $f_1$ only, constant on each $A$-block. ✓ **Carve $B$ (by $f_2$) is lumpable:** leak sums $t - t$, $-t + t$ along the other pairing; base mass into $B$-block-1 is $K_2(f_2, 0)$. ✓ **Both minimal:** any partition strictly between $\Omega$ and $A$ (or $B$) isolates a block on which $d$ has a lone $\pm t$ component. ✓ **These are the *only* nontrivial carvings:** a nontrivial two-block coarsening pairs the four orbits; the leak constraint requires $d$-sums zero per block, and among the three pairings of $\{+1, -1, -1, +1\}$ only the $A$-pairing ($d_1 + d_2 = 0$, $d_3 + d_4 = 0$) and the $B$-pairing ($d_1 + d_3 = 0$, $d_2 + d_4 = 0$) qualify — the diagonal pairing $\{O_{(0,0)}, O_{(1,1)}\} \mid \{O_{(0,1)}, O_{(1,0)}\}$ has $d_1 + d_4 = 2t \neq 0$ (the leak rows $q_{(0,0)} \pm d$ put mass $.53 \pm 2t$ into diagonal-block-1, differing by $4t$), even though the *base* rows alone would pass ($.53/.53$ and $.47/.47$ per block — the diagonal is a carving of the *unleaked* product kernel that the leak destroys). Three-block coarsenings isolate an orbit with a lone $\pm t$ leak component. $\square$

*(A double-correction is recorded honestly in §8: in drafting, the standing adversarial drill first "caught" the diagonal as a third carving — by checking only the base constraint family and forgetting the leak rows; the wrap-up re-read caught the catch. The near-miss is itself informative: the unleaked product kernel has three carvings — one per nontrivial character of $\{0,1\}^2$, tracking $f_1$, $f_2$, and parity — and the leak vector $d$, itself the parity character, kills exactly the parity carve and spares the two it is orthogonal to. The zero-sum condition is character-annihilation; a Fourier-theoretic classification of carvings of product agents looks like a clean small theorem. Observation from one example, not developed; flagged in §7.)*

*ε-reading:* $\delta(P) = \mathrm{TV}(q_{(0,0)} + d,\, q_{(0,0)} - d) = \tfrac12 \lVert 2d \rVert_1 = 4t = 0.25$. At tolerance $\geq 0.25$, $\Omega$ re-enters, all three observables are simultaneously learnable, complementarity dissolves. The construction scales $t$ freely: complementarity with arbitrarily small coupling, and correspondingly thin windows.
