## 0. Verdict in three lines

1. **The conjecture as literally stated is false.** Directed-separation preservation alone does not force isomorphism to $(M_t, G_t)$ — two counterexample families (§2), one of them living inside the framework's own GUC Class 1 showcase.
2. **The failure is structural, not incidental: the update factorization underdetermines the partition, because "purpose" has no free-standing referent in the lifted state.** Every attempted anchor that avoids interventions fails for an identifiable reason (§3), and each failure converts into a named hypothesis.
3. **Relative to a goal-intervention family, uniqueness is a theorem** (§4): the epistemic component of any separation-preserving decomposition satisfying three named hypotheses (R1)–(R3) is canonically isomorphic to $M_t$ — it *is* the quotient of $X_t$ by goal-variation — and the purposeful component is unique up to an explicitly characterized fiber-relabeling gauge. **The counterfactual definition of belief is therefore not one choice among equals; it is the unique completion of the separation condition.** That is a materially stronger paper spine than the naive conjecture being true would have given.

## 1. Formalizing the question — what "decomposition," "preserves directed separation," and "isomorphic" must mean

The conjecture's phrase "structurally isomorphic" was doing undefined work; most of the spike's first hour went to making the statement precise enough to attack. The choices below are the ones under which the question has content; each is argued at its point of use.

**Setting.** The lifted update system of `#form-complete-agent-state`: state space $\mathcal X$, event-indexed update family $F_e : \mathcal X \to \mathcal X$, $F_e(X) = f_X(X, e)$. Stochastic updates are handled in the structural (randomized-function) representation of `#deriv-recursive-update` Attack 5 — $f_X(X, e, \omega)$ with exogenous $\omega$, absorbed into the event index — so every statement below is pathwise. The distributional (kernel-level) version is deliberately left open (§6).

**Definition (separated decomposition).** A pair of maps $\mu : \mathcal X \to \mathcal M'$, $\gamma : \mathcal X \to \mathcal G'$ such that:

- **(D1) product-completeness:** $(\mu, \gamma) : \mathcal X \to \mathcal M' \times \mathcal G'$ is a bijection onto the *product* — the two coordinates jointly exhaust the state and vary independently.
- **(D2) epistemic closure:** there exist maps $\phi_e$ with $\mu(F_e X) = \phi_e(\mu(X))$ for all $X, e$. Equivalently: the equivalence kernel of $\mu$ is a **congruence** of the update system, and $\mathcal M'$ is a factor (quotient) dynamical system whose update references nothing outside $(\mu(X), e)$.
- **(D3) cascade form:** $\gamma(F_e X) = \gamma_e\big(\gamma(X), \mu(F_e X)\big)$ — the purposeful update reads the *updated* epistemic state, matching `#der-directed-separation`'s $f_G(G_{\tau^-}, M_{\tau^+}, e_\tau)$.

Three observations that took real work to get right:

- **The weak purposeful form is free; (D3) is not.** Given (D1)+(D2), $\gamma(F_e X)$ is automatically *some* function of $(\mu(X), \gamma(X), e)$ — bijectivity gives that for nothing. (D3) additionally demands that the $\mu(X)$-dependence factor through the updated value $\phi_e(\mu(X))$. When each $\phi_e$ is injective (epistemically invertible updates — "perfect recall"), (D3) is no restriction; when epistemic updates lose information it genuinely constrains (Lemma 0 in the segment; a concrete breaking example in §5). So the conjecture's content splits into a *quotient-side* condition (D2) and an *ordering-side* condition (D3), and the ordering side is vacuous exactly on the invertible case. This connects (D3) to the orient cascade: the cascade's sequential resolution is what (D3) encodes.
- **(D2) is the entire content of "preserves directed separation."** "A component whose update function doesn't reference purpose" cashes out, in the lifted state, as: doesn't reference the *complementary coordinate*. There is no other candidate meaning available before an independent notion of purpose is supplied — which is the crack the counterexamples walk through.
- **Isomorphism of decompositions** must mean: a pair of bijections $(\alpha : \mathcal M' \to \mathcal M'', \beta)$ commuting with the respective update maps — an isomorphism of factored dynamical systems, not of bare sets. Anything weaker (same cardinalities) is trivially satisfiable; anything stronger (equality of the maps) is trivially refutable by relabeling. This is the definition under which the question is interesting, and the theorem ends up *deriving* the right notion rather than assuming it: same epistemic congruence + fiber bijection intertwining updates.

## 2. The refutation — two counterexample families

**Counterexample A (trivial factor).** $\mu = \mathrm{id}_{\mathcal X}$, $\mathcal G' = \{\ast\}$. (D1) holds; (D2) holds — $f_X$ references no complementary coordinate, because there is none; (D3) holds vacuously. The "epistemic" component is the entire state, purpose included. This is not a trick: it exposes that the separation condition, formalized without an anchor, is satisfied by a decomposition that any reader would reject *on semantic grounds the formalism has not yet supplied*. Objecting "but that component contains purpose" presupposes an independent criterion for purposeful content — which is precisely what must be added as a hypothesis, not assumed as understood.

**Counterexample B (frozen-coordinate migration) — the sharp one.** Take any Part II agent satisfying directed separation whose purposeful substate contains a coordinate $c$ with a goal-blind self-contained update — the simplest case: an objective held fixed over the analysis window, $c_{\tau^+} = c_{\tau^-}$ (a thermostat's setpoint; a commander's standing campaign objective; any agent between objective revisions, which per `#def-strategy-dimension`'s timescale ordering $\nu_M \gg \nu_\Sigma \gg \nu_O$ is the *typical* condition). Write $G = (c, G_{\mathrm{rest}})$ with $\mathcal G = \mathcal C \times \mathcal G_{\mathrm{rest}}$. Then $\big((M, c),\; G_{\mathrm{rest}}\big)$ is a separated decomposition:

- (D1): product structure holds by construction.
- (D2): writing $Y := (M, c)$, the update $Y_{\tau^+} = (f_M(M_{\tau^-}, e), c_{\tau^-})$ is a function of $(Y_{\tau^-}, e)$ alone. Goal-blind, closed.
- (D3): $G_{\mathrm{rest}, \tau^+} = f_{G_{\mathrm{rest}}}\big((c_{\tau^-}, G_{\mathrm{rest}, \tau^-}), M_{\tau^+}, e\big)$, and $c_{\tau^-} = c_{\tau^+}$ is recoverable from the updated epistemic component. ✓

Its epistemic component $\mathcal M \times \mathcal C$ is **not isomorphic** to $\mathcal M$ (strictly more information; no bijection can commute with the updates while collapsing $\mathcal C$, since $c$ is preserved and distinguishes trajectories). The conjecture is refuted — and refuted *inside GUC Class 1*, by the most ordinary agents the framework showcases. A Kalman-filter-plus-LQR agent with a fixed reference is, per the separation condition alone, just as honestly described as "an agent whose beliefs include its reference and whose goals are only its plan revisions."

**What the counterexample means (not a technicality).** Separation is a condition on *information flow through the update*; belief-vs-purpose is a distinction of *semantic role*. A frozen goal has the information-flow signature of a belief — it updates on nothing, references nothing, and is referenced by the purposeful update just as beliefs are. No amount of staring at the update algebra can tell a stored setpoint from a stored map-of-the-world. The distinction lives somewhere else, and the somewhere else is: *what happens under variation of the goal* — which the update algebra of a single agent never exercises. This is the exact sense in which the coupling question carves the partition rather than presupposing it.

**Disposition of canon's cross-cutting counter-case.** The segment's honest hedge — a "relevance-weighted model" $W = w(M, O)$ mixing belief and goal "might be useful without being isomorphic" — now gets an exact classification instead of a hedge. For $(W, G')$ to be a separated decomposition, $W$ must satisfy (D2): $W_{\tau^+}$ a function of $(W_{\tau^-}, e)$. Generically this *fails* (the update of $w(M, O)$ needs $M$ and $O$ separately) — so most cross-cutting decompositions are simply not separation-preserving and were never competitors. The ones that survive closure are exactly those where $w$ is injective and the mixed-in purposeful content is update-frozen — i.e., re-coordinatizations of Counterexample B. Cross-cutting decompositions are thus either non-separated or instances of the frozen-coordinate family; there is no third kind.

## 3. Approaches that failed, and exactly why — each failure becomes a hypothesis

This section is primary content, per the spike doctrine. The order below is the order attempted.

**Approach 1 — prove the conjecture directly from the update factorization.** Killed by §2 within the first serious attempt to write the isomorphism down: the map from an arbitrary separated decomposition back to $(M, G)$ has nothing to grab — the congruence lattice of the update system (Lemma 1 below) contains many separation factors, partially ordered by refinement, with the trivial factor at one end and (typically) many frozen-coordinate variants strictly between it and $M$. *Failure content:* there is no distinguished element in that lattice without external structure. → generates the need for an **anchor**.

**Approach 2 — anchor "epistemic" via prediction-sufficiency, without interventions.** Candidate: define the epistemic component as the minimal factor sufficient for the agent's predictions of future events. Fails twice. (a) *Circularity:* in-framework, predictions are generated *from* $M_t$ (`#form-agent-model` — $M_t$ is "the substrate of prolepsis"); using them to define $M_t$ presupposes the object being carved. (b) *Extensional wrongness in both directions:* a goal-blind but prediction-irrelevant retained coordinate (a dead-reckoning log the policy never consults) belongs to $M_t$ under canon's completeness commitment but would be excluded by prediction-sufficiency; conversely, in agents whose goals genuinely improve prediction of their *own* future observation stream (via action selection), goal content would be *included*. A Myhill–Nerode-style observational quotient (identify states with the same future behavior) fails for the same reason from the other side: behavior is action, action is $\pi(M, G)$, so the behavioral quotient is goal-contaminated by construction. *Failure content:* any anchor built from the agent's single factual trajectory — predictions, behavior, sufficiency — cannot separate the roles, because along the factual trajectory belief and frozen purpose are confounded. The anchor must exercise *counterfactual* goal-variation. → generates **(R2)**, and independently re-derives the paper's thesis.

**Approach 3 — anchor via the policy ("purpose is what only matters through action").** Fails immediately: in a separated agent, $M_t$ *also* affects the world only through $\pi$ — both substates enter the policy (`#der-action-selection`), and the event-update direction cannot distinguish either, since goals may legitimately update on events too ($f_G$ has an $e_\tau$ argument; feasibility-driven objective revision). No asymmetry of the wiring diagram separates the roles. *Failure content:* the $X_t$-internal causal topology is role-symmetric except for the one arrow directed separation names — and §2 shows that arrow underdetermines. Confirms Approach 2's conclusion from a third direction.

**Approach 4 — maximality without an anchor (take the finest congruence).** Congruences are closed under intersection (Lemma 1), so a finest one among any family exists — but the finest congruence overall is equality, whose factor is Counterexample A. Dually, demanding the *coarsest* nontrivial one collapses toward the everything-is-goal decomposition ($\mu$ constant), which satisfies (D1)+(D2)+(D3) symmetrically. *Failure content:* the lattice needs to be cut from both sides — an upper bound on what $\mu$ may contain (no purpose) and a lower bound on what it must contain (all non-purpose). → generates **(R2)** and **(R3)** as a *pair*, and explains why both are needed: they block the two degenerate ends respectively.

**Approach 5 (bookkeeping failure worth recording) — treating injective-but-not-surjective decompositions as admissible.** Without (D1)'s surjectivity-onto-a-product, a "copy trick" manufactures spurious small epistemic components: split $M = (M_1, M_2)$, put $\mu = M_1$ and $\gamma = (M_2, G, \text{copy of } M_1)$, with the copy supplying what (D3) would otherwise be unable to reach. The image is a graph, not a product — the copy coordinate is constrained — so (D1) kills it. *Failure content:* product-completeness is doing real work, not hygiene. → confirms **(R1)**.

## 4. The theorem — canonicity relative to the goal-intervention family

Full derivations in `#deriv-decomposition-uniqueness`; the shape and the checks that mattered, here.

**The anchor.** *[The value-subset indexing $g \in \mathcal G^\dagger \subseteq \mathcal G$ in this paragraph was refuted by independent verification (F1, audit 592047) and repaired — see §8. Full access is unaffected; the text below is preserved as written.]* Assume the goal-intervention family the framework's counterfactual apparatus supplies: operators $\iota_g : \mathcal X \to \mathcal X$, $g \in \mathcal G^\dagger \subseteq \mathcal G$, acting in base coordinates as $\iota_g(m, \gamma) = (m, g)$ — re-initialize the purposeful substate, leave the epistemic substate alone. (Stated with internal operators rather than Pearl's $do(\cdot)$ to keep the segment's dependency set clean; the rung-2 correspondence is Discussion-level.) Let $\sim_{\mathrm{cf}}$ be the equivalence generated by $X \sim \iota_g X$, and $q : \mathcal X \to \mathcal X/\!\sim_{\mathrm{cf}}$ the **goal-variation quotient**.

**Hypotheses on a separated decomposition $(\mu, \gamma)$:**

- **(R1) product-completeness** — (D1) above.
- **(R2) purposeful purity** — $\mu \circ \iota_g = \mu$ for all $g \in \mathcal G^\dagger$: the epistemic component contains nothing that goal-intervention can move.
- **(R3) epistemic completeness** — the induced map $\mathcal X/\!\sim_{\mathrm{cf}} \to \mathcal M'$ is injective: the epistemic component retains everything goal-intervention *cannot* move.

**Theorem (decomposition uniqueness, full intervention access $\mathcal G^\dagger = \mathcal G$; base agent satisfies directed separation).**

1. $\sim_{\mathrm{cf}}$ is a congruence of the update system — the key identity is $F_e \circ \iota_g = \iota_{g'} \circ F_e$ with $g' = f_G(g, f_M(m, e), e)$, checked directly — so the quotient is a separation factor, and its orbits are $\{m\} \times \mathcal G$, giving $\mathcal X/\!\sim_{\mathrm{cf}} \cong \mathcal M$ canonically.
2. Any purity-respecting factor factors through $q$ (constancy on generators ⇒ constancy on orbits).
3. Under (R1)+(R2)+(R3): $\mu = \alpha \circ q$ with $\alpha$ bijective and intertwining the updates. **Every admissible epistemic component is the goal-variation quotient — is $M_t$ — up to canonical isomorphism of factor dynamical systems.**
4. The purposeful component is unique up to **fibered relabelings** $\beta$ with each $\beta_m := \beta(m, \cdot)$ bijective; the sub-family respecting the cascade form (D3) contains all $m$-independent relabelings always, contains *all* fibered relabelings when epistemic updates are invertible, and in general sits strictly between (breaking example in §5). The goal coordinate is a **gauge**, and the theorem characterizes the gauge group rather than pretending it away.

**The unification that made the result feel finished rather than assembled.** *[The restricted-family mechanics in this paragraph inherit the §8 repair: "a restricted family" means a family of goal-fiber maps whose orbits miss $c$, not a target-value subset. The unification itself survives, with condition (C) attached.]* Under full intervention access, Counterexample B is *killed by (R2)*: the frozen coordinate $c$ is intervention-movable ($\iota_g$ can reset the setpoint even though the dynamics never do), so $(M, c)$ is impure. Under a *restricted* family — interventions that cannot reach $c$ — the corollary in the segment says the canonical quotient honestly becomes $\mathcal M \times \mathcal C$: **purpose you cannot vary is, under the counterfactual definition, belief.** The refutation of the naive conjecture and the boundary behavior of the theorem are the *same phenomenon* seen from two sides. This is the exact scoping the FAST paper needs: the belief/intent partition is well-defined *relative to an intervention family*, and its resolution degrades gracefully — coordinate by coordinate — as intervention access narrows. (Convergent with the `spike-partition-isolation-criterion` cycle's independently derived "the contract has to become a parameter before the experiment that would choose it can run": here, the goal has to be a parameter before the partition that depends on it is defined.)

**Separation failure (Class 3).** When the base agent violates directed separation, $\sim_{\mathrm{cf}}$ is not a congruence — goal-intervention changes future *epistemic* trajectories — and no separation factor realizes the goal-variation quotient. The canonical epistemic object is then the quotient by the **congruence closure** $\overline{\sim_{\mathrm{cf}}}$ (exists by meet-closure), which is strictly coarser: motivated reasoning does not merely dirty the belief/goal boundary, it *shrinks the largest belief-like object that can exist at all*. The information gap between $\mathcal X/\!\sim_{\mathrm{cf}}$ and $\mathcal X/\overline{\sim_{\mathrm{cf}}}$ is a structural companion to $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ — architectural where $\kappa$ is distributional. Landed at discussion-grade; a candidate follow-on spike if `03-llm-core/` wants it quantitative.

## 5. Checks run against the derivations (the ones that could have broken it)

- **Commutation identity (Theorem part 1):** $F_e(\iota_g(m, \gamma)) = (f_M(m, e),\, f_G(g, f_M(m, e), e))$ and $\iota_{g'}(F_e(m, \gamma))$ with $g' = f_G(g, f_M(m, e), e)$ agree. Requires $g' \in \mathcal G^\dagger$ — under full access, free; under restricted access this is where reachability-closure enters the corollary. Checked symbol by symbol; this is the theorem's load-bearing computation and it uses directed separation of the *base* agent essentially (with goal-coupled $f_M$ the two sides differ in the first coordinate).
- **(D3) really can break for $m$-dependent gauges under non-invertible epistemics.** Witness: $\mathcal M = \mathcal G = \{0, 1\}$, single event with $f_M \equiv 0$ (total epistemic collapse), $f_G(g, m^+, e) = g$. Gauge $\beta(m, g) = g \oplus m$. Transported purposeful update must produce $g$ from $(g \oplus m,\, m^+ = 0,\, e)$ — impossible, $m$ is destroyed. So the gauge group's (D3)-respecting sub-family genuinely depends on epistemic invertibility; the theorem's part 4 is stated with the two attained endpoints and the middle left open, honestly.
- **The XOR gauge is admissible under invertibility** — same construction with $f_M$ a permutation: $m^- = f_M^{-1}(m^+, e)$ recoverable, (D3) satisfied, giving a genuinely cross-cutting-looking coordinate ($G' = G \oplus h(M)$) that is nonetheless *the same decomposition* by the theorem's isomorphism criterion (identical epistemic congruence; fiber relabeling). Confirms the isomorphism notion is neither too strong (it tolerates this) nor too weak (it rejects Counterexample B).
- **Degenerate ends blocked as claimed:** Counterexample A fails (R2) (the identity factor moves under $\iota_g$); the everything-is-goal decomposition ($\mu$ constant) fails (R3) (constant map not injective on the quotient unless $\mathcal M$ is trivial). Each hypothesis is *individually* necessary — dropping any one readmits a named counterexample: (R1) → copy trick; (R2) → Counterexamples A/B; (R3) → all-purposeful. The hypothesis set is minimal in the checkable sense.

## 6. Honest edges — what is *not* established

- **Distributional (kernel-level) version open.** All results are pathwise under the structural representation. A distribution-level analog would route through strong lumpability of the update kernels, and lumpable partitions are not in general closed under common refinement, so Lemma 1's meet-closure argument does not transfer as-is. I did not construct a counterexample first-hand nor find the fix; flagged as the segment's main open line rather than asserted either way.
- **The (D3)-respecting gauge subgroup is characterized only at its endpoints.** The exact condition on $\beta$ for intermediate (partially-invertible) epistemics is open — likely a measurability-with-respect-to-the-surviving-information condition; not derived.
- **The intervention family is a modeling input, not a derived object.** (R2)/(R3) are relative to $\mathcal I = \{\iota_g\}$; the theorem does not tell you which interventions are *physically available* on a given architecture — that is an empirical/architectural question (and for LLMs, exactly the prompt-vs-weights question the paper will have to face). The theorem's honest character mirrors `#deriv-recursive-update`'s C3: (R1) is structural, (R2)/(R3) are the definitional commitment — the partition is not discovered in the update algebra, it is carved by the intervention structure, and the theorem says the carving is then *unique*. That accounting is in the segment's "derived vs chosen" table.
- **Prior-art search not run this cycle.** The machinery is standard and named in the segment (congruence lattices — universal algebra; automata factorization/bisimulation; Markov lumpability; and, closest in spirit, causal abstraction — intervention-commuting maps between SCMs, Rubenstein et al. 2017 / Beckers–Halpern 2019 — and causal feature learning's coarsest-partition constructions, Chalupka–Perona–Eberhardt). Whether the *specific* result — uniqueness of an epistemic/purposeful state factorization relative to a goal-intervention family, with the gauge classification — appears anywhere is unverified; the citations are from training knowledge with primary-source verification queued, marked as such in the segment per the catalog-citation convention. This is the item to discharge before the paper leans on a novelty claim.
