The headline reading: the *four-tier ladder as a classification* is **not novel** as a structural recognition — game-theoretic decomposition and dynamical-systems-on-games literatures have organized this terrain extensively, and the same partition (point attractor / equilibrium set / cyclic-with-distributional-semantics / mean-field) appears across multiple subliteratures under different vocabulary. What *is* novel — to the resolution this search supplies — is (a) the macro-state-type-vs-dynamic-regime structural identity for finite $N$ together with the explicit naming of R3 as the boundary case where it breaks, (b) the regime-transition-cost-asymmetry framing tied to mechanism-design impossibility, and (c) the candidate floor result for regime from marginals. The framework's contribution is therefore *integration vocabulary that names AAT-internal structural identities*, not invention of the classification axis itself.

## Pillar T1 — The four-tier classification

### Target

The exact partition R0 contraction-regime / R1 equilibrium-regime ($\alpha'$) / R2 cyclic-distributional-regime ($\beta'$) / R3 mean-field-equilibrium-regime as a *single classification* unifying control-theoretic Lyapunov regimes, game-theoretic equilibrium classes, and population-scale dynamics, ordered by progressive weakening of Lyapunov certificate. The question is whether the four-tier partition (or an isomorphic one) is recognized as a single classification anywhere in the prior-art landscape.

### Closest prior art

The single tightest neighbor is **Candogan, Menache, Ozdaglar, Parrilo 2011** (*Mathematics of Operations Research* 36:474–503) *Flows and Decompositions of Games: Harmonic and Potential Games*. This paper proves a Hodge-style canonical decomposition: every finite normal-form game decomposes uniquely as a sum of (i) a *potential* component (the cooperative-coordinate, gradient-flow part), (ii) a *harmonic* component (the conflicting-interests, conservative-flow part), (iii) a *nonstrategic* component. The decomposition induces a classification of games by which component dominates: pure potential games (which contain the R1-potential AAT tier exactly), pure harmonic games (where best-response orbits and no equilibrium-Lyapunov exists — the R2 tier when finite), and mixed games. The decomposition is *coordinate-free* and gives a *cleaner* organizing structure than the AAT four-tier ladder for the R1-vs-R2 boundary.

A second tight neighbor is **Letcher, Balduzzi, Racanière, Martens, Foerster, Tuyls, Graepel 2019** *Differentiable Game Mechanics* (JMLR 20). The paper applies a generalized Helmholtz decomposition to game dynamics, separating the joint pseudo-gradient field into (a) a *potential* component (gradient-of-scalar; convergent under gradient descent) and (b) a *Hamiltonian* component (rotational / conservation-law; cyclic under gradient descent). It introduces Symplectic Gradient Adjustment (SGA) and proves SGA converges locally to stable fixed points. The decomposition gives a continuous version of R1-vs-R2 — potential-dominated games converge, Hamiltonian-dominated games orbit. The same dichotomy AAT names tier-wise is named axis-wise (continuous) in Letcher-Balduzzi.

**Sandholm 2010** *Population Games and Evolutionary Dynamics* (MIT Press) gives the closest *book-length* taxonomy of population-game dynamics aligned to the four-tier shape. Sandholm names: (i) **potential games** (global convergence under broad classes of evolutionary dynamics — AAT's R1 potential), (ii) **stable games** (self-defeating-externalities class; Nash set globally asymptotically stable under many evolutionary dynamics — corresponds approximately to AAT's R1 monotone + a chunk of R0), (iii) **games with cycles / chaos** (replicator on rock-paper-scissors variants; explicit non-convergence; AAT's R2), and (iv) treats mean-field / population-scale objects throughout (AAT's R3). The Sandholm classification is *more refined* than AAT's four tiers within R1, but covers the same outer territory. The mapping is approximately: AAT R0 ⊃ Sandholm strict-monotonicity special cases; AAT R1-potential = Sandholm potential games; AAT R1-monotone ≈ Sandholm stable games; AAT R2 = Sandholm cyclic/chaotic games; AAT R3 = Sandholm continuum-population work.

**Hofbauer-Sigmund 1998** *Evolutionary Games and Population Dynamics* (Cambridge UP) is the canonical reference for evolutionary-dynamics regime classification. The book organizes replicator-dynamics regimes by phase-portrait type: ESS-as-global-attractor (AAT R0 / R1), persistent oscillations / heteroclinic cycles (AAT R2), and (in higher dimensions) strange attractors and chaos. The complete classification of three-dimensional replicator phase portraits (Bomze 1983, extended in Hofbauer-Sigmund) is a worked precedent for the kind of dynamics-class taxonomy AAT is attempting.

**Omidshafiei, Tuyls, Czarnecki, Santos, Rowland, Connor, Hennes, Muller, Pérolat, De Vylder, Gruslys, Munos 2019** *$\alpha$-Rank: Multi-Agent Evaluation by Evolution* (*Nature Scientific Reports* 9:9937) makes the explicit move AAT does — replacing Nash-as-fixed-point with a *dynamical* solution concept (Markov-Chain Components / MCC), grounded in Conley's Fundamental Theorem of Dynamical Systems. $\alpha$-Rank's framing — "standard equilibrium-based metrics fail to select among multiple equilibria, scale tractably, and reflect dynamic behaviors such as cycles, intransitivity, or evolutionary stability" — is essentially the framing AAT gives for why R1 / R2 need different machinery from R0. The MCC dynamical-solution-concept is closer to AAT's dynamic-regime axis than to Nash. $\alpha$-Rank does not articulate a *four-tier ladder* with Lyapunov-certificate-strength as the ordering; its contribution is a single dynamical solution concept that works across regimes.

**Papadimitriou-Piliouras 2019** *Game dynamics as the meaning of a game* (*SIGecom Exchanges* 16:53–63) and the broader Piliouras / Mertikopoulos / Papadimitriou line — including Mertikopoulos, Papadimitriou, Piliouras 2018 *Cycles in adversarial regularized learning*, Cheung-Piliouras-Tao 2021 *Online Optimization in Games via Control Theory: Connecting Regret, Passivity and Poincaré Recurrence*, and the broader Poincaré-recurrence-vs-convergence literature — has made the same structural move AAT is making: when dynamics don't converge to a point, the right solution concept is *the recurrent set*, distributional / cyclic. Recurrence-as-outcome is exactly the structural shape of AAT's R2 macro-state.

A recent (2025) entry, **"Characterizing the Convergence of Game Dynamics via Potentialness"** (arXiv:2503.16285), is worth noting as a *continuous* counterpart to AAT's discrete tiers — it introduces *potentialness* as a continuous metric "how close a game is to being potential" and shows it predicts both Nash existence and no-regret convergence. The continuous metric is the natural Letcher-Balduzzi follow-on; it does not subsume the R0 tier (contraction-from-shared-objective is structurally distinct from potential-with-equilibrium) but does subsume the R1-vs-R2 boundary.

### Verdict

**Substantial Overlap with Named Prior Art.** The four-tier classification is recognized in the prior-art literature in several forms — Candogan-Ozdaglar-Parrilo's decomposition (R1-potential vs R2-harmonic axis-wise, exact), Letcher-Balduzzi's Helmholtz decomposition (same with explicit dynamics), Sandholm's population-games taxonomy (covering all four AAT tiers under different vocabulary, more refined within R1), Hofbauer-Sigmund's evolutionary-dynamics regime classification (covering R0 / R1 / R2 phase-portrait-wise), $\alpha$-Rank's MCC-as-dynamical-solution-concept move (the dynamical-solution-concept shift AAT is making), Papadimitriou-Piliouras's recurrence-as-outcome (the R2 macro-state framing). The *four-tier partition as such* — exactly that partition, ordered exactly that way — does not appear under a single name, but every adjacent piece of it is named in the literature with substantial coverage.

**Confidence: High.** The Candogan-Ozdaglar-Parrilo and Letcher-Balduzzi decompositions are well-known foundational results that any review of multi-agent-dynamics classification reaches; Sandholm's book is the canonical reference for population-game-dynamics taxonomy and is on most adjacent-literature shortlists; the Piliouras / Mertikopoulos / Papadimitriou line is the modern multi-agent-learning dynamics-classification literature. The search has high recall on this question.

### Defense strategy

- **Do not claim the four-tier classification as novel.** Cite Candogan-Ozdaglar-Parrilo 2011 for the potential / harmonic / nonstrategic decomposition; Letcher-Balduzzi 2019 for the potential / Hamiltonian dynamics decomposition; Sandholm 2010 for the potential / stable / cyclic taxonomy of population games; Hofbauer-Sigmund 1998 for the replicator-dynamics regime classification; Omidshafiei et al. 2019 for the dynamical-solution-concept move; Papadimitriou-Piliouras 2019 for recurrence-as-outcome.
- **Frame AAT's contribution narrowly.** AAT's increment over the cited prior art is: (a) explicit *Lyapunov-certificate-strength* as the ordering principle across all four tiers; (b) inclusion of R0 (contraction-from-shared-objective) as a distinct tier upstream of R1, which the game-theory-only literatures do not foreground because they typically start with strategic interaction as given; (c) integration with the rest of the AAT machinery (scope routes C-i through C-iv, the persistence template, the bridge lemma); (d) framework-internal naming consistent with `#disc-separability-pattern`'s ladder shape (separable core / structured repair / general open) and with the GUC class typology.
- **Acknowledge the prior-art reading honestly.** If the AAT segment frames this as the *consolidation* of an axis the framework was running implicitly across ten+ segments (per §F of `03-DYNAMIC-REGIME-AXIS.md`), with the prior-art neighbors cited inline at the points where the consolidation overlaps them, the segment is defensible. If it frames the four-tier ladder as a novel classification, it overclaims and a reviewer with any of the cited references in hand will press immediately.
- **Note one genuine refinement.** AAT's R0 / R1 distinction is *not* equivalent to the potential / harmonic axis. The R0 tier is *contraction from objective-alignment* — a single shared target the composite attracts toward; this is structurally upstream of "what dynamics-class does the game-with-fixed-objectives admit?" The R1 / R2 / R3 tiers are the game-theoretic literature; R0 is the engineering-control / shared-objective-team literature. AAT's contribution is putting these two into one axis under the *Lyapunov-certificate-strength* ordering. Whether that contribution carries weight depends on what a reviewer thinks of the upstream-of-game-theory framing — defensible but contestable.

## Pillar T2 — Macro-state-type / regime structural identity for finite $N$

### Target

The structural identity from §C.1 of `03-DYNAMIC-REGIME-AXIS.md`: for finite-$N$ composites (tiers R0 / R1 / R2), the *macro-state type* (state-variable / fixed-point object / distributional object) is structurally identified by the *dynamic regime*, with mean-field (R3) as the explicit boundary case where the identity breaks. The claim has the form $\mathcal R(X^c) \Leftrightarrow$ type-of-$G^c_\ast$, derived from the regime entry conditions.

### Closest prior art

The structural correspondence is *implicit* across several literatures but does not appear stated as a single identity.

**Hart-Mas-Colell 2000** (*Econometrica* 68:1127–1150) *A Simple Adaptive Procedure Leading to Correlated Equilibrium* establishes that under no-regret learning in finite games, the *empirical joint distribution* converges to the correlated equilibrium set (and at the coarser CCE level, the result is broader and earlier). The R2 macro-state type — *distributional object on $\Delta(\mathcal X^c)$* — is precisely Hart-Mas-Colell's natural object. The identity *empirical distribution converges $\Leftrightarrow$ no pure attractor exists* is implicit but not stated as one half of a regime-vs-macro-state-type structural identity.

**Lasry-Lions 2007** *Mean Field Games* (*Japanese Journal of Mathematics* 2:229–260) and **Carmona-Delarue 2018** *Probabilistic Theory of Mean Field Games with Applications* (Springer; 2 volumes) supply the R3 measure-space setting. In MFG, the macro-state *is* a measure on the agent state space, and the dynamics are on the measure space; this is the distributional object as a *fundamental* macro-state rather than as the empirical distribution of finite-$N$ play. The Lasry-Lions distinction — finite-$N$ vs mean-field limit — is the same distinction §C.1 names as the boundary case where the macro-state-type / regime identity breaks. The literature treats this as a *limit relationship* (finite-$N$ → MFG via propagation-of-chaos) rather than as a *structural identity that breaks at the limit*.

**Omidshafiei et al. 2019** $\alpha$-Rank treats the macro-state of a non-converging game as the recurrent set (Markov-Chain Component / MCC), explicitly noting that "standard equilibrium-based metrics fail to … reflect dynamic behaviors such as cycles, intransitivity, or evolutionary stability." The MCC is a distributional object; the framing is "when no point-attractor exists, take the dynamical-solution-concept." This is the implicit content of one direction of the §C.1 identity (R2 ⟹ distributional macro-state).

**Givan-Dean-Greig 2003** *Equivalence notions and model minimization in Markov decision processes* (*Artificial Intelligence* 147:163–223) and the broader bisimulation / lumpability literature (Buchholz 1994; Tian-Kannan 2006; the Pappas line on consistent abstractions) supply the language for "when does the aggregate variable count as a macro-state at all?" These are about *valid coarse-graining*, which is upstream of the §C.1 question — they establish when a macro-variable carries autonomous dynamics, but do not connect macro-state-*type* (state-variable vs fixed-point object vs distributional object) to the dynamic regime of the underlying interaction.

The cleanest summary: the *components* of the §C.1 identity are present across the prior art (Hart-Mas-Colell for the R2-side direction, MFG for the R3-boundary observation, Omidshafiei for the no-attractor-implies-distributional-macro-state move), but the *identity itself* — as a structural correspondence stated for all three finite-$N$ tiers plus the explicit boundary at R3 — does not appear stated this way.

### Verdict

**Substantial Novelty with Adjacent Literature.** Each direction of the §C.1 identity has prior-art support — R0 ⟺ state-variable is implicit in the contraction-Lyapunov literature; R1 ⟺ fixed-point object is implicit in the Nash-equilibrium-as-game-solution-concept; R2 ⟺ distributional object is the explicit content of Hart-Mas-Colell and $\alpha$-Rank; R3 as the boundary case is the standard MFG framing. The *unification* of these into a single structural identity, with the boundary explicitly named, is a presentation novelty rather than a derivation novelty. The identity itself is a definitional consequence of the regime entry conditions (it's true by the way the tiers are defined), but stating it as a result — and naming R3 as exactly where the identity breaks — does appear to be a framework-internal contribution.

**Confidence: Medium-High.** The literatures cited (Hart-Mas-Colell, MFG, $\alpha$-Rank, bisimulation) are well-covered by the search; a targeted Undermind-style sweep would likely confirm with higher confidence and might surface a closer formulation in the recent (post-2020) MFG-meets-$\alpha$-Rank work.

### Defense strategy

- Frame the identity as *consolidation*, not derivation. The identity is a structural restatement of what each tier's entry condition implies about the macro-state object; the contribution is making the across-tier consistency *legible* — particularly the R3 boundary, which exists in the MFG literature but is not typically framed as "the case where macro-state-type and regime decouple."
- Cite Hart-Mas-Colell 2000 for the R2 ⟺ distributional-macro-state direction; cite Lasry-Lions 2007 + Carmona-Delarue 2018 for the R3 boundary; cite Omidshafiei et al. 2019 for the dynamical-solution-concept move that makes the distributional macro-state legible as a macro-state-type rather than as an empirical-distribution side-effect.
- The "boundary" framing is genuinely useful — most MFG work treats the finite-$N$-to-MFG passage as a *convergence* result (the finite-$N$ dynamics approach the MFG dynamics) rather than as a *structural break* (the macro-state-type / dynamic-regime identity holds for all finite $N$ and fails only at the limit). This is a small but real reframing the segment can claim.

## Pillar T3 — Regime-transition asymmetry no-go

### Target

The asymmetry-of-cost result from §C.3 of `03-DYNAMIC-REGIME-AXIS.md`: self-driven *descent* on the regime ladder (R0 → R1, R1 → R2) is the default under generic perturbation of the objective structure; self-driven *ascent* (R1 → R0, R2 → R1) requires explicit structural commitment that costs more than the descent did to acquire. The §C.3 framing: descent is "downhill" under entropy / adversarial pressure, ascent is "uphill" requiring structural augmentation (mechanism design, symbiogenic absorption, hierarchical-derivation imposition).

### Closest prior art

The cost-asymmetry framing has multiple adjacent neighbors but no exact match.

**Mechanism-design impossibility theorems** are the closest cost-of-ascent neighbors. **Gibbard 1973** *Manipulation of Voting Schemes: A General Result* (*Econometrica* 41:587–601) and **Satterthwaite 1975** *Strategy-Proofness and Arrow's Conditions* (*Journal of Economic Theory* 10:187–217) establish that for any non-dictatorial voting rule over three or more alternatives, there exist preference profiles where some agent gains from misreporting — there is no mechanism that simultaneously achieves universal domain, non-dictatorship, and strategy-proofness. **Myerson-Satterthwaite 1983** *Efficient Mechanisms for Bilateral Trading* (*Journal of Economic Theory* 29:265–281) establishes that no incentive-compatible, individually rational, balanced-budget mechanism can achieve ex-post Pareto-efficient bilateral trade under two-sided private information. **Arrow 1951** *Social Choice and Individual Values* (Wiley) — the impossibility of universally satisfactory social-welfare functions — is the foundational ancestor. These results are structurally upstream of "ascent on the regime ladder requires structural commitment that mechanism design cannot universally supply." AAT's §C.3 names mechanism-design impossibility as the cost-of-ascent floor for R2 → R1 explicitly.

**Price-of-stability** (Anshelevich, Dasgupta, Kleinberg, Tardos, Wexler, Roughgarden 2008, *SIAM Journal on Computing* 38:1602–1623, *The Price of Stability for Network Design with Fair Cost Allocation*) measures the ratio of the cost of the *best* Nash equilibrium to the cost of the social optimum. This is *not* the same as the cost-of-ascent — price of stability measures the equilibrium quality gap, not the cost of moving a game into a regime where good equilibria exist. But it is the natural game-theory-economics cousin of the cost-of-ascent question, and any defense of the §C.3 no-go should distinguish itself from price-of-stability explicitly.

**Sandholm's "stable games" literature** and the broader work on perturbation robustness of equilibria addresses the descent direction — what perturbations destabilize an attractor. Hofbauer-Sigmund's chapter on *Permanence* organizes resistance-to-small-disturbances; replicator-dynamics analysis of evolutionary stability classifies which mixed equilibria survive small perturbations (ESS theory). The descent direction of §C.3 — "alignment is fragile, potential-structure is fragile, both can be destroyed by local perturbations" — is consistent with this literature but is *not* a derived no-go there; it's a stylized observation about which equilibria are perturbation-robust.

**Mertikopoulos, Papadimitriou, Piliouras 2018** *Cycles in adversarial regularized learning* (*SODA*) and related work in the Piliouras / Mertikopoulos line establish concrete instances where small parameter perturbations push games from convergent into cyclic / chaotic dynamics. The R1 → R2 descent under adversarial pressure has explicit empirical instances in this literature; the *general framing* as a no-go does not appear stated.

**Major evolutionary transitions / symbiogenesis** (Maynard Smith-Szathmáry 1995 *The Major Transitions in Evolution*, Margulis 1970 *Origin of Eukaryotic Cells*) supply the biological precedent for the R1 → R0 ascent mechanism (symbiogenic absorption per AAT `#hyp-symbiogenic-composition`). These are descriptive, not derived-cost results.

The §C.3 framing as an *asymmetry-of-cost-on-a-regime-ladder no-go* with mechanism-design impossibility on the ascent side does not appear stated this way in the searched prior art. The pieces — descent under adversarial perturbation is documented; mechanism-design impossibility for the ascent direction is foundational; price-of-stability addresses an adjacent question — are present but not assembled into a regime-transition-asymmetry result.

### Verdict

**Substantial Novelty with Adjacent Literature.** The descent direction is implicit in the perturbation-robustness literature (Sandholm, Hofbauer-Sigmund, Mertikopoulos et al.); the ascent direction has mechanism-design impossibility as a tight cost floor for one specific transition (R2 → R1 via mechanism augmentation); the structural cost-asymmetry framing across the full regime ladder does not appear assembled this way. §C.3's contribution is the *assembly* — naming descent-default / ascent-costly as a structural property of the regime ladder, citing the mechanism-design impossibility theorems as the ascent-cost floor instance.

**Confidence: Medium.** The mechanism-design impossibility line is well-covered and tight. The descent-default direction has more diffuse prior art and a more focused literature search (potentially with social-choice / institutional-economics neighbors not surveyed here) could surface tighter matches. A second-pass Undermind search recommended before any standalone-paper trajectory.

### Defense strategy

- Frame the asymmetry as a *structural-cost observation* with named cost-floor instances on the ascent side (Gibbard-Satterthwaite for R2 → R1 via voting-mechanism design; Myerson-Satterthwaite for R2 → R1 via market-mechanism design; Arrow for the underlying social-welfare-function impossibility) and *default-direction observation* on the descent side (perturbation-of-objectives destabilizes alignment; perturbation-of-payoffs destabilizes potential / monotone structure).
- Distinguish from price-of-stability explicitly: price-of-stability measures equilibrium-quality gap *within a game*; AAT's cost-asymmetry measures the cost of *changing the game's regime*. Different objects.
- Acknowledge that the descent-default direction is less formally established than the ascent-cost direction; cite perturbation-of-stable-games / replicator-dynamics-cycle-onset / Mertikopoulos-cycle-instances as the available adjacent evidence, with §C.3's framing as a *structural conjecture* about descent-default that ties together those instances.
- The strongest standalone framing would tie the cost-asymmetry to a specific cost-of-rewiring metric (something like "structural-Hamming-distance from the current game to a game in the target regime"), which the spike does not currently develop. If pursued, this is where the strongest standalone novelty would land — and is a separate spike, not a single-cycle deliverable.

## Pillar T4 — Identifiability floor for regime from marginals

### Target

The candidate identifiability-floor instance from §H.5 of `03-DYNAMIC-REGIME-AXIS.md`: from component-only data (sub-agent marginals), the composite's regime is in general unidentifiable without composite-level coupling-topology observation. The bit that distinguishes cooperative-regime composites from adversarial-regime composites is specifically the kind of bit Liberzon 2003 establishes is unidentifiable for switched-system coupling.

### Closest prior art

The cleanest structural cousin is **Liberzon 2003** *Switching in Systems and Control* (Birkhäuser / Systems & Control: Foundations & Applications). The book establishes that a switched system formed from a family of asymptotically stable subsystems can be unstable depending on the switching signal, and that the *coupling structure* (which subsystem is active when, equivalently the sign of the coupling between subsystem state and switching law) cannot be recovered from subsystem-marginal data alone — observation of the switching signal or its correlates is structurally required. This is the structural shape AAT's `#disc-identifiability-floor` Instance 3 already cites; the §H.5 candidate floor is a *generalization* of the same shape from "sign-of-coupling bit" to "regime-augmentation bit" (alignment vs strategic, potential-existence vs not).

**Inverse game theory** is the closest *positive* line — what can be identified from observed trajectories of multi-agent play. **Kuleshov-Schrijvers 2015** *Inverse Game Theory* (*Web and Internet Economics / ESEA Workshop on Algorithmic Game Theory*) and related work in the Stanford and CMU lines (Vorobeychik, Stengel, Ling, Pan) establish positive identification programs for game parameters from observed Nash-equilibrium play. **Yu, Davis et al. 2025** *Inverse Noncooperative Games With Indistinguishable Observations* (*IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control* 70:6513–) addresses LQ-game parameter reconstruction from permuted observations. These literatures address the *positive* question (what *can* be identified) from full-trajectory data; they do not address the *negative* question §H.5 names (what *cannot* be identified from component-only marginal data).

**Inverse reinforcement learning** literature (Ng-Russell 2000 *Algorithms for Inverse Reinforcement Learning*; Abbeel-Ng 2004 *Apprenticeship Learning via Inverse Reinforcement Learning*; the IRL identifiability sub-literature including Cao-Cohen-Szpruch 2021 *Identifiability in Inverse Reinforcement Learning*) addresses reward / objective recovery from observed agent behavior. Same direction as inverse game theory — positive identification from trajectories, not negative floor results from marginals.

**Pearl-lineage causal-structure-from-observations** literature (Pearl 2009 *Causality*; Spirtes-Glymour-Scheines 2000 *Causation, Prediction, and Search*) addresses identifiability of causal structure from observational data, including the equivalence-class characterization of Markov-equivalent DAGs. The structural shape — "what features of the underlying structure are recoverable from a class of observations, and what features are not" — is the same shape as AAT's identifiability-floor pattern. But this literature is about DAG-structure-from-observations, not game-regime-from-component-marginals.

A direct match — *the game regime / dynamic-regime classification is unidentifiable from component-only data* as a stated no-go — does not appear in the searched prior art. The closest formal cousin remains Liberzon's switched-system coupling-bit result; the inverse-game-theory literature attempts a *positive* program over the trajectory-observable territory that bypasses the §H.5 setting (which is specifically component-marginal-only).

### Verdict

**Substantial Novelty with Adjacent Literature.** The structural shape (unidentifiability of a coupling-or-regime bit from component marginals) has tight precedent in Liberzon's switched-system work — which is *already* cited in `#disc-identifiability-floor` as the basis for Instance 3 — and the positive-identification literature (inverse game theory, IRL) addresses adjacent questions about what *can* be identified from full-trajectory data. The specific §H.5 claim — regime identification from component-only marginal data is structurally impossible without composite-level coupling-topology observation — does not appear stated as a no-go in the searched prior art.

**Confidence: Medium.** This is the most under-searched of the four targets and the verdict could shift with a targeted Undermind search of the inverse-game-theory + system-identification + identifiability-in-multi-agent-systems intersection. A second-pass search before any landing-as-instance-6 decision is recommended.

### Defense strategy

- Frame the §H.5 candidate as Instance 6 in the `#disc-identifiability-floor` series, following the same shape as Instance 3 (Liberzon 2003 coupling-bit, already cited). Cite Liberzon 2003 directly for the structural-cousin result; cite the inverse-game-theory literature (Kuleshov-Schrijvers 2015; Yu et al. 2025) to mark the boundary of the positive identification program and explain why the negative §H.5 result lives outside that boundary (the §H.5 setting is component-marginal-only; inverse game theory uses full trajectories).
