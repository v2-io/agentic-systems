## Bottom line

**The candidate is established material, stated in three places spanning two research traditions (Dawid and Robins's decision-theoretic statistics; Bareinboim and Pearl's causal RL, which contributes two distinct lines), and none of the three statements depends on the others.** I found no paper that states the learning-agent version in the requester's exact packaging (decision state, own-action data, three named failures), but each ingredient is stated, and in places nearly verbatim:

1. **Dawid and Didelez 2010, §5.2.1** (Robins's "sequential randomization / no unmeasured confounding / sequential ignorability" in decision-theoretic dress): the condition holds "when S's information base contains all the information the doctor D has used in choosing the $(A_i)$", and fails when the chooser "chooses the $(A_i)$ on the basis of private information not represented in S's information base, possibly associated with $L_i$", which "is often described as (potential) confounding." That is the candidate and failure (ii) in one paragraph. **Dawid and Constantinou 2014** then make it a definition: for a control strategy, $A_i \perp U^i \mid (L^i, A^{i-1})$ holds by definition, and "this condition will hold under a control strategy."
2. **Bareinboim, Zhang, and Lee, June 2026** (arXiv 2606.24160, a survey; the most recent consolidated statement): the "no unmeasured confounder" (NUC) condition has two parts, (1) the action's endogenous parents are contained in the history the policy sees and (2) the action's exogenous parents are independent noise; **Lemma 1 ("Experimental NUC")** says NUC holds in the submodel induced by *any* deployed policy $do(\pi)$, "by construction" in the requester's phrase; **Corollary 2** says NUC implies the sequential backdoor condition (Pearl and Robins 1995). Example 26 is failure (i); the positivity assumption in Theorem 2 and its footnote 24 is failure (iii). This survey is dated 23 June 2026, which is inside the window the requester's training may under-represent.
3. **The Bareinboim intent line** (Bareinboim, Forney, Pearl 2015; Forney, Pearl, Bareinboim 2017; the 2026 survey §7): the learner's *intent* (the output of its decision function before execution, a function of its observed inputs and of the unobserved confounder) is the conditioning variable that "blocks" the backdoor paths from the realized action to the reward, provided the learner then randomizes *within* each intent. In the 2017 model the policy is $f_\pi(h_t, i_t)$, so the parents of the executed action are exactly (history, intent). That is the parents-of-treatment adjustment with the decision state as the parent set, though the papers do not use that name.

**What I did not find:** (a) a source that treats an LLM-style agent's hidden activations as a confounder of its own action-outcome pairs (item 3 below; the closest conceptual match is Ortega et al. 2021, which predates the question but is explicitly motivated by language-model sequence models); (b) any source using the phrase "ignorability by design"; the nearest vocabulary is "sequential randomization", "stability", "experimental NUC", "control strategy"; (c) any treatment of introspective access as a *named* identification condition. The 2017 and 2026 intent papers come closest: they require that the agent can *perceive its intent* and say the agent need not identify the confounder itself.

**Three places where the candidate as worded is stronger than what the sources support**, offered as things to check, not as objections (details in "Adjacent observations"):

- "exactly when" is an if-and-only-if only for the estimand "effect conditional on the full decision state". A coarser conditioning set can still be a valid backdoor set (2026 survey Example 34, "Sequential Backdoor does not imply NUC"), and the graph-theoretic iff for adjustment sets is a separate, older result (Shpitser, VanderWeele, Robins 2010; M).
- Failure (ii) is milder than "un-introspectable causes": Dawid and Didelez's wording is "private information ... possibly associated with $L_i$". An unlogged cause confounds only if it carries information about outcome-relevant variables beyond the logged history.
- Failure (iii): under a deterministic policy the policy's *own* value is still identified (by consistency); what is lost is every contrast with an unplayed action.

## Item 1: learning agents learning from their own actions

### 1.1 Bareinboim, Zhang, Lee (2026), the causal RL survey

Bareinboim, E., Zhang, J., Lee, S. "An Introduction to Causal Reinforcement Learning." arXiv:2606.24160v1 [cs.AI], 23 Jun 2026 (JMLR-format monograph; I did not see a published version). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24160 (PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24160).

What it claims, on exactly the requested point:

- Definition 13 (§4.1, p. 45), "No Unmeasured Confounder" for a policy space $\Pi = \{\langle X_i, S_i \rangle\}_{i=1}^H$: for every action $X_i$, "Endogenous parents $PA_i$ are contained in the history $\bar{X}_{i-1} \cup \bar{S}_i$" and "Exogenous parents $U_i$ are independent from exogenous noises $U_j$ associated with all the other endogenous variables $V_j$ in the system." The authors gloss: "Condition 2 says that given the past history $\bar{X}_{i-1} \cup \bar{S}_i$, values of every action $X_i$ are decided by an independent noise $U_i$." This is the requester's "decision state plus environment-independent randomization".
- Lemma 1, "Experimental NUC" (§4.2, p. 51): "For any policy $\pi \in \Pi$, the NUC condition (Def. 13) holds in $\langle M^*_\pi, \Pi, R \rangle$ induced by intervention $do(\pi)$", justified by "in the submodel $M^*_\pi$ induced by intervention $do(\pi)$, all input covariates $S_i$ affecting every action $X_i$ ... are observed and measured." §4.3 opens: "Online learning algorithms ensure the NUC condition (Def. 13) holds by deploying candidate policies in the environment (Lem. 1)." This is the formal "by construction" statement for an agent running its own policy.
- Definition 15 and Corollary 2 (§4.3, pp. 63-64): the sequential backdoor condition of Pearl and Robins 1995 is implied by NUC. Their gloss: the history "effectively summarize[s] all the information that the behavior policy uses to determine values of action $X_i$, which are also relevant to the reward signal $Y$." And: "Whenever the NUC condition (Def. 13) holds ... the variables $\bar{X}_{i-1}, \bar{S}_i$ contain all direct parents of action $X_i$. There is no unobserved confounder affecting $X_i$ and any other variable in the environment. Conditioning on the actions and states' history ... thus 'blocks' all backdoor path from action node $X_i$ to reward nodes $Y$."
- Example 26 (p. 46) is failure (i) in the survey's own terms: a policy space $\Pi' = \{\langle X_1, \emptyset \rangle, \langle X_2, \emptyset \rangle\}$ "decides values of treatment ... regardless of values of other variables"; the history omits the endogenous parent $S_i$, so "Condition (1) of NUC fails." The same example gives a version of failure (ii): a structural coefficient of the exogenous $U$ in $f_{X_i}$ makes "an unobserved confounder affecting treatments $X_1, X_2$ and the primary outcome $Y$ simultaneously", so Condition 2 fails.
- Example 34 (p. 64): "Sequential Backdoor $\not\Rightarrow$ NUC." NUC is sufficient for backdoor admissibility, not necessary.
- §7.1 (pp. 119-124) is the intent line (see 1.2): conditioning on the intended action $X'$ "'blocks' the backdoor paths from the realized action $X$ to the subsequent reward $Y$" (p. 124), and footnote 58: the x-specific effect $E[Y_x \mid x']$ "is not computable from any combination of passive observations and controlled experiments, without the detailed parametrization or additional assumptions", hence intent-specific randomization ("Ctf-RCT", Algorithm 8, §7.2, pp. 135-136). Footnote 59: "whenever the agent pursues an interventional (layer 2) strategy by leveraging Fisherian randomization, the entire deliberation process is bypassed."
- Appendix A (pp. 181-182) contrasts POMDPs with MDPs for the causal hierarchy; I skimmed it only.

Where it does not match: the policy's input set $S_i$ is a given modeling object; the survey never asks what the learner's own *internal* state is, never discusses an agent that cannot read its own inputs, and a keyword search for "LLM" / "large language" returned nothing. It also treats NUC as a property of a policy class, not of a learning loop.

Read: **P** (extracted text; §4.1-4.3, §7.1-7.2 and footnotes 24, 58, 59 read closely; remainder keyword-searched).

### 1.2 Forney, Pearl, Bareinboim (2017), counterfactual data-fusion

Forney, A., Pearl, J., Bareinboim, E. "Counterfactual Data-Fusion for Online Reinforcement Learners." Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Machine Learning, PMLR 70:1156-1164, 2017. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/forney17a.html; technical report R-26 (June 2017, the version I read): https://www.causalai.net/r26.pdf.

What it claims:

- Definition 3.2 ("Intent"): for an action $X$ "(naturally) determined by the structural equation $f_x(PA_x, U_x)$ ... After realization $PA_x = pa_x$ and $U_x = u_x$ (without any external intervention), we say that the output of the structural function given the current configuration of all UCs is the agent's intent, $I = f_x(pa_x, u_x)$." Intent "can be seen as an agent's chosen action before its execution, which, in fact, is a proxy for any influencing UCs." Footnote 3: "Def. 3.2 does not require that all its influencing factors be measured or acknowledged by the agent."
- Definition 3.3 and Figure 2: the policy is $\pi_t = f_\pi(h_t, i_t)$, a function of the history and the current intent; the unobserved $u_t$ decides $i_t$. In the experimental regime the policy "ignores [intent] and listens instead to a random device" (footnote 4). So the parents of the executed action are the history and the intent, and the confounder reaches the action only through the intent.
- Theorem 4.1: "The counterfactual ETT is empirically estimable for arbitrary action-choice dimension (i.e., $|X| = k$ for $k \geq 2$) when agents condition on their intent $I = i$ and estimate the response $Y$ to their final action choice $X = a$." Footnote 5: the ETT "can be computed for binary decisions or when the backdoor criterion holds ... but it was not believed to be estimable for arbitrary dimensions prior to RDC-randomization." The proof is in a supplementary appendix I did not see.
- Operationally (§4-5): at each round the agent "observe[s] the intent $i_t$", chooses its arm using intent-specific estimates, and records the outcome in a table indexed by (arm, intent). The procedure is randomization within intent strata.

Where it does not match: the identified quantity is the effect of the *treatment on the treated* given intent (the regret decision criterion), not a general decision-state-conditional effect, and positivity within intent is manufactured by the agent's randomization rather than assumed. The paper never names the parents-of-treatment adjustment.

Read: **P** (§1-6 read; appendix not available in the file).

### 1.3 Bareinboim, Forney, Pearl (2015), bandits with unobserved confounders

Bareinboim, E., Forney, A., Pearl, J. "Bandits with Unobserved Confounders: A Causal Approach." Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 28 (NIPS 2015), pp. 1342-1350. https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/hash/795c7a7a5ec6b460ec00c5841019b9e9-Abstract.html (PDF: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2015/file/795c7a7a5ec6b460ec00c5841019b9e9-Paper.pdf).

What it claims:

- Definition 3.2 (MABUC): the arm $X_t$ is "decided by Nature in the observational case, and $do(X_t = \pi(x_0, y_0, \ldots, x_{t-1}, y_{t-1}))$ for strategy $\pi$ in the experimental case (i.e., when the strategy decides the choice)." So a strategy that is a function of the logged history gives interventional data; confounding enters only through the unobserved $U_t$ that sets the "natural predilection".
- Footnote 2: "unconfoundedness is (implicitly) assumed not to hold in the general case. Otherwise, the equality between observational and experimental distributions would imply that no randomization of the action needs to be carried out." Footnote 3: "The interventional nature of the MAB problem is virtually not discussed in the literature, one of the few exceptions is the causal interpretation of Thompson Sampling established in [OB10]" (that is Ortega and Braun 2010, entry 1.5).
- The regret decision criterion conditions on the player's predilection; footnote 5: "not all variables are equally legitimate for confounding control in causal settings, while the agent's predilection is certainly one of such variables in our setup", contrasted with treating predilection as a contextual-bandit context.

Where it does not match: bandit setting only, one-shot rounds, the confounder is a per-round $U_t$. No statement of the general decision-state adjustment.

Read: **P** (§2-5, footnotes 2-5, simulations skimmed).

### 1.4 Zhang and Bareinboim (2016), MDPs with unobserved confounders

Zhang, J., Bareinboim, E. "Markov Decision Processes with Unobserved Confounders: A Causal Approach." Purdue AI Lab Technical Report R-23, 19 September 2016 (cited as such in the 2017 paper; I did not establish whether a refereed version of this exact report exists). https://causalai.net/mdp-causal.pdf.

What it claims:

- "If all variables affecting the agent's decisions and rewards (confounders) are recorded ... it would be possible to control for all the biases and the problem would reduce to standard MDP learning. In practice, however, admitting the existence of UCs is the most relaxed scenario since, alternatively, the modeler would need to know a priori all factors that make up the agent's decision, which is a strong requirement in many settings."
- Footnote 6: "It is usual the case that an intricate combination of factors lead to a decision in settings involving humans. In practice, however, these factors are not always fully known or easily articulable by the decision maker herself, which engender the MDPUC problem." This is failure (ii) in the requester's words ("un-introspectable causes of one's own action").
- "POMDPs do not necessarily imply the confounding": partial observability alone does not make an unobserved confounder. "POMDPs and MDPUCs are complementary and cover orthogonal dimensions of the modeling space." This is the nearest explicit statement I found that on-policy confounding needs an unlogged cause of the action, not merely partial observability.
- Counterfactual policies take the *augmented state* $s' = (s, x')$, state plus intuition, and "we can leverage state-of-art MDP algorithms to learn an optimal counterfactual policy ... by simply operating on the augmented state encompassing both state and intuition."

Where it does not match: the abstract says the method leverages observational data to enhance standard MDP algorithms; I did not establish exactly what generates that data. The augmented-state device is the sequential analog of the decision-state idea but it is introduced for the intent-conditioned policy class, not as a general identification result.

Read: **P** (introduction, §"Counterfactual Policies", theorems 1-3 statements; appendix not read).

### 1.5 Ortega and Braun (2010), minimum relative entropy

Ortega, P. A., Braun, D. A. "A Minimum Relative Entropy Principle for Learning and Acting." Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research 38:475-511, 2010 (doi:10.1613/jair.3062; first posted as arXiv:0810.3605, October 2008; I read v3, April 2010). https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.3605 ; https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10659.

What it claims: the adaptive-control problem is posed as minimizing relative entropy to the environment-specific expert; "if the agent is active, then its past actions need to be treated as causal interventions on the I/O stream rather than normal probability conditions" (abstract). The causal model has "each symbol depend[ing] functionally on the environment and all the previously generated symbols" (§3.3). Of the resulting Bayesian control rule: "P updates the weights $v_m$ only whenever a new observation $o_t$ is produced by the environment. The update follows Bayes' rule but treats past actions as interventions by dropping the evidence they provide" (§3.3 discussion). §3.2 motivates this with a statistician who samples $x_2$ from her own predictive distribution and then conditions on it. §6.1 notes that choosing a policy is a Dirac delta, so $\Pr(\omega \mid \pi) = \Pr(\omega \mid \hat{\pi})$ and "the formalization of actions as interventions and observations as conditions is perfectly compatible with the decision-theoretic setup."

Where it does not match: the paper never uses the word "confound", and keyword searches for "unobserv", "hidden", "latent", "common cause" found nothing. The intervention is justified by the agent's *own* mixture-of-experts update, not by identification of an effect from logged data. Ortega and Braun is the earliest source I found for "own actions are interventions on the I/O stream"; Bareinboim et al. 2015 cite it as one of the few treatments of the interventional nature of bandits.

Read: **P-part** (abstract, §1-3, §6.1, keyword search of the whole text; the convergence proof and appendices not read).

### 1.6 Ortega et al. (2021), delusions in sequence models

Ortega, P. A., Kunesch, M., Delétang, G., Genewein, T., Grau-Moya, J., Veness, J., Buchli, J., Degrave, J., Piot, B., Perolat, J., Everitt, T., Tallec, C., Parisotto, E., Erez, T., Chen, Y., Reed, S., Hutter, M., de Freitas, N., Legg, S. "Shaking the foundations: delusions in sequence models for interaction and control." DeepMind Technical Report 2021-10-22; arXiv:2110.10819. https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10819.

What it claims: a sequence model that samples its own action from the predictive distribution and then conditions on it "takes its own actions as evidence about the world ... due to the presence of confounding variables" (§1). The mechanism is a latent task parameter $\Theta$ that, in the training data, causes the expert's action; the deployed model does not know $\Theta$, so its action carries no information about $\Theta$, but the learned joint distribution treats it as if it did. Remark 3 gives the two posteriors: $P(\theta \mid a, o) \propto P(\theta) P(a \mid \theta) P(o \mid \theta, a)$ versus $P(\theta \mid do(a), o) \propto P(\theta) P(o \mid \theta, a)$, and "the intervention causes the dismissal of the evidence $P(a \mid \theta)$ produced by the self-generated action." Summary: "actions (i.e. data generated by the model) are causal interventions, whilst observations (i.e. data generated externally) are standard Bayesian conditions. This prevents incorrect inferences (=delusions) via hidden confounders. Hence, one cannot learn from one's own actions, but only from their effects." Remark 7: "The breakdown in the partially observable case relied on the presence of an unobservable latent factor"; if the observation "(implicitly) contains the task identifier" the problem reverts to the fully observable case. Conclusion: "if the sequence does not possess any latent confounders, then conditioning on the actions will not lead to self-delusions." Remark 4: in control and RL "the controller/agent is never included into the probabilistic model, but regarded as an external process." Footnote 2: "The self-delusions discussed here are unrelated to the problem arising from observational confounding", with references [2], [8], [25]; I looked these up in the reference list: Arjovsky et al., "Invariant risk minimization" (2019); Déletang et al., "Causal analysis of agent behavior for AI safety" (arXiv:2103.03938, 2021); Zolna et al., "Task-relevant adversarial imitation learning" (2019). The reference list does not contain any Bareinboim paper (searched).

Where it does not match the candidate: the confounder lives in the *model's learned joint distribution* (inherited from an expert who knew $\Theta$), not in the deployed agent's true action mechanism. The prescription ("treat as $do(a)$, drop $P(a \mid \theta)$") is a handling rule for a learner that lacks the correct model of its own action, and the paper does not state an identification theorem. It is the nearest source for the requester's question 3, because it is explicitly aimed at language-model sequence models; see "Adjacent observations" for how it sits beside the Bareinboim prescription.

Read: **P-part** (§1, §2 including Remarks 2-8, §5 conclusion, keyword search; the meta-learning sections not read).

### 1.7 Richens and Everitt (2024): does not match

Richens, J., Everitt, T. "Robust agents learn causal world models." ICLR 2024 (oral); arXiv:2402.10877. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10877 ; https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/44a2b9f7bf9aec3f1fa333ad875b0ee0-Abstract-Conference.html.

### 1.8 Suau, Spaan, Oliehoek (2024), "policy confounding": a false friend

Suau, M., Spaan, M. T. J., Oliehoek, F. A. "Bad Habits: Policy Confounding and Out-of-Trajectory Generalization in RL." Reinforcement Learning Conference / Journal 2024 (the PDF is headed "Published as a conference paper at RLC 2024"); arXiv:2306.02419. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02419. A follow-up, "Breaking Habits: On the Role of the Advantage ..." (arXiv:2506.11912, title as shown in a search result), I did not open (S).

What it claims: Definition 9 (§6): a state representation $\Phi$ is "confounded by a policy $\pi$" if for some states and action, $R^\pi(\Phi(s_t), a_t) \neq R^\pi(do(\Phi(s_t)), a_t)$ or the analogous transition inequality. The mechanism is that the policy, by choosing actions as a function of earlier state variables, makes later variables proxies for earlier ones, so a *smaller* representation can be sufficient on-policy ("policies act as confounders, opening backdoor paths between future observation variables $F_{t+1}$ and the variables in the current state $F_t$"). Theorem 1: if some $\pi$-Markov representation uses fewer variables than the minimal state representation, it is confounded by $\pi$. Example 3 shows it "can occur not only when the environment is partially observable ... but also in fully observable scenarios."

Why this matters and why it is a different thing: it is not latent confounding of action and outcome. It is a statement about *representations* learned on-policy, and it is an instance of failure (i) with an extra property: the compression looks sufficient on-policy (no on-policy symptom) and fails only under intervention or off-trajectory. The shared word "confounding" will mislead a reader who expects the Bareinboim/Dawid sense.

Read: **P-part** (Definition 9, Theorem 1, §6-6.1, Example 3 text).

### 1.9 Also read, supporting only

Pearl, J., Robins, J. M. "Probabilistic Evaluation of Sequential Plans from Causal Models with Hidden Variables." Proceedings of UAI 1995, pp. 444-453. https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.4977. ## Item 2: names for "adjust for the full information set the policy used"

There is a standard name for the *condition*, several for its lineage, and no standard name I found for "it holds because the analyst conditions on the exact inputs the policy used".

### 2.1 Dawid and Didelez (2010)

Dawid, A. P., Didelez, V. "Identifying the consequences of dynamic treatment strategies: A decision-theoretic overview." Statistics Surveys 4:184-231, 2010 (doi:10.1214/10-SS081). https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.3425.

What it claims (§5.2.1, quoted): "The simple stability property (6) is our version of a condition termed 'sequential randomization' (Robins, 1986, 1997) or 'no unmeasured confounding' (Robins, 1992; Robins, Hernán and Brumback, 2000) or 'sequential ignorability' (Robins, 2000). ... These alternative names suggest particular situations where stability should be satisfied, such as when the data have been gathered under an observational regime where the actions were indeed physically sequentially randomized; or when S's information base contains all the information the doctor D has used in choosing the $(A_i)$." And: "if $e$ is a control strategy with respect to S's information base, and $o$ an observational regime under which the doctor D chooses the $(A_i)$ on the basis of private information not represented in S's information base, possibly associated with $L_i$, then, for $S = \{o, e\}$, we might well expect (6) to be violated. This is often described as (potential) confounding." Also: stability "can never be empirically established on the basis of data collected under only one (e.g., observational) regime." Definition 5.1 is simple stability, $L_i \perp\!\!\!\perp \sigma \mid (L_{i-1}, A_{i-1})$.

This is the closest statement to the requester's "ignorability by design / conditioning on the policy's inputs". Note that the object is *invariance of the covariate and outcome mechanisms across regimes*, which is the formal content of "the policy's inputs are all the confounding there is".

Read: **P-part** (§5.2-5.3, §6 headings, abstract; the influence-diagram sections and §10 on potential responses not read).

### 2.2 Dawid and Constantinou (2014)

Dawid, A. P., Constantinou, P. "A Formal Treatment of Sequential Ignorability." Statistics in Biosciences 6(2):166-188, 2014 (doi:10.1007/s12561-014-9110-8). https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1077.

What it claims: Definition 5.1 defines a *control strategy* by $A_i \perp\!\!\!\perp U^i \mid (L^i, A^{i-1}; s)$ with a known conditional distribution of $A_i$. Condition 5.3 (sequential randomization) is the same independence in the observational regime, and the text says: "While this condition will hold under a control strategy, in the observational regime it requires that the only information that has been used to assign the treatment at each stage is that supplied by the observable variables." Theorem 5.1: extended stability plus sequential randomization imply simple stability. Corollary 5.1 adds extended positivity. The abstract states that "sequential randomization, where the unobserved variables essentially behave as random noise in their effects on the actions" and "sequential irrelevance, where the unobserved variables do not influence future observed variables" are the two routes to sequential ignorability, and that positivity conditions are not required in the discrete case for the latter.

Why it matters here: this is the formal "holds by definition for a control strategy" statement, and "sequential irrelevance" is an additional route the requester's list does not include (an unlogged cause that cannot reach later observables). I did not check whether it transfers to a learner whose own state is among the unobservables.

Read: **P-part** (abstract, §5.1-5.4, Theorem 5.1, Corollary 5.1).

## Item 4: overlap (positivity) versus ignorability under deterministic policies

- **Dawid and Didelez 2010, Definition 5.2 and §5.2.2** (entry 2.1) give the standard statement in the sequential setting: positivity is absolute continuity of the target regime's joint law with respect to the observational one, $p(E; e) \gt 0 \Rightarrow p(E; o) \gt 0$. Their illustration is the deterministic case: for a static strategy "which always applies some pre-specified action sequence $a^*$, we clearly will be unable to do so using data from an observational regime in which the probability of obtaining that particular sequence of actions is zero." **P-part.**
- **Bareinboim, Zhang, Lee 2026, Theorem 2 and footnote 24** (entry 1.1): inverse propensity weighting "requires what is called the positivity assumption, i.e., the propensity scores $P(x_i \mid \bar{x}_{i-1}, \bar{s}_i) \gt 0$ for every entry"; footnote 24: "This quantitative assumption is called overlap in (Rosenbaum and Rubin, 1983; Imbens, 2004). There are attempts in the literature to relax it by assuming some parametric models that allow the interpolation of the unobserved areas, e.g., refer to (Rosenbaum, 2002; Kallus and Zhou, 2018)." **P-part.**
- **D'Amour, A., Ding, P., Feller, A., Lei, L., Sekhon, J. "Overlap in observational studies with high-dimensional covariates."** Journal of Econometrics 221(2):644-654, 2021 (doi:10.1016/j.jeconom.2019.10.014); arXiv:1711.02582. https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02582. Claim: adding covariates makes unconfoundedness more plausible but overlap harder to satisfy; they bound the covariate-mean imbalance that strict overlap permits, with bounds tightening as dimension grows. This is the general form of the tension between failure (i) (condition on more) and failure (iii) (overlap shrinks). Read: **A**.
- **Rosenbaum, P. R., Rubin, D. B. (1983).** "The central role of the propensity score in observational studies for causal effects." Biometrika 70(1):41-55: strong ignorability is ignorability plus $0 \lt P(T = 1 \mid X) \lt 1$. **M**; the 2026 survey (footnote 24) cites it for the name "overlap".
- **Imbens, G. W., Rubin, D. B. (2015).** *Causal Inference for Statistics, Social, and Biomedical Sciences*, Cambridge University Press: the overlap assumption and its diagnostics. **M**; chapter not located.
- **Westreich, D., Cole, S. R. (2010).** "Invited commentary: positivity in practice." American Journal of Epidemiology 171(6):674-677, and **Petersen, M. L., et al. (2012)** "Diagnosing and responding to violations in the positivity assumption." Statistical Methods in Medical Research 21(1):31-54: the distinction between structural and random non-positivity, which maps onto a deterministic policy (structural). **M**.
- **Regression discontinuity**: Hahn, J., Todd, P., Van der Klaauw, W. (2001), Econometrica 69(1):201-209. A deterministic assignment rule on a continuous decision variable is a case where positivity fails by design yet a local effect at the threshold is identified under continuity of the potential-outcome regressions. **M.** Relevant to whether failure (iii) is total when the decision state is continuous and the outcome regression is smooth.

## Adjacent observations

Marked as mine where they are my own reasoning; none of this is cited work.

**Ortega's prescription and Bareinboim's prescription sound opposite and are not.** Ortega et al. say to treat one's action as $do(a)$ and *drop* the evidence $P(a \mid \theta)$ ("one cannot learn from one's own actions, but only from their effects"). The Bareinboim line says the intent *carries* evidence about $U$, so condition on it and randomize within it. My reading from Ortega's equation (2): the two posteriors, $P(\theta \mid a, o)$ and $P(\theta \mid do(a), o)$, coincide exactly when $P(a \mid \theta)$ does not depend on $\theta$, because that factor then cancels in the normalization. So Ortega's intervention is the right handling when the learner's *model of its own action* carries a dependence on the latent (inherited from expert data) that the deployed learner's real mechanism lacks; with the correct action mechanism, conditioning and intervening agree, which is the "by construction" statement (Dawid and Constantinou's control-strategy independence; the 2026 survey's Lemma 1). Ortega's own conclusion ("if the sequence does not possess any latent confounders, then conditioning on the actions will not lead to self-delusions") is the nearest textual statement. Bareinboim's agent differs in having a real causal channel from the latent into its own action (the drunk gambler's predilection), and the intent is the readout of that channel. A short way to say it: Ortega's delusion is a channel mismatch between the agent that generated the training data and the agent now acting. This is worth a check by whoever derives the spike, since it would let one statement cover both literatures. (Ortega et al. 2021 do not cite the Bareinboim papers, by a search of their reference list; Bareinboim et al. 2015 do cite Ortega and Braun 2010.)

**Failure (ii) may be better stated as un-interceptable intent than as un-introspectable causes.** The 2015, 2017, and 2026 intent papers do not require the agent to know why it wants to do $x'$; they require that it be possible to "interrupt any reasoning agent before they execute their choice, treat this choice as their intention, deliberate, and then act" (2026 survey §7.2, p. 135; the 2015 paper §3 has the same sentence with slightly different wording). Footnote 3 of Forney et al. 2017: the definition "does not require that all its influencing factors be measured or acknowledged by the agent." The 2026 survey adds (p. 135) that if deliberation revises the intent, "only the final choice matters"; Fig. 45 and footnote 59 say randomization that bypasses deliberation discards the intent. For a learning loop the question then becomes whether the pre-execution output of the decision function is readable, rather than whether its causes are. For a language-model agent that readout may be operationally available (for example the pre-sampling action distribution or a drafted action); that is my gloss, not a claim from the sources.

**"Exactly when" is stronger than the sources.** 2026 survey Example 34 exhibits a policy class that violates NUC yet satisfies the sequential backdoor condition (a proper subset of the action's causes suffices because the omitted cause is blocked). So conditioning on the full decision state is sufficient, and necessary only for the specific estimand "effect given the full decision state". For the exact graph-theoretic iff about adjustment sets, the reference I would check is Shpitser, VanderWeele, Robins, "On the validity of covariate adjustment for estimating causal effects," UAI 2010 (**M**, unverified; my recollection is that it proves the adjustment criterion sound and complete for adjustment).

**Failure (ii), refined.** Dawid and Didelez write "private information not represented in S's information base, *possibly associated with* $L_i$." An unlogged cause confounds only if it carries information about outcome-relevant variables beyond what the logged history already carries. My quick check, not formalized: a learner's persistent environment-independent randomness (seed, minibatch noise) does not create confounding, because every path from it into the environment passes through logged actions; Dawid and Constantinou's condition $A_i \perp U^i \mid (L^i, A^{i-1})$ covers this, whereas the letter of the 2026 survey's Condition 2 (each action has its own independent noise $U_i$) does not obviously. What would confound is unlogged *information about the environment*: unlogged observations or retrieved context that the agent used, or weights and pretraining knowledge that are correlated with environment latents across the population of (agent, environment) pairs being pooled (the expert-who-knew-$\Theta$ situation). I have not checked whether the second is a real concern for any agent class the spike cares about.

**Failure (iii), refined.** Under a deterministic policy, consistency gives $E[Y_{\pi(s)} \mid s] = E[Y \mid s, X = \pi(s)]$, so the policy's own conditional value is identified; what is unidentified is $E[Y_x \mid s]$ for $x \neq \pi(s)$ (my reasoning; the survey's Theorem 2 and the Dawid-Didelez §5.2.2 example are the sources for the lost contrasts). The intent papers' device (randomize within the intent stratum) is exactly manufacturing positivity inside the decision state.

**Failure (i) can be invisible on-policy.** Suau et al.'s Definition 9 and Example 1 show a compressed representation that is sufficient under the current policy because the policy made later variables proxies for earlier ones, and that is interventionally invalid. A spike that tests failure (i) only on-policy would see no symptom.

**D'Amour et al.** frames (i) against (iii) as one trade: conditioning on the full decision state is the maximum-ignorability end and the minimum-overlap end at once; overlap is then supplied only by the learner's own stochasticity.
