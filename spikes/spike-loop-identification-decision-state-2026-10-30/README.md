# Spike: loop identification conditional on the decision state (C2)

> **Status (2026-10-30): landed by replacement in `#der-loop-interventional-access`, `#def-value-object`, `#def-model-sufficiency`, `#der-causal-hierarchy-requirement` and `#impl-causal-access` (commit `253d2bca`; account `msc/inside-out-2026-10/part-ii-integration.md`).** Three statements below were refuted by `de-novo-feedback-1.md` and are not canon: Lemma 1(b)'s (P-stream) clause (retaining past *actions* does not restore ignorability at $X_t$; retaining the past *coins* does, since a past action is a collider); Proposition 4 as stated (its two-SCM proof indexed the kernel by the agent's state; the landed no-go is for an unrestricted environment class, with its own check `check-integration-1-deterministic-nogo.py`); and Proposition 5's "iff" (the general condition is $R \perp S \mid Z$, more permissive than either sufficiency). **Date:** 2026-10-30. **Spiker:** Fable (C2 of three parallel C.2 spikes). **Type:** strengthen-first on an owner-review item. **Disposition:** read-only on canon; everything lands through the verifier and integrator the coordinator commissions.

**The question.** `#der-loop-interventional-access` gates identification of $P(o \mid do(a), \cdot)$ from the agent's own action–outcome data on (C1) positivity, (C2) sequential ignorability given history, (C3) known action-mechanism, and says at `:41` that (C2) "fails generically for any agent whose action depends on internal state, plans, or memory that also bear on the outcome." The owner review's Part II explorer proposed that adjusting for the agent's own decision inputs is always a valid back-door adjustment, so the data identifies the effect *given the decision state* exactly when the learner conditions on everything the action depended on (apart from environment-independent randomization) and that randomization gives positivity — with three named failures. Owed: whether `#def-model-sufficiency` is the sufficiency needed; the gate rewrite.

**What happened, in one paragraph.** The proposed strengthening holds and goes further than the brief at the policy's own inputs: for any agent whose action is a function of its complete state $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$ plus randomization the environment cannot see, ignorability is automatic at the full chronica, and at $X_t$ itself when the coins are fresh or the past actions are retained in $X_t$ (Lemma 1 — parent adjustment; the same statement is Dawid & Constantinou's "control strategy" and the June 2026 Bareinboim–Zhang–Lee survey's "Experimental NUC"). The sentence at `:41` is false about (C2) as stated and true about a learner that conditions on nothing. What *does* fail generically is positivity at the decision state (Proposition 4: a deterministic policy identifies no contrast at its own decision state) and, when the learner coarsens to recover support, the outcome-sufficiency of the coarse set (Lemma 2); the two are one trade-off (Proposition 5), and goal variation is the agent's own instrument exactly when its goals carry no outcome information beyond its model. The owed sufficiency question has a precise answer: on-policy one-step predictive sufficiency is the needed (interventional) sufficiency iff positivity (Lemma 3), and `#def-model-sufficiency`'s formula conditions on *realized* future actions, which a two-bit echo-agent example shows can differ from the interventional reading the segment's causal use needs. The found defect: `#def-value-object` conditions $Q_O$ on $M_t$ while the policy reads $(M_t, G_t)$, and canon's own edge-credence learner stores outcome statistics in $G_t$; a simulation shows the $M_t$-conditioned value object confounded by about a tenth at $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$, unconfounded at $X_t$. (C3) is the intention-to-treat gap. The explorer's "un-introspectable cause" is a premise violation, not a third failure.

**Completion state:** strengthened-past at the policy's inputs; a found defect one rung down; one exact no-go (positivity); the sufficiency question answered conditionally. Open items are listed at the end of the derivation.
