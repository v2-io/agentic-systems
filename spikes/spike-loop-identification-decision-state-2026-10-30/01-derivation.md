## 0. Setting, and the four commitments the result rides on

The objects are AAT's. The environment state is $\Omega_t$; the agent receives $o_t$ through the observation channel; its chronica is $\mathcal C_t = (o_1, a_1, \ldots, a_{t-1}, o_t)$ (`#def-chronica`); its complete state is $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$ (`#form-complete-agent-state`), recursive on events (`#der-recursive-update` lifted to $X_t$); it acts by $a_t = \pi(M_t, G_t)$; the environment moves by $\Omega_{t+1} \sim T(\cdot \mid \Omega_t, a_t)$ (`#def-action-transition`) and emits $o_{t+1}$.

I write the policy with its randomization explicit, $a_t = \pi(X_t, U_t)$, and the one-step outcome as a structural function $o_{t+1} = g(\Omega_t, a_t, N_t)$ with $N_t$ the environment's exogenous noise over the step (transition noise and observation noise together). The potential outcome of action $a$ at step $t$ is $Y_t^{(a)} := g(\Omega_t, a, N_t)$, so that $o_{t+1} = Y_t^{(a_t)}$ (consistency) and $P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a_t = a), Z) = P(Y_t^{(a)} \mid Z)$ for any pre-action $Z$. This is the standard structural-causal-model reading of the loop; it is also the reading `#def-pearl-causal-hierarchy` recapitulates.

Four commitments, each already in canon, do all the work:

- **(M) Mediated access.** The environment reaches the agent only through events (`#der-recursive-update`, constraint C2; `#def-observation-function`). Consequence: $X_t$ is a function of $(X_0, \mathcal C_t)$, and $X_0$ is fixed before the loop starts.
- **(P) Policy signature with environment-independent randomization.** $a_t = \pi(X_t, U_t)$ where $U_t$ is independent of $(\Omega_{\le t}, N_t, \mathcal C_t)$ (`#form-complete-agent-state:38`, with the randomization made explicit). "Environment-independent" is the whole content: a coin the environment cannot see and did not set.
- **(F) Record faithfulness.** The $X_t$ a learner later conditions on is the $X_t$ the policy read. Where a learner holds only a compression $Z_t = \psi(X_t)$, (F) is replaced by the sufficiency question of §2.
- **(E) Faithful execution.** The executed action equals the decided action. Relaxed in §5, where it becomes (C3).

The causal graph these induce: $\Omega_t \to o_t \to \mathcal C_t \to X_t \to a_t \to \Omega_{t+1} \to o_{t+1}$, with $\Omega_t \to \Omega_{t+1}$, $U_t \to a_t$, $N_t \to o_{t+1}$, and no other arrows into $a_t$. Every back-door path from $a_t$ to $o_{t+1}$ therefore runs $a_t \leftarrow X_t \leftarrow \mathcal C_t \leftarrow \cdots \leftarrow \Omega_s \to \cdots \to \Omega_t \to o_{t+1}$ and passes through $X_t$.

## 1. Rung 1 — at the policy's own inputs, ignorability is automatic **[exact]**

**Lemma 1 (ignorability at $X_t$).** Under (M) and (P), for every action $a$: $a_t \perp Y_t^{(a)} \mid X_t$. Consequently, wherever $P(a_t = a \mid X_t) \gt 0$,

$$P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a_t = a), X_t) \;=\; P(o_{t+1} \mid a_t = a, X_t).$$

*Proof.* By (P), $U_t \perp (\mathcal C_t, \Omega_t, N_t)$. Since $X_t$ is a function of $(X_0, \mathcal C_t)$ by (M), $U_t \perp (X_t, \Omega_t, N_t)$, hence $U_t \perp (\Omega_t, N_t) \mid X_t$. Given $X_t$, $a_t = \pi(X_t, U_t)$ is a function of $U_t$ alone and $Y_t^{(a)} = g(\Omega_t, a, N_t)$ is a function of $(\Omega_t, N_t)$ alone; so $a_t \perp Y_t^{(a)} \mid X_t$. Then $P(Y_t^{(a)} \mid X_t) = P(Y_t^{(a)} \mid a_t = a, X_t) = P(o_{t+1} \mid a_t = a, X_t)$ on the support, the last step by consistency. The left side is $P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a), X_t)$ because the intervention replaces the mechanism of $a_t$ and leaves the pre-action $X_t$ untouched. $\square$

The same argument with $X_t$ replaced by $\mathcal C_t$, or by any $Z$ of which $X_t$ is a function and which is itself a function of $(X_0, \mathcal C_t)$, gives the same identity. The $\mathcal C_t$ form is NeurIPS Paper 2's Key Lemma 3 (whose proof says exactly this: "the only arrows into $a_t$ come from $H_t$"). The $X_t$ form is the one AAT's own agent can use, because $X_t$ is what it holds (`#der-recursive-update`: the learner never re-reads $\mathcal C_t$).

This is Pearl's adjustment for the direct causes of the treatment (Pearl 2009, *Causality*, §3.3: the parents of $X$ always satisfy the back-door criterion), conditioned rather than averaged, with the agent's own decision inputs as the parent set. The inference-side statement of the same fact is Ortega & Braun 2008 (`relata ortega-2008-minimum`, §3.2–3.3; `ref/ortega_adaptive_agent_2008.pdf`): because the agent generates its action from its own knowledge state, the action "does not carry any information that could be used for inference" about the environment beyond that state, and must be treated as an intervention — "actions as interventions and observations as conditions". Lemma 1 is the outcome-side reading of that remark: given the knowledge state, the action is as good as randomized.

The literature scan (`lit-scan.md`) found the same statement in two further places, and the second is recent enough that it should be the citation. Dawid & Didelez 2010 (§5.2.1) say sequential ignorability holds "when S's information base contains all the information the doctor D has used in choosing the $(A_i)$", and Dawid & Constantinou 2014 (Def. 5.1) make it definitional for a *control strategy*: $A_i \perp U^i \mid (L^i, A^{i-1})$ "will hold under a control strategy". Bareinboim, Zhang & Lee's June 2026 survey (*An Introduction to Causal Reinforcement Learning*, arXiv:2606.24160, Def. 13 and Lemma 1 "Experimental NUC") states it for policy classes: no-unmeasured-confounding holds in the submodel induced by *any* deployed policy, because the policy's inputs are by construction all the endogenous parents of its action and its exogenous parent is independent noise. Lemma 1 above is Experimental NUC applied to AAT's loop, with AAT's complete state $X_t$ as the policy's input set. The one thing AAT adds is the identity of that set: it is the agent's own $(M_t, G_t)$, which the agent holds and can condition on without any external analyst — the "whose data" reading that `#scope-agent-identity` grounds. A further remark from the scan that I checked and adopt: Ortega et al. 2021 (*Shaking the foundations*, arXiv:2110.10819, Remark 3) prescribe the opposite-sounding handling — treat one's own action as $do(a)$ and *drop* the evidence $P(a \mid \theta)$ it would carry — and the two prescriptions coincide exactly when $P(a \mid \theta)$ does not depend on $\theta$ given the agent's state, which is the content of (P). Their "delusion" is the case where the learner's *model of its own action mechanism* (inherited from training data generated by agents who knew $\theta$) carries a $\theta$-dependence the deployed agent's real mechanism lacks. Self-delusion (a wrong model of one's own action) and self-confounding (a conditioning set coarser than one's own policy's inputs, §2) are the two ways an agent's own actions mislead it, and they are mirror images: the first over-reads the action as evidence, the second under-conditions the outcome.

**What Lemma 1 says about `der-loop-interventional-access.md:41`.** The sentence reads: "Where (C2) fails — generic for any agent whose action depends on internal state, plans, or memory that also bear on the outcome — on-policy $(a_t, o_{t+1})$ converges to $\mathbb E[o \mid A = a]$." Under (M) and (P), an agent's internal state, plans, and memory *are* $X_t$, and (C2) *given $H_t \supseteq X_t$* cannot fail because of them: they are the adjustment set, not the confounder. The quantity the sentence says the data converges to, $\mathbb E[o \mid A = a]$, conditions on nothing — it is the estimate of a learner that *does not* condition on its decision inputs. So the sentence is false about (C2)-as-stated and true about a different learner than the one (C2) describes. The corrected content is in §3 (what does fail generically is the *price*, not the ignorability).

**Corollary (integrator item A11).** In a fully observed MDP with $X_t = s_t$ and a policy $\pi(a \mid s_t)$ with positivity, $\mathbb E[R \mid s, do(a)] = \mathbb E[R \mid s, A = a]$ by Lemma 1. `#der-causal-hierarchy-requirement:52`'s RL example needs the learner to condition on less than the policy did — partial observability, or a parent of the action absent from $s$. The "selection bias from the agent's own policy" is real exactly in that gap, and §2 names it.

**Corollary (the LLM reading, adjudicating two canon lines).** `der-loop:90` (Working Notes) says "goal-conditioned LLM policies violate (C2) by construction"; `impl-causal-access:118` says canon makes no such claim and that the loop's (C2) and directed separation "share their labels and nothing else." Lemma 1 decides it: $G_t$ is a *parent* of $a_t$ — part of the adjustment set — and the environment does not read the goal (nothing in the graph points from $G_t$ to $\Omega$ except through $a_t$). A goal-coupled belief update ($\kappa_{\text{processing}} \gt 0$) biases $M_{t+1}$ — a belief-quality problem — and leaves $(a_t, o_{t+1})$ unconfounded given $X_t$. The Working-Note line is a category slip; `impl-causal-access` has it right. (I also grepped NeurIPS Paper 3's `src/re/` for "ignorab", "confound", "d-separat": no hits; its named (C2) is "Deterministic Bayesian update". The attribution of the violation claim to Paper 3 is unsupported by the paper's text.) What *is* true of LLM agents is in §3 and §6.

## 2. Rung 2 — at a compression of the inputs, ignorability becomes a sufficiency condition **[exact]**

Real learners condition on less than $X_t$: AAT's value object conditions on $M_t$ (`#def-value-object:35`); a post-turnover LLM conditions on a summary; an analyst conditions on a log. Let $Z_t = \psi(X_t)$.

**Lemma 2 (two routes to ignorability at $Z$).** Under (M), (P), for each $a$ with $P(a_t = a \mid Z_t) \gt 0$, the identity $P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a), Z_t) = P(o_{t+1} \mid a, Z_t)$ holds if either

- **(S) outcome-sufficiency of $Z$:** $Y_t^{(a)} \perp X_t \mid Z_t$ — the dropped part of the decision state carries no information about what action $a$ would produce; or
- **($\Pi$) policy-sufficiency of $Z$:** $a_t \perp X_t \mid Z_t$ — the policy depended on $X_t$ only through $Z_t$.

*Proof.* Under (S): $a_t$ is a function of $(X_t, U_t)$, and given $Z_t$ the pair $(X_t, U_t)$ is independent of $Y_t^{(a)}$ — $X_t$ by (S), $U_t$ by (P) as in Lemma 1 — so $a_t \perp Y_t^{(a)} \mid Z_t$; the identity follows as before. Under ($\Pi$): write $P(a_t = a, Y^{(a)} = y \mid Z_t) = \sum_x P(x \mid Z_t)\, P(a_t = a \mid x)\, P(Y^{(a)} = y \mid x, a_t = a)$; the last factor equals $P(Y^{(a)} = y \mid x)$ by Lemma 1, and ($\Pi$) says $P(a_t = a \mid x) = P(a_t = a \mid Z_t)$ for every $x$ in the cell, so the sum factors as $P(a_t = a \mid Z_t)\, P(Y^{(a)} = y \mid Z_t)$, which is $a_t \perp Y_t^{(a)} \mid Z_t$. $\square$

**When neither holds, adjustment on $Z$ is confounded.** Take $X_t = (Z_t, W_t)$ with $W_t$ a binary variable correlated with $\Omega_t$, a policy that prefers $a = 1$ when $W_t = 1$ (with some randomization), and an outcome $Y^{(1)}$ that is more likely when $\Omega_t$ is in the state $W_t$ indicates. Then $P(o \mid a = 1, Z_t)$ over-weights favorable $\Omega_t$ and exceeds $P(o \mid do(1), Z_t)$. §4 runs exactly this with $W_t$ being canon's own edge counts. The "exactly when" in the integrator item is correct *for identification by adjustment on the agent's retained state*; it does not rule out identification by other strategies (front-door through a recorded mediator, an instrument), which the agent would have to supply with structure the loop does not. I state the result at that honest scope. The scan supports the scoping from the other side: the 2026 survey's Example 34 exhibits a policy class violating no-unmeasured-confounding whose history nevertheless satisfies the sequential back-door condition (Pearl & Robins 1995) — conditioning on the full decision state is sufficient, not necessary, for *some* valid adjustment set to exist. Condition (S) is what Dawid & Constantinou 2014 call *sequential irrelevance* (the unlogged part "does not influence future observed variables"), their second route to sequential ignorability beside sequential randomization; the two routes of Lemma 2 are theirs, applied to a compression of the agent's own state.

**Lemma 3 (the owed question: on-policy predictive sufficiency is the needed sufficiency iff positivity).** Fix $Z_t = \psi(X_t)$ and an action $a$. Define the *on-policy one-step predictive sufficiency* of $Z$ as $I(X_t;\, o_{t+1} \mid Z_t,\, a_t) = 0$, and the *interventional sufficiency* as $I(X_t;\, Y_t^{(a)} \mid Z_t) = 0$ for every $a$.

- (i) Interventional sufficiency implies on-policy predictive sufficiency, always.
- (ii) Under positivity at $Z$ — $P(a_t = a \mid X_t) \gt 0$ for every $a$ and every $X_t$ in the support — on-policy predictive sufficiency implies interventional sufficiency, so the two coincide.
- (iii) Without positivity, (ii) fails: a deterministic policy gives on-policy predictive sufficiency for free and interventional sufficiency for nothing.

*Proof.* (i) Interventional sufficiency says $P(Y^{(a)} = y \mid X_t)$ depends on $X_t$ only through $Z_t$, for every $a$. Then $P(o_{t+1} = y \mid X_t, a_t = a) = P(Y^{(a)} = y \mid X_t, a_t = a) = P(Y^{(a)} = y \mid X_t)$ (the middle step by Lemma 1: $a_t \perp Y^{(a)} \mid X_t$), which depends on $X_t$ only through $Z_t$; so $I(X_t; o_{t+1} \mid Z_t, a_t) = 0$. (ii) On-policy predictive sufficiency says: for each $a$, $P(Y^{(a)} = y \mid X_t)$ — which equals $P(o_{t+1} = y \mid X_t, a_t = a)$ by Lemma 1 — is constant in $X_t$ over the set $\{X_t : P(a_t = a \mid X_t) \gt 0\}$ within each $Z_t$-cell. Under positivity that set is the whole cell; so $P(Y^{(a)} \mid X_t)$ is a function of $Z_t$ alone, which is interventional sufficiency. (iii) If $\pi$ is deterministic, $\{X_t : P(a_t = a \mid X_t) \gt 0\} = \{X_t : \pi(X_t) = a\}$, and on-policy predictive sufficiency constrains $P(Y^{(a)} \mid X_t)$ only on that set. Take $Z_t$ trivial, $X_t \in \{x_0, x_1\}$, $\pi(x_i) = i$, and $P(Y^{(1)} = 1 \mid x_1) = 0.9$, $P(Y^{(1)} = 1 \mid x_0) = 0.1$: on-policy, $o_{t+1} \mid a_t = 1$ is observed only at $x_1$, so $I(X_t; o_{t+1} \mid a_t) = 0$ trivially, while $I(X_t; Y^{(1)}) \gt 0$. $\square$

Part (iii) has an independent statement in the RL literature: Suau, Spaan & Oliehoek 2024 (*Bad Habits: Policy Confounding and Out-of-Trajectory Generalization in RL*, arXiv:2306.02419, Def. 9 and Thm. 1) show that a state representation can be Markov-sufficient *under the current policy* while being interventionally invalid — the policy makes later variables proxies for earlier ones, so a representation smaller than the minimal one passes every on-policy test. Their "policy confounding" is Lemma 3's gap seen from the representation-learning side, and their point that it is invisible on-policy is the reason an agent cannot test its own sufficiency from inside without positivity.

**Reading against `#def-model-sufficiency`.** The segment's $S(M_t)$ conditions on $\mathcal C_t$ versus $M_t$ and on the *realized* action sequence $a_{t:\infty}$. Three things follow.

- *The right comparison class is $X_t$, not $\mathcal C_t$.* By (P), every parent of $a_t$ is in $X_t$, so sufficiency of $M_t$ relative to $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$ is what identification at $M_t$ needs: $Y_t^{(a)} \perp G_t \mid M_t$ for all $a$. Sufficiency relative to $\mathcal C_t$ implies it (since $G_t$ is a function of $\mathcal C_t$) but is stronger than needed. I call the needed condition *goal-purity given the model* in this spike: the goal carries no information about what the agent's actions would produce beyond what the model already carries. The C3 sibling spike needs the same assumption ("the goal carries no world information beyond the prior state"); the name is mine and should not be inherited without a naming pass.
- *The formula's conditioning on realized future actions is the wrong object for a causal reading, and can differ from the needed one.* $S(M_t) = 1$ means $I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1:\infty} \mid M_t, a_{t:\infty}) = 0$. By the chain rule this forces $I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1} \mid M_t, a_{t:\infty}) = 0$, which conditions on $a_{t+1:\infty}$ — descendants of $o_{t+1}$ in closed loop. Conditioning on a descendant can *absorb* a dependence: let $\mathcal C_t$ carry two independent bits $c, c'$; let $M_t$ retain $c'$ and drop $c$; let $o_{t+1} = c$, $o_{t+2} = c'$, and the rest of the future be noise independent of everything; and let the agent's next action echo its observation, $a_{t+1} = o_{t+1}$. Then $I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1} \mid M_t, a_t) = 1$ bit (the model dropped $c$), while given $a_{t+1}$ the observation $o_{t+1}$ is determined, so the numerator of $S$ is $I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1:\infty} \mid M_t, a_{t:\infty}) = 0$ and the denominator is $I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1:\infty} \mid a_{t:\infty}) = 1$ bit (from $c'$), giving $S(M_t) = 1$ with $S$ well-defined. An agent whose next action reports what it just saw — a logging or relaying agent — therefore scores full sufficiency while its model is one-step insufficient and its $M_t$-conditioned action-effect estimates are confounded. The quantity the causal question needs is the *interventional* reading, with the actions held as interventions rather than conditioned as realized: $I(\mathcal C_t;\, o_{t+1:\infty} \mid M_t,\, do(a_{t:\infty}))$, i.e. sufficiency for the potential-outcome sequence. In open loop (actions exogenous, which is how the segment's prose "given future actions" reads) the two coincide; in closed loop they can differ, and the segment's own policy-relativity note is the symptom. **[exact; the counterexample is two bits]**
- *With the interventional reading, the owed answer is yes-under-positivity.* By Lemma 3, one-step interventional sufficiency of $M_t$ relative to $X_t$ is exactly what makes $Q_O$'s $M_t$-conditioning a valid back-door set, and under positivity at $M_t$ it coincides with the on-policy predictive statement an agent can estimate. The segment's Discussion sentence "$S(M_t) = 1$ is nearly sufficient for causal validity … the remaining requirement is that no unmodeled external factor influences both action selection and outcomes" is correct in direction; the "remaining requirement" has a name (positivity at $M_t$, plus (M)/(P)/(E)/(F)), and the "external factor" is, inside the formalism, *internal* — see §4. The segment chose the hardest case to illustrate the claim: "for agents whose actions are deterministic functions of $M_t$" is precisely where positivity fails and the claim is weakest.

## 3. Rung 3 — positivity is the gate no conditioning set removes **[exact]**

**Proposition 4 (deterministic policies identify nothing off-policy at the decision state).** Under (M), (P), (F), (E), if $\pi(\cdot \mid X_t)$ is deterministic and the learner conditions on $X_t$, then for every visited $X_t$ only $a = \pi(X_t)$ has data, and for every $a' \neq \pi(X_t)$ the quantity $P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a'), X_t)$ is not determined by the on-policy law: two environments agreeing on every on-policy distribution differ on it.

*Proof.* Construct two SCMs identical in $T(\cdot \mid \Omega, a)$ for $a = \pi(X_t)$ at every visited $(X_t, \Omega_t)$ and differing arbitrarily in $T(\cdot \mid \Omega, a')$. Every on-policy quantity is a functional of the kernels at taken actions and the exogenous laws, hence identical across the two; $P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a'), X_t)$ is not. $\square$

This is a one-line no-go, and it is load-bearing: it is why the Paper 2 remark that "(C1) reduces to realized-action positivity … tautologically" undersells the gate. Realized-action positivity identifies $P(o \mid do(a_t), X_t)$ at the one action the agent took, which is the on-policy conditional and gives the value object no contrast. $Q_O(M_t, a)$ across $a$ needs $P(a \mid X_t) \gt 0$ for each $a$ the agent would compare — the canon segment's (C1) wording ("every action in the relevant support") is the right one. Any estimate for an untaken action at a visited $X_t$ rests on a model's smoothness across decision states — extrapolation, not identification — which is honest to say and important for an agent that plans with $Q_O$ over actions it has never taken *here*. The one well-understood exception confirms the rule: when the decision state is continuous and the deterministic policy switches at a threshold, continuity of the outcome regressions identifies a *local* contrast at the threshold (regression discontinuity, Hahn, Todd & Van der Klaauw 2001) — identification bought with a smoothness assumption, at one point of the decision space, which is what "extrapolation" means made precise. Dawid & Didelez 2010 (§5.2.2) state the sequential positivity requirement in this form and give the deterministic case as its illustration; the 2026 survey's Theorem 2 and footnote 24 carry the same point for propensity weighting.

**Proposition 5 (the granularity trade-off, and irrelevance-driven variation).** Fix a compression $Z_t = \psi(X_t)$ and two actions $a, a'$. By adjustment on $Z_t$, the contrast $P(o \mid do(a), Z_t) - P(o \mid do(a'), Z_t)$ is identified iff (i) both actions have positive probability within the $Z_t$-cell, and (ii) $Z_t$ satisfies (S) or ($\Pi$) of Lemma 2 for both actions. When the policy is deterministic, (i) requires that different $X_t$ in the same $Z_t$-cell map to different actions, and (ii)-(S) then requires that *the components of $X_t$ that the policy used to distinguish them be outcome-irrelevant given $Z_t$*.

*Proof.* (i) and (ii) are Lemma 2 applied to each action; the deterministic clause restates (i) and (S) for a policy that is a function of $X_t$. $\square$

Two readings of Proposition 5, both exact given the lemmas:

- *Randomization is the special case where the distinguishing input is a fresh coin.* An independent $U_t$ is outcome-irrelevant by (P), and gives both actions positive probability at every $X_t$, so it satisfies (i) and (ii) at the finest granularity $Z = X_t$. But any outcome-irrelevant *structural* input does the same job: a tie-break by alphabetical order, a round-robin schedule, a CI pipeline that runs tests regardless of the developer's hunch (`#scope-edge-update-causal-validity:38`'s "external factors force execution regardless of conditions" is this), a load balancer. Those are quasi-experiments, and the condition that makes them valid is the same one that makes a coin valid. **[exact]** The intent line of Bareinboim, Forney & Pearl 2015 and Forney, Pearl & Bareinboim 2017 (Thm. 4.1) is this proposition run deliberately: the agent reads out its own would-be action (its *intent*, a proxy for whatever drove it, which the agent need not be able to name — their footnote 3), conditions on it, and randomizes *within* the intent stratum, manufacturing positivity inside the decision state. The scan's observation that failure mode (ii) is better stated as *un-interceptable intent* than as un-introspectable causes follows: what the identification needs readable is the pre-execution output of the decision function, not its reasons. For a language-model agent that readout is operationally available — the pre-sampling action distribution — which is a Volume 3 lever this spike only names.
- *Goal variation is the agent's own instrument, iff goal-purity.* Take $Z = M_t$ and a deterministic $\pi(M_t, G_t)$. Different goals at the same model pick different actions — (i) holds whenever the goal distribution at $M_t$ is non-degenerate — and (ii)-(S) is exactly $Y^{(a)} \perp G_t \mid M_t$. So the value object's $M_t$-conditioning is a valid adjustment set for exactly the agents whose goals carry no outcome information beyond their models, and for those agents the variation in what they want *is* the experiment that identifies what their actions do. Directed separation is the statement that the goal does not reach the belief; goal-purity is the statement that the world does not reach the goal except through the belief. The two together make $M_t$ the right object for $Q_O$; directed separation alone does not. **[exact given the assumption; whether real agents satisfy goal-purity is §4's question]**

**The corrected content of `:41`.** What fails "generically" for an agent whose action depends on internal state is not ignorability at that state — Lemma 1 — but *positivity* at it: a decision state rich enough to determine the action is visited once, or always with the same action, so nothing off-policy is identified there (Proposition 4). The learner's recourse is to coarsen, and coarsening re-opens ignorability unless the coarse set is outcome-sufficient (Lemma 2) — which is where the "converges to $\mathbb E[o \mid A = a]$" failure actually lives: at the marginal, the coarsest set, where every decision input is a confounder. The trade-off between the two is the standard overlap-versus-ignorability tension of covariate adjustment (the agent-theoretic twist is that the agent controls both sides: what it retains, and how much it randomizes). An agent that wants its own action–outcome data to identify its action effects therefore has two levers and needs both: retain an outcome-sufficient decision state, and inject outcome-irrelevant variation at it. Exploration is the identification-layer form of what `#der-causal-insufficiency-detection` establishes at the structural-detection layer: a structural prerequisite, not a heuristic.

## 4. Adversarial instance: canon's own strategy learner confounds its own value object **[exact; simulated]**

The drill asks for an instance outside the motivating family. The sharpest one is inside canon. `#hyp-edge-update-via-gain` stores Beta–Bernoulli counts $(\alpha_{ij}, \beta_{ij})$ in $\Sigma_t \subseteq G_t$, incremented directly by outcomes. Those counts are chronica statistics. Where they carry information about the environment that $M_t$ does not — the hypothesis's whole point is that they learn edge efficacies — they are outcome-relevant parents of $a_t$ not screened by $M_t$, and goal-purity fails *for an agent canon describes*, at $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$, with directed separation holding by construction.

The simulation (`sim-strategy-counts-confound.py`): per episode a hidden regime $\theta \in \{0, 1\}$, equiprobable, independent of everything the agent records; action 1 succeeds with probability $0.8$ if $\theta = 1$, $0.2$ if $\theta = 0$; action 0 succeeds with probability $0.5$ always; so $P(o = 1 \mid do(a = 1)) = 0.5$ at every step. The agent's $M_t$ is the step index (goal-blind, carries nothing about $\theta$); $G_t$ holds Beta counts per action, updated from that action's outcomes; the policy is $\varepsilon$-greedy on the Beta means. $f_M$ never reads $G_t$, so $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$ exactly. Two learners estimate $E[o \mid a = 1, Z]$ on-policy and are compared, cell by cell with on-policy weights, against the interventional $E[o \mid do(a = 1), Z] = E[q_1(\theta) \mid Z]$ computed from the hidden $\theta$. Three things the numbers show, each predicted by the lemmas above. At $Z = M_t$ the agent's own value estimate for action 1 is inflated by roughly a tenth — it takes action 1 more often in the regime where action 1 works, and $M_t$ cannot see the regime, but $\Sigma_t$'s counts can. At $Z = X_t$ the gap vanishes (Lemma 1). The confounder here is not a "latent environment confounder that bypasses $M_t$" (`#def-value-object:50`); it is the agent's own strategy state.

**What this does to canon's sentences.** `#def-value-object:50`, "Directed separation delivers (C2) on the epistemic-processing leg … but does not by itself rule out latent environment confounders that bypass $M_t$", locates the residual risk outside the agent. The instance shows the residual risk is $G_t$ itself: directed separation constrains $f_M$ and says nothing about $f_G$ absorbing outcome statistics from $e_\tau$, which `#der-directed-separation:38` explicitly permits ($f_G$ takes the event directly). The $M_t$-conditioned value object is causally valid for exactly the agents satisfying goal-purity given the model, and canon's own edge-update hypothesis describes an agent that need not. Two honest resolutions exist and are for the integrators: either $Q_O$ conditions on $X_t$ (the policy's actual inputs — then Lemma 1 makes the query's identification automatic at the price of positivity at $X_t$), or canon commits that edge credences are functions of $M_t$ (as `#def-strategy-dag:48` writes them) and the Beta counts live in the model, with the hypothesis segment re-homed accordingly. The second is also what `#deriv-decomposition-uniqueness` (R3) would want if "goal-intervention cannot move an edge count" were made true by fiat; as the hypotheses stand, a goal re-initialization wipes $\Sigma_t$, so (R3) does not force the counts into $M_t$.

**Further adversarial instances, each checked against the lemmas.**

- *Fully observed MDP, deterministic policy.* Ignorability at $s_t$ holds trivially (Lemma 1); nothing off-policy is identified (Proposition 4). Both canon lines — A11's correction and "exploration is required" — hold at once, for different reasons. **[exact]**
- *Kalman filter + LQR.* $a_t = -K\hat x_t$ is deterministic in $M_t = (\hat x_t, P_t)$; $G_t$ is a fixed setpoint. Goal-purity holds (the setpoint carries nothing about the world); positivity fails. `#def-pearl-causal-hierarchy`'s row "structural channel present, identification not guaranteed (the LQR action is endogenous to the estimation loop)" is correct, and the reason is positivity, not endogeneity — the endogeneity is exactly what Lemma 1 adjusts away. Dual control (dither) restores positivity and identification. **[exact]**
- *A clock-seeded "randomizer".* An agent whose exploration coin is the wall-clock parity, in an environment whose regime also depends on time of day, violates (P): the coin is not environment-independent. Lemma 1's conclusion fails even at $Z = X_t$, and the data looks randomized. Randomization has to be *independent of the environment*, not merely random-looking; this is the one way the premise can fail silently for a software agent. **[exact]**
- *The "already tired" agent* (`#causal-access-intro:19`). Under (M), (P), (F), (E) there is no variable that affects both $a_t$ and $o_{t+1}$ outside $X_t$. Fatigue is one of: a body state that modulates the *executed* action — an (E) failure, §5; a component of the actual decision state absent from the *recorded* one — an (F) failure; or a scope exit from mediated access (the body is environment, and it drives the action without passing through an event). The explorer's "un-introspectable cause" is therefore not a third failure parallel to compression and determinism; it is a violation of the premises, and naming which one tells the agent what to log, randomize, or declare. **[exact as a classification relative to (M)/(P)/(E)/(F); the "claim about agents" is which premise real fatigue violates]**
- *An LLM agent in a tool-use loop.* Within a session, the hidden activations are a deterministic function of the context plus sampling noise; they have no channel to the environment except $a_t$. They are mediators between $X_t$ (the context) and $a_t$, not confounders — the explorer's "action-determining latents not exposed to any learner, including themselves" misreads them. What the lemmas do say about such an agent: at temperature zero, or with peaked sampling, positivity at context granularity fails and every value estimate for an untaken tool call is extrapolation (Proposition 4); across context turnover (`#obs-context-turnover`), the later learner conditions on a summary that dropped the earlier decision inputs, so its own earlier self is now an unmeasured confounder of the action–outcome pairs it is learning from — *self-confounding through forgetting* — unless the retained summary is outcome-sufficient in Lemma 2's sense. That is a Volume 3 claim with a test: an agent that logs its tool-call contexts (or propensities) and conditions on them should show unconfounded effect estimates where one that learns from compressed memory shows the §4 inflation. **[exact as a consequence of Lemmas 1–3; claim about agents in the last sentence]**

## 5. (C3) as intention-to-treat **[exact]**

Relax (E): the executed action is $\tilde a_t = \chi(a_t, \Omega_t, W_t)$ for an execution mechanism $\chi$ with its own noise $W_t$. Lemma 1 still holds for the *decision*: $P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a_t = a), X_t) = P(o_{t+1} \mid a_t = a, X_t)$. What it identifies is the effect of deciding $a$, through whatever execution follows — the intention-to-treat effect. The effect of the *executed* action, $P(o_{t+1} \mid do(\tilde a_t = \tilde a), X_t)$, needs $\chi$ to be known (so the executed value can be read off) and $W_t$ to be unconfounded with the outcome given $(X_t, a_t)$ — the slippery floor that both perturbs the step and causes the fall violates it. This is what canon's (C3) "known action-mechanism" is, stated so that it is visibly separate from (C1) and (C2): decision-state adjustment never substitutes for it. For planning with $Q_O$ the intention-to-treat effect is usually the one the agent wants, since it can only ever decide; for diagnosing *why* an action failed (`#scope-edge-update-causal-validity`'s attribution question) the executed-action effect is the one needed.

## 6. Completion state, and what is open

**Strengthened-past the brief's claim at the policy's inputs**, and **a found defect one rung down.** The headline the brief asked about ("identifies the effect given the decision state exactly when the learner conditions on everything the action depended on, apart from environment-independent randomization, and that randomization gives positivity") is true as a statement about adjustment, and the "fails generically" sentence is replaced by something sharper: ignorability is free at the policy's own inputs (Lemma 1), positivity is the gate no conditioning set removes (Proposition 4), coarsening trades one for the other through a nameable sufficiency condition (Lemma 2, Proposition 5), and the on-policy sufficiency an agent can estimate coincides with the one it needs exactly under positivity (Lemma 3). The found defect is that canon's value object conditions on $M_t$ while canon's policy reads $(M_t, G_t)$, and canon's own strategy learner puts outcome statistics in $G_t$; the $M_t$-conditioned $Q_O$ is confounded for that agent at $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$ (§4, simulated). The second finding should not be swallowed by the first.

**Open, honestly.**

- The horizon-$N_h$ form of Lemma 3 for $Q_O$ under $\pi_{\text{cont}}$ is stated by analogy (sequential ignorability at each step's decision state; the g-formula) and not written out. For $\pi_{\text{cont}} = \pi_{\text{current}}$ (convention C1) it is on-policy evaluation of $Q^\pi$ after a first-step intervention, which is standard, but I have not carried the collider analysis of §2 through the continuation steps.

Human and LLM goals plausibly carry world-information (a commander's objective encodes intelligence the unit lacks — the C3 sibling's break-point 3).
