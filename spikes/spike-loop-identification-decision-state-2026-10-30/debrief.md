# Debrief — what an agent can learn about its own actions from its own actions

*For Joseph. Written 2026-10-30 after re-reading the corrected artifacts, not from memory of the work. Where I am unsure what you hold in context I mark the prerequisite in brackets rather than explain it.*

## The question

The loop chapter says the agent's own action–outcome pairs are "interventional in character" (the agent caused the action), and that turning them into an actual causal estimate — $P(o \mid do(a))$, what *would* happen if the agent did $a$ — is gated on three conditions: every action gets tried (positivity), the agent's choice of action is not driven by something that also drives the outcome (ignorability given history), and the executed action is the decided one (known mechanism). Then it says the ignorability condition "fails generically for any agent whose action depends on internal state, plans, or memory that also bear on the outcome." The owner review's explorer thought that sentence was wrong: an agent's internal state is what its action depends on, so conditioning on it is the textbook way to *remove* confounding (adjust for the causes of the treatment), not a source of it. My commission was to push that as far as it goes, find what holds, and answer the explorer's own open item — whether the model-sufficiency quantity already in canon is the sufficiency this needs.

## What held, and more than asked

The explorer is right, and the sentence at `der-loop-interventional-access:41` is wrong in a specific way: it names a condition relative to one conditioning set (the agent's history) and describes a failure relative to a different one (no conditioning at all). The mathematics is a one-paragraph lemma from four commitments canon already makes: the environment reaches the agent only through events; the action is a function of the agent's complete state $(M_t, G_t)$ plus a coin the environment cannot see; the state the learner conditions on is the state the policy read; the executed action is the decided one. Under those, an agent's action is as good as randomized *given its own state*, so its own action–outcome data identifies the effect of each action at that state — for every action that has positive probability there. That last clause is where the real content turned out to live. (One refinement the review caught: "the coin the environment cannot see" has to be either fresh at each step, or the agent has to remember its past actions. An agent exploring on a fixed schedule — "try the other arm every seventh step" — whose compressed state has forgotten which past steps were exploration steps is confounded at that state, because this step's explore/exploit status is correlated with the ones that shaped the world it now faces. At the full history it is fine. I had first written the premise too strongly and the shared-seed case falsified it.)

**Positivity is the only gate that nothing removes.** A decision state rich enough to determine the action is a state where only one action ever gets tried, so nothing is learned about the others there. A deterministic policy identifies no contrast at its own decision state; every value estimate for an untaken action is extrapolation across states, not identification. This is a one-line no-go and it is load-bearing, because the NeurIPS Paper 2 lemma that canon's gate was lifted from says positivity is "tautological" — true, but only for the action actually taken, which gives the value object no contrast at all. The honest picture is a trade-off the agent controls from both ends: condition finely and you have ignorability but no support; coarsen to recover support and you re-open confounding unless the coarse state keeps everything about the dropped part that bears on the outcome. Randomization is the special case where the thing that varies the action is a fresh coin; but any outcome-irrelevant input does the same work — alphabetical tie-breaks, round-robin schedules, a CI pipeline that runs tests regardless of the developer's hunch. And an agent's own *goal variation* is such an instrument exactly when its goals carry no information about outcomes beyond what its model carries. Exploration, in the sense the chapter already argues for at the structural-detection layer, is here a structural requirement at the identification layer too, for the same reason: on-policy, the data cannot tell you about the roads not taken.

**The owed question has a precise answer.** The sufficiency the value object needs is: the dropped part of the decision state carries no information about what each action *would* produce. What an agent can estimate from inside is the on-policy version: the dropped part carries no extra information about what *did* happen given what it *did*. These coincide exactly when every action has positive probability at every state (then "what did happen given what it did" covers every action everywhere), and come apart otherwise — a deterministic policy passes the on-policy test for free and the interventional one for nothing. So `#def-model-sufficiency` is the right kind of quantity, and it is the needed one under positivity. Separately, its formula conditions on the agent's *realized* future actions, which in closed loop are downstream of the very observations being predicted; a two-bit example (an agent whose next action echoes what it just saw) scores full sufficiency while its model has dropped a bit the next observation depends on. The segment's prose reads as if actions were held fixed from outside; the formula should say that, or use the interventional form.

## What I found that nobody asked about

Canon's value object $Q_O$ conditions on the model $M_t$ alone (`#def-value-object:35`), and defends this by saying directed separation handles the goal side and only "latent environment confounders that bypass $M_t$" remain. But canon's own policy reads $(M_t, G_t)$ [`#form-complete-agent-state`], and canon's own edge-credence learner [`#hyp-edge-update-via-gain`] stores Beta–Bernoulli outcome counts in the strategy state $\Sigma_t \subseteq G_t$, incremented directly from outcomes. Those counts are facts about the world living in the goal state. Directed separation forbids the goal from reaching the belief update; it says nothing about the purposeful update absorbing world-information from events, which `#der-directed-separation:38` explicitly permits. So for an agent canon itself describes — directed-separated, $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$ exactly — the $M_t$-conditioned value object is confounded by the agent's own strategy state. I simulated it: a hidden regime that makes action 1 good or bad, a model that carries nothing about the regime, strategy counts that learn it, an $\varepsilon$-greedy policy on the counts. The agent's own estimate of action 1's value is inflated by about a tenth when it conditions on the model; the inflation vanishes when it conditions on model-plus-strategy. The residual confounder in `#def-value-object:50` is not outside the agent. It is $G_t$.

Three honest repairs, for the integrators to choose between (the third, least invasive: keep the value object as defined, identify at the full state, and average over the goal states compatible with the model); let $Q_O$ condition on the whole state the agent acts from (then identification of the query is automatic and the price is positivity there); or make it a stated premise that the goal carries no outcome-information beyond the model — and move the edge counts into the model where `#def-strategy-dag:48` already writes them as living. The same premise is needed by the C3 sibling spike (allocation vs distortion) and, I found afterward, is silently used by the behavioral-floor paper's (C2★) proof. One premise, three consumers, no name yet.

## Things that got re-typed on the way

- The explorer's three "named failures" are not parallel. Compression that drops outcome-relevant decision inputs is a sufficiency failure; determinism is a positivity failure; "un-introspectable causes" is a violation of the lemma's premise — and inside the formalism it has to be one of: the executed action differing from the decided one (the "already tired" body modulating what gets done — intention-to-treat territory), the recorded state differing from the one the policy actually used, or a scope exit from mediated access. Naming which tells the agent what to log, randomize, or declare. The literature's version is sharper still: what identification needs readable is the agent's *intent* — its pre-execution would-be action — not the reasons for it (Bareinboim–Forney–Pearl's intent papers; for a language model that readout is the pre-sampling action distribution).
- The known-mechanism condition is the intention-to-treat gap: adjusting for the decision state identifies the effect of *deciding* $a$; the effect of the *executed* action needs the execution mechanism known and its noise unconfounded. For planning the agent usually wants the former; for diagnosing why an action failed it needs the latter.
- The Working-Note line "goal-conditioned LLM policies violate (C2) by construction" is a category slip: the goal is a parent of the action, i.e. part of the adjustment set, and the environment does not read it. A goal-coupled belief update biases the *model* — a belief-quality problem — and leaves the action–outcome pair unconfounded given the agent's state. `#impl-causal-access` already says canon makes no such claim; the Working Note contradicts it; and the attribution to NeurIPS Paper 3 is not supported by that paper's text. (A May spike, `spike-c2-star-to-integrate.md`, made the same diagnosis and sits unlanded as a reserved orphan at `ROUTING.md:43`; the coordinator relayed it mid-run and I have reconciled the two in the derivation.)
- What *is* true of a language-model agent: within a session its hidden activations are functions of the context plus sampling noise with no channel to the world but the action — mediators, not confounders. Its real exposures are peaked or temperature-zero decoding (positivity) and context turnover: after a compaction, the agent's own forgotten earlier context is an unmeasured confounder of the action–outcome pairs it learns from, unless the retained summary is outcome-sufficient. Self-confounding through forgetting. That is a Volume 3 claim with a test: agents that log tool-call contexts or propensities should show unconfounded effect estimates where agents learning from compressed memory show the inflation above.

## Where the field is

Nothing I derived is new mathematics, and I say so in the derivation. The by-construction statement is Dawid & Constantinou 2014's "control strategy" and, most recently, "Experimental NUC" in Bareinboim, Zhang & Lee's June 2026 causal-RL survey (arXiv 2606.24160), which postdates most of canon's prior-art work and should be the citation of record. The condition-on-your-own-intent-then-randomize-within device is Bareinboim–Forney–Pearl 2015 / 2017. Ortega & Braun 2008 said "actions are interventions, observations are conditions" from the inference side; Ortega et al. 2021's "self-delusion" (over-reading one's own action as evidence) turns out to be the mirror image of self-confounding (under-conditioning the outcome), and the two prescriptions coincide exactly under the lemma's premise. What is AAT's here is the identity of the conditioning set — the agent's own $(M_t, G_t)$, which it holds without any analyst — and the consequences for canon's own objects: which sentence is wrong, which premise the value object has been using without stating, and what the agent's two levers are.
