# Neighborhood map — loop identification conditional on the decision state

*Written 2026-10-30 14:20 MDT by the C2 spiker (Fable), before any derivation, from whole reads of the segments named below, the Part II outline, the 2026-05-30 transportability spike, NeurIPS Paper 2 Key Lemma 3 and its proof, and the owner-review explorer §3.4/§7.1. The map's job is to say what canon already holds that bears on the question, where the tensions are, and what the proposed strengthening would genuinely add — so that the derivation does not inherit an ill-posed brief. Line numbers refer to the files at HEAD `8ef2862e`.*

## 1. The question as posed, and what the brief got right

The integrator item (owner-review, C2) proposes, verbatim: *"Own action-outcome data identifies the effect of an action, given the decision state, exactly when the learner conditions on everything the action depended on apart from environment-independent randomization, and that randomization gives positivity; three named failures (compressed-away inputs; un-introspectable causes; deterministic policies). Replaces `der-loop-interventional-access.md:41`'s 'fails generically'. Owed: whether `#def-model-sufficiency` is the sufficiency needed; the (C1)–(C3) gate rewrite."*

The mathematics underneath it is Pearl's adjustment for the direct causes of the treatment (parent adjustment, a special case of the back-door criterion), conditioned rather than averaged, applied to an agent whose action's parents are its own decision inputs. That mathematics is standard and correct. What the brief leaves to this spike is (a) whether the sentence at `:41` is actually wrong, or only about a different conditioning set than (C2) names; (b) which conditioning set each canon segment actually uses, and whether canon's own learner satisfies the premise at that set; (c) the positivity side, which the explorer flagged as "the price" and which I believe is where the only non-removable gate lives; (d) the owed sufficiency question.

## 2. What canon already establishes (read, not inferred)

**The loop segment itself.** `#der-loop-interventional-access` (status `exact`, stage `draft`): the exact content is *availability of the interventional-character channel*; identification of $P(o \mid do(a), \cdot)$ is gated on (C1) positivity "every action in the relevant support is taken with nonzero probability across the agent's history", (C2) sequential ignorability "$a_t$ is $d$-separated from $o_{t+1}$ given history $H_t$" in the mutilated graph, (C3) known action-mechanism. Line 41, verbatim: *"Where (C2) fails — generic for any agent whose action depends on internal state, plans, or memory that also bear on the outcome — on-policy $(a_t, o_{t+1})$ converges to $\mathbb{E}[o \mid A = a]$, the conditional, rather than to $\mathbb{E}[o \mid do(a)]$, the interventional effect."* Note what this sentence does: it names (C2) as a condition *given $H_t$*, then describes the failure as convergence to the *marginal* $\mathbb E[o \mid A=a]$ — a quantity that conditions on nothing. The (C2) gate and the failure it describes are about two different conditioning sets. This is the seam the strengthening sits on.

**Where (C1)–(C3) came from.** The 2026-05-30 transportability spike (`spikes/.integrated/spike-causal-access-transportability-2026-05-30.md` §3.1) lifted the triple from NeurIPS Paper 2 into the body, with the reasoning: "Where the policy *is* confounded (generic for any agent with internal state, plans, or memory — the segment itself says so two segments upstream in `#der-causal-hierarchy-requirement`), on-policy $(a_t, o_{t+1})$ is associational with respect to the latent confounder, and converges to $\mathbb E[R \mid s, A=a]$." The "generic" sentence at `:41` is that spike's wording, carried into canon on 2026-05-31 (commit `52d85b2`). Its warrant is `#der-causal-hierarchy-requirement:52`'s RL example — which the owner review's item A11 already finds false for a fully observed MDP (where the policy depends only on $s$, so $\mathbb E[R \mid s, do(a)] = \mathbb E[R \mid s, A=a]$). So `:41` inherited its genericity claim from a sentence that is itself under correction.

**NeurIPS Paper 2, Key Lemma 3** (`~/src/neurips/02-unified-convergence-rl/src/re/05-mechanism.md:31`, proof at `B-key-lemma-proofs.md:92–100`). The lemma's $H_t$ is "the agent's information state at round $t$ (model state $M_t$, history of observations and actions)"; the policy is $\pi_t(a \mid H_t)$; the proof is literally parent adjustment: *"the only arrows into $a_t$ come from $H_t$ … deleting them severs the only confounding path."* The proof's own remark: *"(C1) reduces to realized-action positivity ($\pi_t(a_t \mid H_t) \gt 0$ tautologically since $a_t$ was drawn from $\pi_t$); (C3) reduces to agent-policy-queryability … for the on-policy identification claim of this lemma, (C2) is the substantive condition."* Two observations. First, the lemma already *is* the decision-state strengthening, at the conditioning set $H_t$; the explorer's §7.1 rediscovers it. Second, the "tautological positivity" remark identifies $P(o \mid do(a_t), H_t)$ only *at the realized action* — which is exactly the on-policy conditional and says nothing about any $a' \neq a_t$. For the value object, which needs $Q_O(M_t, a)$ *across* $a$, that positivity is not tautological at all. The canon segment's (C1) ("every action in the relevant support") is the one that does work; Paper 2's remark undersells it.

**The value object.** `#def-value-object:35` defines $Q_O(M_t, a) = \mathbb E[V_{O_t}(\tau) \mid M_t, do(a_t = a), a_{t+1:} \sim \pi_{\text{cont}}]$ — conditioned on $M_t$, not on $H_t$ and not on $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$. Its (C2) line (`:50`): *"Directed separation delivers (C2) on the epistemic-processing leg — a goal-blind $f_M$ blocks the $G_t \to f_M \to M^+$ confounding path — but does not by itself rule out latent environment confounders that bypass $M_t$."* The segment's Working Notes (2026-05-31) say the $G_t$-independence of the *query* consumes only $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$. The identifiability of the *estimate* from $M_t$ is the stronger claim, gated on (C1)–(C3).

**The policy's inputs.** `#form-complete-agent-state:38`: $a_t = \pi(M_t, G_t)$ — *"Action is the single point where the two substates interact."* So within AAT's own formulation the parents of $a_t$ are exactly $(M_t, G_t)$ plus whatever randomization $\pi$ uses. `#der-recursive-update` constraints: C1 temporal ordering, C2 mediated access ("the agent has no direct access to the environment beyond events"), C3 completeness ($M_t$ retains everything the agent retains — lifted to $X_t$ by `#form-complete-agent-state`). Together these three commitments say: every cause of $a_t$ is in $X_t$ or is the policy's own randomization; the environment reaches $X_t$ only through events, i.e. through the chronica. That is the structural fact the whole result rides on, and it is already canon.

**Directed separation.** `#der-directed-separation:36–38`: $M_{\tau^+} = f_M(M_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$ with no $G_t$ argument; $G_{\tau^+} = f_G(G_{\tau^-}, M_{\tau^+}, e_\tau)$ — the purposeful update takes the event *directly*. Directed separation constrains $f_M$; it says nothing about $f_G$ absorbing world-information from $e_\tau$. The segment's own scope line: separation is about *processing*, not *selection* — goal-driven action is the permitted channel.

**Where $G_t$ can carry world-information.** `#def-strategy-dag:48` writes the edge credence as $p_{ij} = \mathrm{Cr}(j \text{ advances} \mid i \text{ completed}, M_t)$ — conditional on $M_t$. But `#hyp-edge-update-via-gain` stores Beta–Bernoulli counts $(\alpha_{ij}, \beta_{ij})$ in $\Sigma_t$, incremented directly by outcomes; those counts are statistics of the chronica living in $G_t$. `#deriv-decomposition-uniqueness` (R2) "purposeful purity" and (R3) "epistemic completeness" force into $M$ only what goal-intervention cannot move; a goal re-initialization wipes $\Sigma_t$ with its counts, so learned credences are not forced into $M$ by that result. So canon leaves it open — and in one live segment realizes the possibility — that $G_t$ holds outcome-relevant information not in $M_t$. This matters for the $M_t$-rung below.

**Model sufficiency.** `#def-model-sufficiency:22`: $S(M_t) = 1 - I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1:\infty} \mid M_t, a_{t:\infty}) / I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1:\infty} \mid a_{t:\infty})$ — conditioning on the *realized* future action sequence. Discussion (`:45`): *"For agents whose actions are deterministic functions of $M_t$ … $S(M_t) = 1$ is nearly sufficient for causal validity — the remaining requirement is that no unmodeled external factor influences both action selection and outcomes."* Working Notes follow-up: this sentence should name its back-door warrant. The segment also flags policy-relativity of $S$ (`:47`). Two things to test: whether the realized-action conditioning in the formula is the right object for the causal question (future actions are descendants of $o_{t+1}$ in closed loop; conditioning on descendants can create or absorb dependence), and whether "deterministic functions of $M_t$" is the case where the claim is *weakest*, since determinism kills positivity.

**The agent-as-learner.** `#der-recursive-update:30`: the learner is $f_M(M_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$ — it never re-reads $\mathcal C_t$. So whatever AAT's own agent conditions on when it processes an outcome is the model state it holds at that moment, not the full history. The decision was made from $(M_t, G_t)$; the outcome is processed by $f_M$ reading $M_t$ (and, after the lift, by $f_G$ reading $(G_t, M_{t+1}, e)$). The gap between what the policy read and what each learner reads is where confounding would enter *inside* the formalism.

**The chapter intro's confounder.** `#causal-access-intro:19`: *"the agent was already tired, or the environment was already shifting."* Under mediated access there is no variable that affects $a_t$ except through $X_t$; "already tired" has to be either (i) a body state that is part of the environment and modulates the *executed* action — a decision→execution gap, which is (C3)'s territory; (ii) a component of the actual decision state not present in the *recorded* $X_t$ — a record-faithfulness failure; or (iii) a scope exit from mediated access. None of these is a (C2)-at-$X_t$ failure. The explorer's "un-introspectable cause" failure mode therefore needs re-typing against the formalism, not inheriting.

**Related canon claims that bear on the answer.** `#def-pearl-causal-hierarchy:41` ("the agent chose the action (it was not determined by the same causes that determine the observation)") is the textual hook for unconfoundedness; its Kalman+LQR row already says "structural channel present, identification not guaranteed (the LQR action is endogenous to the estimation loop)" — which, read through the result below, is a *positivity* failure (deterministic $a_t = -K\hat x_t$), not an ignorability one. `#scope-ciy-observational-proxy` Regime A: "randomized, or known-and-adjusted so that sequential ignorability and positivity hold … mere action variation is not sufficient when the variation is policy-driven by state or history that also bears on the outcome." `#scope-edge-update-causal-validity` (C3) "execution conditions vary" mixes selection (ignorability) with variation (positivity), as the explorer noted. `#der-causal-insufficiency-detection` is the sibling no-go at the *structural-detection* layer: on-policy data cannot detect a latent common cause of two sibling plan steps; its escape routes include $\varepsilon$-exploration, i.e. positivity. `#impl-causal-access:118` keeps the loop's (C2) distinct from directed separation and says *"Whether Coupled components also fail the loop's sequential-ignorability condition is a claim canon does not make"* — while `#der-loop-interventional-access:90` (Working Notes) says *"Goal-conditioned LLM policies violate (C2) by construction"* and attributes this to NeurIPS Paper 3. I grepped Paper 3's `src/re/` for "ignorab", "confound", "d-separat": no hits; Paper 3's named (C2) is "Deterministic Bayesian update" (`E-proofs.md:55`). The Working-Note attribution is not supported by the paper's current text.

**The A5 collision.** Five distinct "(C1)–(C3)" ladders exist in Volume 1 (integrator item A5); the loop's triple shares labels with `#scope-edge-update-causal-validity`'s (action-leaf / attributable / conditions-vary), with `#der-recursive-update`'s constraints, with the wrapping conditions, and with the value-object conventions. Any gate rewrite from this spike should propose a distinct index family rather than add a sixth meaning to "(C2)".

## 3. Live tensions the derivation must respect

1. **Which conditioning set.** Three sets are in play and canon slides between them: $H_t$ (the loop segment's (C2), Paper 2), $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$ (the policy's actual inputs), $M_t$ alone (the value object). Identification is a different claim at each. The derivation must be a *ladder*, each rung with its own hypothesis, not one sentence.
2. **Positivity is where the real gate is.** Once ignorability is secured by conditioning on the policy's inputs, the price is support: a deterministic policy gives $P(a \mid X_t) \in \{0,1\}$, so nothing off-policy is identified at that $X_t$. "Exploration is a structural requirement" (already in canon at the detection layer) acquires its identification-layer form here. Any coarsening to recover support re-opens ignorability unless the coarse set is sufficient — the standard overlap–ignorability tension, with the agent in the unusual position of controlling both sides (what it retains, how it randomizes).
3. **Canon's own strategy learner may violate the $M_t$-rung premise.** If $\Sigma_t$ holds outcome statistics (`#hyp-edge-update-via-gain`), then $G_t$ is an outcome-relevant parent of $a_t$ not screened by $M_t$, and $Q_O$'s $M_t$-conditioning is a confounded set *for an agent canon itself describes*, even at $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$. If that holds, `#def-value-object:50`'s "directed separation delivers (C2) on the epistemic-processing leg" is the sentence that is wrong, not `:41`.
4. **The sufficiency definition's realized-action conditioning.** The owed question ("is `#def-model-sufficiency` the sufficiency needed?") turns on whether $S$'s conditioning on $a_{t:\infty}$ (realized, closed-loop) coincides with the interventional conditioning a causal question needs. I expect the answer to be "the *interventional* reading of $S$ is exactly the needed sufficiency; the formula as written is the on-policy reading, and the two coincide under positivity and can differ without it." To be derived, with a counterexample if the gap is real.
5. **The explorer's LLM claim.** "Action-determining latents are not exposed to any learner, including themselves" — in a tool-use loop the hidden activations are a function of the logged context plus sampling noise, with no channel to the environment except $a_t$; they are mediators, not confounders. The real LLM issues are compression across context turnover (the later learner conditions on a summary that dropped the earlier decision inputs) and peaked or temperature-0 decoding (positivity). The derivation should test this before anything about Volume 3 inherits the explorer's framing.
6. **Failure mode (ii) is a scope boundary.** Per the chapter-intro reading above: "un-introspectable cause" is, inside the formalism, either record-unfaithfulness ($X_t^{\text{recorded}} \neq X_t^{\text{actual}}$), a decision→execution gap ((C3)), or a scope exit from mediated access. The theorem's premise should be stated so that this is visible, rather than listing it as a parallel third failure.

## 4. What the strengthening would genuinely add, if it holds

- A corrected statement at `:41`: (C2)-given-$H_t$ holds by construction for any agent whose action is a function of its recorded inputs plus environment-independent randomization; what fails "generically" is identification at any *coarser* set the learner can afford, and positivity at the fine set.
- The $M_t$-rung condition, named: goal-purity-given-model, $Y^{(a)} \perp G_t \mid M_t$ — the converse of directed separation (world does not reach the policy through $G_t$ except via $M_t$). The C3 sibling spike needs the same assumption in its allocation/distortion split ("the goal carries no world information beyond the prior state"); the integration should give it one name.
- A precise answer to the owed sufficiency question, with the interventional form of $S$.
- The positivity–granularity trade-off as the one non-removable gate, with the deterministic-policy no-go and the "goal variation is the agent's own instrument iff goal-purity" corollary.
- The (C3) reading as intention-to-treat: decision-state adjustment identifies the effect of the *decision*; the executed action's effect needs the execution mechanism.
- For Volume 3: context turnover as self-confounding — the agent's forgotten earlier context becomes an unmeasured confounder of its own action–outcome pairs; the sufficiency of the retained summary is the gate.
