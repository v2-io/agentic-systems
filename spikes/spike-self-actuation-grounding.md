Per the brief and verified against `der-orient-cascade.md` step 5d,
`form-objective-functional.md`, and `def-strategy-dimension.md`'s
`Update source` row:

- **𝔄 is the internalization of step 5d.** The orient cascade
  ( `#der-orient-cascade` ) terminates, when $\delta_{\text{sat}} \gt 0$
  persists across $M_t$-correction, $\Pi$-expansion, and convention
  escalation, in step **5d: revise $O_t$**. Define the *self-actuation
  operator*

  $$\mathfrak{A}: (M_t,\, O_t,\, \Sigma_t,\, \mathcal{C}_t) \;\mapsto\; O_t'$$

  An **actuated** agent's step-5d exits to a principal: $O_t'$ is
  externally sourced (this is precisely the `Update source: External`
  cell for $O_t$ in `#def-strategy-dimension`). A **self-actuated** agent
  performs step-5d on itself: $\mathfrak{A}$ is an agent operation, goal
  autonomy stacked on the solution autonomy that $\Sigma_t$-revision
  already gives every actuated agent.

- **Unconstrained 𝔄 is degenerate.** $O_t$'s *sole* interface to the
  theory is $V_{O_t}:\text{trajectories}\to\mathbb{R}$
  ( `#form-objective-functional` ). The satisfaction gap is
  $\delta_{\text{sat}} = V_{O_t}^{\min} - A_O(M_t;\Pi,N_h)$
  ( `#def-satisfaction-gap` ). If $\mathfrak{A}$ may return *any*
  $O_t'$, it returns the $O_t'$ whose $V_{O_t'}^{\min}$ is met by the
  current trajectory — drive $\delta_{\text{sat}}\to 0$ by moving the
  target onto the arrow already in flight. This is the formal shadow of
  wireheading / reward corruption (prior art §5). It is not a marginal
  failure: it is the *generic* outcome of an unconstrained $\mathfrak{A}$,
  because $\arg\min$ over all objectives of $\delta_{\text{sat}}$ always
  contains a trivially-satisfied objective.

The open hard part is everything between "unconstrained 𝔄 is degenerate"
and "here is the exact class of well-formed 𝔄."

## 2. The strong claim, made exact

### 2.1 Well-formedness forces an invariant

**Claim WF.** A self-actuation operator $\mathfrak{A}$ is *non-degenerate*
only if there exists a functional $\Phi$ — fixed across the revision —
such that every admissible revision preserves $\Phi$:

$$\Phi(O_t') = \Phi(O_t) \quad\text{(or }\Phi(O_t')\ \succeq\ \Phi(O_t)\text{ under a fixed order)} \qquad \forall\, O_t' \in \mathrm{range}(\mathfrak{A}).$$

*Argument.* "Non-degenerate" means: it is not the case that for every
reachable trajectory there is an admissible $O_t'$ making
$\delta_{\text{sat}}\le 0$ vacuously. Equivalently, the admissible set
$\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{adm}} = \mathrm{range}(\mathfrak{A})$ is a *proper*
subset of all objectives, and the restriction that carves
$\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{adm}}$ out cannot itself depend only on
"is $\delta_{\text{sat}}$ small" (that is the degenerate selector).
A restriction on the set of admissible objectives that is invariant
across the revision *is* a map from objectives to "admissible / how
admissible," i.e. a functional $\Phi$ on objectives that the revision
holds fixed or monotone. There is no third thing: either the revision is
unconstrained (degenerate), or it is constrained by something held
constant across it, and anything held constant across a map on objectives
and used to *rank/admit* them is a functional of the objective. $\square$

This is not new physics; it is the exact AAT-internal restatement of
Everitt et al.'s observation that tamper-resistance requires a *reference
point* the agent does not get to move (prior art §5). The contribution
here is that AAT's single-interface commitment
( `#form-objective-functional` : $O_t$'s only handle is $V_{O_t}$) makes
the step *forced* rather than a design choice: there is nowhere else for
the constraint to live.

### 2.2 The recursion, stated precisely

By WF, a non-degenerate $\mathfrak{A}$ presupposes $\Phi$. But $\Phi$
ranks objectives and is held fixed/monotone across the revision —
**$\Phi$ has exactly the type of an objective's value functional, one
level up.** Concretely: define the *meta-trajectory* of an agent that
revises its objective to be the sequence
$\big(O_t, O_{t'}, O_{t''},\dots\big)$ of objectives it adopts along its
single causal trajectory $\mathcal{C}_t$ ( `#scope-agent-identity` — the
trajectory is what makes this sequence well-defined and non-forkable).
Then $\Phi$ is a value functional on meta-trajectories:

$$\Phi:\ \{\text{objective-sequences along }\mathcal{C}_t\}\ \to\ \mathbb{R}.$$

That is *literally* the `#form-objective-functional` type signature with
"trajectory" instantiated as "objective-sequence." So the agent that
self-actuates under invariant $\Phi$ **is an actuated agent whose
objective is $\Phi$ and whose action space includes "revise $O_t$."**
Self-actuation at level $0$ is actuation at level $1$. The question
recurses: is $\mathfrak{A}^{(1)}$ (revision of $\Phi$ itself) groundless?
By WF applied at level $1$, non-degenerate $\mathfrak{A}^{(1)}$
presupposes $\Phi^{(1)}$, and so on.

### 2.3 The well-foundedness step (this is the load-bearing argument)

Let the **meta-objective tower** be
$\Phi^{(0)} = O_t,\ \Phi^{(1)} = \Phi,\ \Phi^{(2)},\dots$, where each
$\Phi^{(k+1)}$ is the invariant that makes revision of $\Phi^{(k)}$
non-degenerate (WF, applied at level $k$).

**Result G (well-foundedness / no groundless self-actuation).**
Exactly one of the following holds for the tower:

1. **Terminal grounding.** The tower is finite: there is a top level
   $K$ at which $\Phi^{(K)}$ is *not revised by the agent* — a terminal
   grounding invariant. The whole tower is then well-formed and
   non-degenerate, anchored by $\Phi^{(K)}$.

2. **Infinite ascent.** The tower is infinite: every level's invariant
   is itself an agent-revised objective. Then $\mathfrak{A}$ is
   degenerate.

*Argument for (2)⇒degenerate.* Suppose every $\Phi^{(k)}$ is revised by
the agent ($\mathfrak{A}^{(k)}$ exists and is an agent operation, no
level exempt). The agent's behavior along $\mathcal{C}_t$ is produced by
its policy $\pi(M_t, G_t)$ with $G_t=(O_t,\Sigma_t)$ —
**a single, finite computation per step** (this is a hard structural
fact, not an assumption: `#der-directed-separation`'s update functions
$f_M, f_G$ and the policy $\pi$ are the *entire* dynamical system; there
is no oracle layer outside $(M_t, G_t, \pi)$ in AAT's scope). A single
finite computation can consult only finitely many levels of the tower
before it must emit an action. Let $L$ be the deepest level any step
actually consults. Levels $\gt L$ never enter $\pi$; they are causally
inert — by `#der-directed-separation` they have no path to $a_t$ that
does not pass through some consulted $\Phi^{(k)},\ k\le L$. So the
agent's *effective* invariant is $\Phi^{(L)}$, and by hypothesis (2)
$\Phi^{(L)}$ is itself revised by $\mathfrak{A}^{(L)}$ with **no
invariant above it that the agent actually consults**. An invariant with
nothing the agent consults constraining its revision is, by WF read
contrapositively at level $L$, exactly the unconstrained case:
$\mathfrak{A}^{(L)}$ can move $\Phi^{(L)}$ to whatever makes the
level-$L$ gap vacuous, which propagates down (each level's
non-degeneracy was *contingent* on the level above; collapse the top
consulted level and the contingency chain unwinds) to make
$\delta_{\text{sat}}$ at level $0$ trivially satisfiable. Hence
degenerate. $\square$

The key move — and the reason this is exact, not heuristic — is that
**AAT already commits to a finite per-step computation with no
out-of-band oracle** (`#der-directed-separation`: the dynamical system is
exactly $f_M, f_G, \pi$; `#scope-agent-identity`: a single non-forkable
causal trajectory). "Consults only finitely many levels before acting"
is not a boundedness assumption added for the proof; it is forced by the
scope AAT *already has*. An agent that genuinely needed to evaluate an
infinite tower before each action would have to *not act* (no finite
$\pi$ output) — i.e., it is "stuck, not purposeful," exactly the
disqualification `#form-objective-functional` Epistemic Status §1
(revealed preference) already uses to justify the scalar codomain. The
no-infinite-regress conclusion rides on a commitment the theory has
already paid for.

**Therefore the strong claim is exact:** *non-degenerate self-actuation
requires the meta-objective tower to bottom out on a terminal grounding
invariant the agent does not revise. Groundless self-actuation
(infinite ascent, every level agent-revised) is degenerate.* The
grounding invariant is the formal object that makes the difference
between a self-actuated agent and a wirehead.

### 2.4 What the terminal grounding invariant can be (the constructive half)

Result G says the tower must bottom out; it does not by itself say *on
what*. AAT already contains at least three candidate terminal invariants,
which is why the result is constructive and not merely a no-go:

- **The persistence bound** ( `#result-persistence-condition` ).
  A revision $O_t \mapsto O_t'$ that pushes the agent's operating point
  outside the persistence region — $R^\ast \ge \lVert\delta_{\text{critical}}\rVert$,
  or below the structural threshold $\alpha \le \rho/R$ — is
  *self-defeating*: the agent that adopts it cannot maintain bounded
  mismatch and so cannot reliably satisfy $O_t'$ either. "Do not revise
  $O_t$ to an objective whose pursuit breaks your own persistence
  condition" is a terminal invariant that is **not itself an objective
  the agent picks** — it is a property of the correction dynamics
  ( `#result-persistence-condition` is `type: result, status: exact`,
  living on $M_t$ and the correction machinery, *not* on $O_t$).
  This is the cleanest candidate $\Phi^{(K)}$: it is exact, it is
  architectural rather than chosen, and it bottoms the tower on Section I
  machinery rather than on a further objective. It is the AAT-internal
  analog of a non-revisable reference point — and unlike Everitt's
  $R_{\text{now}}$ (an arbitrary externally-fixed snapshot), it is
  *principled*: the bound is forced by the dynamics, not stipulated.

- **The continuity stance**, for stances that are terminal
  ( `#disc-continuity-stance` ). For a *morally-continuous* agent,
  "loss of continuity constitutes harm" is a terminal component of
  $O_t$; for a *negotiated* agent it is renegotiable. Result G gives the
  exact reason `#disc-continuity-stance` line ~44's assertion is true and
  makes it *derivable* (the brief flagged this as desired): the negotiated
  stance is "the only stance that is internally renegotiable" precisely
  because in a negotiated agent the continuity term sits *below* the
  terminal grounding invariant (it is a consulted $\Phi^{(k)},\ k\lt K$,
  hence revisable), whereas in a morally-continuous agent the continuity
  term *is* (part of) $\Phi^{(K)}$ and so is not revisable without
  degeneracy. The orthogonality claim of `#disc-continuity-stance` plus
  Result G yields: *stance is the agent's choice of where in the tower
  the continuity term sits; "negotiated" = continuity below $\Phi^{(K)}$,
  "morally continuous" = continuity in $\Phi^{(K)}$.* This is a strict
  strengthening of the existing discussion-grade assertion to a derived
  one.

- **An exogenous principal commitment.** The actuated-agent case is the
  degenerate-tower-height-1 case: $\Phi^{(1)}$ is "do what the principal
  assigns," not revised by the agent. Actuated and self-actuated are then
  the *same* construction at different tower heights — actuated = tower
  bottoms at level 1 on an external principal; self-actuated = tower
  bottoms at level $K\ge 1$ on an agent-internal terminal invariant
  (persistence, terminal stance, or a constitutional objective the agent
  treats as non-revisable). This unifies `#def-strategy-dimension`'s
  External/Internal `Update source` cut: it is not a binary, it is the
  *height at which the tower grounds*.

## 3. The conditional (b): the fixed-point objective is real — and it is grounded

The brief correctly flags the genuine adversary: a *fixed-point*
objective — an $O_t$ that is a fixed point of its own revision under the
agent's current evaluation, $\mathfrak{A}(O_t)=O_t$. The
reflective-oracle / self-referential-utility literature exists precisely
to build well-defined self-referential value. Does a non-degenerate
fixed-point $\mathfrak{A}$ refute Result G?

**No — it instantiates it. Here is the exact reason.**

A fixed point $O^\star$ with $\mathfrak{A}(O^\star)=O^\star$ is selected
by *some* rule: $O^\star = \mathrm{fix}(\mathfrak{A})$ under a chosen
fixed-point operator (least fixed point, the Kakutani/Brouwer fixed point
the reflective oracle delivers, a contraction's unique fixed point,
etc.). Two cases, and only two:

- **(b-grounded) The fixed-point selection rule is held fixed across the
  revision and ranks/admits objectives.** Then *the selection rule is
  exactly $\Phi$* (WF, §2.1): "be a fixed point of your own evaluation
  under operator $\mathrm{fix}(\cdot)$" is a functional on the
  meta-trajectory that the agent does not revise. The fixed-point
  objective is non-degenerate, well-formed — **and the terminal grounding
  invariant is the fixed-point selection rule itself.** The tower has
  height $K$ with $\Phi^{(K)} = $ "select $\mathrm{fix}(\mathfrak{A})$."
  This is Result G branch (1), not a counterexample. The recursion did
  not vanish; it bottomed out on the *commitment to take a fixed point a
  particular way*.

- **(b-degenerate) The fixed-point selection rule is itself revisable by
  the agent.** Then the agent can choose *which* fixed point (every
  trivially-satisfied $O_t'$ is a fixed point of "revise toward what's
  already satisfied" — the degenerate operator's fixed-point set is
  enormous and includes the wirehead). With the selector revisable, the
  agent picks the fixed point that minimizes $\delta_{\text{sat}}$:
  degenerate. This is Result G branch (2).

So the fixed-point construction is **not** groundless self-actuation; it
is self-actuation grounded *on the choice of fixed-point operator*. The
existence of a well-defined fixed point (which reflective oracles
guarantee — they always exist, by randomizing the tie case to dodge
diagonalization) is a statement about the *evaluation* being
self-consistent, **not** about the *evaluation criterion being
self-generated**. The reflective oracle takes as *given* an exogenous
utility/payoff that it then finds a consistent fixed point *of*; it does
not manufacture that utility. In AAT terms: reflective oracles solve
"given $\Phi^{(K)}$, does a consistent $O^\star$ exist?" — they answer
**yes, robustly**. They are silent on "may $\Phi^{(K)}$ be agent-revised
with nothing above it?" — which is the actual content of Result G, and
the answer there is still **no**.

**This is the precise sense in which (c) fails: the literature builds
groundless self-*reference*, never groundless self-*actuation*.**
Self-reference (a value function that mentions itself / other reasoners
and is still consistent) is exactly what reflective oracles deliver, and
AAT does not need to deny it. Self-*actuation* groundlessness (the
evaluation criterion at the top of the tower is itself something the
agent freely rewrites with no fixed reference) is what Result G forbids,
and nothing in the reflective-oracle or fixed-point-utility line
constructs that — *because every such construction starts from an
exogenous payoff/utility it then makes self-consistent*. Conflating the
two is the trap; keeping them apart is the whole resolution.

## 6. Exactly where the strong version would break (the honest edges)

Result G is exact **given two premises AAT already carries**. Naming them
precisely is the failure record that prevents future re-attempts and
tells Joseph exactly what a future segment's Epistemic Status must state:

1. **Finite per-step computation, no out-of-band oracle (load-bearing).**
   The (2)⇒degenerate argument needs "the policy consults only finitely
   many tower levels before emitting $a_t$." This is *forced* by
   `#der-directed-separation` (the dynamical system is exactly
   $f_M, f_G, \pi$ — no oracle layer) and `#scope-agent-identity`
   (single finite causal trajectory). **Where it would break:** if AAT
   ever admits agents with a genuine reflective-oracle *as a primitive*
   (an out-of-band consistent-fixed-point resource consulted "for free"
   at every step), then the finite-consultation step fails and an
   infinite tower could be discharged in one oracle call. This is the
   one scenario where (c) would become live. **It does not arise in
   current AAT scope** — and the moment it did, AAT would have left the
   "stuck, not purposeful" / revealed-preference commitment that
   `#form-objective-functional` Epistemic Status §1 already makes. So the
   premise is not free-floating; it is entailed by a commitment the
   theory has *already paid for*. A segment must state this scope
   explicitly: *Result G holds for agents without a primitive reflective
   oracle; agents granted such an oracle as a free per-step primitive are
   out of scope (and are the only construction under which groundless
   self-actuation is not refuted).* That is the precise, honest boundary
   — and it is exactly where the (c) literature lives, which is why (c)
   is "refuted-by-scope," not "refuted."

2. **WF's "anything held fixed and used to rank objectives is a
   functional of the objective" (the §2.1 step).** This is tight given
   `#form-objective-functional`'s single-interface commitment ($O_t$'s
   only handle is $V_{O_t}:\text{traj}\to\mathbb{R}$). **Where it would
   break:** under a genuine vector-valued / Pareto objective extension
   ( `#form-objective-functional` Epistemic Status "Scope restriction:
   scalar comparability"; `spikes/.integrated`-adjacent
   `spike-scalar-objective-scope`), $\Phi$ could be an
   *incomparability structure* rather than a functional, and "preserve
   $\Phi$" weakens from an equality/monotonicity to a set-containment.
   Result G's *shape* survives (you still need a non-revised top), but
   "is a value functional one level up" weakens to "is a non-revised
   admissibility structure one level up." A segment should state Result G
   for the scalar-objective scope and flag the vector-valued
   generalization as the same theorem with $\Phi$ retyped — consistent
   with how `#form-objective-functional` already scopes its other
   results. This is a *scope annotation*, not a hole: the strong claim
   does not weaken, its $\Phi$-type does.

Neither edge is a softening of the strong claim. (1) is the precise
scope statement that makes (c) "refuted-*by-scope*" honest rather than
overclaimed. (2) is a $\Phi$-retyping under an already-scoped extension.
The claim *"there is no well-formed groundless self-actuation"* stands
exactly within the scope AAT already occupies.

   Result G from "must bottom out somewhere" to "always bottoms out on

   contrast row to §5 and tie Result G to an existing AAT prior-art
