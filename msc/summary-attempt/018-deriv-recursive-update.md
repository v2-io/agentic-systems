# Recursive Update — Uniqueness Derivation (Appendix A derivation — exact)

This appendix derivation works through the rigorous proof that the recursive update form derived in Part I Chapter 3 is not merely *one* consistent update form but the *unique* one given three constraints: the arrow-of-time postulate, the partial-observability scope condition, and the completeness commitment in the model formulation. The structure is an elimination argument over the universe of information potentially accessible to the update mechanism at any event time: the environment state, the full chronica, all prior model states, the current event, and future events.

The arrow-of-time constraint eliminates dependence on future events (in a classical universe, future information is simply not available; predictions about the future, however accurate, are *internal computations* and therefore part of the prior model state). The partial-observability scope eliminates direct access to the environment except through the lossy observation channel. The completeness commitment compresses *all prior past information* — the full chronica and every prior model state — into the immediately-prior model state, because by construction $M$ is the agent's complete internal state and any information available to the update but not in $M$ would mean $M$ was misspecified. After all three eliminations, only the prior model state and the current event remain in the dependency set, and the update is forced into the recursive form. A measure-theoretic version of the argument via the Doob-Dynkin lemma is supplied as a cleaner technical proof.

The derivation then mounts seven adversarial attacks to test whether the result really holds. Simultaneous events are absorbed by treating the event as a bundle (the form preserves). Continuous environmental coupling is genuinely outside the event-driven formulation — but the analogous result reached by the same three-constraint argument in continuous time is the general state-space representation $\dot{M} = g(M, u)$ from classical control theory, so the underlying structure survives. The objection that the completeness constraint is circular (anything not in $M$ gets absorbed into $M$ by definition) is acknowledged as the deepest objection and the derivation is honest about it: the real content of the claim is an *analytical commitment* that by defining $M$ as complete, one commits to Markovian analysis, which then makes the model-sufficiency measure the right quality metric. Shared state, external randomness, time-dependence, and agents that store full history each reduce to the recursive form by appropriate enlargement of the model space or interpretation of the function.

The honest epistemic accounting at the end is one of the more rigorous parts of the volume. The temporal constraint (C1) is a physical postulate and the partial-observability constraint (C2) is a scope definition — both do *eliminative* work. The completeness constraint (C3) is a *definitional commitment* — it cannot be "violated" because any apparent violation is absorbed by expanding $M$. The recursive form's uniqueness is therefore conditional on accepting the three-constraint set, not on the constraints being independently inescapable. C3 in particular *could* be refused, at the cost of leaving AAT's scope and accepting non-Markovian analysis. The Markov property is not a discovered law but the unique form compatible with the modeling commitment.
