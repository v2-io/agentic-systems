# Persistence Cost (appendix derivation — conditional)

The information-rate cost shadow of persistence. Under Model S with Gaussian-OU signal, **the sustained Shannon information rate the agent must acquire from observations to maintain the sector-persistence ultimate bound is at least $n\alpha/2$ nats per unit time** — a Landauer-analog lower bound that depends only on the signal's second-order statistics and the sector constant, and that the Kalman-Bucy filter *saturates* in steady state.

The bound is *filter-agnostic*: it holds for any correction mechanism achieving the ultimate bound under the stated scope. The bound is also *substrate-agnostic*: Landauer's principle converts it to a thermodynamic dissipation floor in any physical realization. **There is no clever filter that escapes this**. The information rate is what persistence *is*, viewed from the information-theoretic side.

The corollary the framework lifts is **a channel-capacity floor as first-class persistence prerequisite**: the observation channel must supply Shannon capacity at least *tempo / 2* nats per unit time per dimension, or persistence fails regardless of how good the correction function is. This is the formal grounding for *bandwidth-as-binding-constraint* in three regimes: biological systems with finite neural channel capacity (quantitative minimum on sensory bandwidth at any given adaptive rate); bandwidth-constrained distributed systems over noisy or low-bandwidth links; context-window-limited language models where effective information rate per unit of cognition is bounded by context size and token throughput.

The complementary observation: **two agents with identical persistence guarantees can face wildly different sustained demands** because the cost scales linearly with the sector constant. *Faster correction is more expensive in information-rate terms*; the bound is tight; there is no trick. Survival, in this framework, is not a state you achieve once — it is a *sustained burn rate* of information, and the channel that supports it is part of what keeps the agent in scope.
