# Symbiogenic Composition (hypothesis — robust-qualitative)

The framework names an *asymmetric* composition mechanism distinct from peer coupling: **symbiogenesis** — one agent (the *host*) integrates another (the *endosymbiont*) as a specialized sub-component, with the endosymbiont's objective *gradually subsumed* into the host's. Symbiogenesis is *how composite agents come into existence* by crossing the composite-agent scope condition from below. Without it, the sub-agents were a multi-agent system but not a composite; after consolidation, the resulting composite is subject to all of AAT's composition machinery. The phenomenon is empirically well-attested (eukaryote formation, firm mergers, legal-precedent adoption, language families) but formally underspecified within AAT — the segment captures the phenomenon and flags the formalization gaps.

The mechanism is specified by three coupled dynamics. **(S-1) Objective absorption**: the endosymbiont's objective transforms toward derivation from the host's, so route (C-ii) of the composite-agent scope (hierarchical derivation) eventually applies. **(S-2) Function transfer**: structural content from the endosymbiont's state (model and strategy elements) transfers to or becomes accessible by the host — biology's gene transfer; an acquired firm's processes, patents, know-how integrated into the acquirer's operations. This is the *cross-agent* form of the grafting operation from Part II. **(S-3) Autonomy reduction**: the endosymbiont's effective action space contracts; many of its choices become fixed by the host's coordination. The endosymbiont retains *enough* autonomy to avoid catastrophic transfers (mitochondria retain some genome to handle local fast-timescale responses that would be hazardous to route through the host nucleus) but loses most independent decision-making.

The framework is careful to distinguish *three* composition mechanisms now in scope: **peer coupling** (sub-agents already exist and interact through shared environment; closure defect measures faithfulness of projection); **extreme transition motif** (Miller 2022 — population-level restructuring via neutral drift, niche creation, cascading displacement); and **symbiogenesis** (hierarchical absorption — the composite *comes into being* from two previously-independent agents). The framework names *why* symbiogenesis cannot be modeled as either of the others: peer coupling presupposes the composite exists and measures how faithfully the macro tracks the micro, but in symbiogenesis the endosymbiont's *objective itself changes* during the process — no fixed projection yields the post-symbiogenic composite; extreme transitions operate at the population level with many agents and neutral drift, while symbiogenesis is bilateral asymmetric integration between specific agents.

A rate-distortion reading is offered: under the framework's information-bottleneck framing, peer coupling is IB compression with the relevance variable defined by a shared objective. **Symbiogenesis is the process by which the relevance variable itself shifts** — from two separate IB problems (each sub-agent's own survival objective) to a single IB problem (the composite's survival objective). The symbiogenic transition *creates* the shared relevance variable, which in turn makes the IB frontier well-defined for the composite. A structural shift in the IB problem, not a compression along a fixed frontier.

Examples are given across domains: archaeal host + α-proteobacterium → eukaryotic cell (mitochondrion persists as organelle); acquiring firm + acquired firm → merged firm; base platform + integrated component → composite product; host language + adopted vocabulary → creolized language; legal system + adopted precedent → evolved jurisprudence. In each case: asymmetric integration, autonomy reduction of the absorbed entity, gradual objective subsumption, functional specialization.

A speculative but striking observation: under the *hypothesis* of a nonlinear coordination penalty in autonomous multi-agent dynamics, a saddle-node bifurcation analysis would predict that at critical environmental volatility, autonomous multi-agent systems lose all stable equilibria. The symbiogenic escape route is structural: the composite merges sub-agent objectives and state, eliminating the coordination-penalty term and recovering linear dynamics. The analysis would predict **symbiogenesis as a *mathematically forced phase transition* at critical environmental volatility, rather than as a contingent organizational choice**. The framework is honest that this is conditional on deriving the coordination penalty from a more fundamental construct (the natural candidate is shared-intent mutual information between sub-agent models under coupled-evidence regimes) — without that derivation the threshold is a formulation, not a theorem.
