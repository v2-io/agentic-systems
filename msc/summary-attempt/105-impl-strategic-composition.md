# Strategic Composition — Additional Implications (discussion — Part III closing synthesis)

Five threads close Part III.

**(1) Strategic composition lifts contraction to equilibrium.** The strategic-composition derivation closes the framework's contraction-to-equilibrium hand-off that the prior chapter named as a boundary (the three convergent obstructions). The chapter's deepest structural commitment becomes precise: **when sub-agents are individually Class-1 Separated but pursue partially-opposing objectives, the composite is necessarily Class-3 Coupled**. The architectural classification from Part II is *not preserved under composition with goal divergence*. Together with the wrapping construction (Class-3 component → Class-1 composite via external scaffolding) and now this result (Class-1 sub-agents → Class-3 composite under goal divergence), the framework's posture is that the architectural classification is a *property of the dynamics*, not of the components alone. Goal divergence is one of two operations (alongside adversarial coupling pressure) that move modularity in the wrong direction.

A candidate **identifiability-floor Instance 5** is flagged: mechanism-design impossibility theorems (Gibbard-Satterthwaite, Myerson-Satterthwaite, Arrow) map onto strategic composition with the structural shape of the framework's identifiability-floor pattern, with the equilibrium-convergence machinery as the unique broadly-available escape.

**(2) Symbiogenic composition as asymmetric-parameter limit.** The chapter recovers the empirical pattern of biological symbiogenesis (mitochondria, chloroplasts, gut microbiota-host integration) and organizational acquisition (objective absorption, function transfer, autonomy reduction) as the *asymmetric-parameter limit* of the closed-form critical-mass derivation. When sub-agent parameters are strongly asymmetric, the composite sector constant collapses toward the dominant sub-agent's, and the absorbed sub-agent's objectives are progressively folded in through teleological unity. The framework's posture: **symbiogenesis is not a separate kind of composition — it is the asymmetric-parameter limit of the same composition machinery** that handles the matched-Tier dyad and heterogeneous cases.

**(3) Modular safety architectures fail under goal divergence.** The most consequential implication for AI-safety practice. Modular safety architectures of the form "compose modular safety modules with a central planner" are *structurally guaranteed to fail under goal divergence*. The argument is direct: when safety modules and the central planner have non-aligned objectives — *exactly when the safety modules are needed* (to constrain the planner against its own objectives that may produce undesired behavior) — the composite is structurally Class-3 Coupled, and the modular guarantees of the individual safety modules do not transfer to the composite. Red-team penetrations of constitutional AI; mesa-optimizer formation within nominally-modular systems; the recurring difficulty of "scalable oversight" without per-module identifiability — all instantiate the same structural failure.

The framework's prescription is not that modular safety architectures cannot work — it is that they require **either goal-alignment commitment by design** (sub-agents constructed to share objectives, removing the partial-opposition condition) **or Class-3 Coupled treatment from the start** (acknowledging the composite is structurally Coupled and applying the wrapping construction at the composite scope, with the resulting Brooks's-Law tempo cost and structural-leakage bound). Where goal alignment cannot be designed-in by construction — the empirical case for most current deployment settings — the framework prescribes the wrapping construction with *explicit leakage accounting*.

**(4) Agent opacity and the 16-cell adversarial targeting.** Observation quality (how well the agent sees the world) and agent opacity (how well the world sees the agent) are formal duals — both quantify information flow through the agent-environment boundary, in opposite directions, with the same opacity quantity *sign-flipped in value to the agent depending on coupling sign*. Cooperative coupling rewards low opacity (allies must predict to coordinate); adversarial coupling rewards high opacity (adversaries cannot neutralize what they cannot predict). The sign-flip is *not a separate posit*; it falls out of the signed-coupling structure.

The **16-cell joint composition** — four emitter regimes (high/low opacity, cooperative/adversarial) × four recipient regimes (informative / magnitude-shock / structural-shock / ambient-noise) — produces a precise closed-form arg-max for adversarial targeting decisions. The framework therefore *replaces the OODA metaphor* "inside the opponent's loop" *with a precise bilinear optimization problem*: the attacker chooses the cell that maximizes the recipient-side regime-induced repair cost, given the attacker's opacity budget. The framework predicts that high-opacity attackers get *quadratic opacity-leverage on top of quadratic tempo-leverage* — total quartic scaling in the opacity-times-tempo regime. This is the formal grounding for "operating inside the OODA loop": tempo and opacity multiply, and the multiplication is quadratic-in-quadratic at the structural extreme.

**(5) The OODA self-bound from composing opacity with detection latency.** A cross-segment finding without a single segment home. The 16-cell adversarial-targeting matrix is *self-bounded*: **the most damaging cell to attack is the one the defender's detection latency makes hardest to recognize**. The composition: agent-opacity multiplier on tempo (from this chapter) plus expected-detection-latency floor (from an appendix derivation) makes the cell with maximal damage the one with *maximal opacity and minimal regime footprint given the defender's accumulated experience*. Adversarial targeting therefore concentrates *where the defender's response is slowest*, and the framework predicts the empirical patterns that **cybersecurity incidents cluster on long-trusted dependencies** (longest accumulated experience) and that **adversarial-ML examples cluster on high-confidence model regions**. Both observed empirically; the framework supplies the unifying mechanism.

The framework's prescription is concrete: defenders should *short-circuit accumulated trust* on critical dependencies (forced forgetting to reset experience) and *probe high-confidence model regions* with explicit interventional checks. These are *not optional optimizations* — they are the structural countermeasures the adversary's optimal-targeting argument forces the defender to deploy.
