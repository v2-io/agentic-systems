# Part II Preface: Agentic Systems — Actuated Adaptation (volume framing prose)

Part II adds objectives and strategy alongside the reality model. Part I developed adaptive systems in general — agents that track reality. Part II develops the goal-directed layer built on top — agents that not only track but *aim*. The volume names this the *actuation* half of Adaptation and Actuation Theory.

Part II's distinctive results are stated upfront. The **satisfaction gap / control regret split** — two orthogonal diagnostics for purposeful-agent performance, the first measuring the unreachability of the agent's ideal value from its current model, the second measuring the suboptimality of its current policy against the best policy achievable in its current model. The split is governed by a **convention hierarchy** (C1 / C2 / C3) that controls the inferential force of each diagnostic from local through global scope. The **$G_t$ complexity bound** — a structural constraint that the agent's purposeful substate cannot be more complex than the model state can support, derived in the orient-cascade segment. The **graph-structure uniqueness argument with CMC-based Markov property** — four postulates plus causal sufficiency force the strategy DAG with a Markov property derived from the Causal Markov Condition rather than postulated. The **survival classification** showing that Part II's definitional and structural architecture *transfers exactly* to fully-coupled (Class 3) agents in 16 out of 24 cases, with the remaining results either approximating with bounded error (5 cases) or naming the precise modification required (2 cases, plus one boundary-defining failure). The Class 3 observation-ambiguity bias bound underlying the approximate-survival category is given as a conditional theorem under named sub-scopes, not order-of-magnitude guidance.

Part II's results sit at different points in a **stacked lattice of scope conditions** rather than a uniform scope. Reading inward from broadest: (1) adaptive scope — observation under uncertainty (Part I machinery applies without further restriction). (2) Agency scope — adaptive + at least binary action choice + at least one action with Pearl-Level-2 causal contrast (Part II's definitional architecture is already non-vacuous here). (3) Learning-agent scope — agency + the agent must acquire or refine its interventional structure during operation; this excludes pre-compiled controllers like PID, LQR, and hardcoded reactive policies, whose causal mapping was supplied by an external designer with Level-2 access (most of Part II's dynamics operate in this scope). (4) Class 1 (Separated) architecture — directed separation holds *by construction*: epistemic processing has no causal path from the purposeful substate. Examples: Kalman filter plus LQR, modular RL with separate world model, military intelligence separated from operations, tool-use AI agents with separate perception and planning modules. Class 3 (Coupled) agents — including transformer-based language models where attention processes goals and observations together — lie outside Class 1. Class 2 (Partial) sits between, with coupling quantified by a processing-coupling scalar.

The lattice *stacks*: Kalman plus LQR is in Class 1 (Separated) but outside learning-agent scope (the control law is pre-computed); a designer applying Part II's diagnostic core to Kalman plus LQR uses only the architecture-survival half, not the learning-dependent dynamics. The orient cascade in particular requires both Class 1 directed separation and learning-agent scope. The bias-bound theorem and the survival classification trace exactly which Part II results require which lattice entries.

Part III's composite-agent scope sits below this lattice as a fifth tier and inherits a Class-1-sub-agents → Class-2-composite refinement of directed separation.

A crucial scope clarification: **Part I machinery applies regardless of architecture**. Adaptive dynamics on the epistemic substate — mismatch, gain, tempo, persistence — operate independently of how the update function relates to the purposeful substate. Class 3 (Coupled) agents are adaptive systems in the Part I sense; what they lose is the clean factorization that gives Part II's coupled-formulation results their definitional simplicity. The coupled formulation Class 3 agents require is the subject of Volume 03, which inherits Part II's surviving 16/24 architecture and adds the additional machinery (observation-ambiguity modulation, ambiguity-gated bias bounds) that the coupled regime calls for.

The preface closes with Seneca: *"If a man knows not to which port he sails, no wind is favorable"* — and the dependency graph for Part II.
