# Composite Tempo Inequality (derived — sketch)

The adaptive tempo of a composite agent is *bounded above* by the sum of its sub-agents' tempos. The gap between aggregate potential and realized composite tempo is the **coordination overhead** — tempo consumed by *internal reconciliation* rather than external mismatch correction.

The intuition is direct. Tempo is a rate of mismatch correction. When the composite's closure defect is zero (the composite tracks the micro-system exactly), every sub-agent correction cycle contributes directly to the macro loop without friction. When closure defect is positive, sub-agents spend part of their tempo correcting *internal* mismatches generated by other sub-agents (Alice changes an API; Bob must update his model) or taking actions that counteract each other. That fraction is the coordination overhead and is *unavailable for external mismatch correction*.

The framework derives the connection between closure defect and coordination overhead. The closure defect introduces internal mismatch at a rate (closure defect times macro update rate), measured in distance-per-time — *the same units as environmental disturbance*. This internal mismatch must be corrected by the composite's correction capacity, and the correction consumes tempo that would otherwise address external mismatch. The dimensional accounting works cleanly: tempo carries inverse-time units, disturbance rate carries distance-per-time units, and conversion between them requires division by the task-adequacy distance scale — mirroring the persistence-condition form used at the single-agent level.

The composite therefore faces total *effective disturbance* combining external and closure-defect contributions. The coordination overhead is bounded below by the closure-defect-driven internal disturbance rate divided by the critical-mismatch scale. **This makes Brooks's Law precise**: adding sub-agents increases potential aggregate tempo, but if it also increases closure defect (more coordination links to maintain), the realized composite tempo can *fall* — adding people to a late project makes it later when the coordination overhead from the additional links exceeds the tempo contributed.

The framework names the result as a *sketch* rather than exact because the coordination-overhead connection depends on the bridge lemma in the composition-closure formulation — which requires a contraction assumption on the macro-update map that is *structurally motivated* by the (A4) sector condition but not formally derived from it. The remaining gap is whether the lower bound on coordination overhead is tight or whether additional coordination costs contribute beyond the closure-defect mechanism.
