# Operationalization — Estimation Procedures (appendix detail — conditional)

A practical guide *bridging the measurement gap* between AAT's formal objects and deployment-grade estimation. The segment catalogs the framework's core quantities — model uncertainty, observation uncertainty, mismatch magnitude, tempo, sector parameter, disturbance rate, critical-mismatch tolerance, model-class fitness, satisfaction gap, control regret — and for each names the *typical unit*, the *minimum data needed*, and a *recommended estimation procedure*.

The estimation recipes are domain-honest. Model uncertainty can be estimated from predictive posterior variance, ensemble spread, or Bayesian-neural-network uncertainty. Observation uncertainty can be estimated from channel calibration or residual variance. Mismatch magnitude can be measured directly from prediction-versus-observation residuals. Tempo can be measured from event rates times effective gain. The sector parameter requires either the gain-sector bridge (under conditions where the bridge applies — Bayesian on exponential families; gradient on strongly convex) or empirical fitting via correction-rate-as-function-of-mismatch regression. Disturbance rate can be estimated from environmental change rate estimates or from observed mismatch growth during pause windows. Critical-mismatch tolerance is a *domain parameter*, not an estimated quantity — encodes "how wrong can the model be before the agent's actions become harmful or ineffective?"

The framework is *honest* about which quantities are easily measured and which are *operationally challenging*. Model-class fitness is named as particularly challenging (cannot be directly computed; requires searching over all models in the class; the *observable signature* is persistent mismatch despite adequate learning). The satisfaction gap and control regret require evaluating attainability — generally intractable in exact form, requiring approximations whose quality depends on the policy class and horizon.

The segment provides the framework's *bridge to practice*: every chapter-level result invokes quantities that require operational estimation procedures to apply in real deployment, and this segment supplies the recipes. The framework's commitment is that the *abstract machinery is operationalizable*, with stated quality trade-offs at each estimation step, rather than being an analytical idealization with no path to practice.

The segment also names *measurement-induced sub-scope distinctions* the rest of the framework uses. The intervention-rich / partial-intervention / observation-only regime classification depends on which estimation procedures are *available* — Regime A admits direct estimation of causal-information yield via interventional execution; Regime B requires assumptions; Regime C requires structural priors. The Class 1 / 2 / 3 architectural classification has measurement signatures: the processing-coupling scalar can be estimated empirically via the behavioral estimator pattern (probe the processor under different goal-primings; measure divergence in epistemic content).
