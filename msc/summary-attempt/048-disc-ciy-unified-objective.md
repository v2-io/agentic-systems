# CIY Unified Policy Objective (discussion — discussion-grade summary; underlying derivation exact)

The framework's *unified policy objective* expresses the exploration-exploitation tension as a single decision rule: choose the action that maximizes a weighted sum of expected value (exploitation) and a causal-information surrogate (exploration). The first term is the exploitation contribution; the second is a *heuristic exploration term* using causal information yield as a tractable surrogate for expected information gain. The weighting parameter $\lambda$ controls the balance — large under high model uncertainty (when distinguishable actions are also informative to an uncertain agent), small under low uncertainty (when exploration buys little), larger for long planning horizons (because information compounds), larger in fast-changing environments (because perpetual uncertainty means perpetual value to exploration).

The weight $\lambda$ carries units of *value per unit of information*, and in specific well-understood domains it reduces to known quantities exactly: the Gittins index for Bayesian bandits; the probing cost in quadratic objectives for Kalman dual control; precision on epistemic affordance for active inference; the explicit variance-over-information ratio in information-directed sampling (Russo & Van Roy). For RL-with-UCB, the connection is heuristic and tuned. So the unified objective is not a novel formalism but a *family-level abstraction* that recovers known scalarizations of the exploration-exploitation trade-off across communities.

The framework is honest about the heuristic status of the scalar form. The scalar objective is now formally *superseded* by an exact tensor trace-product form derived in the appendix: the action maximizes the value plus the trace of (a positive-semidefinite shadow-price matrix $\Lambda$) times (the Fisher Information Matrix evaluated at the action). This is the exact Lagrangian relaxation of the Linear Matrix Inequality governing Lyapunov persistence. The structural form is fully grounded in AAT's physical survival bounds plus standard semidefinite programming, eliminating the need to treat exploration as an ad-hoc heuristic.

A striking observation is highlighted: **two parallel exploration drives** emerge from the framework, acting at opposite ends of the uncertainty spectrum. **First**, an *epistemic information-gain* drive ($\lambda$ proportional to model uncertainty): the agent explores to reduce model uncertainty. This drive dominates when the agent is uncertain. **Second**, a *survival-imperative* drive ($\lambda$ proportional to *inverse* model uncertainty): an agent with high confidence in a drifting environment mathematically guarantees its own death by ignoring noisy observations; to force the necessary correction, the Lyapunov persistence constraint dictates an *immense shadow price* (the survival weighting blows up as model uncertainty drops in a drifting environment), forcing the confident agent to seek unambiguous observations (the same exploration drive, from the *opposite* uncertainty regime). The dark-room problem (which plagues some preferences-as-priors formulations) is *bypassed entirely* by the survival imperative: exploration is not driven by preferences-as-priors but by the literal physical boundaries of the Lyapunov sector constraint.

A precise comparison to active inference is offered. The expected free energy in active inference decomposes into pragmatic value (preferences-aligned outcomes) and epistemic value (expected information gain about hidden states); AAT's unified objective is *structurally isomorphic* — value plus information — but with two substantive differences. **First**, AAT grounds exploration in explicitly *causal* information (action-distinguishability under intervention) rather than entropy reduction over hidden states. Not all uncertainty reduction is equally valuable for purposeful action; causal information specifically enables better intervention. **Second**, AAT does *not* encode preferences as priors over outcomes (a key move in the active-inference form). AAT's objective is a value functional on trajectories, and the satisfaction-gap / control-regret diagnostic structure depends on this distinction — the diagnostic structure does not survive the priors-as-preferences collapse (this is what makes AAT immune to the "dark-room critique" that has been raised against the active-inference treatment).

The connection back to the zero-mismatch ambiguity from Part I is sharpened: an agent that *only exploits* (acts to maximize predicted value) tends toward confirmation bias — observing only what its model already explains. Exploration via CIY-maximizing actions is the *mechanism* by which the agent actively tests its model.
