# Critical-Mass Composition (appendix derivation — conditional)

The framework's cleanest demonstration of internal economy. The composite sector constant is *derived* — not merely bounded from below — for the symmetric-matched-Tier-1 two-agent case, yielding a **closed-form critical-mass inequality**. The result *unifies* four prior persistence-flavored results as *signed special cases of one inequality*: single-agent persistence (no coupling, no coordination); team-persistence (cooperative coupling enters as a *negative* term in effective disturbance); adversarial-destabilization (positive coupling amplifies disturbance); coordination-dominated failure (coordination overhead exceeding intrinsic correction rate — **Brooks's Law as a derived inequality** rather than folklore).

The construction takes two Tier-1 sub-agents with identical structural parameters, applies the signed-coupling disturbance decomposition from team-persistence, and uses the bridge lemma to lift to a composite sector constant in closed form. The signed coupling parameter and the teleological-unity parameter enter explicitly: cooperative regimes have negative effective coupling; adversarial regimes have positive effective coupling; the unity quantity acts multiplicatively as a scope-gate.

The framework's posture: this is **one inequality, with signed parameters indexing the regime, recovering what previously read as four separate results**. The closed form makes Brooks's Law a *derived inequality*: the composite's effective correction rate is the intrinsic rate minus the coordination overhead; once overhead exceeds the intrinsic rate, the composite's persistence fails *regardless of individual agent quality*. The threshold is sharp, not gradual, and the inequality predicts exactly where it bites.

The result also delivers the **third formal instance of the identifiability-floor pattern** in its fullest form: composite contraction certification from component-marginal data is structurally impossible — the *coupling-sign bit* distinguishing cooperative from adversarial regimes is *unidentifiable from component marginals* (Liberzon 2003's common-Lyapunov-nonexistence is the external no-go). The escape comes via Pearl-style intervention at the composite scope: an observer (or the composite acting on its sub-agents) performs interventions on one sub-agent and observes the response of another. This is *Mode 2* of the loop-Level-2 deployment pattern, formally available because the framework's interventional-access result extends to the composite layer.

A second consequence: the framework's symbiogenic composition mechanism is recovered as the *asymmetric-parameter limit* of this closed form. When sub-agent parameters are strongly asymmetric (one sub-agent dominates), the composite sector constant collapses toward the dominant sub-agent's, and the absorbed sub-agent's objectives are progressively folded in through the teleological-unity multiplier. *Symbiogenesis is therefore not a separate kind of composition* — it is the asymmetric-parameter limit of the same composition machinery.
