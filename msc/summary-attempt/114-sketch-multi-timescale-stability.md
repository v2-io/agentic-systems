# Multi-Timescale Stability (appendix sketch — sketch status)

A formal sketch of multi-timescale stability via singular perturbation theory, lifting the temporal-nesting result from Part I to the $N$-timescale case. The general system has nested state variables at progressively slower timescales (fastest level being reactive or parametric, slowest being architectural or meta-structural). The composite stability result: under sufficient timescale separation, *if each level is stable given fixed slower-level parameters*, the composite $N$-level system is stable.

The framework states this as a sketch rather than a fully derived result because making it rigorous for AAT requires specifying dynamics at deeper adaptive levels — what counts as "stability of structural adaptation given fixed architectural state" requires defining structural-adaptation dynamics formally. The single-perturbation reasoning is classical (Tikhonov 1952 / Khalil 2002), but the AAT instantiation needs the structural-change dynamics formalized.

The sketch supplies the *qualitative consequence* the chapter prose carried as load-bearing: a slower process must not act before the faster process beneath it has converged. If it does, the slower process is adjusting based on transient behavior rather than settled dynamics, and the system oscillates. The convergence constraints between adjacent timescales are what license the framework's abstraction across levels — without them, the framework's clean separation between parametric, consolidation, structural, and architectural adaptation would be a verbal convenience rather than a structural property.
