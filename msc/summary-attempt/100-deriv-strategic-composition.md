# Strategic Composition via Equilibrium Convergence (derivation — conditional)

When two or more AAT agents interact with **partially-opposing objectives**, the composition-level question is *not* "does the trajectory contract to zero closure defect?" but "does the coupled best-response dynamics admit an equilibrium and converge to it?" Contraction to shared truth is the *special case* where teleological unity equals 1; strategic composition is the *companion regime* where teleological unity is less than 1, and the correct primitive is **fixed-point existence and stability**, not Lyapunov contraction on a shared state.

The framework names three composition-level questions for strategic composites. **(SC-1) Existence**: does an equilibrium joint policy exist where no sub-agent has a unilateral mismatch reduction available? **(SC-2) Stability**: do coupled best-response dynamics, initialized near the equilibrium, remain there? **(SC-3) Convergence**: do coupled dynamics from arbitrary initialization converge to the equilibrium set? *None of these is a Lyapunov contraction question on a shared state variable* — they are fixed-point, local-stability, and reachability questions respectively.

The framework provides two sub-scopes with progressively weaker conditions. **Sub-scope $\alpha'$ — potential and monotone games**: under Monderer-Shapley's potential-game condition (a scalar potential exists such that each sub-agent's unilateral improvement matches the potential's improvement) plus each sub-agent's directional-fidelity condition, the joint best-response dynamics drive the potential toward stationarity, *and the sector-persistence template transfers to the gradient of the joint potential*. AAT's persistence machinery recovers at the equilibrium layer — with the state variable being deviation from Nash equilibrium and the sector parameter the joint correction rate. Rosen 1965's monotone-game framework provides a parallel result using variational inequalities with a weighted norm. **Sub-scope $\beta'$ — non-potential non-monotone games**: only set-convergence to coarse correlated equilibria is available (Hart-Mas-Colell 2000 under no-regret dynamics). The framework names this honestly as a *scope exit* — the persistence template does not transfer for these games; only weaker convergence guarantees are available.

A subtler architectural consequence is named: **composites of Class-1-Separated sub-agents with partially-opposing objectives produce Class-2-Partial composites**. Each sub-agent individually retains its goal-blind belief update; but the composite's coupled state acquires intrinsic coupling because *each sub-agent's model includes a model of other sub-agents' policies*, which are themselves goal-dependent. The composite-level directed separation fails through *across-agent* coupling, not within-agent coupling. Strategic composition is therefore the canonical *Class-1-sub-agents → Class-2-composite* case, distinct from the within-agent coupling failures in the directed-separation classification.

A mechanism-design candidate identifiability-floor instance is flagged: in some strategic composites, the equilibrium structure cannot be identified from observable-coupling data alone — only the equilibrium-existence layer is identifiable; the equilibrium-selection layer requires structural priors on the mechanism. The framework names this as a candidate fourth-or-fifth instance of the identifiability-floor pattern at the composition layer; the formal characterization is left as an open direction.
