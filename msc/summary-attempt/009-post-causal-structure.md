# Causal Structure (postulate — axiomatic)

The framework adopts as a primitive postulate the irreducible causal structure that grounds the agent-environment loop: actions precede their consequences, observations follow from the state they observe, and the temporal ordering of events is constitutive of what causes what. The notion of causality adopted is the most primitive one — *event A can be a cause of event B only if A temporally precedes B* — and it is weaker than (and logically prior to) statistical notions of causal influence. It is a statement about the structure of *possible* influence, not actual influence.

The postulate is not derived from the second law of thermodynamics, the light-cone structure of relativity, or the arrow of psychological time, though each enforces it. It is simply noted as a precondition: the theory applies to agents embedded in a universe where time has a direction.

A subtle but important consequence: the causal structure of the feedback loop is preserved *independent of the magnitude of coupling*. The loop has the same skeletal structure whether the agent's actions strongly determine the environment (robot manipulation), weakly affect it (small financial trades), or only choose what to observe with negligible effect on the environment itself. Only when actions provably affect *neither* the environment nor the observation distribution does the loop collapse to a one-way channel — at which point the system falls out of the agency scope and into the adaptive-scope-only regime. The causal-structure postulate still holds for such purely-adaptive systems (temporal ordering of observations is still constitutive), but the Pearl-style causal *hierarchy* (Level 2 interventional, Level 3 counterfactual) becomes inaccessible without action-contingent observation.

The architectural consequences of the temporal arrow are stated cleanly. Model updates must be directed (only on prior events, never future ones). The mismatch signal is retrospective (prediction-before vs observation-after). Action selection is prospective (current model influencing future events). The chronica grows monotonically (events added, never removed). And updates should give more weight to observations that are causally downstream of the agent's own actions than to observations that would have occurred regardless — action-contingent observations carry interventional (Pearl-Level-2) information; action-independent observations carry only associational (Level 1) information. The framework will return to this distinction formally when it constructs *causal information yield* later in Part I.
