# Strategy Structure — Additional Implications (discussion — chapter-end synthesis)

Four threads from Chapter 3 read better at the chapter-end than separately.

**(1) Theorem-backed structure, convergent-choice parameterization.** The chapter is careful that downstream segments rely on a distinction: what falls out of theorems and what is a *parameterization choice* within the theorem-backed structure. Acyclicity is *proved* from temporal ordering. The Markov factorization is *proved* under causal sufficiency via the Causal Markov Condition theorem. The argument parallels Cox's theorem in form (operational desiderata force a measure) but not yet in strength — Cox is necessary-and-sufficient; this is sufficient only. The AND/OR parameterization and single-parameter edges are *chosen*, but the chapter introduces a third epistemic category worth naming: **convergent representational choices**, where every investigated alternative either failed or recovered the same structure. AND/OR converged across three independent formalism attempts; alternatives were rejected for non-identifiability or parameter explosion; single-parameter edges replaced an earlier two-parameter form when AND/OR rules absorbed the redundant parameter's role. This category — between *derived from first principles* and *arbitrary framework decision* — is itself a methodological commitment: a research-trail-honesty artifact that tells the reader "we didn't just pick this; we tried everything else and it didn't work."

**(2) The diagnostic split, lifted by the convention hierarchy.** The chapter's orthogonal diagnostic separates "the world doesn't permit it" from "you're not doing it well enough"; the 2×2 cell map routes four regimes to four corrective actions; the orient cascade in the next chapter uses this routing to direct revision pressure to the right substate. The lift the chapter delivers but does not fully surface is that the diagnostic is *convention-indexed*, not absolute. The value-object's three named conventions form a monotonicity hierarchy on the satisfaction gap. The 2×2 cell map is therefore not a single object but a *convention-indexed family*, and the monotonicity result tells the practitioner exactly what additional inferential force each convention buys for what additional computational cost. Most decision-theory frameworks treat convention choice as a purely computational tradeoff; AAT adds the *inferential-force semantics* that makes the choice diagnostic-meaningful rather than merely budget-meaningful.

The deliberate divergence from active inference is restated: the diagnostic structure depends on the objective being a *value functional* on trajectories rather than a *log-prior on outcomes*. The active-inference "preferences-as-priors" form collapses the two-gap structure by making "wanting $o$" and "expecting $o$" formally the same operation. AAT's value-functional reformulation is the framework's downstream architectural response to the dark-room critique of preferences-as-priors formulations.

**(3) The Correlation Hierarchy and identifiability-floor Instance 2.** The chapter's strategy-DAG segment carries an honest scope statement about correlated failure that is more consequential than it reads on first encounter. The substantive finding lives in the boundary between L1' with observable common cause and L1' with *unobservable* common cause. When the common cause is unobservable, the per-conditional decomposition is not "open" — it is **structurally refuted by the Cramér-Rao floor** (the per-trial Fisher information matrix is rank 1 rather than the rank required), and no unbiased online estimator on the joint conditional vector admits a sector parameter $\alpha > 0$. This is the framework's **second formally-named instance of the identifiability-floor pattern**. The agent's three escape routes — augment observability via an instrument variable, run multi-child joint observations under exploration, or fall back to L0-on-marginals (losing per-conditional diagnostics) — are the operational instantiation of *naming the floor and naming the unique broadly-available escape via AAT machinery*. Each identifiability floor is a *constructive use of impossibility*, where the no-go elevates the load-bearing role of the specific AAT machinery that supplies the escape.

Read against Chapter 4's coming on-policy detection no-go (Instance 1), this gives the pattern's first two formal instances as duals: Instance 2 says identification fails inside a single agent under unobservable common causes; Instance 1 says self-diagnosis fails under policy-perfect execution. The single escape AAT names — interventional access supplying the joint outcome data — applies to both.

**(4) Chain-confidence decay, coordinate forcing, and the bridge into Strategy Dynamics.** The chapter opens with the elementary chain-rule identity in log-space, but it is the *first instance* of a meta-pattern AAT deploys at four representational layers: log-probability at the chain layer (Cauchy functional equation on the probability chain rule); reverse-KL at the divergence layer; log-odds at the update layer; Fisher information metric at the metric layer (under the parameterization-invariance axiom plus Čencov's uniqueness theorem). The four are interlocked by cross-cites; the convergence across independent axioms onto a single exponential-family Legendre-Fenchel geometry is the substance.

The methodological implication: **AAT's coordinates are not chosen by convention**. They are *forced by AAT-internal axioms via classical uniqueness theorems on additivity or invariance*, and they all live on the same geometric object. Lyapunov quadratic and the IB Lagrangian are adjacent family members (Bregman divergence on Euclidean potential, and adopted-rather-than-derived respectively). The framework's posture is that where a coordinate matters, the framework can usually point at the axiom that forces it; where the axiom isn't AAT-internal, the framework can say what was adopted from outside. The chapter's elementary identity is the canonical example of a load-bearing AAT claim that looks like algebra and turns out to be the entry point to a four-layer architectural commitment.
