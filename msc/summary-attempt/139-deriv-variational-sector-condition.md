# Variational Approximate Sector Condition (appendix derivation — conditional)

The framework's extension of the persistence template to **variational-inference agents** under a *controlled-KL budget*. Variational / approximate-posterior agents (VI, amortized VI, active-inference-style variational free energy) currently sit in sub-scope β: their correction functions target the *best-in-class variational posterior* rather than the true posterior, and the approximation gap can rotate the correction direction enough to break directional fidelity.

Under a KL bound between the variational approximation and the true posterior, the framework derives **ε-fidelity directional fidelity**: directional fidelity recovers in quantifiable form with *sector-constant degradation scaling as the square root of the KL budget* (Pinsker-tight). The sector-persistence template then applies under a *Regime-A / Regime-B decomposition*: clean sector bound on an annulus away from the projection-error floor; approximation-dominated on a ball around the target whose radius scales as the square root of the KL budget.

This promotes *controlled-KL VI from sub-scope β to a new intermediate tier α'* within the sector-condition partition, complementing the previously-derived α₁ (fixed-gain), α₂ (adaptive-gain under meta-gain conditions), and β (assumed) tiers.

The result has a *striking operational consequence*: **mean-field VI agents are structurally suboptimal as adaptive systems by a factor proportional to the square root of the KL-budget, regardless of compute or training data**. The mean-field family does *not* match the Fisher metric, so the sector parameter degrades by the square-root factor. **Natural-gradient VI recovers the full sector parameter** because the natural-gradient direction matches the Fisher metric — no degradation. The framework's prescription is that *the choice of variational family is structurally consequential for persistence*: where persistence-optimality matters, the natural-gradient family is *not optional*. The decade-plus Bayesian-deep-learning debate about MF-VI versus natural-gradient on heuristic grounds has a *principled answer* — if persistence-optimality matters, there is no choice.

The result composes with the persistence-information-rate floor: variational agents pay in *more bandwidth* to compensate for the structural sub-optimality. Moving from natural-gradient VI to mean-field VI requires more observation bandwidth to maintain the same target distortion, because the effective sector constant is smaller. The framework's bandwidth-cost-of-persistence analysis takes the variational-family choice into account.
