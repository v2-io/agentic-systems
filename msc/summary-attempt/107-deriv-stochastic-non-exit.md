# Stochastic Non-Exit No-Go (appendix derivation — exact)

A *no-go* derivation establishing that **under additive stochastic forcing there is no finite bound on the infinite-horizon first-exit probability** — the agent's mismatch *will* eventually leave any fixed region with probability one over an unbounded horizon, for every correction strength. *And the natural mathematical route to a bound on the exit probability provably cannot work*.

The construction: under stochastic disturbance the Itô-Lyapunov machinery succeeds at producing a *finite-horizon mean-square bound* but fails to extend to a *horizon-independent* exit-probability bound. The natural candidate — a Ville/Doob maximal inequality on a compensated supermartingale — is shown to be unattainable because the only candidate supermartingale is *sign-indefinite inside the persistence basin* (the compensated drift is not non-positive across the entire region), and the scale function is *unbounded*. Both failures are structural rather than artifacts of a clever trick being missed; the framework presents this as a *reusable no-go signature* for similar problems in stochastic stability analysis.

The downstream consequence is a *certain-eventual-event* result. Under Model S — stochastic disturbance — pathwise containment in any fixed region is *unattainable at any correction strength*. The implication is that for any sufficiently long-lived stochastic-environment agent, the *need for structural adaptation* (a different model class, not just better parameter tuning) is a *certain eventual event* rather than an edge case. The framework integrates this finding into the structural-adaptation-necessity result: structural change is not just necessary for agents that *happen* to encounter inadequate model classes; it is *certain to be needed* for any long-lived agent operating under stochastic environmental forcing.

The framework treats this as a *constructive use of impossibility*. The no-go demonstrates that the standard maximal-inequality route cannot establish pathwise containment, and that this is structural rather than incidental. The corollary result (Cor A.1S.1) takes the *exact* form that the no-go forced: a containment dichotomy — pathwise containment fails categorically, while finite-horizon and mean-square bounds remain available with their respective scalings. The "exact no-go" is the framework's preferred form: rather than offering a softened approximation, the framework states the precise structural impossibility and uses it to organize the remaining results.
