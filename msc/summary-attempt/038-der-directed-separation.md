# Directed Separation (derived + scope — conditional, robust-qualitative classification)

The structural backbone of Part II. The epistemic update function is **goal-blind**: it processes incoming events without reference to the agent's objectives or strategy. The purposeful update *depends* on the (updated) epistemic state. Action couples all substates through the policy. This *directed asymmetry* — epistemic update independent of purpose; purposeful update dependent on epistemic state — is what gives Part II its clean factorization.

The condition is honest about what "goal-blind" means: it is goal-blindness *conditional on the realized event*. The observation channel may still be action-dependent (the agent can select what to observe through its actions), so the *event* that arrives may depend on the agent's goals through the action channel. But the *processing* of that event — once it has arrived — must not refer to goals. No goal-dependent attention thresholds, no confirmation bias baked into the update function. Directed separation is about the **processing** of events, not the **selection** of events.

Whether directed separation holds is determined by the agent's **processing topology** — a structural property of the architecture, not a tunable parameter. The framework partitions agents into three classes. **Class 1: Separated** — separate estimator and planner connected through a state-estimate interface; the estimator has no causal path from the purposeful substate; directed separation holds by construction. Kalman filter + LQR, modular RL with separate world model, military intelligence separated from operations. **Class 3: Coupled** — a single mechanism handles both epistemic and strategic processing; goals are causally upstream of every computation; directed separation fails by construction. Transformer LLMs (where attention processes goals and observations together) are the canonical case; possibly human cognition under motivated reasoning. **Class 2: Partial** — some shared infrastructure, some separate pathways. Biological cortex (shared sensory areas, separate prefrontal). The degree of coupling in Class 2 can be quantified as a *processing-coupling scalar* — the conditional mutual information between the goal state and the post-update model state, given the event and prior model state — but the architectural classification is the primary tool; the scalar is a diagnostic for the Partial cases where the architecture is ambiguous. The classes are *structural and discrete*, not points on a smooth scalar (this replaces an earlier coupling-as-scalar framing).

The framework draws a crucial pedigree point. Directed separation is structurally the **Pearl-blanket reading of the Markov blanket apparatus** in active inference — the technical conditional-independence statement — without the contested **Friston-blanket reading** (the metaphysical claim that Markov blankets demarcate self-from-other). The architectural classification's explicit scope exit for Class 3 (Coupled), with the coupled formulation handed off to Volume 03, is precisely the kind of scope honesty Bruineberg et al. (2022) argue is missing from the Friston-blanket reading: AAT names where its formalism *fails by construction* rather than treating the failure as an unenforced approximation.

The implications for theory scope are stated cleanly. **Class 1** agents: Part II's results apply exactly; the sequential orient cascade is the correct analysis. **Class 3** agents: require coupled formulation from the start; this is the scope of Volume 03. But importantly, *Class 3 components can be wrapped into Class-1 composites* via the class-coercion-via-wrapping construction in Part III — at the cost of more component calls per macro-step (a Brooks's-Law-style tempo overhead) and a residual leakage rate bounded structurally (strict wrapping) or behaviorally (partial wrapping). **Class 2** agents: the sequential cascade is an approximation whose quality depends on the coupling scalar.

A subtler refinement of Class 1 is offered: **Class 1 by structure** vs **Class 1 by behavior**. The strict-wrapping construction gives Class 1 by structure (directed separation holds by structural commitment of the wrapper's type signatures, no goal argument in the belief-update path, leakage bounded structurally by pretraining-induced query-content/goal-content mutual information). Partial wrapping gives Class 1 by behavior (the structural separation lives at the *write boundary*; the *query boundary* still passes the goal to the underlying component; separation at the wrapper level is behavioral — bounded by the component's compliance with the prompted instruction-to-separate, with no structural upper bound). The two are distinguishable by inspection (does the belief-update query carry the goal in its input or not?) and operationally distinct because behavioral compliance is empirical and adversarially fragile.

A composite-level extension is also stated. The class partition applies to individual agents based on *within-agent* coupling. Composition introduces a second form of coupling — *across-agent* coupling through the shared environment. The result: a composite of Class 1 (Separated) sub-agents with *aligned* objectives is a Class 1 (Separated) composite; a composite of Class 1 (Separated) sub-agents with *partially-opposing* objectives becomes a *Class 2 (Partial) composite from Class 1 (Separated) sub-agents* — each sub-agent individually remains Separated with respect to its own goal, but the composite's coupled state acquires intrinsic coupling because each sub-agent's model includes a model of other sub-agents' policies, which are goal-dependent. Composite-level directed separation fails through across-agent coupling, not within-agent coupling.

A subtle *bounded-signaling assumption* is named — until now implicit throughout the framework. Directed separation as stated covers the *belief-update* side; symmetrically, the framework implicitly relies on the channel from the purposeful substate to the world running *only* through action choice. The action coarseness upper-bounds the rate at which goal information leaks to observers. This works well for agents whose entire externally-observable behavior is the action sequence (chess engines, programmatic controllers) but *fails operationally* for agents whose behavioral output is rich relative to action coarseness — sophisticated goal-inference from prosody, micro-behavior, attention patterns, response latency, hesitation, code-style signatures. When the bounded-signaling assumption fails, external observers can infer goals at bit-rate exceeding what the formal action channel implies; in composition this surfaces as adversarial-coupling-pressure saturation, where adversaries exploit the rich-leakage signal to drive target coupling beyond what the architectural classification predicts.
