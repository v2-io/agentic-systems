# Part I Validation Simulations (appendix observation — empirical)

A documentation of the *simulation program* that validated and *refined* Part I's analytical predictions. Six simulation variants tested mismatch dynamics, adversarial coupling, observation noise, correction-function robustness, and anisotropic persistence. The simulations modeled discrete-time mismatch dynamics as autoregressive processes with five correction functions (linear, saturating, threshold/dead-zone, sigmoid, structural-breakdown), sweeping parameter spaces across gain, disturbance rate, coupling strength, observation noise, and dimensional structure.

The framework names the simulations as **theory-shaping, not merely confirmatory**. Three findings emerged from simulation that *forced refinements* to the analytical treatment. (1) **The adversarial tempo exponent turned out to be regime-dependent** (deterministic drift versus stochastic noise; coupling-dominant versus non-coupling-dominant), forcing a regime split that the linear ODE left ambiguous — this is the source of the framework's $b=2$ / $b=3/2$ / $b\to 1$ regime classification. (2) **Observation noise was found to gate the adversarial advantage**, partially recoverable by optimal gain — a finding that *had no analytical treatment before the simulations* and led to the "gated tempo advantage" result. (3) **Per-dimension persistence emerged as load-bearing**: anisotropic systems with gain varying across dimensions produced mismatch concentration on the weak direction that the scalar persistence condition systematically overestimated by up to 72%.

The framework treats the simulations as *part of the framework's methodology* rather than as external validation. Each simulation variant produced findings that *propagated back to the analytical derivations*: the regime-decomposition of the adversarial exponent; the observation-noise gating; the per-dimension persistence necessity; the matrix-Loewner sharpening; the structural-breakdown trigger as a precondition for structural adaptation. The simulation program is *theory development at the empirical layer*, not theory-testing.

The framework's overall posture: AAT's analytical derivations and simulation program are *interleaved* in the methodology. Where simulation surfaces something the analytical treatment misses, the analytical treatment is refined; where simulation confirms what the analytical treatment predicts, the result lifts in confidence. The two together cover the framework's terrain in a way *neither could cover alone*. The simulations have been part of the framework's machinery since the predecessor TFT work and continue through the AAT framework's current state.
