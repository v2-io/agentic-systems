# Strategy-Cost Regret Bound (appendix derivation — robust-qualitative; reverse-KL conditional on additivity axiom)

The derivation that **the KL direction in the framework's strategy-cost objective is forced** as the non-vacuous regret-bound form, and that *reverse-KL is uniquely selected* within the direction-forced family by an AAT-internally-motivated additivity axiom.

The argument proceeds in steps. Define strategy-induced regret as the value-loss of the strategy's induced action distribution against the optimal deterministic policy. Under bounded value range, regret is bounded above by *value-range times total-variation distance*. By Pinsker's inequality, total variation is bounded by the *square root of (KL-divergence-in-the-direction-with-optimum-first)*. The opposite-direction KL (strategy first) becomes *infinite* under deterministic optimal policy whenever the strategy places any mass off the optimum, giving a vacuous bound. **The regret-bound argument therefore forces the KL direction with optimum-first** (reverse-KL in the variational-inference vocabulary).

Within the direction-forced family, the framework derives a *uniqueness theorem* for reverse-KL. Under the *chain-rule additivity axiom* — that the divergence over a chain of conditional factorizations equals the sum of conditional divergences (motivated as the divergence-layer analog of the chain-confidence-decay identity from Part II) — the standard Hobson-Csiszár-Shore-Johnson uniqueness machinery via the Cauchy functional equation forces reverse-KL *uniquely* among f-divergences. This is the **second instance of the additive-coordinate-forcing meta-pattern**: a uniqueness theorem operating on an AAT-internally-motivated additivity axiom forces a specific coordinate at a specific layer.

A *sharpening* of the regret bound is also derived. Under AAT's canonical deterministic-optimal-policy scope, the **Bretagnolle-Huber identity** holds exactly (not as inequality), and the strategy-cost regret obeys a *tight two-sided bound*: the regret is bounded above by *value-range times (1 minus exp(negative KL))* and below by the *minimum gap times the same expression*. This is **strictly sharper than Pinsker** by a factor of 2 in canonical regimes — a quantitative improvement that matters for practical RL bound-tightness on the common deterministic-optimum case.

Secondary supporting properties of reverse-KL are surfaced as *convergent evidence* rather than independent uniqueness grounds: gradient tractability, alignment with variational-inference machinery (Friston et al. 2017 active inference; Da Costa et al. 2020; Parr & Pezzulo 2022), MDL-style coding interpretation, compatibility with Amari-Nagaoka Fisher geometry. The framework is explicit that *Fisher-metric-at-second-order is not distinguishing within f-divergences* (Eguchi 1983); only the chain-rule axiom does the uniqueness work.

The result is one of AAT's *cleanest demonstrations* of the framework's posture on coordinates: where a coordinate matters, the framework can point at the axiom that forces it; where the axiom isn't AAT-internal, the framework can say what was adopted from outside. The KL direction here is forced by a *decision-theoretic regret bound*; the choice within the direction-forced family is forced by an *AAT-internal additivity axiom* via a *classical uniqueness theorem*. Three sources of force compose; no single arbitrary choice survives the derivation.
