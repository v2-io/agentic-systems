# Structural-Adaptation Genericity (appendix sketch — exploratory)

A *self-critical sketch* isolating an open question in the framework's bridge from the Model-S containment result to the structural-adaptation-necessity result. The Model-S no-go establishes (exactly) that under additive stochastic forcing the persistence-region first-exit probability is 1 — a long-lived agent leaves the sector-condition region with certainty. The framework's prose has claimed this *sharpens the hand-off* to structural adaptation, making structural adaptation *generic, not exceptional*, for sufficiently long-lived agents.

The sketch surfaces the **gap**: the structural-adaptation-necessity result derives necessity from a *different trigger* — model-class inadequacy, where no model in the current class can represent reality. It does not reference Model-S, region-exit, or the containment dichotomy; its mechanism is *fitness-driven*, not *noise-driven*. And the no-go's explicit instance (the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck benchmark) is *positively recurrent* — the process exits any bounded region almost surely *and returns* almost surely. Exit-and-return, not escape.

The framework names this honestly as a *narrow licensed claim vs disputed increment* problem. What is licensed: under Model S, region exit is certain. What is *not* licensed without an additional argument: that this implies structural adaptation is needed *generically* (rather than that the agent will repeatedly cross and re-enter its region until something else — model-class inadequacy from a different mechanism — triggers structural change). 

The sketch flags this as a candidate for *strengthening the proof* rather than softening the prose. Three possible bridges are named for future work: derive the bridge from a sub-scope condition (specific properties under which exit implies structural inadequacy); derive an excursion-times-class-fitness coupling (the cumulative excursion budget interacts with class-fitness erosion); derive a timescale-separation argument (frequent region exits force structural adaptation through the temporal-nesting mechanism). The framework treats this as an open derivation flagged for future work, not as a defect or as a softening — consistent with the *strengthen-before-soften* discipline that runs through the methodology.
