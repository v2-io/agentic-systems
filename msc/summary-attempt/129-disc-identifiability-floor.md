# The Identifiability Floor (appendix discussion — meta-pattern, discussion-grade)

The framework names a **class of structural no-go results** as the *identifiability floor* meta-pattern. Each instance arises by applying an external information-theoretic theorem to a specific AAT setting; the floor is negative in form but **positive in consequence** — it precisely characterizes what additional structure is required to escape the floor, and *strengthens the load-bearing role of the AAT machinery that supplies the escape*.

The pattern has four parts per instance. **(1) Setting**: an AAT inferential task under a specific information regime. **(2) External theorem**: an information-theoretic limit (Pearl-Bareinboim causal hierarchy theorem; Cramér-Rao bound; Liberzon common-Lyapunov-nonexistence; Čencov's uniqueness theorem combined with a heteroscedastic counterexample). **(3) No-go**: the inferential task is impossible under the regime. **(4) Escape route**: additional structure that *AAT machinery already provides* unlocks the inferential task.

The framework lists **four formal instances** plus candidates. **Instance 1 — on-policy L0-insufficiency detection** (Part II Ch.4): an L0 agent cannot detect its own causal insufficiency from on-policy data alone; Bareinboim's causal hierarchy theorem provides the no-go; *joint sibling observability under exploration* (the loop-interventional-access result, Mode 1) is the unique broadly-available escape. **Instance 2 — L1' mixture identifiability under unobservable common cause** (Part II Ch.3): the per-conditional decomposition is identifiability-obstructed by the Cramér-Rao floor; escape via instrument variable or multi-child joint observation. **Instance 3 — composite contraction certification from component marginals** (Part III): the coupling-sign bit cannot be recovered from component marginals; Liberzon's common-Lyapunov-nonexistence is the no-go; *observer-on-sub-agent interventional access* (Mode 2 of the loop-Level-2 pattern) is the escape. **Instance 4 — universal information-to-distance constant** (Part II Ch.5): no universal constant on the Class-3 bias bound under Euclidean-parameter norm; heteroscedastic-Gaussian counterexample is the no-go; the parameterization-invariance commitment plus Čencov's theorem forces the Fisher information metric as the escape. **Candidate Instances 5+**: Mehra non-identifiability in adaptive Kalman; mechanism-design impossibility (Gibbard-Satterthwaite, Myerson-Satterthwaite, Arrow); others flagged for future formalization.

The framework names a *cross-instance pattern* about the escapes. All four formal instances escape via **Pearl-style interventional access**, but the *deployment mode* differs by layer. **Mode 1 — agent-self-intervention**: the agent performs interventions on its own action space as part of the adaptive loop (Instance 1). **Mode 2 — observer-on-sub-agent**: an observer external to a composite performs interventions on one sub-agent and observes another (Instance 3). **Instrument-variable mode** for Instance 2; **parameterization commitment** for Instance 4. The unification is at the pattern level (Pearl-style intervention as escape from observational-equivalence no-goes); the specific intervention quantity varies by layer.

The framework's posture: **each identifiability floor is a *constructive use of impossibility*** — the no-go elevates the load-bearing role of the specific AAT machinery that supplies the escape. *Naming the floor and naming the unique broadly-available escape* is what the framework offers against discouraging-no-go readings of similar results in adjacent literatures.
