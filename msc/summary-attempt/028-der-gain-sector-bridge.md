# Gain-Sector Bridge (derived — conditional on directional-fidelity)

One of the chapter's named load-bearing moves: the **demotion of the sector-condition parameter $\alpha$ from an opaque postulate to a derived property**. The bridge theorem states that the gain-based update rule from Chapter 3 produces correction dynamics satisfying the sector condition (with parameter $\alpha = \eta^\ast \cdot c_{\min}$, gain times worst-case directional fidelity) whenever the update rule has *directional fidelity* — i.e., the correction actually points roughly toward reality. For gradient-based agents, local strong convexity of the loss is sufficient for the one-point sector condition at the equilibrium and is *bidirectionally equivalent* to the stronger two-point / incremental sector condition (this gives the agent class of all gradient descents on strongly convex losses an exact equivalence to the sector-condition framework).

A list of *verified instances* — where the bridge produces an explicit $\alpha$ — covers most agents the framework cares about: scalar and matrix Kalman filters, Beta-Bernoulli conjugate updates, exponential family updates in natural parameters (over a bounded interior scope), gradient descent on strongly convex (or L2-regularized convex) losses, linear correctors with positive-definite gain-observation product, and even SPR-tuned PID controllers (where the sector constant turns out to be the phase margin and the tempo turns out to be the crossover frequency). For all of these, the sector parameter is no longer a free parameter — it is a function of the gain and the correction geometry.

Five named **failure modes** are precisely the cases where the bridge fails, and each is given a concrete mechanism. *Directional infidelity*: the mismatch transform rotates the correction away from the mismatch (pathological parameterizations or severe model-observation misalignment; for optimal Bayesian updates this is structurally ruled out). *Gain collapse*: the optimal gain heads to zero while disturbance persists, so the sector parameter heads to zero and the persistence condition fails (not a failure of the bridge but of persistence itself). *Nonlinear saturation*: the correction function saturates at large mismatch and the sector ratio decays — this is exactly what the local one-point sector condition is designed to handle. *Unobservable directions*: when some directions have no observational handle, the sector condition holds only in the observable subspace. *Model misspecification*: the model class doesn't contain the truth, the gradient direction is wrong, and directional fidelity fails — and *this is the trigger condition* for the structural-adaptation-necessity result later in the chapter, now given a precise geometric characterization: structural adaptation is needed when the agent crosses an inflection surface of its loss landscape.

A subtler note is added about the geometry: under a parameterization-invariance axiom (PI) — that AAT's theorems should not depend on arbitrary choice of coordinates on the model state — Čencov's uniqueness theorem forces the Fisher information metric uniquely on statistical-manifold sub-cases. This upgrades the matrix-Kalman and exponential-family-natural-parameter sector statements from "derived conditional on choice of inner product" to "derived AAT-internally forced" via Čencov. The framework is honest that this is the Fisher-layer analog of similar uniqueness-theorem-based coordinate forcings in other layers, and that under non-adoption of (PI) the same statements survive in Euclidean form with explicit conditioning penalties.

The framework is also honest about what the bridge does *not* eliminate. Some agent classes — non-gradient rule-based systems, severely misspecified agents, variational approximate posteriors, stochastic-gradient methods on noisy losses, non-convex gradient agents beyond the basin — genuinely require the sector condition as a primitive posit. The honest architectural statement is *scope narrowing* (a sub-scope $\alpha$ where the bridge holds rigorously and a sub-scope $\beta$ where the sector condition remains an empirical claim), not universal derivation. The prediction chain at the end is summarized cleanly: gain principle plus directional fidelity gives the sector condition; the sector condition plus Lyapunov machinery gives persistence, adaptive reserve, and the adversarial-scaling results. The full formal chain of Part I is now complete.
