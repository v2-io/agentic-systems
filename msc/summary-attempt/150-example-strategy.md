# Strategy DAG Worked Example — 3-Arm Bandit (appendix worked example — conditional)

A worked instantiation of Part II's strategy DAG machinery on a 3-arm bandit problem. The framework constructs an explicit strategy DAG (root objective node, three OR-arm nodes representing the three arms as alternative paths to the objective, edge credences capturing per-arm success-rate beliefs); applies the status-propagation forward pass to produce the strategy-plan-confidence score; computes the satisfaction gap and control regret diagnostic quantities; runs the orient cascade in its full five-step form on a worked scenario where the diagnostic 2×2 cell map routes the agent through several iterations.

The worked example demonstrates the chapter-level results in their concrete form. The chain-confidence decay shows up because each arm requires multiple steps (action then observation then update); the observability-dominance result shows up because unobservable arms have frozen credences; the satisfaction gap and control regret separate cleanly into the four diagnostic cells; the orient cascade routes between them in the prescribed order (epistemic update first, then attainability check, then strategy revision if needed).

The framework's *concrete contribution* of the worked example is to show that **the abstract machinery is operational**. Every chapter-level result has a concrete instantiation in this bandit example; the framework's machinery is *not just analytical idealization* but produces measurable predictions on a small enough problem to verify exhaustively.

A subtler demonstration: the worked example shows the **AND/OR semantics being load-bearing**. The 3-arm bandit has OR-structure at the root (any arm sufficient); a variant with three steps that must *all* succeed (AND-structure) produces dramatically different chain-confidence-decay profiles. The framework's parameterization choice of AND/OR (rather than weighted combinations or noisy-OR) is *exercised* in the worked example; the resulting predictions match what bandit theory predicts independently for OR-structures and what classical chain-reliability theory predicts for AND-structures.

The worked example is the framework's *bridge to strategy-revision practice*: where a practitioner needs to instantiate the orient cascade for their own problem, this example provides the template — construct the strategy DAG, propagate to get the plan-confidence, compute the diagnostic quantities, route through the cascade steps based on the diagnostic 2×2 cell map.
