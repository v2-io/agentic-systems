# Resource-Bounded Destabilization (appendix derivation — conditional, exploratory off-spine)

A *companion derivation* to the resource-budget formulation. The result: **a hard-budget agent self-depletes to certain finite-time destabilization against even a *constant-effectiveness* adversary** — closing the effects-spiral mechanism from the adversarial-destabilization treatment *not by formalizing its open coupling term but by making that term unnecessary*.

The argument: instantiate the sector-persistence template with the target's mismatch as state variable, with the sector parameter *resource-gated* per the resource-budget gating assumption. Under hard-budget regime (no replenishment) plus the cost assumption (drain rate non-decreasing in mismatch), the agent's resource budget *deterministically depletes* over time. As the budget falls, the sector parameter falls (gating); as the sector parameter falls, the persistence-margin shrinks; at the critical-budget threshold, the persistence inequality flips and the agent crosses the destabilization threshold — *in finite time, with certainty*, against even a constant adversary coupling.

The framework names this as a *cleaner alternative* to the adversarial-destabilization segment's *effects-spiral* corollary. The effects-spiral story was discussion-grade because the link "degrading model implies stronger adversary coupling" requires specifying how model degradation feeds back into the dynamics — which the adversarial-destabilization segment's machinery does not carry. The resource-budget approach supplies the feedback through a *different and cleaner channel*: **the agent's own correction rate, not the adversary's coupling**. The agent's correction rate degrades because its resource depletes; the adversary's coupling is constant; the result is the same — certain finite-time destabilization.

The framework names this carefully as *orthogonal to* the deferred joint-Jacobian problem in the adversarial-destabilization segment. It does not *resolve* the effects-spiral derivation; it provides an *alternative path* to the same destabilization conclusion under a different set of stated conditions. The two derivations operate on *different mechanisms*: the effects-spiral's mechanism is mismatch-driven coupling amplification (currently unspecified); the resource-bounded mechanism is resource-driven correction degradation (specified under the two named posits).

The framework's overall posture toward this result: it is *off-spine* (no Part I, II, or III result depends on it) and *exploratory*. The conditional derivation gives a *substitute* for the effects-spiral story, available under explicit assumptions about resource dynamics. Whether this becomes the framework's *primary* account of adversarial-pressure-driven destabilization, or remains an alternative path, depends on whether the resource-budget axis is eventually lifted to first-class scope status.
