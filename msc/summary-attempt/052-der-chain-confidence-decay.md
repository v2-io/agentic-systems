# Chain Confidence Decay (derived — exact)

A simple but load-bearing identity: for a chain of uncertain steps in a strategy, the log-probability of the whole chain is the sum of conditional log-probabilities of each step given the steps before it. Since each conditional log-probability is non-positive, chain confidence *decays monotonically with depth*. This is the chain rule of probability lifted to log-space — a mathematical identity, true under no assumptions beyond the probability axioms.

A quick illustration with independent uniform-probability edges drives the point home: at 90% per-step confidence, a 10-step plan is 35% likely; at 80% it is 11%; at 20 steps with 80% it is 1%. The independent case is the *simplest* special case, not the general result — when steps have positive conditional dependence (success at step $k$ makes step $k+1$ more likely), decay is slower; when steps have negative dependence (resource depletion, adversary adaptation), decay is faster.

The framework draws *structural pressures* that this identity creates on strategies. Short plans are favored over long ones. Parallel fallback paths (OR-branches) provide alternative routes when one chain fails. Critical links — steps appearing in every path — warrant disproportionate investment in reliability. Early monitoring detects chain failure early rather than discovering it at the end. These are *consequences*, not prescriptions — an agent that ignores chain decay will experience more strategy failures, lower effective tempo, and (if the failures are costly) faster reserve depletion.

A subtler corollary: **AND-nodes amplify decay**. When multiple parent chains must all succeed (conjunctive combination), their confidences multiply. A node requiring $k$ parents each at depth $d$ with per-edge confidence $p$ has aggregate confidence $p^{kd}$, not $p^d$. *Deep conjunctive strategies are exponentially more fragile than deep disjunctive ones*. This asymmetry is what motivates the AND/OR distinction the framework will formalize in the next segment.

The framework explicitly names a **triple depth penalty** on chain length, with the chain-confidence decay being only the first piece. Chain depth creates three independent penalties that compound: (1) **confidence decay** — deeper chains have lower aggregate confidence (this segment); (2) **evidence starvation** — a downstream edge in a chain is tested only when all upstream edges succeed, so its effective correction rate is attenuated by the product of upstream success rates (formalized in an appendix derivation of edge dynamics); (3) **cognitive cost** — deeper chains have higher description length, consuming more representational capacity (formalized in a separate strategy-complexity-cost segment). The maximum useful chain depth is the minimum over these three independent constraints.

The chain-rule identity also has a special structural role across the framework: it is the **anchor of the additive-coordinate-forcing meta-pattern**. Three further uniqueness theorems in AAT at other layers use the same log-additive structure as motivation for an AAT-internal axiom on which a uniqueness theorem then operates: reverse-KL at the divergence layer, log-odds at the update layer, Fisher metric at the metric layer. An elementary identity at the head of this chapter does load-bearing work three layers deep across the framework.
