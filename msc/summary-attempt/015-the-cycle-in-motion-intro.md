# Chapter Introduction: The Cycle in Motion (discussion — framing prose)

Chapter 2 left the model as a static object — a compressed snapshot of the chronica with measurable sufficiency. Chapter 3 now sets that object in motion. Events arrive; the agent corrects; the dynamics of the adaptive cycle as it actually runs become the chapter's subject.

The first two results, the intro foreshadows, fall out of a commitment already made. Because $M_t$ was committed as the agent's *complete* state — anything remembered is in $M_t$, and new information enters only through events — two things follow immediately. The update from before-event to after-event depends only on the prior model and the incoming event, and nothing else (the agent does not need to, and structurally cannot, re-process its entire history at each step). And the agent's action depends on $M_t$ alone. Both feel obvious in hindsight; both are derived, not chosen.

The cycle's engine is the **mismatch signal** — the gap between predicted observation and actual observation. The intro previews that mismatch decomposes cleanly into two parts: reducible model error (which better modeling can shrink) and irreducible observation noise (which no amount of smarter modeling can eliminate). This second piece sets a *floor* on prediction error; chasing below it amounts to fitting noise.

Given a mismatch, how much should the agent trust the new observation against its prior model? The intro states the answer in a startlingly simple form: the optimal **update gain** is the ratio of model uncertainty to total uncertainty (model uncertainty plus observation uncertainty). When the agent is highly uncertain about its model, gain approaches 1: trust the observation. When the agent is confident and the channel noisy, gain approaches 0: trust your model. For linear-Gaussian agents this is exactly the Kalman gain; outside that case it is a robust qualitative result — any rational adaptive process must approximate this functional form whether or not it explicitly computes a variance ratio.

Multiplying gain by event rate and summing across channels produces **adaptive tempo** — the rate at which the agent turns observations into useful corrections, speed times quality. The intro flags this as the chapter's central object and the load-bearing capacity variable for the rest of the framework: every persistence threshold in Chapter 4 will have tempo on the left-hand side, and every adversarial-coupling result in Part III will depend on tempo ratios.

The chapter closes with a heuristic preview: under linear correction with bounded environmental disturbance, steady-state mismatch is *(rate of world change) / (tempo)*. Mismatch stays bounded when the agent corrects faster than reality drifts. This is the persistence condition in its simplest form. Chapter 4 will generalize it to nonlinear correction under a sector condition where the *shape* of the result remains the same. Causal information yield (CIY) enters here because action-coupling is where causality first bites — it scores actions for informational value to the cycle and will be lifted to strategy-revision contexts in Part II.
