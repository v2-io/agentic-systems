# Compression Operations in AAT (appendix discussion — robust-qualitative)

The framework names *four compression operations* that share the *Information Bottleneck shape*. The model is the IB-optimal compression of the chronica for prediction. The strategy DAG is the IB compression of the chronica for guidance (the strategy-cost objective). Shared intent is the IB compression of the sender's purposeful state for coordination behavior. The composition projection is the IB-shape constraint on the closure-defect.

The framework's posture: **the IB shape recurs because it is the *natural form for bounded-cognition compression with a relevance criterion***. Each compression operation has a *what gets compressed* (the source) and a *what must be preserved* (the relevance variable). The IB Lagrangian trades off compression-cost against relevance-preservation. All four AAT compressions fit this shape.

What this *does not claim*: that the four operations reduce to a single optimization problem. The framework is careful that *cross-instance theorems do not follow from the shared shape alone* — several conditions (Lipschitz regularity, dimensional reduction in the Gaussian case, interventional relevance for Level-2 edges) remain outside the IB frame and require separate treatment per instance.

The framework also handles the *one underspecified source* — the ontologically ambiguous "true causal structure" the strategy compression's relevance variable would point to in the standard IB form. The strategy-cost objective uses a *sibling form* (the information-theoretic-MDP form, KL-to-target-policy rather than mutual-information-to-relevance-variable) precisely because the relevance variable for guidance is *the optimal policy*, not an observable. The two compression forms (IB and IT-MDP) descend from Shannon rate-distortion theory and admit Lagrangian relaxation; the choice depends on whether the compressed variable should preserve information about an observable or match a target policy.

The compression-operations framework also establishes that *composition admissibility is the Lagrangian-dual of a standard IB objective*. The closure-defect framework's bound trades off compression aggressiveness (how aggressively the macro-state compresses the micro-state) against fidelity (how well the macro-dynamics track the micro-dynamics). This is the IB shape applied at the *composition* level: the closure defect is the rate-distortion gap between micro-trajectory information and macro-trajectory-representable information.

The framework's overall posture: AAT's compression operations are *unified at the shape level but not at the content level*. The IB shape is a *family of objectives* sharing rate-distortion structure; each AAT compression operation is an instance with its own relevance variable, its own trade-off parameter, and its own scope. The framework's commitment is to *name the shared shape* (the IB family) and to *handle each instance honestly* (the differences in relevance variable, scope, and required additional conditions).
