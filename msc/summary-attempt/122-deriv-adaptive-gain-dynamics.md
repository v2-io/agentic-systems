# Adaptive-Gain Dynamics (appendix derivation — conditional)

The framework's extension to agents that *learn their gain* dynamically — adaptive Kalman (learning the noise model), IMM (regime-switching), RMSProp / Adam (online step-size), MAML (gain optimization across tasks). The gain becomes a *state variable* with its own update dynamics, and the question is whether the sector-persistence machinery extends.

The derivation introduces an *augmented-state* Lyapunov argument with the gain as additional state. Under four named *meta-gain conditions* — MG-1 through MG-4 — the primary and meta-gain sector conditions *compose* via standard two-timescale Lyapunov machinery (Khalil Theorem 4.18) rather than via Tikhonov reduction. The result: AAT's sub-scope $\alpha$ from the gain-sector bridge splits further into **sub-scope $\alpha_1$** (fixed-gain, the original case) and **sub-scope $\alpha_2$** (adaptive-gain under the meta-gain conditions); sub-scope $\beta$ catches the rest where the sector condition must be primitively assumed.

The result has *operational consequences for several well-known optimizer and inference-method choices*. **AMSGrad is a meta-gain repair that restores meta-gain condition MG-1 by construction**, which is *why it outperforms Adam* on ill-conditioned problems — the empirical superiority is now derived from the framework rather than left as folklore. **MAML's outer loop sits in sub-scope $\beta$**, which is *why its non-convexity is structurally forced* rather than a specific algorithmic accident. **IMM regime transitions sit in sub-scope $\beta$ across the transition**, which is *why adaptive Kalman in regime-switching settings needs explicit dwell-time machinery* — the meta-gain conditions fail during transition.

**Mehra non-identifiability in adaptive Kalman regime-switching** is flagged as a *candidate fifth instance of the identifiability-floor pattern*. The structural shape matches: a classical no-go from a different mathematical literature (Mehra's identifiability result on adaptive Kalman parameter estimation) maps onto an AAT setting (sub-scope $\beta$ regime transitions) and identifies a unique broadly-available escape via AAT machinery (instrumented dwell-time at the regime boundary). Formal promotion of Instance 5 is left as an open direction.

The framework's overall posture: **decades of empirical RL and approximate-inference folklore about optimizer choice and inference-method selection has structural derivation in this segment**. The choice between gain mechanisms is *not free*: it has structural consequences for which sub-scope the agent's persistence guarantee lives in, and therefore for how much observation bandwidth the agent must consume to hold its persistence guarantees (per the information-rate floor). Moving from sub-scope $\alpha_1$ to $\alpha_2$ or $\beta$ pays in the persistence-bandwidth requirement: the same target distortion costs more information per unit time because the effective sector constant is smaller.
