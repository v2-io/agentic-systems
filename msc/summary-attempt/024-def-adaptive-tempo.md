# Adaptive Tempo (definition — exact, scalar form; tensor form exact in Fisher-local regime)

The framework's central capacity quantity is defined: **adaptive tempo** $\mathcal{T}$, the effective rate at which an agent acquires useful information from its environment, given as the sum across all observation channels of *channel event rate times optimal update gain* on that channel. In the single-channel case, tempo is simply event-rate times gain. A tensor extension is also defined: in the Fisher-local regime where gains are matrix-valued, tempo becomes a matrix sum whose eigenvalues give per-direction adaptation rates. The scalar form is recovered in the shared-eigenbasis collapse (when the prior precision and each channel's likelihood precision commute, as always in 1-D and along the natural-gradient direction in higher dimensions).

Tempo combines two factors that the framework emphasizes are *both* load-bearing and *substitutable*: loop **speed** (event rate, how fast the agent cycles) and update **quality** (gain, how well it extracts information per event). An agent can achieve a given tempo via a fast noisy loop or a slower calibrated one; improvements to both factors compound multiplicatively. The deeper consequence is observation-noise gating: because the gain on each channel is the model-uncertainty / total-uncertainty ratio, a noisy observation channel collapses tempo regardless of how fast the loop runs. *You cannot outrun a bad observation channel by iterating faster.* This is presented as the formal grounding of Boyd's emphasis on Orient quality over raw OODA speed.

Tempo is the load-bearing capacity variable for the rest of the framework. It appears on the left-hand side of the persistence condition (Chapter 4), determines adversarial advantage in Part III, and connects to code quality as observation infrastructure in the software-domain instantiation (Volume 2). The strategic analog (strategic tempo over strategy-edge revision) extends the same structure into Part II.

Two important honesty caveats are surfaced. First, the additive multi-channel formula *assumes informationally independent channels*. When channels are correlated (overlapping sensors, repeated teammate reports, redundant telemetry), the additive formula *overcounts* effective tempo — the correct quantity is bounded above by the sum, with the gap a *redundancy penalty* governed by mutual information between event streams conditioned on the current model. Second, scalar tempo assumes *isotropic* correction capacity. When the agent corrects some dimensions faster than others, scalar tempo overestimates effective adaptation along the weak dimensions; simulation confirms this can produce dramatic overestimates (one simulation reports 72%). The correct formulation in anisotropic regimes is per-dimension, and under cross-dimensional correction the canonical sharp form is a matrix-Loewner persistence condition that the framework treats as strictly sharper than the per-coordinate form (and which can be *unsafe* if the eigenbasis of the tempo tensor misaligns with the coordinate axes the agent thinks in).
