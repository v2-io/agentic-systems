# Consolidation Dynamics (formulation — robust-qualitative)

A formal regime of the agent's between-event dynamics. **Consolidation** is the regime in which the agent applies updates driven not by external events but by *replayed or internally-generated pseudo-events* synthesized from its retained trace — replay buffers, hippocampal reinstatement, remembered episodes, re-read paragraphs. The recursive-update form is preserved; what distinguishes consolidation is the *objective* these updates optimize. The consolidation objective is the **information-bottleneck Lagrangian evaluated against the accumulated chronica**, in contrast to online update's one-step predictive mismatch objective.

A subtle but important property follows: under the framework's completeness commitment, the pseudo-event carries *zero new external information*. Yet the update still does work — it *redistributes* existing information across the factorization structure of the model. The distinguishing content is not the information brought in (zero by construction) but the rate-distortion gap closed (nonzero, when the agent has not yet reached the IB-optimal compression).

The framework names a **scope condition**: the consolidation regime is well-defined only when the consolidation rate is much smaller than the online event rate. This is a direct application of the temporal-nesting result — consolidation sits at an intermediate timescale between parametric update (fast) and structural adaptation (slow), and violating the convergence constraint produces the same oscillation failures temporal nesting warns about.

A **necessity condition** is identified: consolidation is *necessary* (online-only cannot reach the IB optimum) when *both* of the following hold: **(N1) sub-state factorization** — the model factors into two sub-states with divergent compression-prediction trade-offs, capturing cross-episode regularities versus verbatim traces (the *Complementary Learning Systems* factorization from McClelland-McNaughton-O'Reilly 1995); and **(N2) bounded per-event budget** — the per-event processing budget is strictly less than the integration cost for updating the slow sub-state against cross-episode regularities. When either condition fails, consolidation is a *luxury*: Kalman filters, conjugate-Bayesian agents with full posterior, and linear-Gaussian systems with online Riccati updates all satisfy neither condition and have no consolidation need. When *both* hold, consolidation is a *necessity*: no online-only policy reaches the IB optimum under the joint constraint.

The framework derives a **stability-plasticity feasibility window**. The strategy-persistence schema gave a *plasticity lower bound* on the forgetting rate (forget fast enough to track non-stationarity). Consolidation supplies a *stability upper bound* (forget slow enough to let consolidation integrate cross-episode patterns before they are discarded). Between these bounds is the **feasibility window**. An *empty window* — rapid non-stationarity with slow consolidation cadence — is the **catastrophic-forgetting regime**: no forgetting rate satisfies both constraints and the agent's long-run IB objective is strictly worse than what a slower environment or faster consolidation would yield.

Consolidation also turns out to be the operating regime where *structural adaptation* becomes executable. Parametric update has a hard timescale constraint (mismatch decays at the tempo rate; delayed updates accumulate at the disturbance rate). Structural adaptation tolerates much larger delay because the slow process operates on what tolerance is being measured *against* — the model class itself. The per-event budget is irrelevant when updates are offline; interleaved replay supports stability-preserving structural change. *All finite-budget agents require consolidation for quality-preserving structural change.* Pure online structural adaptation is the luxury case where per-event budget equals or exceeds the integration cost for structural-class operations — where Bayesian nonparametric agents with unlimited compute sit.
