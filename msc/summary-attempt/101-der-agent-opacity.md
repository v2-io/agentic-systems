# Agent Opacity (derived — conditional)

The formal *dual* of observation quality. Where observation quality characterizes how well the agent sees the world, **agent opacity** characterizes *how well the world sees the agent*. Quantified as the **backward predictive entropy** of the agent's future actions given another agent's filtration — observer-indexed, horizon-indexed, trajectory-indexed. Adopted from Hafez et al. (2026) as a first-class multi-agent quantity.

Each of the four indexing arguments is load-bearing. **Observer-indexed**: different observers (allies with shared infrastructure; adversaries with limited instrumentation; the environment itself) have different filtrations; opacity varies accordingly. **Horizon-indexed**: immediate-next-action opacity and long-horizon-plan opacity *decouple* — an agent may be predictable at immediate action but unpredictable at plan level, or vice versa. **Trajectory-indexed**: per the framework's singular-trajectory scope, opacity is the opacity of *this* trajectory's continuation, not a type-level claim. **Time-indexed**: opacity may drift with learning (as the observer's model of the agent improves, opacity decreases).

The framework's central structural observation: **opacity is sign-flipped in value across regimes**. *Low opacity (legibility) enables cooperative coordination*: teammates need to predict each other to coordinate; teams with high inter-member opacity have effective disturbance dominated by each member's inability to model the others. *High opacity enables adversarial effectiveness*: an attacker whose actions are predictable to the target can be anticipated and absorbed; an opaque attacker generates surprise residuals at the boundary of the target's sector region. **The sign-flip is a direct consequence of AAT's existing signed-coupling structure** rather than a separate posit — cooperative coupling wants low opacity for both directions; adversarial coupling wants low opacity *from the attacker's view* (high prediction of the target) and high opacity *of the attacker* (low prediction by the target).

This is what makes opacity the **dual of observation quality** rather than an independent quantity. The same agent in different relationships has different opacity values to different observers; the cooperative-adversarial signed structure determines which value is desirable in each direction.

The framework also derives an **emitter-side four-regime classification** as the dual of the recipient-side classification from the prior chapter. An emitter generating an event for a recipient can do so in four regimes determined by opacity (whether the event is predictable to the recipient or surprising) and content (whether the event is informative or large). The composition of the recipient-side and emitter-side four-regime classifications produces a **16-cell joint composition** with closed-form arg-max for adversarial-targeting decisions — the chapter's machinery for *what attack to choose against what target*. The attacker's optimal targeting strategy minimizes the recipient's repair effectiveness; the 16-cell decomposition tells the attacker which combination of emitter regime and recipient regime accomplishes that.
