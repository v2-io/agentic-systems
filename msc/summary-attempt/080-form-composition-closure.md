# Composition Closure Criterion (formulation — conditional)

The formal definition of when a group of interacting agents constitutes a *valid composite macro-agent*. The criterion: the closed-loop dynamics *approximately commute with coarse-graining* — projecting micro-states to macro-states and then running macro-dynamics yields approximately the same result as running micro-dynamics and then projecting. The **closure defect** is the minimum achievable mismatch between these two paths over all admissible projections and macro-dynamics.

The construction is careful and technical. The projection maps micro states, observations, and actions to macro versions, with explicit accommodation for a *timescale ratio* — the number of micro-timesteps per macro-step — that lets the framework handle both lockstep composites (synchronous-update) and composites that operate strictly slower than their sub-agents (the regime temporal nesting asserts as natural). When the ratio is large, the composite's description need not — and should not — track the micro-trajectory between macro-boundaries; timescale abstraction is recovered.

The closure-defect framework applies *only* to sets that satisfy the composite-agent scope condition from the prior chapter. Given scope-satisfaction, a set forms a *meaningful* composite agent when the closure defect is below a chosen threshold. Without scope-satisfaction, the closure-defect infimum is still well-defined as a projection property, but the resulting "composite" has ill-defined purposeful substate and composition-level machinery (team persistence, composite tempo) does not apply.

The framework specifies **admissibility constraints** on the macro-dynamics — conditions (A1)-(A4) the macro-dynamics must satisfy for the macro-agent to be AAT-shaped: (A1) the macro-state decomposes into model and purposeful components with a recursive update; (A2) a macro-mismatch signal is well-defined; (A3) macro-tempo is well-defined as a sum-or-aggregate of sub-agent tempos; (A4) the macro-correction function satisfies the sector condition. Together these make the composite a proper AAT agent rather than just a low-dimensional projection.

A **bridge lemma** is the technical heart of the result. The lemma gives conditions under which Part I's sector-persistence template applied at the composite level yields a *strictly stronger* contraction assumption than just (A4). For different *tiers* of sub-agent quality (Tier 1: Bayesian/linear correctors on exponential families with positive-definite gain; Tier 2: locally convex nonlinear; Tier 3: non-convex or rule-based), Part I/II results transfer to the composite differently: Tier 1 transfers *exactly*; Tier 2 transfers locally with bounded degradation; Tier 3 requires per-domain verification.

The closure defect itself is the **effective disturbance** at the composite level — it enters the composite's persistence inequality as the rate at which the macro-tracking diverges from the micro-truth. The composite persists when the macro sector parameter exceeds the sum of the external disturbance rate and the closure-defect-driven internal disturbance rate, divided by the macro reserve. This makes precise the *cost of imperfect composition*: a composite with high closure defect carries that defect as a perpetual disturbance source against its own persistence.
