# Information Bottleneck (formulation — exact, applied external theorem)

This segment imports Tishby's information bottleneck (1999) directly as the framework's characterization of optimal model compression. The agent's compression function $\phi$ is, optimally, the one that *minimizes* what it retains of the chronica while *maximizing* what it preserves about future observations conditioned on future actions, with a trade-off parameter $\beta$ governing the balance. The Markov chain *(future observations) – (chronica) – (model)* holds by construction — the model state has access to history but not directly to future observations — so Tishby's theorem applies exactly under AAT's bindings. The choice to characterize the optimum via IB rather than via MDL or a Bayesian-sufficiency criterion is a representational choice, but given that choice the form of the optimal compression and its trade-off structure are exact consequences of the imported theorem.

A subtle but important point is stated: it is tempting to claim that an agent in a more volatile environment should *actively* lower $\beta$ to favor aggressive compression. The segment refuses this as a double-counting error. Volatility already natively degrades the mutual information between old history and future observations — old history mathematically loses its predictive power as environmental drift increases — and the optimal compression discards that useless old information automatically, even if $\beta$ stays constant. Therefore $\beta$ adjustments track changes in the agent's *internal* cost of memory or computational capacity, not changes in environmental volatility; the agent adapts its *actions* (more exploration) in response to volatility, but the optimal IB representation adapts natively through the joint distribution.

What the formulation does *not* claim is that real agents explicitly solve the IB optimization. Variational IB in deep-learning practice is a parametric approximation; biological agents approximate the trade-off through forgetting, attention, abstraction, and summarization. The IB form characterizes the optimum, not the procedure.

The segment also draws an important architectural distinction the framework relies on later. AAT will use *two sibling forms* of compression, both descendants of Shannon rate-distortion: the canonical IB form (mutual-information-to-a-relevance-variable, used for the model $M_t$, for shared intent, and for the convention hierarchy $\Lambda$); and the information-theoretic-MDP form (KL-divergence-to-a-target-policy, used in strategy-cost compression). Neither reduces to the other without changing what the compressed variable should preserve, and naming both as siblings rather than rivals lets the framework use both honestly. It also identifies the IB form as the rate-distortion specialization of the variational free-energy decomposition used in active inference — borrowing the form without committing to active inference's preferences-as-priors stance or to expected free energy as a master objective.
