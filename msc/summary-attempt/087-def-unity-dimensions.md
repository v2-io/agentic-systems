# Unity Dimensions (definition — discussion-grade)

The quality of a composite's composition is parametrized along *two architecturally distinct axes*. The **content axis** has four dimensions naming *what sub-agents share*: epistemic unity (shared model), teleological unity (shared objective), strategic unity (coordinated action), and perceptual unity (shared observations). The **structural axis** has one dimension naming *whether sub-agents implement the same correction rule* — update-rule homogeneity. Together they parametrize the rate-distortion curves for the closure-defect components from the prior chapter. The two axes are *independent*: agents can share a model perfectly while updating it differently, and conversely.

The content dimensions are quantitative. **Epistemic unity** is the multi-information-to-total-correlation ratio between sub-agent models — 1 for identical models, 0 for independent models. **Teleological unity** is the value-correlation between sub-agent objectives over encountered trajectories — +1 for identical, -1 for opposing, 0 for orthogonal. **Strategic unity** measures how close the joint policy is to a hypothetical jointly-optimal policy (requires knowing the jointly-optimal policy, a strong assumption). **Perceptual unity** is the fraction of total observation information that reaches all sub-agents — full unity means all agents observe the same signals; zero means private observations only.

The framework is explicit about scope: the unity decomposition applies only to composites that satisfy the composite-agent scope condition. Teleological unity plays a role in the (C-i) alignment route via value-correlation, but the scope condition is a *disjunction* of four routes, not a scalar threshold on teleological unity. The quality-metric role this segment defines presumes scope-satisfaction via *some* route. When sub-agents fail all four routes, composition-level quantities are not well-defined.

The structural axis — **update-rule homogeneity** — captures something the content axis cannot. In pure Part I composition (passive estimators, no purposeful state), agents with identical content can still produce non-zero closure defect if their update rules differ. The content axis cannot detect this; the structural axis is required. Worked instance: heterogeneous Kalman filters with different gains produce closure defect proportional to the gain difference even when their models contain the same information.

A central methodological point: **unity (in either sense) does not directly predict closure-defect magnitude — it controls the *compressibility* of the corresponding state, observation, or action component under projection**. Higher unity along either axis permits more aggressive compression at lower closure defect; neither axis alone is sufficient. The closure-defect components are *functions of both axes* — the relevant content unity and the structural unity together, plus projection aggressiveness.
