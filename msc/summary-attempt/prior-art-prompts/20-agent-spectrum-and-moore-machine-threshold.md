## The Core Idea / Claim
Agents partition along **two independent dimensions** — *model richness* (how rich the agent's internal predictive model is, from absent/degenerate to full world-model) and *objective richness* (how richly the agent's preferences are structured, from absent/degenerate through scalar setpoint through trajectory functional). The cross gives four region-archetypes — reactive systems (both axes degenerate), adaptive trackers (rich model, no objective — passive Bayesian learners, Kalman filters), blind seekers (rich objective, degenerate model — gradient followers, PID), actuated agents (both rich) — treated as *continuum regions, not discrete categories*. Agents migrate; the boundaries shift with auto-tuning, exploration phases, training. The framework names a striking empirical anchor: **Moore-machine model organisms (Miller 2022, *Ex Machina*) show that the 1-state-to-2-state threshold is *the* critical computational boundary for social behavior**. One-state machines produce fixed output regardless of input and are *provably incapable of social behavior in any game-theoretic setting* — no game, no payoff structure, no amount of interaction produces cooperation, coordination, or exchange. A two-state machine makes a quantum leap into the adaptive-tracker / blind-seeker boundary — branching, one bit of history, Tit-For-Tat-class strategies. *The two-state machine is the minimal AAT agent*. A complementary bridge: Hafez et al. (2026) measure *bi-predictability* P = MI(S,A;S')/C as the information structure of the agent-environment coupling. Bridge simulations confirm bi-predictability moves with framework tempo but the two are scale-invariantly different objects — bi-predictability measures the *architecture* of the coupling (scale-invariant, blind to absolute mismatch magnitude); tempo measures *performance within the coupling* (not scale-invariant). The two are *complementary* — architecture-measurement vs performance-measurement of the same coupling.

## Boundaries of the Claim
- Domain: cognitive architectures, finite-state-automaton models of agency, evolutionary game theory, information-theoretic measures of agency, agent taxonomies, complex adaptive systems.
- Focus: the *2D-axis decomposition* (model × objective richness as continuous, not discrete) AND the empirically/theoretically grounded *minimum-complexity threshold* for social behavior AND the architecture-vs-performance distinction in agency measures.

## What Kind of Match Counts
- 2D-axis (or multi-axis) taxonomic decompositions of agents based on internal-state structure rather than behavior-only classification.
- Empirical or theoretical results on *minimum-state-count thresholds* for social behavior, cooperation, or coordination in finite-state-automaton models of agency.
- Information-theoretic measures of agency that are *scale-invariant* (architecture-level) rather than performance-bound — distinguishable from raw performance metrics.
- Bridges between Moore-machine / finite-state-automaton models of agency and continuous-state agent models.
- Coevolving-automata / agent-based model frameworks with structural threshold findings.
- Distinctions between *what an agent can in principle do* (architecture) and *what it does in this environment* (performance).

## What Would NOT Count
- Standard agent taxonomies (reactive / deliberative / hybrid; Brustoloni / Russell-Norvig) without the 2D-continuum-axis decomposition.
- Game-theoretic state-machine work without the social-behavior-emerges-at-2-states finding.
- Generic IPD / Axelrod cooperation literature without the minimum-complexity-threshold framing.
- Information-theoretic agency measures that conflate architecture with performance.
