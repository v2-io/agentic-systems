## The Core Idea / Claim
Optimal communication between cooperative agents ("Shared Intent") is the Information Bottleneck compression of the sender's purposeful state. Under bandwidth constraints, there is a mathematically optimal hierarchy ("Auftragstaktik"): sharing objectives > coordinating strategies > synchronizing models. When agents update based on communication, the update gain is discounted by three factors: channel noise, source competence (epistemic uncertainty), and source alignment (teleological uncertainty). Because misplaced trust causes a fatal "effects spiral," trust meta-models must evaluate risk asymmetrically (using a conservative posterior quantile).

## Boundaries of the Claim
- Domain: Multi-agent RL communication protocols, mechanism design, organizational economics, Bayesian trust models.
- Assumptions: Agents are bandwidth-constrained and operate in high-stakes environments where trusting a bad actor/incompetent agent is catastrophic.

## What Kind of Match Counts
- Formal proofs supporting "Auftragstaktik" / Mission Command: showing it is mathematically superior to share goals rather than plans/models under bandwidth constraints.
- Applying Information Bottleneck / rate-distortion theory to optimize what is communicated between MARL agents.

- Risk-asymmetric trust evaluation (using quantiles/CVaR rather than expected mean) specifically to avoid catastrophic spirals in decentralized systems.

## What Would NOT Count

- Simple reputation/trust systems that bundle intent and competence into a single generic "reliability" score.
