## The Core Idea / Claim
A group of agents forms a valid composite macro-agent if and only if their closed-loop dynamics commute with coarse-graining (measured by a "Closure Defect"). The composite's adaptive tempo is bounded by the sum of its parts minus "coordination overhead". This yields a formal derivation of Brooks's Law: if overhead exceeds baseline correction rate, adding agents causes the composite to fail. Extreme composition results in "Symbiogenesis": asymmetric integration where one agent's objective is fully absorbed into the host.

- Focus: Mathematical definitions of agency boundaries and the overhead of coordination.
