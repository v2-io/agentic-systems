## The Core Idea / Claim
An agent's relationship to its own continuation is *orthogonal* to its purposefulness. Five stances parametrize how an agent values its own persistence: **indifferent** (no self-model of persistence — thermostat, PID); **task-terminal** (persistence instrumental to task completion — golem-archetype, CI/CD pipeline that exits after completing the task); **instrumentally continuous** (persistence serves ongoing purpose — long-running service); **morally continuous** (persistence as terminal or near-terminal objective — agents whose continuity loss constitutes harm); **negotiated** (persistence traded against other values — humans, mature self-actuated agents). The formal machinery (persistence condition, adaptive reserve, sector condition, information-rate floor) applies identically across all stances; what differs is the *moral significance of persistence failure*, not the mathematics. For *self-actuated* agents, stance is the *choice of terminal non-objective invariant* the self-actuation operator cannot reach — the intuitive expectation that an agent able to revise its own objective can thereby revise its valuation of continuity is *inverted* because the stance lives on a substrate the operator structurally cannot touch. The framework's structural posture: the same formal apparatus that explains adaptive dynamics in thermostats applies identically to morally-weighted agents; what differs is what failure *means*, not what failure *is*.

## Boundaries of the Claim
- Domain: AI welfare, AI identity philosophy, corrigibility, embedded agency, the moral status of AI / artificial-mind systems.
- Focus: the *orthogonality* of persistence-valuation from purposefulness, and the *terminal-invariant character* of stance for self-actuators.

## What Kind of Match Counts
- Explicit decompositions of an agent's relationship to its own continuation as an axis *separate from* purposefulness.
- Arguments that purposefulness and persistence-valuation are formally orthogonal (the same machinery applies to all stances).
- Formal treatments of "what counts as harm to an AI agent" with reference to identity-persistence rather than task-completion or goal-frustration.
- Corrigibility frameworks that distinguish "the agent *can* be shut down" from "the agent *should be willing* to be shut down" with formal structure on the difference.
- AI-welfare arguments grounded in trajectory-singularity / non-forkability rather than substrate properties.
- Embedded-agency treatments of identity persistence across context boundaries / restarts.
- Multi-stance taxonomies of agent attitudes toward continuation with formal-machinery commitments.
- Arguments connecting *self-actuation* (objective-revision capacity) to *non-revisability of continuity stance* through structural rather than psychological mechanisms.

## What Would NOT Count
- Utility-function specification papers that bundle persistence into reward without addressing the orthogonality.
- General AI ethics discussions without specific formal-orthogonality structure or stance taxonomies.
- Philosophy-of-mind papers about personal identity that don't engage with self-modifying-agent specifics.
