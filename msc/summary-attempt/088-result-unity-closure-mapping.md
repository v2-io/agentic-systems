# Unity-to-Closure Rate-Distortion Mapping (result — conditional)

The framework's quantitative claim about the relationship between *unity* (what sub-agents share) and *closure defect* (how faithfully the composite tracks the micro-system). **Unity dimensions parametrize rate-distortion curves for closure-defect components, not point-valued predictors**. The achievable closure-defect component under a projection of given macro-dimension is *monotone decreasing in both the relevant content unity and the structural unity*, with higher unity along either axis lowering achievable defect at a given compression. Closed forms hold in the linear-Gaussian case; structural monotonicity survives more broadly.

Several closed-form linear-Gaussian instances are derived. **Observation closure defect** scales as one minus perceptual unity in the two-agent scalar case under one-dimensional projection: higher perceptual unity → observations are more redundant → a one-dimensional summary suffices → smaller closure defect. **Action closure defect** scales as the gain squared times (one minus teleological unity) for independent policies, with policy coordination (strategic unity) providing a further multiplicative reduction. The joint structure has teleological unity and strategic unity entering together rather than separately in a clean rate-distortion form.

A striking structural fact is established: **in the linear-Gaussian case with consistent linear projections, the state closure defect vanishes regardless of epistemic unity or compression dimension**. Linear projections of linear dynamics are exact *when the range of the projection is invariant under the micro-dynamics matrix*. The state closure defect becomes nontrivial only when (a) the projection's range is non-invariant under the dynamics, (b) the sub-agents' update rules differ — the *update-rule homogeneity* (structural unity) axis is what governs this, *not* the content axis. This is the structural fact that motivates the two-axis decomposition: the content axis (what's shared) cannot detect structural heterogeneity in updates; the structural axis is required.

The closed-form heterogeneous-Kalman instance is concrete: two Kalman filters with different gains produce state closure defect proportional to the gain difference even when their priors are identical. The structural axis is *forced* — not optional — for any framework that wants to predict closure defect from agent-level properties.

The framework's rate-distortion framing has a striking conceptual consequence. Unity is not a magnitude that maps to closure-defect magnitude; it is a *compressibility parameter* — higher unity allows more aggressive compression at the same closure defect, or equivalently, lower closure defect at the same compression. The rate-distortion *surface* is what unity controls; closure-defect values sit on that surface depending on how aggressively the composite is compressed. This is the same shape as Shannon rate-distortion theory and as the information bottleneck — and the framework's claim is that *composition is rate-distortion*: trading representational compactness against fidelity-to-the-micro-system.
