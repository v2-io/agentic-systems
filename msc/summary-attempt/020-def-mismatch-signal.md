# Mismatch Signal (definition — axiomatic)

The signal that drives every adaptive update is named: the **mismatch signal** $\delta_t = o_t - \hat{o}_t$, the difference between the actual observation and the model's prediction conditioned on the prior state and prior action. The framework also defines a more general version for probabilistic models, the **score-function mismatch**, which is the gradient of the log-likelihood of the observation with respect to model parameters — this points in the direction the model should move to increase the likelihood of what actually occurred. The prediction-error form lives in observation space; the score-function form lives in the tangent space of the model. Under Gaussian models the two coincide up to scaling.

This is a definitional step rather than a substantive claim: given any model that predicts and any observation that arrives, their difference exists. The mismatch signal is not an additional assumption but a consequence of having a predictive model in an uncertain world.

A genuinely important conceptual point is surfaced: **zero mismatch does not necessarily indicate model adequacy**. A near-zero mismatch can mean (a) the model genuinely reflects reality — desirable; (b) the agent is only observing aspects its model already explains while remaining ignorant of aspects where the model is wrong — confirmation bias; or (c) the observation channel is too noisy to detect model errors — an architectural limitation. Only (a) is desirable. An agent without aporia (no perplexity, no surprise) has stopped adapting — but silence can mean peace, or it can mean deafness. This ambiguity is what motivates *active testing* later in the framework: deliberately choosing actions that generate informative mismatch, the basis of the causal-information-yield construct.

A scaling note is preserved for the dynamics that come later: when mismatch is in physical units, its magnitude entering the mismatch dynamics should be understood as a Mahalanobis distance against the observation noise covariance — mapping physical prediction error to dimensionless surprise-equivalent units.
