# Recursive Update (derived — conditional, exact under stated commitments)

The first major *derived* result of the volume: the model update function must be recursive. Each new model state depends only on the previous model state and the incoming event — not on the full chronica. The result follows from three constraints: the temporal-ordering postulate (the model at time $t$ can only depend on prior events), the partial-observability scope (the agent has no direct access to the environment beyond events), and the completeness commitment in the model formulation ($M_t$ summarizes everything the agent retains). Together these force the update into the recursive form; the appendix derivation works through seven counterexample attacks to confirm there is no escape from this form once the three constraints are accepted.

The epistemic character of this result is honest: temporal ordering is a physical postulate and partial observability is a scope condition, but the completeness commitment is *definitional* — it cannot be "violated" because any apparent violation is absorbed by expanding $M_t$. The Markov structure of the update is therefore not discovered in the environment but chosen through the definition of $M_t$ as complete. This is not a weakness; it is the precise character of the claim. The result also gives the agent a *computational* necessity: for finite agents, re-processing the full history at each event is infeasible, so recursion is forced by computational realism too.

The formulation also introduces between-event dynamics — autonomous evolution of the model state during the gaps between observation arrivals — and surfaces these as load-bearing rather than filler. Between-event dynamics include prediction generation (anticipating the next observation), uncertainty growth (confidence decay over time without new data), and internal reorganization (consolidation, abstraction). When between-event dynamics are driven by replayed or internally-generated pseudo-events and the objective is information-bottleneck-gap reduction rather than one-step mismatch minimization, the framework names this the *consolidation regime* — a separate operating mode with its own scope condition (consolidation rate much less than online rate) treated formally later in Part II.
