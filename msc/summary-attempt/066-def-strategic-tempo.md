# Strategic Tempo (definition — conditional)

The strategic analog of adaptive tempo. **Strategic tempo** sums per-edge correction capacities across the strategy DAG, weighted by each edge's *causal identifiability coefficient*. The structural parallel with epistemic tempo is exact: edge observation rate times per-edge update gain times identifiability coefficient, summed over edges (versus channel rate times gain summed over channels for epistemic tempo). The identifiability factor — capturing the regime distinction — is where edge-causal-validity enters the operational machinery. *An agent cannot improve the parts of its strategy that it cannot test interventionally.* Regime-A (intervention-rich) edges contribute full tempo at their observation rate; Regime-C (observation-only) edges contribute essentially nothing regardless of how fast the agent acts.

A *key structural difference* from epistemic tempo: edge rates are **endogenous**. Epistemic-tempo channel rates are largely exogenous (the environment generates observations at its own pace). Strategic-tempo edge rates depend on the agent's *action policy* (which edges get tested) and on *upstream success* (downstream edges are tested only when upstream edges fire). This endogeneity is the source of the structural differences between epistemic and strategic persistence.

The framework decomposes strategic tempo by topology. **AND-chains are depth-gated**: each edge at depth $k$ is tested at the upstream-success-attenuated rate (product of upstream success probabilities times base rate). For a uniform chain, total strategic tempo converges to a finite limit as depth goes to infinity — *total strategic tempo is bounded even for arbitrarily deep chains*, but the marginal contribution of each new edge decays geometrically. The deep edges are *evidence-starved*. **OR-nodes are exploration-gated**: under epsilon-greedy exploration, the rate allocated to the greedy arm gets nearly all the action budget while exploratory arms get only the exploration fraction divided by their count. Pure greedy makes non-greedy arms *permanently uncorrectable*. The bottleneck is the least-explored alternative.

**Per-edge persistence** then inherits the same weakest-link structure as per-dimension persistence: for the strategy to persist, every edge must maintain bounded mismatch. The aggregate strategic tempo is at most the sum over edges and at least the minimum across edges — exceeding total disturbance is *necessary* but not sufficient. *Persistence is bottleneck-limited by the weakest edge, not governed by the aggregate.* Scalar strategic tempo overestimates effective strategic adaptation for the same reason scalar adaptive tempo overestimates epistemic adaptation: it averages over heterogeneous correction capacities.

A practical consequence: given a fixed action budget, the topology that maximizes strategic tempo is *shallow and OR-heavy* (more edges directly observable, no attenuation); the topology that minimizes it is *deep AND-chains*. This complements the chain-confidence-decay argument from earlier in the chapter, jointly favoring *flat, option-rich strategies* over deep sequential plans. The framework also notes that as observations accumulate per edge, the per-edge gain shrinks (diminishing returns) — strategic tempo *declines over time even in a static environment* because the agent's corrections become smaller as edges converge.
