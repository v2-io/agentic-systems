# Part III Preface: Agentic Composites (volume framing prose)

Part III's scope is multiple agents interacting through a shared environment — or equivalently, the internal structure of composite agents. The composition postulate from Part I requires that the theory apply at every level of description where the scope condition is met. Part III develops what "applies at every level" *means formally*: the composition closure criterion (which requires an additional contraction assumption beyond Part I's sector condition), what happens when composition is *imperfect*, and what the dynamics of inter-agent interaction look like.

The preface names a methodological stance the chapters share. **Correlated observations are the default; independence is the special case requiring justification**. Adversarial dynamics are *one end of a teleological-unity spectrum, not a separate theory*. This means cooperation and adversariality are framed as opposite signs of the same coupling structure rather than as distinct phenomena needing different machinery.

Three primary sources are acknowledged. The simulation-validated adversarial dynamics from TFT (the predecessor framework — multi-agent simulations in the track-b suite). The composition spike, which derives composition consistency from the scope condition's level-independence. **Miller's Coevolving Automata Model** (*Ex Machina*, 2022), which provides constructive mechanisms for composition dynamics — *how composites form, undergo phase transitions, and restructure through neutral drift and niche creation*. Agent opacity is adopted from Hafez et al. (2026).
