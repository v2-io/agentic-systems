# The Stability Certificate — Cross-Sectional Spine (appendix discussion — discussion-grade)

The framework's **spine** of three meta-patterns it has been developing. The three meta-patterns (the separability pattern, the identifiability floor, the additive-coordinate-forcing) had been read separately as three independent organizing insights. This segment names *the object they are facets of*: the **equilibrium stability certificate** — the positive-definite metric whose existence certifies that the agent can correct itself faster than its world drifts.

The framework's reading of the three meta-patterns *as facets of one object*: **operator-sector is the certificate's interior** (the region where contraction holds at the chosen rate); **the separability pattern is the certificate's scope of existence** (which agent-coordinate decompositions admit certificates); **the additive-coordinate-forcing is the certificate's forced identity** (which coordinates the certificate is forced to live on by AAT-internal axioms via classical uniqueness theorems); **the identifiability floor is the certificate's boundary** (no-go theorems about what cannot be inferred from limited observation). **Composition is the question of whether the certificate survives projection** (the bridge lemma's requirement that strong monotonicity hold at the macro-level).

The unification is not a fourth meta-pattern *alongside* the three; it is the *spine the three are projections of*. The framework names *three failure modes provably distinct* (Helmholtz / Sylvester / Mori-Zwanzig — corresponding to identifiability failures, separability failures, and composition-projection failures respectively): **the plurality is the content**.

The framework treats this as one of the deeper structural recognitions in AAT. *Each derived result in the framework can be read as either an interior result on the certificate's body, a scope statement about the certificate's existence, a uniqueness theorem forcing the certificate's coordinates, or a no-go statement about the certificate's boundary*. The four readings cover the framework's machinery; the certificate is the common object.

The segment notes this as a *provisional position* — the framework's commitment to the spine reading is at the discussion-grade tier, gated on broader review. What is *not* provisional: the *certificate existence equivalence* (Lyapunov's theorem at the linearized level), the *operator-sector reading* (the framework's sector condition is a one-point strong monotonicity condition in some metric), and the framework's posture that *coordinates are forced* (the AAT-internal-axioms-via-uniqueness-theorems reading). The spine reading composes these three established commitments into a unifying structural claim.
