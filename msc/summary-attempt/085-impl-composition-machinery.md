# Composition Machinery — Additional Implications (discussion — chapter-end synthesis)

Four cross-segment threads.

**(1) Closure defect, bridge lemma, and the meaning of composition.** The closure-defect framework gives a *control-theoretic loss bound* on treating a collection as one agent — something the Markov-blanket apparatus does not produce, even when the boundary criterion is in hand. The framework is consistent with the approximate-information-state and influence-based-abstraction lines from PAC-MDP work but applies the same predictive-loss-to-control-error machinery at the *agent boundary formation* scope rather than at the single-agent compression scope. The framework's distinctive content is the *use* of bounded-loss composition as an agent-boundary criterion, together with admissibility conditions that force the macro-description to be AAT-shaped. A subtle honesty: zero closure defect does *not* mean *optimality* — two non-communicating Kalman filters achieve zero closure defect while being suboptimal versus a joint filter. The framework diagnoses *representability* of a composite as an AAT agent, not optimality against alternative architectures. *Strong monotonicity is the hinge* — sub-agents that are merely Lyapunov-stable do not compose into a coherent macro-agent.

**(2) The critical-mass closed form unifies four signed special cases.** The chapter's deepest cross-segment finding (with the appendix derivation). For the symmetric-matched-Tier-1 dyad, the composite sector constant is now derived in closed form, and *the single inequality recovers four prior results as signed special cases*: **single-agent persistence** (no coupling, no coordination — reducing to Part I's central inequality); **team persistence with cooperative coupling** (negative coupling parameter enters as effective-disturbance *reduction* — which is formally how teams persist where individuals cannot); **adversarial destabilization** (positive coupling amplifies disturbance); and **coordination-dominated failure** (when coordination overhead exceeds the composite's intrinsic correction rate, the effective rate goes negative regardless of individual quality — **Brooks's Law as a derived inequality** rather than folklore). The teleological-unity quantity enters *multiplicatively* on the coupling parameter plus scope-gate, not additively.

For a practitioner, the engineering anchor: Brooks's Law has a *physics-level* derivation here. A development team's macro-correction rate is the intrinsic rate minus the coordination overhead; once overhead exceeds the intrinsic rate, adding people *degrades* persistence regardless of individual quality. The threshold is sharp, not gradual, and the inequality predicts exactly where it bites.

The closed form also delivers the framework's **third formal instance of the identifiability-floor pattern** (composite contraction certification from component-marginal data is structurally impossible — the coupling-sign bit distinguishing cooperative from adversarial regimes is unidentifiable from component marginals; Liberzon's common-Lyapunov-nonexistence is the external no-go). The escape comes via Pearl-style intervention at the composite scope: an observer (or the composite agent acting on its sub-agents) performs interventions on one sub-agent and observes the response of another. This is **Mode 2 of the loop-Level-2 deployment pattern** (observer-on-sub-agent), formally introduced in Part II. The closed-form critical-mass derivation gives the mathematical form of the recovered coupling; the loop-Level-2 access at the composite level gives the data substrate that recovery requires.

**(3) Class coercion via wrapping — what the construction unlocks.** The wrapping construction is the *constructive bridge* that resolves how AAT's Part II results (derived for Class 1) apply to Class 3 substrates. Without it, Class 3 components would sit outside Part II's persistence machinery as a scope exit; with it, the wrapped system at the wrapper level is Class 1 *by construction*, inherits the sector-persistence template, and operates under the standard AAT results. The cost is paid in two places: a *Brooks's-Law tempo overhead* (more component calls per macro-step) and a *residual leakage rate* bounded structurally (in the strict-wrapping regime, by the wrapper's query design) or behaviorally (in the partial-wrapping regime, by the component's instruction-following fidelity).

The implications for AI-safety practice are concrete: **scaffolded loops around LLMs are not just engineering convenience — they are the structural mechanism that converts a Class 3 substrate into a Class 1 composite**, and the leakage bounds tell the practitioner what residual coupling remains. Most production tool-using LLM frameworks (ReAct, Reflexion, Toolformer, MemGPT) sit in the partial-wrapping regime. The Generative Agents observation-then-memory step is the canonical strict-wrapping instance. Human bicameral / dual-process models are strict wrapping when the deliberative system constrains the intuitive system through structural commitment to goal-blind belief-update queries; organizations where executive scaffolding recovers cascade ordering across coupled individuals are at the organizational scale.

The framework also names class coercion as one of *two operational mechanisms for truthification* in the modularity-state-dynamics meta-pattern (alongside defensive scaffolding). **The wrapping construction is a structural commitment that *moves* a composite agent's modularity state from no-wrapping toward strict-wrapping under explicit cost**, and the cost is the framework's quantitative content for the truthification operation.

**(4) Tempo composition, Brooks's Law, and the bandwidth connection.** Coordination overhead acts as *effective disturbance at the composite level*. The composition with Part I's information-rate floor is a cross-segment finding: each agent in the composite pays its own bandwidth cost; the composite as a whole pays the sum *minus what coordinated sub-agents save on shared observation*. But there is a complementary cost: closure defect has its own information-rate signature, because maintaining the bridge-lemma's strong-monotonicity condition at the macro-update requires the composite to acquire information about how its sub-agents are interacting. *Composite persistence pays for both the sub-agents' individual bandwidth and the coordination-overhead bandwidth*, and the trade-off between adding agents and increasing coordination overhead has a *precise inflection point*: past it, additional sub-agents cost more bandwidth than they buy. **The sweet spot for sub-agent count is bounded by the coordination-overhead growth rate, not by individual sub-agent capacity.**

The bridge to the next chapter is the closure-defect itself. The dimensions of unity (content axes and structural axis) parametrize *what sub-agents can share*; the unity-closure-mapping result shows that unity parametrizes a rate-distortion curve for closure defect. The chapter here gives the closure defect a precise form; the next chapter gives unity its quantitative content.
