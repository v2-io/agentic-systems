# Gain-Sector Bridge — Proofs and Verification (appendix derivation — conditional)

The complete derivations underlying the gain-sector-bridge result from the chapter. Three propositions are proved. **Proposition B.1 (Scalar Kalman)**: for scalar linear-Gaussian systems with Kalman gain, the correction function satisfies the sector condition with sector parameter *exactly equal to the gain*. **Proposition B.2 (Matrix Kalman)**: for vector-valued systems with matrix Kalman filtering, the sector condition holds in the inverse-prior-covariance-weighted inner product, with sector parameter equal to the smallest eigenvalue of the gain-times-observation product on the observable subspace. **Proposition B.3 (Beta-Bernoulli conjugate)**: for Beta-Bernoulli edges in the strategy DAG, the sector condition holds globally with sector parameter $1/(n+1)$ where $n$ is the accumulated experience.

**Proposition B.4 (gradient-sector equivalence)**: for gradient descent on a loss function, the sector condition with sector parameter $\eta \cdot \mu$ (learning rate times curvature) is equivalent — at the *two-point* or *incremental* form — to the loss being locally $\mu$-strongly convex. The *one-point* form (the version actually used in the framework's persistence derivations) is *weaker*: strong convexity *implies* the one-point sector, but the converse fails. The framework provides an explicit counterexample showing a function whose one-point sector at the origin is satisfied globally yet which is not convex on any neighborhood of the origin. This is honest scope: the framework's persistence machinery uses the one-point form; full bidirectional equivalence to strong convexity requires the two-point form needed by the composition bridge lemma.

The framework also documents *numerical verification* across several test cases including quadratic, logistic, exponential-family, and non-convex losses; saturating, sigmoid, and threshold correction functions. The qualitative claim — sector parameter monotone increasing in tempo — holds across all tested cases; the specific functional form (e.g., $\alpha \approx 0.76 \mathcal{T}$ for sigmoid) is empirical.

The framework's overall posture: the gain-sector bridge is **conditional, not unconditional**. There is no escape from the directional-fidelity hypothesis. Pathological update rules exist that violate it (a counterexample satisfying every framework postulate and the gain-based update form has zero inner product between mismatch and correction). *Scope plus gain structure alone does not force directional fidelity*; some optimality / coherence / rationality constraint (Bayesian coherence, gradient-of-a-convex-loss) is required. The framework's contribution is *characterizing which agent classes give the bridge for free* (sub-scope $\alpha$) and *which agent classes require the sector condition as a primitive posit* (sub-scope $\beta$).
