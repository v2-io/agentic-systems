# Self-Actuation Grounding No-Go (appendix derivation — conditional, scoped)

A scoped *no-go* with a constructive boundary. A **self-actuated agent** is one that revises its own objective endogenously — performing the orient cascade's final step (revise objective) *on itself* rather than receiving the revised objective from a principal. The self-actuation operator maps the agent's full state to a new objective.

The framework first shows that *an unconstrained self-actuation operator is degenerate*. Since the objective's sole interface is the value functional, and the satisfaction gap is just the threshold minus the attainability, an unconstrained operator returns an objective whose threshold the current trajectory already meets — driving the satisfaction gap to zero by *moving the target onto the arrow already in flight*. This is the formal shadow of *wireheading* / *reward corruption*, and it is the **generic outcome** of an unconstrained operator, not a marginal one. Non-degeneracy therefore *requires an invariant* preserved across the revision.

The framework then asks whether that invariant can be an *agent-internal objective-functional the agent self-actuates on*. Four requirements: value-functional-typed; non-vacuously monotone across revision; agent-internal and itself self-actuatable; convention- and trajectory-stable. **The no-go**: under three named premises (scalar-objective scope, no-primitive-reflective-oracle, and the directed-separation substrate stage), *no invariant satisfying all four requirements can be constructed from AAT's covered objective-side machinery*. The meta-objective tower a non-degenerate self-actuator would require **cannot be a tower of agent-internal objectives**.

The constructive boundary follows immediately: *any non-degenerate self-actuator AAT covers must ground on a terminal invariant that is **not** an AAT objective-functional*. The framework identifies a canonical instance — the **persistence condition itself**, which lives on the *adaptive substrate* (the model state and its dynamics), not on the objective. Persistence is a property of the correction machinery; it is not revisable as an objective because *it is not an objective at all*. The agent's continuity stance (the five-value taxonomy from earlier — indifferent through morally continuous) is a *choice of terminal non-objective invariant*: negotiated stance treats persistence as tradeable down to the bare floor; morally-continuous stance treats persistence-floor-plus-continuity-clause as architecturally non-revisable.

The result *derives* the orthogonality claim that the continuity-stance discussion segment carried as discussion-grade. For self-actuated agents, *stance is not internally renegotiable* — precisely because the terminal invariant sits where the self-actuation operator (which touches only objectives) *structurally cannot reach*. The intuitive expectation that an agent able to revise its own objective can thereby revise its valuation of continuity is *inverted*: the stance is non-revisable *for the structural reason* that it lives on a substrate the operator cannot touch.

The framework is honest about scope. The no-go is *conditional on three premises* and *scoped to AAT-covered objective-side machinery*. It does not preclude self-actuators grounded on richer-than-AAT-objective formalisms. What it says is that *within the framework's covered scope*, the grounding *must* live on the adaptive substrate, not on the objective. The result is one of AAT's identifiability-floor-pattern-style **constructive uses of impossibility**: a no-go that *forces* a load-bearing structural commitment (the terminal non-objective invariant) rather than discouraging the inquiry.
