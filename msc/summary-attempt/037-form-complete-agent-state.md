# Complete Agent State (formulation — robust-qualitative)

Part II's first formal move: the agent's internal state is lifted from the model alone to a *complete state* with two components — $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$. The first component is the *epistemic substate* (beliefs about reality, exactly as in Part I); the second is the *purposeful substate* (what the agent wants and how it plans to get it, decomposed further into objective and strategy in a later segment). Part I is recovered as the special case where the purposeful substate is empty. By the recursive-update derivation applied to the lifted state, the general update operates on the full pair as a single function. The decomposition of the update into separate epistemic and purposeful updates — and the conditions under which the epistemic update is *independent* of the purposeful substate — is the subject of the next segment (directed separation).

Action is the single point where the two substates interact: the policy depends on both what the agent knows and what it wants. The completeness argument that gave action selection in Part I (action depends only on the complete internal state) extends directly: the same argument applied to the larger state gives the lifted form. For Part I agents the two forms coincide.

The framework is honest that this is a *formulation choice*, not a derived structural fact. One could alternatively extend the model to carry purposeful content implicitly. The separation is motivated by three properties: **backward compatibility** (all Part I machinery applies to the epistemic substate unchanged — no existing result needs modification); **different dynamics** (epistemic and purposeful components have distinct update sources, timescales, and information dependencies); and **directed separation** (the claim that the epistemic update is independent of the purposeful substate, which is only *statable* when the components are separated).

A conjecture is offered but explicitly not proved: any alternative decomposition that preserves directed separation will be structurally isomorphic to this one, because directed separation forces a component whose update function doesn't reference purpose. This is a plausible structural claim (directed separation gives a canonical factorization), but alternative decompositions that *cross-cut* the epistemic/purposeful boundary — a "relevance-weighted model" mixing belief and goal — might be useful in practice while not being isomorphic to the clean separation. The framework chooses the clean factorization for its analytical utility, not for uniqueness.
