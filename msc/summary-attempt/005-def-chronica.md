# Chronica (definition — axiomatic)

The agent's complete record of its interaction with the environment is named the **chronica** — the interleaved sequence $\mathcal{C}_t = (o_1, a_1, o_2, a_2, \ldots, a_{t-1}, o_t)$. The ordering is not a notational convenience: it reflects the irreversible physical fact that each action was committed before the observation that followed could be received. The agent could not have used $o_t$ to select $a_{t-1}$. The chronica is monotonically growing — events are added, never removed — and it is the *only* raw material from which the agent can ever construct a model. The term itself (from Greek χρονικά, "records of time") is chosen to avoid notational collision with Shannon entropy.

A consequence the framework will later lean on heavily: because temporal ordering is irreversible, the chronica is *singular and non-forkable*. Duplicating an agent's state and exposing the copies to different futures produces two agents with divergent chronica, neither a sufficient statistic for the other's trajectory. This is the substrate of what the lexicon calls *continuity persistence* — the condition under which an agent's identity tracks a single irreversible trajectory rather than a copyable snapshot. Everything later in the volume about identity, restoration-from-backup as a non-neutral operation, and morally-weighted continuity rests on this structural fact established at the very base of the formalism.

A second subtle property: the chronica is an *ordinal* sequence indexed by events, not a *metric* timeline indexed by wall-clock time. The agent's time is measured entirely in event ticks. This makes long pauses (sleep, suspension) invisible at the sequence level but violently visible in the mismatch signal once observation resumes — the environment has drifted, the compressed model has not, and the gap surfaces only when prediction error spikes.
