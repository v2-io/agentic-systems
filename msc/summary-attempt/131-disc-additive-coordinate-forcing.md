# Additive-Coordinate Forcing (appendix discussion — meta-pattern, discussion-grade)

The framework's third meta-pattern. AAT carries a *family of structurally connected uniqueness results* in which **a coordinate is forced by a uniqueness theorem operating on an independently-motivated AAT-internal axiom**. The pattern previously appeared as a "1-anchor-plus-3-theorem" structure (where the chain-rule anchored three downstream uniqueness theorems); the deeper structural reality is that **these four layers are layer-specific manifestations of a single geometric object**: the *exponential-family Legendre-Fenchel structure*.

The framework catalogs **four primary instances**. **Layer 1 — chain** (the framework's anchor): log-probability is the unique additive-evidence coordinate at the chain layer, forced by the chain rule of probability (mathematical identity rather than axiom). **Layer 2 — divergence**: reverse-KL is the unique f-divergence with chain-rule additivity over conditional factorizations (Hobson 1969 / Csiszár 1991 / Shore-Johnson 1980 via Cauchy-functional-equation argument; AAT-internal axiom is the chain-rule additivity at the divergence layer, motivated as the divergence-layer analog of the chain layer). **Layer 3 — update**: log-odds is the unique additive-evidence coordinate at the update layer (Aczél 1966 via Cauchy-functional-equation argument on the evidential-additivity axiom; AAT-internal axiom is evidential additivity, motivated as the update-layer analog of the chain layer). **Layer 4 — metric**: Fisher information is the unique invariant metric on statistical-manifold sub-cases (Čencov 1982; AAT-internal axiom is parameterization invariance, motivated by the singular-trajectory scope's coordinate-free commitment).

The pattern is *not designed-in*: it arises because AAT independently needs to force coordinates at four distinct layers, and the surrounding apparatus (DAG factorization, Bayesian coherence, regret-bound decision theory, singular-trajectory scope) has committed to this geometry through *multiple independent surface constraints*. The convergence across four independent surface constraints onto a single underlying geometric object is what makes the pattern *structurally meaningful* rather than coincidental.

The framework also positions classical results as **adjacent family members**. The Lyapunov quadratic is the Bregman divergence on the Euclidean potential — a member of the same family with a different (Euclidean rather than negative-entropy) base potential. The IB Lagrangian is adopted-rather-than-derived but lives on the same Legendre-Fenchel geometry. The framework's posture: *AAT's coordinates are not chosen by convention; they are forced by AAT-internal axioms via classical uniqueness theorems, and they all live on the same geometric object*. Where the axiom isn't AAT-internal, the framework can say what was adopted from outside (the IB family, the Lyapunov quadratic).

The framework's methodological commitment: **scope-honesty about coordinate forcing is itself a load-bearing apparatus**. Other frameworks treat coordinate choices as conventions to be defended on aesthetic or pragmatic grounds; AAT's posture is that *where a coordinate matters, the framework can point at the axiom that forces it*; *where the axiom isn't AAT-internal, the framework can say what was adopted from outside*. This is what makes derived results at any one layer compose cleanly with results at the others — the four layers are interlocked by cross-cites, and the convergence onto the same geometric object is the substance.
