# Introduction: Inescapable Foundations of Agency (volume introduction — framing prose)

The volume opens by claiming that an agent — any system that holds a partial model of an environment it can only see incompletely, acts on that environment, and must keep adapting to it — is not an open-ended mystery but a temporal feedback system characterized by a small number of critical quantities. The relationships among those quantities are sharp enough, the introduction argues, that many of them are forced by derivation and can serve as foundational theorems of adaptation, then of adaptation-under-purpose. The bounds these theorems set are domain-agnostic: the same inequality that governs a Kalman filter tracking a maneuvering target governs a software team keeping a codebase maintainable, and an organization keeping pace with a market. The claim that this is *one* inequality rather than an analogy among three is itself one of the volume's results.

Four anchoring results are named upfront. The **persistence threshold** is a single inequality comparing correction rate against the rate the world drifts (normalized by how wrong the agent can afford to be) — below it, adaptive systems do not merely degrade; they lose bounded behavior in a categorical sense. The **containment dichotomy** is its stochastic counterpart: against a bounded disturbance a sector-stable corrector holds error inside a fixed region forever with certainty, but against genuinely stochastic disturbance no correction strength buys that — over an unbounded horizon the system will leave any fixed region with probability one, making the eventual need to change *what kind of model* an agent is (not merely its parameters) a certain eventual event rather than an edge case. The **stability equivalence** is the claim that "an adaptive system is one whose correction outpaces its target's drift" is not a guiding description but identically the classical Lyapunov stability theorem. The **architectural classification** distinguishes agents whose belief-update is structurally insulated from what they want (Class 1: Separated) from those for which it is not (Class 3: Coupled — transformer-based language models among them), with a constructive route to recover separation at a quantified cost.

The volume's organizing move is a cascade of scope conditions. Broadest: *adaptive systems* (observation under residual uncertainty) — the full machinery of Part I (mismatch, gain, tempo, the persistence threshold) applies here. Narrower: *agentic systems* (action with causal contrast) — the agent's own loop becomes a source of Level-2 (Pearl-interventional) causal data. Narrower still: *actuated agents* (explicit objective and strategy distinct from beliefs) — the diagnostic core of Part II. Narrower still: language-constituted and morally-weighted agents, treated in the companion volumes. Each narrowing buys a strictly stronger set of results. The conservatism is presented not as timidity but as the source of compositional strength: a theory that narrows honestly and earns more machinery at each step is stronger than one that claims everything and quietly retreats.

The pieces are explicitly classical — Lyapunov stability, Itô calculus, Pearl's interventional hierarchy, the information bottleneck, monotone-operator theory, Čencov's uniqueness theorem. The contribution claimed is the integration: exact and conditional bounds on when an adaptive, purposeful agent can correct, plan, and persist, expressible only over objects the framework had to construct. The scope-honesty discipline running through every claim is presented as what *makes* the contribution compose. In a theory whose sharpest results are bounds, the introduction insists, the scope condition is often itself the theorem.

The volume positions itself as a substantive answer to a need the field has been naming from several directions — notably Miehling et al. (2025), whose call for a systems-level theory of agency was independently arrived at here from cross-domain pattern matching, with a third convergent line from information-theoretic measurement of agency. Active inference is recoverable here as a special case under three explicit restrictions, which makes visible exactly which architectural commitments separate the two.

A maturity gradient is stated openly: Part I is mathematically closed with simulation validation; Part II has an exact diagnostic core and a maturing operational layer; Part III has bridge results and open frontiers named as such. A single recurring worked instance — a driver holding a road through a snowstorm — runs as a literal (not metaphorical) adaptive-agent worked example across the chapters.
