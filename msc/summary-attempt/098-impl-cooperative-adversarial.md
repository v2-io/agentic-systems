# Cooperative and Adversarial — Additional Implications (discussion — chapter-end synthesis)

Four threads close Part III Chapter 4.

**(1) Cooperative and adversarial are one signed coupling, not two theories.** The chapter's central architectural commitment, now formal. Effective disturbance for a sub-agent decomposes with adversarial contributions as *positive* terms (amplification) and cooperative contributions as *negative* terms (reduction). The signed-coupling form is what makes the two regimes *quantitatively comparable* rather than addressed in separate vocabularies. The persistence template absorbs both regimes as instantiations of a *single Lyapunov argument*.

The duality between persistence and destabilization is the deeper implication: destabilization is the *negation* of persistence under the same coupling model — the same inequality, viewed from opposite directions. Adversarial attacks are *not* a separate kind of phenomenon requiring separate machinery; they are the same machinery, with the attacker's contribution entering the target's effective disturbance via the signed-coupling structure. The framework's closed-form unification (in the prior chapter's implications) recovers four prior persistence results — single-agent, team-persistence, adversarial-destabilization, coordination-failure (Brooks's Law) — *as signed special cases of one inequality*.

**(2) Universal taxonomy of inter-agent events — what the recipient sees.** The chapter's distinctive AAT-internal contribution. The same signal from one agent lands on a recipient as one of four qualitatively different things, determined by three independent boundary conditions stated entirely in the recipient's existing AAT quantities. The emitter-side scalar that previous segments used as a stand-in for coupling strength *loses this distinction*; the recipient-side classification recovers it. **The framework's repair vocabulary diversifies accordingly**: informative updates respond to tempo investment; magnitude shocks respond to gain investment (better observation quality); structural shocks respond to *model-class expansion* — no amount of tempo or gain suffices; ambient noise does not call for response at all — investing in detecting it is wasted bandwidth.

The implication for adversarial robustness: **an attacker who knows the target's regime boundaries can choose events that *miss* the target's repair vocabulary** — structural shocks against a tempo-investment defender, magnitude shocks against a model-class-expansion defender. The framework's design prescription is that defenders should *cover all four repair paths*, not optimize for one.

The composite-level payoff is the **regime-typed effective disturbance decomposition**: the per-regime decomposition tells the practitioner *where the disturbance is coming from*, not just how much there is. For composite-agent design under uncertainty about the disturbance regime, the framework's prescription is to instrument the four regime detectors independently.

**(3) Adversarial tempo advantage as superlinear scaling — and the disturbance-model split.** The headline result. The two exponents (squared under Model D, three-halves under Model S) are *not arbitrary parameters* — they fall out of Part I's persistence-template structure ($1/\alpha$ versus $1/\sqrt{\alpha}$ scaling), applied to the coupling model. The framework's posture: *the same template that explains why adaptive systems persist also predicts the precise rate at which they lose to a faster attacker* — the dual is structural.

The exponent splits further in the next chapter, with the coupling-non-dominant case dropping to near-linear scaling. The non-superlinear regime is operationally important: **when observation noise is high enough that the attacker's tempo advantage doesn't penetrate the defender's observation channel cleanly, the superlinear scaling collapses to roughly linear**. The framework predicts that *cheap noise injection* into the defender's observation channel actually *helps* the defender against a high-tempo attacker by reducing the exponent. This is the structural reason for the empirical pattern that highly-noisy environments produce more cooperative-game-like dynamics than expected.

The operational anchor: Boyd's OODA-loop aphorism acquires mathematical content. *Inside the opponent's loop* means a tempo ratio above a regime-dependent threshold, and what the inside-the-loop position buys the attacker is *superlinear destabilization rate* rather than mere tempo advantage. The chapter's framing — "superlinear tempo advantage as regime change rather than quantitative gap" — is the implications-flavored point: at the destabilization threshold, the target does not gradually degrade. It *loses bounded behavior*, the way a balance held just barely beneath a tipping point is qualitatively different from one well above it.

**(4) Three adversarial obstructions to contraction — the equilibrium hand-off.** A consequential structural recognition. *Contraction-metric machinery cannot handle strategic / adversarial regimes for three convergent reasons*, each individually classical from a different mathematical literature. **Slotine 2003 compositional applicability fails**: saddle-point equilibria break the attracting-fixed-point assumption that contraction analysis requires. **Passivity universality fails**: adversarial inputs drive any storage function, so passivity-based stability does not give bounds. **Daskalakis et al. 2018 last-iterate non-convergence**: for generic non-zero-sum games, learning dynamics fail to converge to equilibrium in the last-iterate sense — only in the time-average sense.

Three *unrelated* mathematical obstructions converge on the same conclusion. The convergence makes the limit *structural rather than framework-specific*: it is not that AAT's contraction machinery is too weak; it is that the regime is outside the scope condition contraction analysis depends on across multiple formal traditions. The framework's posture is to *recognize the limit precisely and hand off cleanly to equilibrium-theoretic methods at the boundary* — supplied in the next chapter. *Scope-honesty is load-bearing architecture*: knowing where each tool applies cleanly is more useful than trying to extend one tool across all regimes.

A quieter consequence: **cooperative coupling that is *too strong* can produce equilibrium-stability failures even without adversarial components**. When sub-agents' cooperative coupling drives them toward a saddle-point equilibrium rather than a stable attractor, the failure mode is *structurally identical to the adversarial case* — contraction analysis fails, equilibrium machinery is required. This is empirically the situation in tightly-coupled multi-agent ML systems where reward-shaping creates implicit competition among cooperators.
