# Gated Tempo Advantage (observation — empirical)

A specific empirical refinement of the adversarial-tempo-advantage result. **Observation noise collapses the adversarial tempo advantage**. When agents observe their mismatch through a noisy channel, the faster agent's additional corrections become *noisy*, partially offsetting its tempo advantage.

The mechanism is direct: a faster attacker makes more corrections per unit time, each noisy because of observation noise; the *additional* corrections add proportional noise, partially canceling the additional speed. Simulation confirms this: at observation noise comparable to process noise, the exponent in the steady-state mismatch ratio scaling drops dramatically — fixed-gain agents lose nearly all of the squared-scaling advantage; optimal-gain agents (Riccati gain matched to the noise level) recover *some* but not all of it.

The framework's prescription: **the optimal gain principle partially restores the advantage but cannot fully recover the noise-free level**. The Riccati gain mitigates the loss by *reducing the gain to match the noise level* — correcting less aggressively but more accurately. The framework calls this *gated* tempo advantage because observation noise gates the regime: the squared scaling that defines the adversarial-tempo-advantage result applies cleanly only in the low-noise regime; in higher-noise regimes the exponent collapses smoothly to linear or sub-linear.

This composes with the regime-decomposition result from the prior segment: high observation noise is what *gates* the adversarial advantage into the non-coupling-dominant regime where the exponent collapses. The empirical pattern that highly-noisy environments produce more cooperative-game-like dynamics than expected receives an additional defensive design implication: *observation noise on the defender's side reduces the attacker's superlinear advantage*, which is the structural reason cheap noise injection into the defender's channel helps the defender against a high-tempo attacker.
