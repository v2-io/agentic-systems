# Loop Provides Interventional Data Access (derived — exact)

The chapter's central architectural result. By the temporal-ordering postulate, the agent's action causally precedes the next observation; the agent *chose* the action; the environment responded. The feedback loop therefore generates **intervention-produced data** — data whose causal character differs from passive observation because the agent's action was a genuine cause of the subsequent observation. The mismatch signal conditioned on the agent's action carries interventional information: it tells the agent how the environment responded to *its specific intervention*, relative to what the model predicted.

The framework is *unusually careful* to distinguish two claims that look alike. "Action-generated data" is **not** the same as "cleanly identified do-estimates." The action-observation pair is produced *under intervention* (interventional in character) — but between intervention-produced data and a usable estimate of the interventional outcome distribution stand: (1) **coverage** — the agent must have tried diverse actions, not just one policy; (2) **confounding within a time step** — unobserved state variables that affect both action choice and outcome; (3) **delay** — consequences may appear much later than the next observation; (4) **partial observability** — each observation reveals only part of the outcome. The strength of causal identification varies by regime: strong in intervention-rich domains (software development, laboratory science), moderate with partial intervention (organizational), weak under observation-only constraints. The claim is about the *character of the data* (interventional, not observational), not about the agent's ability to extract clean causal estimates from it.

The result is *exact* as a logical consequence of temporal ordering and the feedback-loop structure. It is about *data availability*, not reasoning capacity — the loop *provides* interventional data whether or not the agent *exploits* it for Level-2 reasoning. Whether the agent uses this data to build causal models depends on its update mechanism and model class.

The framework gives **honest credit** to the action-perception-loop framing in the broader literature (active inference, cybernetics, control theory): the substantive observation that the agent's actions cause its observations, and therefore that loop data is interventional in character, is *implicit* in any framework built around an action-perception loop. AAT's distinctive contribution is not the observation itself but the *explicit lift* of this observation to a load-bearing theorem connected to Pearl's causal hierarchy via Bareinboim et al. (2022). Three moves AAT makes that the implicit treatments do not: (1) the Bareinboim-hierarchy connection (active inference rests on Bayesian-network generative models — Level-1; AAT positions the strategy as a *causal* DAG rather than a Bayesian-network DAG by invoking the strict-hierarchy theorem); (2) regime-indexed strength of causal identification (the A/B/C regime partition rather than uniform identifiability assumptions); (3) explicit scope honesty (the formal expression carefully distinguishes intervention-produced data from cleanly identified estimates, the conservative form that Bruineberg et al.'s Pearl-vs-Friston critique argues is missing from some AI-literature treatments).

A subtler structural connection is established: the loop's interventional character *rests on the singular-trajectory scope* from Part I. When the agent executes an action and observes a consequence, the observation is the response to *this* agent's intervention on *its* single trajectory. Replaying the same action from a checkpointed model against a different event stream would *not* constitute an intervention on the same trajectory — it would be an intervention on a different trajectory that happened to share a prefix. Pearl's $do$-operator presumes a definite causal system acted upon; AAT inherits this presumption via the singular-trajectory scope. Agents whose ontology is *type-like* (equivalence classes of copies) rather than *token-like* (singular trajectories) are outside AAT's formal scope. This is the ontological ground that makes the "action-generated data is Level-2" claim honest rather than metaphorical.

The result is identified as a *load-bearing escape* from observational-equivalence no-goes that recur at multiple layers of the theory. At the agent-internal layer, it is the unique broadly-available escape from the L0-causal-insufficiency-detection no-go (Bareinboim's causal-hierarchy theorem applied to detection of latent common causes from on-policy data). At the composite layer, it extends to a different mechanism — an observer external to a composite intervenes on one sub-agent's actions and observes another sub-agent's response, revealing cross-coupling that component-marginal observation cannot identify (the Liberzon common-Lyapunov no-go). The two modes — agent-self-intervention and observer-on-sub-agent — share the Pearl-$do$ structure but apply at different layers of the theory.
