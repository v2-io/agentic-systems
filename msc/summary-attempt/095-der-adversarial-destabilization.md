# Adversarial Destabilization (derived — conditional)

The persistence template applied with *coupling-amplified disturbance*. When two agents are coupled such that one's action contributes to the other's disturbance rate, the faster agent can generate mismatch in the target faster than the target's correction can resolve it — driving the target outside its operating reserve and breaking the correction mechanism entirely.

The construction is clean: the persistence template's effective disturbance for the target is its base disturbance plus the attacker's coupling effectiveness times the attacker's tempo (Model D — deterministic drift coupling) or with a square-root variant (Model S — stochastic noise coupling). **Destabilization is the *negation* of the template's persistence condition** — the same inequality viewed in the opposite direction. *Persistence and destabilization are the same inequality viewed in opposite directions.*

The destabilization threshold has a clean form. Under Model D, the attacker destabilizes when its tempo times its coupling effectiveness exceeds the target's *adaptive reserve* (the target's correction-rate-times-reserve minus base disturbance). Under Model S, the threshold replaces the linear-in-correction-rate scaling with a square-root-in-correction-rate scaling — the same scaling distinction from Part I propagating through the destabilization negation.

A central observation: **the scaling difference is the structural source of the superlinear adversarial-advantage scaling**. The $1/\alpha$ versus $1/\sqrt{\alpha}$ split in the persistence template propagates through the destabilization condition to give the squared (Model D) versus three-halves-power (Model S) tempo-ratio scaling in the next segment's headline result. It is *not* a separate derivation — both follow from the persistence template applied with coupling.

The framework also names a striking corollary: **the effects spiral**. When an agent is pushed past its adaptive reserve, its corrections become unreliable. *Unreliable corrections make the agent more legible to the adversary* — the agent's responses become predictable in the panic state, increasing the attacker's coupling effectiveness. Higher coupling effectiveness increases the disturbance the target faces, pushing it further past reserve, making corrections more unreliable. The spiral terminates only when the agent undergoes structural adaptation (a different model class, a different mode of operation) or ceases to function as an adaptive agent entirely. *Resilience is not just high correction rate or high reserve — it is also having a mechanism to break the spiral before structural collapse, which usually means external intervention from outside the agent.* This is the framework's mathematical model of *panic*.

A scope-honest counterpoint: **speed without coupling is useless**. If the attacker's coupling to the target is weak, the attacker can have *infinite* tempo and still fail to destabilize. Being fast in a vacuum accomplishes nothing; the attacker has to be fast *and* coupled. Both are jointly required and neither suffices.
