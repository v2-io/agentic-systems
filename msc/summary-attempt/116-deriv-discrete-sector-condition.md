# Discrete Sector Condition (appendix derivation — conditional)

Discrete-time analogs of the continuous-time Lyapunov results, closing the fluid-limit gap between event-driven dynamics and continuous-time analysis. The derivation works through the contraction-mapping framework applied to the discrete update. The framework establishes three discrete-time results: **DA.1 (Model D)** — under deterministic disturbance, discrete steady-state mismatch equals continuous steady-state with *zero gap*; **DA.1S (Model S)** — under stochastic disturbance, discrete and continuous steady-state variance differ by an additive term proportional to *(gain × Lipschitz constant)*, quantitatively small when this product is small; **DA.2 (adaptive reserve)** — the discrete adaptive reserve matches the continuous form modulo the same small additive term.

A critical observation: the discrete sector condition needs a *strictly stronger Lipschitz upper bound* on the correction function — strictly stronger than an inner-product upper bound — because the discrete contraction involves the squared norm of the correction. This is automatically satisfied for Bayesian updates (the posterior lies between prior and data) and for gradient descent on smooth losses (where the Lipschitz constant is the smoothness parameter). With this constraint plus a step-size condition on the gain, the fluid limit is formally justified.

The result is what gives the framework's continuous-time analysis its operational relevance for discrete-event-driven agents (the typical case). Without the discrete-time bridge, the chapter prose would be a continuous-time idealization; with it, the same persistence inequality applies (under stated step-size and Lipschitz conditions) to agents whose updates are *event-triggered* rather than continuous.
