# Cognitive Cost of Strategy (formulation — discussion-grade for IB; conditional for depth bound)

A formulation connecting strategy structure to its *maintenance cost* — making the explicit-strategy condition from Chapter 2 quantitative. The minimum description length of a strategy DAG decomposes into structural bits (encoding topology — node identities, edge connectivity, AND/OR labels) and parameter bits (encoding the edge credences given the topology), scaling as $O(|E|\log|V|)$ for moderate-precision credences.

The framework offers a **strategy IB objective** — the strategy is the information-bottleneck compression of the chronica *for guidance*, parallel to the model as IB compression *for prediction*. The variational form trades off the compression cost (mutual information between chronica and strategy) against a KL divergence from the optimal-policy reference to the strategy-induced policy. The **KL direction** is forced by a regret-bound derivation: under bounded value range and deterministic optimal policy, the strategy-induced regret is at most the value range times the total variation distance, which by Pinsker's inequality is bounded by the square root of the KL divergence (in the direction *optimal-policy first*). The opposite-direction KL becomes vacuous (infinite) under deterministic optimal policy whenever the strategy places any mass off the optimum. This is *not* a heuristic — the direction is forced.

Within the direction-forced family, the specific choice of *reverse-KL* (rather than other f-divergences) is *uniquely selected* by a chain-rule additivity axiom — the divergence-level analog of the chain-confidence-decay layer's log-additive identity from earlier in the chapter. This is the **second instance of the four-layer additive-coordinate-forcing meta-pattern**: a uniqueness theorem operating on an AAT-internally-motivated additivity axiom forces a specific coordinate.

The framework derives a **maximum useful chain depth** for AND-chains: given a base observation rate, per-edge success probability, effective sample size, and disturbance-to-capacity ratio, there is a finite depth beyond which the deepest edge cannot maintain bounded mismatch. Beyond this depth, evidence starvation makes the edge uncorrectable faster than the environment invalidates it; the agent *accumulates strategic mismatch on deep edges regardless of how fast it acts at the top*. High evidence requirements and volatile environments severely limit useful chain depth — under modest parameters, even a single edge can fail to persist.

A central observation is named: **deep AND-chains suffer a triple depth penalty** that compounds. (1) **Confidence decay** (from earlier in the chapter): aggregate confidence decays geometrically with depth — *the plan is less likely to succeed*. (2) **Evidence starvation**: effective observation rate at deeper edges decays geometrically — *the plan is harder to calibrate*. (3) **Cognitive cost** (this segment): each additional depth level adds bits to description length — *the plan is more expensive to maintain*. All three are multiplicative in depth, making deep sequential strategies exponentially costly along three independent dimensions. This creates systematic pressure toward *shallow, OR-heavy strategies* over deep AND-heavy ones.

The framework also makes the maintenance cost from Chapter 2 *concrete* by decomposing it into representation cost (proportional to description length), revision cost (proportional to strategic tempo), and monitoring cost (proportional to the number of edges with partial identifiability). Each component maps to a quantity defined elsewhere in the theory.

A practical consequence for language-constituted agents: a context window of $W$ tokens imposes a hard upper bound on description length, making the IB trade-off *non-optional* — the agent *must* compress its strategy, and the depth bound becomes a context-window-limited quantity. A 128K-token context may support a 500-edge DAG encoded in natural language; a 4K-token window may support only a 15-edge sketch. The Miller (2022) coevolving-automata result provides a complementary constraint: a complex strategy whose edges are only tested over a short interaction horizon gains nothing from its depth — the untested structure is *indistinguishable from a simpler strategy*. The interaction horizon compresses the strategy space from above, just as maintenance cost and evidence starvation compress it from below.
