# Sector Condition Stability — Lyapunov Derivation (appendix derivation — exact)

The Lyapunov machinery underlying the sector-condition stability result and the central persistence inequality. The dynamics are taken in general nonlinear vector form: mismatch evolves at rate (negative correction function) plus disturbance, with the correction function required to satisfy only *qualitative* properties — *zero correction at zero mismatch* and the *local sector condition* (the correction function's inner product with the mismatch is at least $\alpha$ times the squared magnitude inside a radius-$R$ region). The Lyapunov function is the standard quadratic; the steady-state results follow from standard Lyapunov machinery.

The derivation produces the framework's central results in their precise form. **Proposition A.1 (Model D — deterministic bounded disturbance)**: under bounded disturbance and the sector condition, mismatch is ultimately bounded by *(disturbance rate / sector parameter)* — exactly the persistence threshold the chapter cites. **Proposition A.1S (Model S — stochastic disturbance)**: under Itô-Lyapunov analysis with stochastic disturbance, the steady-state RMS mismatch scales as $\sigma_w \sqrt{n/(2\alpha)}$ — the $1/\sqrt{\alpha}$ scaling that gives Model S its distinct character from Model D's $1/\alpha$ scaling. **Proposition A.2 (adaptive reserve)**: the difference between sector parameter times region radius and disturbance is the additional disturbance the agent can absorb before mismatch leaves the valid region.

The framework names the *sub-scope* structure carefully. **Sub-scope $\alpha$** (Bayesian on exponential families, gradient on strongly convex losses, linear PD agents): the sector condition is *derived* from the update rule's structure via the gain-sector bridge. **Sub-scope $\beta$** (PID, rule-based, human-judgment agents): the sector condition is *assumed* as a primitive posit; the bridge does not eliminate this assumption universally — it sharpens which agent classes admit derivation and which require empirical verification.

A consequential finding the segment carries: under **Model S** the appendix contains a no-go result showing that *no horizon-independent non-exit bound* exists under additive stochastic forcing — the natural Ville/Doob maximal-inequality route *provably cannot exist* (the only compensated supermartingale candidate is sign-indefinite inside the persistence basin; the scale function is unbounded). The framework treats this as a load-bearing proof step rather than as a discouraging result: under Model S, the agent *will* eventually leave any fixed region with probability one over an unbounded horizon — making the *need for structural adaptation* (not merely parametric update) a *certain eventual event* rather than an edge case for any sufficiently long-lived stochastic-environment agent.
