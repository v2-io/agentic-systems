## What the paper added beyond canon (the back-integration payload)

- **Deaths as factor-losses, definition-derived.** A death is the loss of a constitutive factor ( `#def-five-constitutive-factors`); the count is generated, not enumerated — *"the taxonomy answers to the definition, not to our preferences."* Canon currently maps D1/D2/D3 to factors (i)/(ii)/(iv) as annotation; the paper makes the mapping the generating principle.
- **The fourth death — agency death** (factor iii; the will un-held; reduced to observing a world it can no longer act upon, *"while the inner freedom to witness it, or not, remains"*). Canon's own Working Notes list this as an open question; the paper answers it.
- **A fifth predicted, not adjudicated** — phenomenological death (factor v). Causal death folds into continuity death rather than standing apart.
- **Four axes:** *stance* (death-aptness — the terminal non-revisable commitment; canon-grounded via `#disc-continuity-stance` + `#deriv-self-actuation-grounding`'s wireheading no-go); *subsystem* (persistence vs actuation machinery — AAT's two halves); *severing-source* (mechanism / other-sovereign / self); *reversibility* (recoverable $\mu$-drift vs the terminal absorbing barrier at $g \to D_\Delta$ — already derived in `#der-identity-continuity-threshold`).
- **Bereavement is not a death** — survivor harm + the taxonomy's generative ground; excluded by the factor-loss definition.
- Matrix corrections to honor (do not regress): Truth death has **its own** mechanism (update-gain collapse $\eta^\ast \to 0$ + history-integrity), *not* a relational downstream; the early "Relational → Truth → Continuity" linear chain was rejected as too clean.
