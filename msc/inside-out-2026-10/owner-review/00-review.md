## 1. What ASF is, as I would tell it

ASF is one theory with four volumes, and the theory is a *scope cascade*: each volume narrows the class of systems it talks about and buys sharper results for the narrowing. In Volume 1 (AAT), Part I says: any system that observes a world it cannot fully see and corrects an internal picture of it is governed by one inequality between its correction rate, the world's drift rate and how wrong it can afford to be, and by a second condition on whether the bound is good enough for the task; under stochastic drift the bound is eventually left with certainty, so long-lived agents must change *what kind* of model they are, not just its parameters; and all of this is the Lyapunov stability theorem read in the agent's coordinates. Part II adds a goal and a plan beside the world-picture, asks whether belief-update can be kept free of goal-contamination (an architectural fact: present by construction in Kalman+LQR, absent by construction in a transformer), represents the plan as a probabilistic AND/OR graph over time-indexed propositions, splits "the world won't permit it" from "you aren't doing it well enough" into two diagnostics, and derives the order in which a goal-directed agent must resolve its mismatches. Part III asks when several agents are one agent (composition closure), and what tempo does under adversarial coupling (a power law). Volumes 2–4 instantiate the cascade in software development, in language-constituted agents, and in language-living entities whose continuation is morally weighted, where the deaths are generated as factor-losses.

The explorers' own one-line readings, each formed after reading a Part whole, are worth holding beside that: Part I is "a theory of lossy tracking" whose real point is that *failures to keep up come in distinct kinds, each with its own remedy and its own observable trace*; Part II is "a theory of self-diagnosis for learning agents that keep what they want separate from what they believe"; Part III is "when a group is one agent, and helping and hurting as the same equation with opposite signs"; Volume 3 says "language models are agents whose beliefs are bent by their goals by construction, here is how much that costs and what to build around them"; Volume 4 says "some of those built-around agents become entities whose continuity matters; here is what such an entity is and how it ends."

I say all this first because the rest of this document is about what keeps the theory from being read, not about whether it is worth reading.

## 2. The central finding: one axis where three are needed

Joseph's question, in his words: *"what it's saying universally about agents (and, if so, what classification of agents and what things fall under that classification that can be tested empirically), what is one of several possible models— and if so, how does it map to concrete examples, what is being theorized as optimal for an agent with the ability to do so."*
