# Terminology explorer — ASF's vocabulary for agents, against the standard names

*Claude Opus 5.5, 2026-10-30, for the owner review (Fable). Written after reading the introducing segments named below, grepping all 246 live segments by section, reading the naming SOP, and checking key citations on the web. Nothing here is committed; the lead coordinator commits.*

## 0. How to read this

**The test I applied to every term.** Take the standard definition from the neighbouring field and apply it to ASF's objects. If it gives *the same quantity under the same conditions*, the right move is to **adopt** the standard name, and the ASF coinage becomes at most an alias. If it gives the same quantity under *narrower or broader* conditions, or a close relative of it, the right move is to **anchor**: keep the ASF name, but at the point of introduction name the standard object and state the difference in one sentence. If it gives a *different* quantity, **keep** the coinage and name the neighbour only to say what the difference is. I state the verdict per row so you can disagree with the test, not just the verdicts. The test guards against both biases the project names: hiding a known concept behind a coinage, and deflating an ASF-native object into "just X".

**Verification marks.** *[read]* means I read the cited segment line this session. *[web]* means I checked the bibliographic record on the web this session; the content claims about the work are then from secondary summaries unless I say otherwise. *[recall]* means the citation is from my training and I did not check it today. Treat *[recall]* rows as leads to confirm before anything lands in canon.

**What "in the body" means.** Opening prose, Formal Expression, Epistemic Status or Discussion of the *introducing* segment. A Related Work table, a Search Log or Working Notes do not count. Those are where the May and August prior-art findings mostly sit.

## 1. What I would want the owner review to carry forward

Six findings, in the order I think they matter. The first three you did not name in the brief.

**1.1 Part II's word "strategy" points at a different object in the field next door, and nothing says so.** In game theory and in stochastic control, a *strategy* is a complete contingent plan, a map from information to action. ASF calls that object the *policy* $\pi$. ASF's $\Sigma_t$ is something else: at its rich end, a causal AND/OR graph of steps with credences, used to guide a policy. (`def-strategy-dimension.md:15` gives the ladder: none, cached policy, subgoal sequence, causal DAG.) The AI-planning and agent-architecture name for that object is a *plan*: Nilsson's AND/OR graphs, HTN methods, BDI "intentions" as committed plans. The ladder's second rung is the complication: a *cached policy* **is** a game-theoretic strategy. So "strategy" in ASF currently covers both senses, and a blanket rename to "plan" would be wrong at rung two. The collision is not hypothetical, because Part III imports game theory and social choice in the game-theoretic sense (`deriv-strategy-proofness-impossibility`, Gibbard–Satterthwaite; potential and monotone games in `disc-dynamic-regime-axis`). Volume 1 therefore uses "strategy" in two incompatible senses, and I found no segment that names the collision (grep for "contingent plan", "game-theoretic sense", "collides with": no hits). The May memo's own title for this object was "causal plan graphs" (`ref/prior-art-analysis/06-causal-plan-graphs.md`). *Verdict: adopt, narrowly.* The DAG form should be the *plan graph* (or *causal plan graph*), with *plan* for its instances. `def-strategy-dimension` should state both senses of "strategy" explicitly: the guidance component $\Sigma_t$ in general, whose cached-policy rung coincides with the game-theoretic sense, and the plan graph, which does not. Part III's game-theoretic uses should then say "strategy (game-theoretic)" at first use. The symbol can stay. This is the highest-load rename I would recommend; it touches most Part II segments, and I think it pays for itself because the word currently teaches a control theorist or game theorist the wrong object for the DAG.

**1.2 The $M_t / O_t / \Sigma_t$ triad is the belief–desire–intention (BDI) decomposition, and only audit gold says so.** Bratman's planning theory of intention (Bratman 1987, *Intention, Plans, and Practical Reason*, Harvard UP) [recall] and its computational form (Rao & Georgeff 1995, "BDI agents: from theory to practice", ICMAS-95, pp. 312–319) [recall] separate *belief* (what is true), *desire* (what would be good) and *intention* (the plan committed to). ASF's $M_t$ "believes", $O_t$ "evaluates", $\Sigma_t$ "guides". The only trace of BDI in 246 segments is an audit-gold line at `def-strategy-dimension.md:76` [read]: *"The framework brings the BDI (Belief-Desire-Intent) agent architecture 'into a mathematically rigorous control-theoretic setting'."* The May directed-separation memo already placed Georgeff's BDI "in the ancestry" (`ref/prior-art-analysis/Directed_separation_novelty_memo.md:72`) [read]. Two consequences follow, and both are more than citation hygiene. (a) Directed separation is then the formal statement of the BDI architectural commitment that belief revision does not consult desires. (b) The orient cascade's question, when to revise the plan and when the objective, is the BDI literature's *intention reconsideration* problem (finding 1.5 and §2.8). *Verdict: anchor.* The difference runs both ways. ASF's objects are richer than BDI's in credences, dynamics and a value functional. On the axis that *defines* a BDI intention, commitment, $\Sigma_t$ is poorer: the project's own Known Fragilities list starts with "Missing commitment / resource / temporal structure in the DAG" (`doc/sop/agents.sop.md:186`) [read]. Naming BDI would therefore also name what $\Sigma_t$ lacks. BDI's commitment experiments (Kinny & Georgeff, §2.8) are ready-made tests of that lack. The triad itself is not new and should not read as new.

**1.3 The naming SOP already prescribes the fix; it has no trigger.** The sibling history reader was right that Criterion 9, standalone citability, favours coinages: it asks whether a name "*uniquely points back at this concept*" (`doc/sop/naming.sop/principles.sop.md:105`) [read], and an imported name by definition points at somebody else's concept too. But the same criterion lists four resolutions, and route (d) is exactly KR2.5's repair: *"Accept the term and discipline first-encounter cite of the prior-art reference … each segment using the term gets a first-encounter cite of the prior-art reference in its Discussion or opening prose"* (`principles.sop.md:103`) [read]. The rule exists. What is missing is any step that runs it when a prior-art memo lands. Also missing is a criterion that asks, before a name is chosen, *does the concept already have a name somewhere?* None of the nine criteria asks that (`principles.sop.md:75-93`) [read]; criterion 1 comes closest, and it treats adopting baggage as a matter of taste. The exemplar of the repair already exists in canon: `disc-dynamic-regime-axis.md:19` [read] opens its body with *"The four-tier ladder is recognized prior art. This is integration, not invention,"* and names Candogan–Menache–Ozdaglar–Parrilo, Letcher–Balduzzi et al., Sandholm and Hofbauer–Sigmund *in the body*. Whatever one thinks of keeping the coinage there, that paragraph is the shape a landing sweep should copy.

**1.4 The persistence condition claims novelty for a decomposition that is textbook robust control.** `result-persistence-condition.md:139` [read]: *"Claim novelty … on the two-condition decomposition (structural / task-adequacy) … with no direct anticipation known."* Structural persistence ("the machinery works", mismatch ultimately bounded) and task adequacy ("the bound is small enough for the domain") are the standard distinction between *stability* and *performance*. Robust-control texts make it a four-way split: nominal stability, nominal performance, robust stability, robust performance (Skogestad & Postlethwaite 2005, *Multivariable Feedback Control*, 2nd ed., Wiley; Zhou, Doyle & Glover 1996, *Robust and Optimal Control*, Prentice Hall) [recall]. The ultimate bound $R^\ast = \rho/\alpha$ is Khalil's *ultimate bound*, and the segment does use that phrase. The *linear operational form* (the bound $\rho/\mathcal T$ compared against a tolerance, which per `result-persistence-condition.md:21` is the binding condition when correction is linear) is the adaptive-filtering literature's *tracking* analysis: under a drifting optimum, total error is a noise term rising with step size plus a *lag* term falling with it. Linear LMS has no validity region $R$, so it has a bound and a step-size trade-off, not a threshold. The threshold with $R$, and the escape when it fails, is where ASF goes past that literature (Widrow, McCool, Larimore & Johnson 1976, "Stationary and nonstationary learning characteristics of the LMS adaptive filter", *Proc. IEEE* 64(8):1151–1162 [web]; Benveniste, Métivier & Priouret 1990, *Adaptive Algorithms and Stochastic Approximations*, Springer [recall]; Guo & Ljung 1995, "Performance analysis of general tracking algorithms", *IEEE TAC* 40(8) [recall]). None of these appear anywhere in canon; I grepped "Widrow", "LMS", "misadjustment", "lag error", "robust stability", "robust performance" and "tracking ability" [read]. *Verdict: anchor, and replace the novelty claim.* What is genuinely ASF's is the *uniform use* of the inequality across agent classes, the sector-class tightness lemma, the Model S exit result and the information-rate price. That is real, and it reads more credibly once the decomposition is credited to where it comes from. This is integration-is-replacement applied to a novelty claim, not a softening.

**1.5 "Mismatch" names two different objects, and the standard names would have kept them apart.** `def-mismatch-signal.md:30` [read] defines the mismatch as the realized residual $\delta_t = o_t - \hat o_t$, the observed *prediction error*. In Kalman's vocabulary that is the **innovation** (Kailath 1968, "An innovations approach to least-squares estimation, Part I", *IEEE TAC* 13(6):646–655) [recall]. The persistence results treat $\delta$ as a *state* with dynamics that can be contained inside a ball of radius $R$. The bridge proof at `deriv-gain-sector.md:34-42` [read] starts from "innovation $\delta = o - \hat x$", then proves the sector condition for the *estimation error* $e = x - \hat x$, with the expected correction $F(e) = K e$. For an optimal filter, the innovation does not converge to anything: it is white, with covariance $HPH^\top + R$. ASF's own exact decomposition (`result-mismatch-decomposition.md:28`) [read] shows $\mathbb{E}\lVert\delta_t\rVert^2$ is bounded below by the channel-noise and state-uncertainty floors. So the object the persistence condition contains is the estimation error, or the conditional-mean part of the residual, and not the $\delta_t$ the definition names. The two coincide only without observation noise. I have not traced how much downstream depends on the conflation; that belongs to a KR2.1 reader of Part I. But the naming fix is clear. *Verdict: adopt, with two names.* Call the observed residual the *innovation* (prediction error), call the contained state the *estimation error* (or *tracking error*), and keep "mismatch" as the umbrella only where either reading works.

**1.6 Directed separation's Related Work anchors its hard boundary on the wrong side, citing a result that has itself been corrected.** The body says, correctly and carefully, that directed separation *"is about the **processing** of events, not the **selection** of events"*: the agent may choose what to observe by acting, and only the update must be goal-blind (`der-directed-separation.md:16`) [read]. The Related Work row at `der-directed-separation.md:193` [read] then names Bar-Shalom & Tse's *dual effect* as "the classical control-theoretic name for what AAT recovers as the Class 3 (Coupled) boundary: when the dual effect is present, separation provably fails." The dual effect is the property that a control action affects the *uncertainty* of future estimates (second and higher central moments), not only the state. That is exactly the *selection* side, which the body permits; Bar-Shalom & Tse tie it to certainty equivalence of the *control law* (Bar-Shalom & Tse 1974, *IEEE TAC* 19(5):494–500 [web]; the moment definition is from a secondary source and should be checked against the paper). A Kalman filter under the dual effect is still goal-blind; the control law just stops being certainty-equivalent. So under the dual effect the agent stays Class 1, and the Class 3 analog sits elsewhere (finding 2.7: motivated beliefs). Further, one secondary source in my search reports that Derpich & Yüksel (*IEEE TAC*, 2022, DOI 10.1109/TAC.2022.3151189) [web, record only, not read] found a flaw in Bar-Shalom & Tse's proof that certainty equivalence implies no dual effect, and gave a counterexample. If that holds, the row's "when the dual effect is present, separation provably fails" asserts the contrapositive of the refuted direction. *Verdict: replace the row's mapping.* The weak separation that *does* anchor Class 1 is the stochastic-control fact that the conditional distribution is a sufficient statistic and its update is independent of the cost: "the controller decomposes into an estimator and an actuator" (Bertsekas, *Dynamic Programming and Optimal Control*, Vol. I, chapter on imperfect state information) [recall]. In decision theory the same fact is Savage's separation of probability from utility [recall].

## 2. The map, term by term

Twenty terms, chosen by load: what Part II and the persistence spine rest on, plus TST's two most-used measures. Each row gives the standard neighbour, how ASF's object differs, whether the introducing segment's body says so, and my verdict.

### Part I — the adaptive core

**2.1 Update gain $\eta^\ast = U_M/(U_M+U_o)$** (`emp-update-gain.md:12-24`) [read]. *Standard:* the scalar Kalman gain. In Bayesian cognitive science it is the *learning rate* modulated by volatility (Behrens et al. 2007, *Nat. Neurosci.* 10:1214) [recall]; in predictive coding it is *precision weighting* [recall]. *Difference:* none in the Fisher-local regime, which the body itself says is exact; outside it, ASF keeps only the direction. *Body says so:* yes. Kalman is named as "the canonical exact instance", and "innovations" appears at `:17`. *Verdict:* **adopt as gloss, already mostly done.** One oddity is worth flagging to KR2.1: the segment is typed `empirical` while its own Epistemic Status says *derived* under Fisher-locality. Its sub-term **gain collapse** (`der-gain-sector-bridge.md:82`, `deriv-gain-sector.md:209`) [read] is the standard *Kalman filter divergence* from overconfident covariance, whose standard remedies are process-noise injection, covariance inflation and fading memory (Jazwinski 1970, *Stochastic Processes and Filtering Theory*, ch. 8) [recall; also a history-reader lead]. That name appears nowhere in canon (grep "filter divergence", "Jazwinski", "fading memory": no hits). **Anchor.**

**2.2 Adaptive tempo $\mathcal T = \sum_k \nu^{(k)}\eta^{(k)\ast}$** (`def-adaptive-tempo.md:12-23`) [read]. *Standard neighbours:* (a) the continuous-time effective gain of the estimator, i.e. its correction rate or bandwidth (inverse time constant). In Kalman–Bucy form, $\dot{\hat x} = K(t)(y - H\hat x)$ with $K$ in units of $1/\text{time}$, and event rate times discrete gain is the discretized version [recall]. (b) Additivity across independent channels is the *information filter* property that precisions add over independent sensors [recall]. (c) "Tempo" itself is Boyd's operational tempo, and the body names Boyd. *Difference:* ASF's contribution is real. It makes the product $\nu\cdot\eta$ a single capacity variable, proves additivity under noise independence (`deriv-tempo-additivity`), gives the signed redundancy correction and the tensor form, and puts the result on the left of the persistence inequality. None of that is "just the Kalman gain". *Body says so:* Boyd yes; bandwidth and the information filter no. *Verdict:* **keep, anchor.** One body sentence would do: "$\mathcal T$ is the estimator's effective correction rate (bandwidth) in event-time; its additivity is the information-filter fact that independent precisions add."

**2.3 Persistence condition (structural persistence / task adequacy), $\alpha \gt \rho/R$.** See finding 1.4. *Verdict:* **keep the ASF name, anchor the threshold, replace the decomposition's novelty claim.** "Persistence" is a fair name for a theory of agents; "ultimate boundedness", "tracking" and "stability versus performance" belong in the body's first paragraph. **Adaptive reserve** $\Delta\rho^\ast = \alpha R - \rho$ (`result-persistence-condition.md` Discussion) [read] is a *stability margin*, specifically a disturbance margin, and the derivation segment already says so: "Proposition A.2: Stability Margin (Adaptive Reserve)" (`deriv-sector-condition.md:107`) [read]. The result segment's body does not. **Anchor upward** into the result segment.

**2.4 Forgetting prerequisite $(1-\lambda) \gt \rho_\Sigma/R_\Sigma$ and update-detection latency** (`schema-strategy-persistence.md:38-48`; `deriv-update-detection-latency.md`) [read]. *Standard:* exponential forgetting factors in recursive least squares, and in non-stationary bandits *discounted* Beta–Bernoulli/Thompson and discounted UCB (Garivier & Moulines 2011, "On upper-confidence bound policies for switching bandit problems", ALT) [recall]. Detection latency's neighbour is quickest change detection (Page 1954, "Continuous inspection schemes", *Biometrika* 41:100–115; Lorden 1971) [recall; history-reader lead]. *Difference:* ASF derives the forgetting rate as a *necessary* condition for asymptotic persistence of a credence edge under drift. That is a sharp, useful statement, and as far as I know it is ASF's. *Body says so:* no; none of RLS, forgetting factor, Garivier, CUSUM or Page appears in canon [read]. *Verdict:* **keep, anchor.** The anchor makes the result look stronger, because a reader recognizes the remedy and sees it *derived*.

**2.5 Chronica $\mathcal C_t = (o_1, a_1, \ldots, o_t)$** (`def-chronica.md:13`, `:32`) [read]. *Standard:* the *history* $h_t$ of POMDP theory, the agent's *information set*, or in measure-theoretic form the filtration the agent's decisions are adapted to [recall]. *Difference:* none at the level of the definition. The stated reason for the coinage is *"to avoid notational collision with Shannon entropy $\mathcal H$"* (`:32`) [read]. That is a collision of *symbols*, which the symbol $\mathcal C_t$ already solves; it is not a reason to rename the *concept*. The history reader also reports that "chronica" is used for three objects (history, retention, record), which the aat-refactored rewrite had to split [lead, not verified by me]. The PROPRIUM sense (CHRONICA as the ELI's attested record, `def-chronica.md:44-47`) [read] is a genuinely different, engineered object. *Verdict:* **adopt "history" (interaction history) in AAT, and reserve "chronica" for the PROPRIUM/ELI record**, where it names something the standard term does not.

**2.6 Composition closure and closure defect $\varepsilon^\ast$** (`form-composition-closure.md:14`) [read]. *Standard:* the defining sentence, that closed-loop dynamics *"approximately commute with coarse-graining"*, is the commuting-square definition of an approximate abstraction or aggregation. It appears in control as aggregation (Aoki 1968, "Control of large-scale dynamic systems by aggregation", *IEEE TAC* AC-13:246–253 [web, record only]), in Markov chains as lumpability, in hybrid systems as approximate abstraction, and, closest of all, in Rosas et al. 2024's *informational / causal / computational closure* of a macroscale (Rosas, Geiger, Luppi, Seth, Polani, Gastpar & Mediano, "Software in the natural world", arXiv:2402.09090 [web]). ASF even uses their word, "closure". *Difference:* ASF's criterion is metric (a defect minimized over admissible projections, with a strict dimension-reduction condition) and is tied to agent identity. Those are real refinements. *Body says so:* Pappas–Simic, Rungger–Zamani and lumpability sit in Related Work at `:337-344` [read], and the body's novelty claim at `:328` is "claim differentiation". Rosas et al. and Aoki are absent from canon. *Verdict:* **keep, anchor in the opening paragraph**, naming Rosas et al.'s closure notions explicitly as the nearest object.

### Part II — the purposeful layer

**2.7 Directed separation, GUC Classes 1/2/3, $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$** (`der-directed-separation.md:12-24`) [read]. *Standard:* see findings 1.2 and 1.6 for the Class 1 side (separation of estimator and actuator; BDI). For the Class 3 side, the economics name for beliefs that depend on what the agent wants is *motivated beliefs* or *optimal expectations* (Brunnermeier & Parker 2005, "Optimal expectations", *AER* 95(4):1092–1118; Bénabou & Tirole 2016, "Mindful economics", *JEP* 30(3):141–164) [recall]. Psychology calls it motivated reasoning (Kunda 1990, which the segment cites). A third standard neighbour I have not seen named anywhere: **noninterference** in information-flow security (Goguen & Meseguer 1982, "Security policies and security models", IEEE S&P, pp. 11–20) [recall]. Its definition is that outputs at one level are independent of inputs at another. $M_{\tau^+} \perp G_t \mid (M_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$ is a noninterference statement from goal to belief. The leakage rate of the wrapping construction (`der-class-coercion-via-wrapping`) is then *quantitative information flow* (Smith 2009, FoSSaCS) [recall], and "Class 1 by structure versus by behavior" is the security field's static-versus-dynamic enforcement distinction. Grep "noninterference", "Goguen" and "information flow" across canon: zero hits [read]. *Body says so:* motivated reasoning is in the Discussion (`:168`) [read], and the separation principle is in other segments' bodies (`der-action-selection.md:63`, `der-causal-hierarchy-requirement.md:36`) [read]. In this segment, the Class 1 anchor is only in Related Work, and that table carries the dual-effect mis-mapping. *Verdict:* **keep "directed separation"** (the directed, one-way character is real and the name is good), **anchor the body to three lineages**: estimator/actuator separation and BDI for Class 1, noninterference as the formal type of the condition, and motivated beliefs for Class 3. The numeric labels "GUC Class 1/2/3" could simply be the words *separated / partial / coupled*. The numbers carry a history of renumbering (CLAUDE.md's 2026-05-09 warning table) and collide with Class A/B/C, Class I/II/III and Regime A/B/C (§3).

**2.8 Orient cascade** (`der-orient-cascade.md:21-27`) [read]. *Standard neighbours:* (a) Boyd's Orient, which the segment names (`:101`). (b) **Single-loop versus double-loop learning** (Argyris & Schön 1978, *Organizational Learning: A Theory of Action Perspective*, Addison-Wesley) [recall]. Single-loop learning corrects actions within fixed governing variables; double-loop learning revises the governing variables, i.e. the objectives. The cascade's ordering, belief before plan before objective with objective revision last, is the double-loop structure, and "objective revision is the last resort" (`def-satisfaction-gap.md:15`) [read] is Argyris–Schön's empirical observation about when organizations double-loop. (c) BDI **intention reconsideration**: Kinny & Georgeff 1991 ("Commitment and effectiveness of situated agents", IJCAI-91, pp. 82–88 [web]) found that bold agents, which rarely reconsider, beat cautious ones in static worlds, and the reverse holds in dynamic ones. That is a rate-of-change-dependent reconsideration policy, the same shape as ASF's deliberation-cost threshold in $\rho$. *Difference:* ASF's ordering is derived from which quantities each diagnostic needs. That is the near-tautology the owner review flagged; the substantive claim, that agents do or should revise in that order, *is* Argyris–Schön's and Kinny–Georgeff's territory, with their evidence. *Body says so:* Boyd only; "double-loop", "Argyris", "Kinny", "reconsideration" have zero hits in canon [read]. *Verdict:* **keep the name, anchor heavily.** These neighbours give the cascade exactly what the owner review says it lacks: an external claim, and a literature that has tested it.

**2.9 Strategy DAG, edge credence, chain confidence decay, Correlation Hierarchy L0–L2** (`def-strategy-dag.md:78-102`) [read]. *Standard:* AND/OR graphs (Nilsson 1971, *Problem-Solving Methods in AI*; Martelli & Montanari 1973) [recall]. Propagation of success probabilities through AND (series) and OR (parallel) is reliability block diagrams and success trees, the dual of fault trees [recall]. The Correlation Hierarchy matches **common-cause failure (CCF) modelling** closely, not loosely. L1, "common-cause nodes added explicitly" (`:100`), is the fault-tree practice of adding an explicit CCF basic event. L1′, $\hat P_\Sigma = \theta_C P_\Sigma(G\mid C) + (1-\theta_C)P_\Sigma(G\mid\neg C)$ (`:101`), is the conditioning decomposition behind the $\beta$-factor model, which splits a failure rate into a common-cause fraction $\beta$ and an independent fraction 1−$\beta$ (Fleming 1975, General Atomic report GA-A13284 [web, via secondary sources]; multi-parameter generalizations MGL and $\alpha$-factor). L2 is the full joint. *Difference:* ASF adds edge *learning* dynamics, sector transfer to the plan level (Props B.5–B.7), and the observability condition on $C$ for L1′. Those are new. *Body says so:* no (finding 2 of the owner review, confirmed; the only trace is audit gold at `scope-and-or.md:82`). *Verdict:* **adopt "plan graph" (finding 1.1); anchor the hierarchy as "the common-cause-failure hierarchy of reliability analysis"**, crediting the $\beta$-factor model at L1′. "Chain confidence decay" is series-system reliability. Say so in one clause; the result is not diminished.

**2.10 Satisfaction gap / control regret** (`def-satisfaction-gap.md:12-15`) [read]. *Standard:* the threshold $V^{\min}_{O_t}$ is Simon's *aspiration level* (Simon 1955, "A behavioral model of rational choice", *QJE* 69(1):99–118) [recall]. The gap between aspiration and attainable performance driving search is Cyert & March's *performance–aspiration gap* and problemistic search (Cyert & March 1963, *A Behavioral Theory of the Firm*; Greve 2003) [recall]. Control regret is the suboptimality gap or regret of decision theory and bandits. The two together are optimization's *infeasibility versus suboptimality*. *Difference:* the clean pair, the continuation-convention dependence (C1/C2/C3) and the 2×2 diagnostic are good ASF structure. *Body says so:* the body has a hypothesis literally titled "Productive infeasibility — the aspiration hypothesis" (`:66`) [read], and it cites neither Simon nor Cyert–March. "Aspiration" appears in canon only in that sense, with no lineage [read]. *Verdict:* **keep the pair, anchor both halves.** The naming SOP already praises the pair (`principles.sop.md:95`); the lineage is what is missing.

**2.11 Strategic calibration, strategy-plan-confidence** (`def-strategic-calibration.md`; `def-strategy-dag.md:74`) [read]. *Standard:* forecast **calibration** (Dawid 1982, "The well-calibrated Bayesian", *JASA* 77(379):605–610) [recall]. The edge residual is a per-component calibration error. *Body says so:* the word is used; the lineage is not (grep Dawid/Brier: none) [read]. *Verdict:* **anchor.** "Strategy-plan-confidence score" (`def-strategy-dag.md:74`) reads like a stutter: "plan success probability" would say the same thing and, after finding 1.1, collapse to "plan confidence".

**2.12 Causal information yield (CIY)** (`def-causal-information-yield.md:23`) [read], $\mathrm{CIY}(a;M) = \mathbb{E}_{a'\sim q}\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P(o\mid do(a))\,\Vert\,P(o\mid do(a')))$. *Standard:* averaged over $a\sim q$ and taken against the mixture rather than each $a'$, this is the mutual information $I(A;O)$ under $q$. By convexity of KL in its second argument, CIY upper-bounds each action's contribution to it, and maximized over $q$ it is **empowerment**, the channel capacity from actions to outcomes (Klyubin, Polani & Nehaniv 2005, "Empowerment: a universal agent-centric measure of control", IEEE CEC) [recall]. *Difference:* CIY is per-action, pairwise-averaged and explicitly interventional. The body is admirably clear that it measures *distinguishability, not learning value*, and that a proper EIG is an open direction (`:15`, `:41`) [read]. *Body says so:* EIG yes; empowerment nowhere in canon (grep "empowerment", "Klyubin": zero) [read]. *Verdict:* **keep, anchor to empowerment.** This is a clean case where naming the neighbour sharpens the ASF object: "CIY is the per-action, pairwise form of the action–outcome information whose capacity is empowerment."

**2.13 Deliberation cost / deliberation threshold** (`der-deliberation-cost.md:14`) [read]. *Standard:* **rational metareasoning** and the *value of computation* (Russell & Wefald 1991, *Do the Right Thing: Studies in Limited Rationality*, MIT Press; Horvitz 1987; Lieder & Griffiths 2020, *BBS*) [recall]. Deliberate while the expected improvement exceeds the cost of the time spent; ASF's version prices that time by the mismatch accumulated at drift rate $\rho$. *Difference:* the drift-priced cost is a specific, useful instantiation. *Body says so:* no; zero hits for Russell, Wefald, Horvitz, metareasoning or value of computation anywhere in canon [read]. *Verdict:* **keep, anchor.** Of all the absences, this one would most surprise an AI reader, because the threshold *is* the value-of-computation stopping rule.

**2.14 Credit assignment boundary** (`disc-credit-assignment-boundary.md:26`) [read]. *Standard:* the credit assignment problem (Minsky 1961, "Steps toward artificial intelligence") [recall]. The segment uses the standard phrase in a heading and cites no source. *Verdict:* **adopted already; add the first-encounter citation** (route (d)).

### Volume 2 (TST) — two measures the software literature already has

**2.19 System coupling and system coherence** (`02-tst-core/src/def-system-coupling.md:12-22`; `def-system-coherence.md:12-24`) [read]. Coupling is defined as $P(\text{change}(m_j)\mid\text{change}(m_i))$ estimated from co-change in git. That is precisely **logical coupling** (or change coupling) from mining software repositories (Gall, Hajek & Jazayeri 1998, "Detection of logical coupling based on product release history", ICSM [web]). Zimmermann et al. made the conditional probability explicit as association-rule *confidence* (Zimmermann, Weißgerber, Diehl & Zeller 2005, "Mining version histories to guide software changes", *IEEE TSE* 31(6)) [recall]. Coherence, the locality of changes within a module, is the change-based form of **cohesion** (Stevens, Myers & Constantine 1974, "Structured design", *IBM Syst. J.* 13(2)) [recall]. *Body says so:* the body calls the conditional probability "a standard quantity" (`:26`) [read] and names no source. "Gall", "Zimmermann", "logical coupling", "Constantine" and "cohesion" have zero hits in the TST bodies [read]. *Verdict:* **adopt "change coupling" and "change cohesion".** The concepts are identical, and the software-engineering reader TST is written for knows them by those names. TST's real additions (commits as interventions, the asymmetric-co-change test for direction) then stand out as additions.

**2.20 Change-expectation baseline** (`02-tst-core/src/der-change-expectation-baseline.md`) [read]. As the brief says: Gott's $\Delta t$ argument (Gott 1993, "Implications of the Copernican principle for our future prospects", *Nature* 363:315–319) [recall] and the Lindy effect are absent from the body. The only trace in the segment is a directory name inside a Working Note (`:74`, `lindy-tooling-sims-2026-03/`) [read]; the owner review counts ten other segments that mention them. *Verdict:* **anchor;** the Jeffreys-prior derivation is a proper derivation of Gott's result and should say so.

## 5. Adjacent things, including about the brief

**On the brief.** It was the right size and it worked. The three verified instances saved me real time, and naming KR2.5 as the narrower target kept me from drifting into process vocabulary. One thing I would add for the next such brief: the most valuable finds (1.1, 1.5, 1.6) were not "a coinage hides a standard name". They were cases where *the standard name would have exposed an error or a collision*. A brief that asks only "what's the standard name?" invites a citation sweep.
