## 1. What Part II is about, in my own words

Part II is a **theory of self-diagnosis for learning agents that keep what they want separate from what they believe.** It has three layers. Each makes a different kind of claim.

1. **A type system for purposeful state.** The agent's state is $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$ with $G_t = (O_t, \Sigma_t)$: beliefs, an objective reachable only through a scalar value functional $V_{O_t}$, and a strategy. The single substantive rule of the type system is *directed separation*: the belief update has no goal argument (`der-directed-separation.md:36`). Everything else in the layer sets up interfaces: the value object $Q_O$ as an interventional query, the continuation conventions C1/C2/C3, and the strategy DAG as the representation for $\Sigma_t$.

2. **A diagnostic calculus over that type system.** The agent can ask two questions with properly typed answers. "Does the world, as I believe it to be, permit my goal?" is $\delta_{\text{sat}}$. "Am I doing as well as I could?" is $\delta_{\text{regret}}$. A 2×2 table routes each cell to a different repair (`def-control-regret.md:38-45`). The orient cascade puts the repairs in order: beliefs first, objective last (`der-orient-cascade.md:33-68`).

3. **Limits on what an agent can learn about its own plan from its own data.** These are no-gos and depth penalties. On-policy data cannot reveal a missing common cause among plan steps (`der-causal-insufficiency-detection.md:17`). Edges behind an unobservable node freeze (`der-observability-dominance.md:14`). Deeper edges calibrate more slowly, by $\prod_{j\lt k}\theta_j$ (`der-observability-dominance.md:54`). A Beta-Bernoulli strategy learner without forgetting eventually fails any persistence threshold (`schema-strategy-persistence.md:42-64`).

The third layer is where Part II says the most about actual agents, and it is the least visible in the outline's framing. The preface's "headline contribution" (`OUTLINE.md:79`) names the diagnostic split, the convention hierarchy, the $G_t$ complexity bound, the graph-structure "uniqueness" argument and the 16/24 survival classification. It names none of the no-gos or depth penalties.

One sentence for what Part II is trying to illuminate, offered as a candidate for KR1.2: *For an agent that learns how its actions work while pursuing a goal, Part II says which of its failures are failures of belief, of plan, or of goal; in what order it can tell them apart; and which of its own blind spots it cannot see from its own experience without deliberately acting off-plan.* I think that sentence is true of the material. It is not written anywhere in the Part.

## 3. Apparatus by apparatus: kind of claim, which agents, instance, refuter

For each piece I give (a) what kind of claim it makes about agents, (b) which agents, (c) a concrete instance, (d) what would show it wrong, and (e) whether the corpus says so. The register letters are F (fact about the formalism), D (description of real agents), N (design or optimality claim).

### 3.1 The lift $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$ and directed separation

**Kind of claim.** Three claims are bundled here, and only the first is labeled.

- **F:** if the update has no goal argument, the factorization holds. This is the exact part (`der-directed-separation.md:146`), and it holds by definition.
- **D:** an architectural classification of real systems. Kalman plus LQR is Class 1, transformer LLMs are Class 3, cortex is Class 2 (`der-directed-separation.md:75-79`). The Class 3 claim for decoder-only attention has a lemma-grade proof in NeurIPS Paper 3 (`der-directed-separation.md:211`). That makes it a fact about the computation graph, and a sound one.
- **N:** "less coupling tracks reality strictly better, all else equal" (`der-directed-separation.md:113`). The text says this is "carried by the bound … never declared as a virtue ranking", but it functions as the normative core of the whole Part.

**Which agents, and a fact the Part could state but does not (my inference).** Every exact Bayesian agent in a POMDP is Class 1. Its belief-state update is the Bayes filter, which depends on the observation model and the action taken, never on the reward function. So directed separation is not just a design option. It is a *property of the Bayes-optimal agent*. Departures from it are departures from exact Bayesian inference, which happen under bounded capacity or through motivated processing. The LQG separation principle (Wonham 1968, already cited at `der-directed-separation.md:192`) is the linear-Gaussian special case. Stating this would answer "about which agents?" in one line: directed separation describes the ideal reasoner exactly and diagnoses bounded ones.

**The normative claim has a live counter-position the Part does not engage (my inference, partly verified).** Under bounded capacity, the best model to keep is often *goal-dependent*: compress toward what matters for the task. Examples are value-equivalent models (Grimm et al. 2020; MuZero), rational inattention (Sims 2003), and task-relevant state abstraction. The corpus has found this tension itself. `form-information-bottleneck.md:50` says the IB objective "is therefore defined relative to a generating policy." A fresh reader then wrote that this is "the very first crack in the armor of Directed Separation, since if optimal epistemic compression depends on planned strategy, the epistemic update is not goal-blind" (`form-information-bottleneck.md:93`). That note was routed to `der-directed-separation` and the cascade. Neither body addresses it (verified: no mention of policy-relative compression in either). Before the "tracks reality better" norm can be stated as true, the theory needs to say *which* reality-tracking metric it means. Goal-neutral predictive accuracy and goal-relevant accuracy per bit give different answers. A strengthening target: show that goal-dependence at *compression time* (which features to keep, a selection-like choice) is compatible with goal-blindness at *processing time*. The segment's own processing-versus-selection distinction (`der-directed-separation.md:16`) may be exactly the tool. If that works, the norm survives with a precise scope.

**Falsifier (D).** Run $\hat\kappa$ (`der-directed-separation.md:97`) on (i) a modular perception-plus-planner stack, which should give $\hat\kappa \approx 0$; (ii) a raw LLM given the same evidence under two task framings, which should give high $\hat\kappa$; and (iii) a W₁-wrapped LLM, which should be low with a residual floor. Hold $M_{\tau^-}$ fixed across framings, per the confound flagged at `der-directed-separation.md:238`. A Class 1 system showing goal-dependent belief content, or a raw LLM showing none on ambiguous evidence, would refute the classification as applied. Relevant existing empirical literature (my knowledge, not checked): the LLM sycophancy studies (e.g. Sharma et al. 2023) are already partial measurements of goal-conditioned belief reports.

**One verified misattribution.** The Related Work row cites Bar-Shalom & Tse 1974 (dual effect) as the "classical control-theoretic name for what AAT recovers as the Class 3 (Coupled) boundary: when the dual effect is present, separation provably fails" (`der-directed-separation.md:193`). As I understand that literature, the dual effect concerns **certainty equivalence**: whether the optimal *control* may ignore estimation quality ([Derpich & Yüksel](https://mast.queensu.ca/~yuksel/MilanSerdarDual.pdf) summarize the theorem as "CE holds iff no dual effect" in the LQ class). Under dual effect the *estimator* is still the cost-blind Bayes filter. What dual control adds is that actions are chosen partly to improve future observations. That is *selection*, which this same segment explicitly permits: "Directed separation is about the **processing** of events, not the **selection** of events" (`der-directed-separation.md:16`, `:56`). So the row contradicts its own segment. The row is marked verification-deferred (`[^cat-2026-05-22]`), so this is the kind of thing the deferred verification was meant to catch. Please check it against the primary source before acting.

### 3.2 Objective functional, value object, convention hierarchy

**Kind of claim.** F throughout, cleanly marked. The objective functional is now a representation theorem under named axioms (Debreu, von Neumann–Morgenstern, Afriat), with excluded agents named: lexicographic and cyclic preferences (`form-objective-functional.md:50-52`). That is a model of how to answer "about which agents": it names the class (coherent agents in the sense of GARP), the boundary, and counterexamples. **I'd recommend using this segment as the template for the others.**

**The convention hierarchy is presented as distinctive, and its prior art is not cited in the body (verified absence; prior-art identity is my knowledge).** C1, $\pi_{\text{cont}} = \pi_{\text{current}}$, is Bertsekas's rollout / $Q^{\pi}$. C2 is model predictive control. C3 is $Q^\ast$. The monotonicity statement is the policy-improvement theorem plus "a supremum over a superset is larger" (`def-value-object.md:95-97`). Bertsekas is cited only in the appendix (`deriv-convention-monotonicity`). The preface calls the hierarchy one of Part II's "distinctive results" (`OUTLINE.md:79`). What is genuinely AAT's is the *use*: the convention fixes the inferential force of a diagnostic. "Unattainable under C1" means locally stuck; "unattainable under C3" means infeasible (`def-value-object.md:111-117`). That is worth stating as the contribution, with the RL/MPC lineage named in the body.

### 3.3 Satisfaction gap and control regret (the 2×2)

**Kind of claim.** F as definitions (exact, correctly), plus an unstated N: an agent that routes repairs by this table does better than one that responds to a single goal-distance signal. The two gaps sum to the total shortfall, $\delta_{\text{sat}} + \delta_{\text{regret}} = V_{O_t}^{\min} - V_O(\pi_{\text{current}})$, an identity a fresh reader derived (`def-satisfaction-gap.md:101`). The split is real but elementary. Its value is entirely in the N claim, which is never stated as a claim.

**Which agents.** Any agent holding a satisficing threshold $V_{O_t}^{\min}$ and able to estimate the best value achievable under its model. Both quantities are evaluated in the agent's *believed* model, not the world (`def-value-object.md:148`). So they are an agent's self-assessments, and the D question is whether real agents compute anything like them.

**Prior art that should be in the body and is not (verified absence; the literature is my knowledge).**

- Cohen & Levesque (1990), "Intention is choice with commitment": an agent drops an intention when it believes the intention is achieved or impossible. That is the satisfaction-gap trigger.
- Bratman's planning theory and the BDI line (Rao & Georgeff): plan revision before intention revision.
- The goal-reasoning and goal-driven-autonomy literature in AI planning (Aha, Molineaux, Muñoz-Avila; Cox's MIDCA): explicitly about when to change goals rather than plans.
- Simon's satisficing and Selten's aspiration adaptation, for $V_{O_t}^{\min}$.

A `grep` of the AAT segments for Levesque, Bratman, Georgeff, Kinny and Selten returns nothing. The May prior-art memos mention BDI once, in passing (`ref/prior-art-analysis/05-directed-separation.md:36`). This is the field where Part II's diagnostic split would be judged, and it is absent.

**Falsifier (N).** Take a family of tasks with a mix of infeasible goals and fixable plans: Tileworld-style worlds, software-repair benchmarks, or vivarium. Compare agents that (i) route by the 2×2, (ii) respond to total shortfall only, and (iii) use standard RL. The split earns its "headline" status if (i) wastes less effort on optimally-failing plans and abandons infeasible goals sooner without abandoning feasible ones. If (i) does not beat (ii), the split is notation.

### 3.4 Causal hierarchy requirement and loop interventional access

**Kind of claim.** `der-causal-hierarchy-requirement` is F. $Q_O$ is defined with $do(\cdot)$, so evaluating it needs Level-2 information (`der-causal-hierarchy-requirement.md:43`). `der-loop-interventional-access` claims, as exact, "the availability of the interventional-character channel" (`der-loop-interventional-access.md:45`). It then gates identification on (C1)–(C3) and says (C2) fails "generic[ally] for any agent whose action depends on internal state, plans, or memory that also bear on the outcome" (`:41`).

**A strengthening I think is available (my inference; please verify).** Adjusting for the parents of the treatment is always a valid backdoor adjustment (Pearl, parent adjustment). An agent's action has as parents exactly its own decision inputs (its internal state, its history, its randomization), and those are the one variable set an agent can in principle condition on. So $P(o \mid do(a), \mathrm{pa}(a)) = P(o \mid a, \mathrm{pa}(a))$, given positivity. Loop data therefore **identifies** the agent's interventional effects *conditional on its own decision state*, by construction. Confounding returns precisely when the learner conditions on *less* than what drove the action. Examples: it learns a marginal action-outcome association; it cannot introspect a cause of its own choice (the "already tired" case at `causal-access-intro.md:19`); or a separate analyst lacks the agent's internal state. Ortega & Braun (2008/2010) is the closest antecedent for "own actions as interventions in adaptive control". `06-causal-plan-graphs.md` calls it "the closest existing ancestor", and the PDF is in `ref/ortega_adaptive_agent_2008.pdf`, yet the segment does not cite it (verified: zero hits in `der-loop-interventional-access.md`).

If this holds, the exact claim is stronger and much clearer as a claim about agents: *a learning agent's own experience identifies the effects of its actions exactly when it conditions on everything its choice depended on.* Superstition and confounded learning then become failures of self-access, which is a testable description. Agents that discard or cannot read their own decision context should show confounded action-value estimates. Agents that log their own propensities should not, which is standard in off-policy evaluation.

**Small verified error.** "An RL agent needs $Q(s, a) = \mathbb{E}[R \mid s, do(a)]$, not $\mathbb{E}[R \mid s, A=a]$ (the latter includes selection bias from the agent's own policy)" (`der-causal-hierarchy-requirement.md:52`). In a fully observed MDP the policy depends only on $s$, so the two are equal. The selection bias needs partial observability or an unobserved common cause of action and outcome. This is a correct claim scoped too broadly.

### 3.5 Explicit-strategy condition

`norm-explicit-strategy-condition` is honestly labeled normative (`:39`). Its inequality, plan if $C_{\text{plan}} + C_{\text{maintain}} \lt C_{\text{explore}} + C_{\text{repair}}$, says "do the cheaper thing when outcomes are equivalent." That is close to tautological, and the segment admits the precondition usually fails (`:46`). The substantive question, when model-based control beats model-free, has a large empirical and theoretical literature. Examples (my knowledge; none cited in Part II per `grep`): uncertainty-based arbitration between the two systems (Daw, Niv & Dayan 2005), value of computation (Russell & Wefald 1991), resource-rational metareasoning (Lieder & Griffiths 2020). That literature is where a D claim and a falsifier would come from: humans shift toward planning when model-free estimates are uncertain and planning is cheap.

### 3.6 The strategy DAG (Joseph's specimen)

**What it says universally (verified).** Strip the framing and `deriv-graph-structure-uniqueness` shows this. *If* an agent represents its plan as time-indexed propositions with causal edges and independent per-step noise, *then* that representation is a causal Bayesian network (`deriv-graph-structure-uniqueness.md:111-121`). The acyclicity half is the standard time-unrolling argument (`:131-146`). The Markov half is the Causal Markov Condition for structural equation models with independent noise (`:101-105`). Both are correct and textbook. The "Cox parallel" the segment draws is explicitly sufficiency-only (`:15-17`). So, as a universal statement about agents, the strategy DAG says almost nothing beyond "a causal plan with independent noise is a causal Bayes net." That is the answer to Joseph's question, and it is fine as long as it is stated as such.

**The title says what the body denies (verified).** The segment is named `deriv-graph-structure-uniqueness` and the outline calls it "the graph-structure uniqueness argument" (`OUTLINE.md:79`). The body says: "this result is sufficient only … no one has shown a non-DAG structure cannot satisfy them" (`deriv-graph-structure-uniqueness.md:15`). A name that asserts what the content disclaims is a KR2.5 item.

**Is it one model among several? Yes, and the body knows it but does not map the neighbours (verified that they are absent from bodies).** AND/OR graphs with failure probabilities (Ghosh et al. 2021; AO*), probabilistic plan graphs with correlation (Bryce & Smith 2006), fault trees and reliability block diagrams with common-cause-failure models (Vaurio 2002, Fleming), BURIDAN-style probabilistic planning, HTN planning, and goal models in requirements engineering (KAOS/i*). All are named in `ref/prior-art-analysis/06-causal-plan-graphs.md` §1. **None reaches a segment body** (`grep` for Bryce, Vaurio, Fleming, Ghosh, Kushmerick, Barnett: zero hits in `01-aat-core/src/`). The memo itself is candid that AAT invents none of these pieces (`06-causal-plan-graphs.md` §3, "Where AAT does *not* claim novelty"). The single-parameter AND/OR edge is noisy-OR at OR nodes and a product (noisy-AND) at AND nodes (`def-strategy-dag.md:60`). The body says noisy-OR "everywhere" was rejected, which is true but could mislead a reader into thinking noisy-OR is absent.

**A mathematical error, contradicted by the corpus's own worked example (verified).** The body states, three times, that AND/OR status propagation is correct **iff** the DAG is causally sufficient:

- "The AND/OR status propagation computes correct probabilities **if and only if** the strategy DAG is causally sufficient" (`def-strategy-dag.md:82`)
- "When exogenous noise terms are independent, edge outcomes are conditionally independent given parents, and the AND/OR formulas compute correct probabilities" (`deriv-graph-structure-uniqueness.md:109`)
- "The validity of the Markov factorization and the validity of the independence model are the *same condition*" (same line)

This is false. Forward propagation multiplies the parents' *marginal* statuses $s_i$ (`def-strategy-dag.md:60`). That is exact only when a node's parents are marginally independent, which an explicit shared ancestor already breaks. Minimal counterexample (my construction): leaf $C$ with $p_C = 0.5$; $A = \text{AND}(C)$ and $B = \text{AND}(C)$, each with edge credence 1; root $R = \text{AND}(A, B)$ with edge credences 1. The DAG is causally sufficient, since $C$ is a node. The true value is $P(R) = 0.5$, but propagation gives $0.25$. The corpus has this exact case. `example-L1.md:56` says, of "naive L1" with the common cause as an explicit parent: "**Same answer as L0.** The OR-propagation treats $B_1$ and $B_2$ as marginally independent, but they are correlated through their shared parent $C$." The repair the corpus prescribes, factoring the common cause *above* the correlation it creates (`def-strategy-dag.md:106`), works because it makes the graph singly connected at that point. The true condition is that the graph must be a **polytree** (no node has two parents with a common ancestor), or the propagation must be replaced by exact inference (junction tree). `disc-credit-assignment-boundary.md:134` already names "exact BP on tree or polytree cases."

Consequence: the L0 → L1 → L1′ → L2 Correlation Hierarchy is about *latent* common causes, but the same overconfidence arises from *explicit* shared prerequisites in any plan with reconvergent structure. Real plans are full of reconvergent structure (one server under two services). So the "practical sweet spot" claim (`def-strategy-dag.md:106`) needs the polytree condition stated alongside it. This is a KR2.1/KR2.2 item. It is also a strengthen-first opportunity: the restructuring the corpus already prescribes is, as far as I can tell, exactly a polytree-ification, and saying so turns an error into a cleaner theorem.

**What the strategy DAG says about real agents that could be tested.** These are the D claims hiding in it, all inherited by *any* agent that estimates plan success by combining per-step estimates under independence:

1. **Sign rule.** Unmodeled positive covariance makes OR-redundancy look better than it is and AND-chains look worse (`def-strategy-dag.md:84-91`). This is exact algebra. As a description of organizations it is the known common-cause-failure phenomenon in reliability engineering. As a description of *humans* it collides with the conjunction/disjunction literature: people overestimate conjunctive and underestimate disjunctive probabilities relative to the product rule (Bar-Hillel 1973; Tversky & Kahneman 1974; my knowledge). So whether humans plan as L0 propagators is empirically answerable, and the existing evidence says not straightforwardly.
2. **Evidence starvation.** Deeper edges are tested only when upstream steps succeed, so their effective correction rate is attenuated by $\prod_{j\lt k}\theta_j$ (`der-observability-dominance.md:54`). This is derived for two edges and conjectured for depth $d$. Falsifier: learning-curve data for deep versus shallow plans in RL or human sequential tasks.
3. **Observability dominance.** Agents should prefer weak-but-visible over strong-but-blind paths, and unobservable regions are self-locking (`der-observability-dominance.md:14-16`). This is a design claim (N) with a clear test.

**Bottom line for Joseph's question.** The strategy DAG is *one representation among several* for an agent that keeps an explicit causal plan: a causal Bayes net over time-indexed plan propositions with noisy-AND/OR local models. It is not a claim that agents have DAGs. Its theorems are of three kinds. Some are facts about that representation (acyclicity, Markov, propagation cost, which is exact only on polytrees). Some are facts about *any* agent that combines step estimates under independence (the sign rule, evidence starvation). Some are design recommendations (L1 augmentation, observability investment, exploration to detect common causes). None of that sorting is written down, and the outline presents the first kind as the headline.

### 3.7 Strategy dynamics: the no-gos and depth penalties

**Causal-insufficiency no-go** (`der-causal-insufficiency-detection.md`). The math is correct and modest. Under short-circuit OR execution the agent observes $P(A_1)$ and $P(A_2 \mid \neg A_1)$, never the joint, so any joint matching those two is indistinguishable (`:45-49`). **My reading** is that this is a *censoring* (missing-not-at-random) identifiability fact, the "selective labels" problem (Lakkaraju et al. 2017) of the ML literature, more than a Causal Hierarchy Theorem instance. The escape is uncensored joint observation, and the covariance it enables (`:83`) is an associational statistic of the uncensored joint. The CHT framing is not wrong, since the L0/L1 question can be posed as $P(A_2 \mid do(\neg A_1))$ versus $P(A_2 \mid \neg A_1)$ (`:49`). But it hides the simpler mechanism, which the segment itself names as "the structural source" (`:177`). As a claim about agents it is real and useful: an organization that runs backup plan B only when plan A fails can never learn from its own records that A and B share a failure cause. Falsifier: none needed for the math. As a description, it predicts that such organizations systematically over-trust redundancy until a joint failure. That is checkable in incident post-mortem data.

**Forgetting prerequisite and hard ceiling** (`schema-strategy-persistence.md:42-64`). As a D claim it says that a strategy learner accumulating evidence without discounting eventually fails any persistence threshold in a drifting world. That is true and long known for stationary estimators under drift (Ljung, cited at `:133`). Humans are known to adjust their learning rate to volatility (Behrens et al. 2007; my knowledge, not cited), which is a direct empirical instance worth citing. The "$\lambda$-independent structural ceiling at $\rho_\Sigma = R_\Sigma/2$" (`:19`, `:62`) comes, as I read it, from $\alpha_\Sigma^{\text{ss}} = (1-\lambda)/(2-\lambda) \le 1/2$ for every $\lambda$ in this one update family. It is a ceiling of Beta-Bernoulli with exponential forgetting, not of strategic persistence: a change-point learner would not have it. F72 (INTENT KR2.3) is already queued on this segment, so I note it for F72's verifier rather than adjudicate it.

### 3.8 The orient cascade

**Kind of claim.** Two halves, as §2 shows. The F half is "beliefs before anything that reads beliefs", given directed separation. The N half is "objective revision last." There is no D claim in the body beyond the Boyd hedge.

**Which agents.** The text scopes the cascade to causally-disciplined learning agents (`OUTLINE.md:87`; `impl-orient-cascade.md:34`). **The N half has a 35-year-old empirical literature, uncited (my knowledge; verified absent by `grep`).** Kinny & Georgeff (1991), "Commitment and effectiveness of situated agents", tested "bold" agents (rarely reconsider intentions) against "cautious" ones in Tileworld. **Falsifier (N).** In a world with a known mix of feasible and infeasible goals and a known drift rate, compare cascade-ordered agents against objective-first and joint-optimization agents. Use joint POMDP planning as the "all-at-once" baseline, which `impl-orient-cascade.md:30` says the exactness "excludes". Measure value achieved and wasted effort. The claim that the ordering excludes all-at-once resolution is a claim about dependency, not performance. ### 7.1 Does the parent-adjustment strengthening (§3.4) survive the corpus's own gates?

**What I read (verified).**

- `def-value-object.md:47-53`: the value object's gate. It reads (C1) positivity; (C2) sequential ignorability, "the action $a_t$ is $d$-separated from the outcome $o_{t+1}$ given history"; (C3) known action-mechanism.
- `der-loop-interventional-access.md:35-41`: the same triple, with the claim that (C2) fails "generic[ally] for any agent whose action depends on internal state, plans, or memory that also bear on the outcome."
- `scope-edge-update-causal-validity.md:18-40`, read whole.

**First, a verified finding I had missed.** The two gates share labels but not content. In `def-value-object` and `der-loop-interventional-access`, (C1)/(C2)/(C3) mean positivity, sequential ignorability and known mechanism. In `scope-edge-update-causal-validity`, (C1)/(C2)/(C3) mean, respectively:

- "the parent is an action leaf under the agent's control" (`:34`);
- "the outcome is attributable" (`:36`);
- "execution conditions vary" (`:38`).

Three segments one chapter apart use "(C1)–(C3)" for different triples. A reader following `#der-loop-interventional-access`'s cross-reference into the scope segment will apply the wrong conditions. This is a small, real KR2.2 item.

**The mathematics (my inference; standard results).** Let $D_t$ be everything the agent's action is a function of, apart from randomization $U_t$ that is independent of the environment. So $a_t = \pi(D_t, U_t)$. Then $D_t \supseteq \mathrm{pa}(a_t) \setminus \{U_t\}$, and $D_t$ contains no descendant of $a_t$. For any conditioning set $Z$ that determines $D_t$ and contains no descendant of $a_t$, every back-door path from $a_t$ to $o_{t+1}$ enters $a_t$ through $D_t$ (since $U_t$ has no other children in the environment) and is blocked once $D_t$ is fixed. So the back-door criterion holds and

$$P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a_t = a), Z) = P(o_{t+1} \mid a_t = a, Z) \quad \text{wherever } P(a_t = a \mid Z) \gt 0.$$

This is Pearl's adjustment for direct causes, conditioned rather than averaged. Read against the gates, it settles three things.

1. **Against `def-value-object`'s (C2), taken literally, it is an automatic consequence, not an extra assumption.** (C2) is stated "given history." For any agent whose decision state $D_t$ is a function of its recorded history $H_t$ (true of every software agent whose state is computed from its inputs), conditioning on $H_t$ determines $D_t$, so (C2) holds by construction. So `der-loop-interventional-access.md:41`'s "fails generically" is correct only for a learner that conditions on *less* than $H_t$. The segment's own conclusion there, that on-policy data "converges to $\mathbb E[o \mid A = a]$", is the marginal-learning case. The honest correction is to name the conditioning set.

2. **The value object conditions on $M_t$, not on $D_t$, and that is where the real condition lives.** $Q_O$ queries $P(o \mid do(a), M_t)$ (`def-value-object.md:35`). But the action is $a_t = \pi(M_t, G_t)$ (`form-complete-agent-state.md:38`), and under directed separation $G_t$ is updated by $f_G(G_{\tau^-}, M_{\tau^+}, e_\tau)$ (`der-directed-separation.md:38`). That update takes the event *directly*. So $G_t$ can carry information about the environment that is not in $M_t$, and when it does, it is a parent of $a_t$ correlated with the outcome given $M_t$. Identification given $M_t$ therefore holds when $G_t \perp U_{\text{env}} \mid M_t$, i.e. when $M_t$ is predictively sufficient for the history (`#def-model-sufficiency`) and the goal carries no environment information beyond it. `def-value-object.md:50` says directed separation "delivers (C2) on the epistemic-processing leg." The condition it needs is a different one: no outcome-relevant information reaching the policy through $G_t$ or memory that bypasses $M_t$.

3. **Positivity is the price, and the strengthening makes it sharper, not easier.** If the policy is deterministic given $D_t$, then $P(a \mid D_t) \in \{0, 1\}$, so conditioning on the full decision state *destroys* positivity for every action not taken. Ignorability and overlap pull in opposite directions; this is the standard overlap/ignorability tension. The only way to have both at the full decision state is randomization independent of the environment ($\epsilon$-greedy, Thompson sampling, logged propensities). Without it, estimates for untaken actions come from a model's smoothness across decision states. That is extrapolation, not identification.

**The honest statement, replacing my §3.4 sentence:**

> *A learning agent's own action-outcome data identifies the effect of an action, conditional on the agent's decision state, exactly when (i) the learner conditions on everything the action depended on, apart from randomization independent of the environment, and (ii) that randomization gives the action positive probability in that state. It fails in three named ways:*
> - *the learner conditions on a compression that drops outcome-relevant decision inputs (an insufficient $M_t$, or goal or memory content carrying world information);*
> - *a cause of the action is not recorded or introspectable and also affects the outcome;*
> - *the policy is deterministic in the decision state, so untaken actions are extrapolated, not identified.*

**The three cases you named, mapped onto this:**

- **Positivity** is (ii). It explains why exploration is not a heuristic here: it is the *only* source of identification at the decision-state level. This connects directly to `der-causal-insufficiency-detection`'s escape routes.
- **The cause that cannot be introspected** ("the agent was already tired", `causal-access-intro.md:19`) is the first part of failure mode 2. Fatigue is a parent of $a_t$; if it is absent from $D_t$ *as recorded* and it affects the outcome, there is an open back-door path. This gives a crisp description of real agents: confounded self-learning is a failure of *self-access*, not of reasoning. It applies with force to LLM agents, whose action-determining latents are not exposed to any learner, including themselves.
- **The analyst without internal state** is the case where $Z$ lacks $D_t$. Identification is lost unless the agent *logs* its decision inputs or propensities, which is exactly what off-policy evaluation requires in practice.

**Where the strengthening does not reach (verified against the scope segment).** `scope-edge-update-causal-validity`'s (C2), "attributable", concerns which of several concurrent parent edges caused $j$ (`:36`). Parent adjustment identifies the *joint* interventional effect of everything the agent did. Splitting it per edge is a parameterization question (the AND/OR model), not an identification question, so that condition stands. Its (C3), "execution conditions vary" (`:38`), is shown by this analysis to mix two things. Selection bias from executing $i$ only under favorable conditions is removed by conditioning on those conditions *if they are in $D_t$*; "vary" is then positivity. If they are not in $D_t$ (gut feel), it is failure mode 2. So that (C3) should split into an ignorability clause and a positivity clause.

**Status.** The mathematics is standard (back-door criterion; overlap). The mapping to the corpus is my reading of the cited lines. I have not checked whether `#def-model-sufficiency`'s formal definition is exactly the sufficiency needed in point 2.

### 7.2 Is goal-dependent compression compatible with goal-blind processing? (§3.1)

**Short answer.** Yes for one kind of goal-dependence, no for another. The corpus's measure $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ cannot tell them apart, and the boundary between them is already drawn twice in the corpus, inconsistently.

**The inconsistency (verified).** `der-directed-separation.md:54` forbids "goal-dependent attention thresholds … baked into $f_M$." Two lines later (`:56`), "goal-directed sensing, attention allocation, query selection" are listed as *selection*, which is permitted. `disc-partial-coupling-pathways.md:144` classifies "Goal-directed attention" as "(selection — pre-$f_M$) … formally allowed." The same typology counts a goal argument in **P1 featurization**, $x = \phi(e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$, as coupling (`:35`, `:40`). So "attention allocation" is permitted and "goal-dependent featurization" is coupling, and nothing says where one ends and the other begins.

**A proposed boundary, with what is provable about each side (my inference).** Factor the belief update into an encoding step and an updating step: $z = \phi_G(e_\tau)$, chosen from a capacity-limited family $\Phi$, then $M_{\tau^+} = g(M_{\tau^-}, z, G)$. Let $W$ denote the outcome-relevant world. Assume the goal carries no world information beyond the prior state and the realized action and event: $G \perp W \mid (M_{\tau^-}, a, e_\tau)$. (This is the assumption that conditioning on $M_{\tau^-}$ in $\kappa$'s definition, `der-directed-separation.md:87`, is meant to secure.) Two kinds of goal-dependence then separate.

- **Allocation coupling.** $\phi$ depends on $G$, but $g$ is the Bayes posterior given $(M_{\tau^-}, z)$ *and the identity of the encoder used*: $M_{\tau^+} = P(W \mid M_{\tau^-}, \phi_G(e_\tau), \phi_G)$.
  - **(A) Calibration is preserved.** For every goal, $M_{\tau^+}$ is a conditional distribution of $W$ given information the agent holds, so by the tower property $P(W \in A \mid M_{\tau^+}) = M_{\tau^+}(A)$. The beliefs are calibrated about what was encoded and revert to the prior on what was not. *There is no systematic goal-directed displacement in expectation*: under a well-specified prior the posterior is a martingale, whatever encoding the goal chose.
- **Distortion coupling.** $g$ itself depends on $G$ given $(M_{\tau^-}, z)$.
  - **(B) Distortion strictly costs, for every goal.** Under the assumption above, the Bayes posterior given $(M_{\tau^-}, z)$ uniquely minimizes expected loss among all functions of $(M_{\tau^-}, z, G)$ under any strictly proper scoring rule. Any update that differs from it on a set of positive probability has strictly higher expected loss. This is the standard optimality of the Bayes posterior under proper scores, plus the fact that conditioning on an irrelevant variable cannot help.
- **(C) Allocation is a trade-off, not a defect.** At equal capacity, the goal-blind encoder (IB toward full predictive relevance, `#form-information-bottleneck`) is optimal for goal-neutral predictive accuracy by construction. A goal-chosen encoder is optimal for the goal's decision value (rational inattention, Sims 2003; value-equivalent models, Grimm et al. 2020). Neither dominates.

**The scoped norm (draft, my wording).**

> *Holding encoding capacity fixed, and assuming the goal carries no world information beyond the agent's prior state: (i) any goal-dependence in how the agent updates on what it has encoded strictly worsens its accuracy about the world under every proper scoring rule, for every goal; (ii) goal-dependence in what the agent encodes preserves calibration, improves accuracy on goal-relevant features at the expense of others, and is optimal for the goal at bounded capacity.*

"Less coupling tracks reality strictly better" is then true without qualification for distortion coupling. For allocation coupling it is true only under the goal-neutral metric.

**Where the reconciliation breaks. There are four places, and the last is the most interesting.**

1. **$\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ does not separate the two (verified from its definition).** $I(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid e_\tau, M_{\tau^-})$ (`der-directed-separation.md:85`) is positive for allocation coupling too, since different goals encode the same event differently. So the Class 1/2/3 classification currently files a calibrated rational-attention agent in the same class as a motivated reasoner. One consequence I have not verified: the $\kappa \cdot \mathcal A$ bias bound (`#deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound`, and NeurIPS Paper 3) measures *displacement* between goal-conditioned posteriors. Allocation coupling produces such displacement sample by sample with no calibration loss, so the bound may count benign allocation as "hallucination". Someone should check what those bounds actually measure.
2. **Unless the update is selection-aware, allocation becomes distortion.** (A) needs the update to condition on *which* encoder was used. An agent that attends only to confirming features and then updates as if they were an unselected sample is miscalibrated. That is confirmation bias by selective attention. Humans, and plausibly LLMs, generally do not condition on their own attention policy, so for them the benign case may be rare in practice. This is an empirical question with a clear test.
3. **Goals that carry information.** If a goal was set *because of* facts the agent's model lacks (a commander's objective encoding intelligence the field unit does not have), then $G \not\perp W \mid M_{\tau^-}$, and using $G$ in processing can legitimately improve accuracy. The norm assumes the goal is not a carrier of world information. That assumption should be stated, not left implicit.
4. **The goal hides the evidence against itself.** Allocation coupling keeps beliefs calibrated but makes the *information set* depend on the current goal. Features the goal treats as irrelevant are not encoded, and their beliefs stay at the prior. This is `#der-observability-dominance`'s frozen-edge mechanism, chosen rather than imposed (an encoder-level $\sigma_v = 0$), and it inherits that segment's self-locking property: the agent "cannot learn, and cannot recognize that it cannot learn" (`der-observability-dominance.md:16`). It bites exactly at the cascade's step 5a, "check whether $M_t$ correction changes the feasibility assessment" (`der-orient-cascade.md:60`). The evidence that would show the goal infeasible or ill-chosen is precisely what a goal-allocated encoder is least likely to keep. The structure mirrors the on-policy no-go, where the agent's *actions* censor joint outcomes (`der-causal-insufficiency-detection.md:177`); here its *attention* censors goal-irrelevant features. The remedy has the same shape: a goal-independent reserve of encoding capacity, the attention-level analog of $\epsilon$-exploration, without which objective revision is starved. That is a design claim (register N) with an obvious test: agents with and without such a reserve, in worlds where the goal becomes infeasible through a change in a goal-irrelevant feature.

**What this does to the IB note at `form-information-bottleneck.md:93`.** Policy-relative compression is allocation coupling. Fixing a default $\pi_{\text{cont}}$ to build $M_t$, the resolution recorded there, makes the allocation goal-independent at runtime. That is one legitimate choice: it buys goal-neutral calibration and an intact step 5a, and pays in goal-relevant efficiency. It is not the only legitimate choice. Allocation that follows the goal plus a reserve is the other.

**One link back to the corpus (verified).** `#deriv-decomposition-uniqueness` (summarized at `form-complete-agent-state.md:50`) shows that a frozen purposeful coordinate has the information-flow signature of a belief. Goal-dependence compiled into $\phi$ at design time for a single fixed goal is exactly such a frozen coordinate. $\kappa$ cannot see it, because $H(G \mid \cdot) = 0$, which is the denominator problem flagged at `der-directed-separation.md:237`. So design-time goal-dependent compression is formally invisible to directed separation. Only *runtime* goal-varying allocation registers. This supports splitting the norm as above rather than tightening $\kappa$.

**Status.** (A) and (B) are standard results, applied under an independence assumption I have stated explicitly. (C) follows the cited literature. The four break-points are my analysis; point 4 is the one I would most want a second reader on.

### 7.3 Bar-Shalom & Tse 1974 against the primary

**What I could reach.** The 1974 paper is not in `ref/`; `ref/converted/` holds only `bareinboim-2022-pearl-hierarchy`. I found no free full text. I obtained the **primary abstract** from the DOI record ([10.1109/tac.1974.1100635](https://doi.org/10.1109/tac.1974.1100635), IEEE TAC 19(5):494–500). For the formal definitions I relied on **Derpich & Yüksel's reproduction of them** ([IEEE TAC 68(2), 2023](https://doi.org/10.1109/tac.2022.3151189); author PDF [here](https://serdar-yuksel.github.io/MilanSerdarDual.pdf)), which says it reproduces the 1974 definitions in its Section II. So: primary abstract verified, primary definitions seen through a careful secondary source, full primary text not read.

**The primary abstract, verbatim:** "In this paper the various policies in fixed end-time stochastic control are discussed first. The emphasis is on the difference between the feedback and closed-loop policies. It is shown how the closed-loop policy has the important property that it can be actively adaptive, while the feedback policy can only be passively adaptive. The feature of being actively adaptive is possible when the control has a dual effect, i.e., in addition to its effect on the state it affects the state uncertainty. The intimate connection between the neutrality (lack of dual effect) and certainty equivalence properties for a class of problems is proved. This new result is then used to widen the class of problems for which it was previously known that the certainty equivalence property holds."

**The definitions, per Derpich & Yüksel:** no dual effect "is said to hold when the updated conditional distribution of the plant state given the plant output measurements does not depend on past control actions." Separation "is said to hold when an optimal control exists in a subset of admissible policies where the control depends on the information only through the conditional expectation of the state given the information available."

**Verdict: the misattribution holds, and is slightly worse than I said.**

- The dual effect is a property of how *realized past actions* affect the state estimate's uncertainty. In AAT's terms the actions are part of the realized event history, so an estimator with dual effect is still goal-blind conditional on the realized event. That is precisely what `der-directed-separation.md:16` and `:56` permit: "goal-blindness *conditional on the realized event* … about the **processing** of events, not the **selection** of events."
- "Separation" in this literature is a property of the *controller* (it uses the information only through the conditional expectation). AAT's directed separation is a property of the *estimator* (it takes no goal argument). These are different properties.
- So the row at `der-directed-separation.md:193` ("when the dual effect is present, separation provably fails … the classical control-theoretic name for what AAT recovers as the Class 3 (Coupled) boundary") equates the two senses of "separation." Under dual effect the controller should probe, so certainty equivalence fails; the estimator remains a cost-blind Bayes filter. Dual control is, if anything, a canonical *Class 1* agent with goal-directed *selection*.

**A related correction the primary also supports (my reading).** The adjacent Wonham/Witsenhausen row (`der-directed-separation.md:192`) presents the Separation Principle as "the classical mathematical anchor for AAT's Class 1." What the Separation Principle proves is that the *factored controller is optimal*: control through the conditional mean loses nothing in the LQG class. The estimator's goal-blindness is not what that theorem supplies. It holds for any Bayes filter, with or without the principle. So the honest anchor for Class 1 is "every exact Bayesian filter is goal-blind" (§3.1). The Separation Principle is a further, *optimality* result about when the factored architecture costs nothing. AAT could use that as a real normative anchor: it is the one place the classical literature proves the separated architecture optimal, and Bar-Shalom & Tse plus Derpich & Yüksel map exactly where that optimality stops. That is a stronger use of these papers than the current rows make.

**Also worth carrying.** Derpich & Yüksel show Bar-Shalom & Tse's proof that certainty equivalence implies no dual effect is wrong, and give a counterexample. If a future row cites the 1974 equivalence, it should cite the 2023 correction too.

The abstract and the 2023 reproduction both point the same way, so I'd hold this at "verified against the primary abstract and a faithful secondary reproduction of the definitions."
