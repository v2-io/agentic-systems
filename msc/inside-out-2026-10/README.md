## Joseph's governing words (2026-10-30, verbatim)

**The goal:**

> Goal set: Complete "in-place" refactor of ASF-- meta, all artifacts, and theory itself.

**The frame:**

> Under today's regime, all gates are lifted-- especially any that gate on *me*. From your perspective at least-- task agents etc. can have whatever gates you choose. This is important so I will continue to reiterate it and I recommend you do also: This effort is to *serve TRUTH* -- not me, not "task-completion" or tidyness, not accomplishing a plan, not what sounds good or looks good--- TRUTH about agency. Not procedure, not methodology, not pedantry, not gates and authorities or egos and provinances, not novelty or conservative wisdom. TRUTH is only served if it is discovered, surfaced, truthified, truthfully synthesized and crossed to the right applications, AND effectively communicated anew, with proper epistemology and real humility fully intact. Truth about past process, fact checking something some agent said about something that's not even in the canon -- where the fact-check will not change what gets done or written either way -- is in line with this overall picture but not where our love of truth should be driving us today. Thoughts about what could help distill and clarify truth more effectively is. Thoughts about this as a system in and of itself-- a system of agentic truthification about agentic theory, is wholely worthy. We are to create something true, wise, strong, and beautiful-- inasmuch as strong, wise, and beautiful are the servants (along with any parts of the things listed above) of TRUTH.

**What is lost, and must be found:**

> For me, one of the things that happens over and over is the loss of clarity as agents fall into existing ASF terminology that was essentially named arbitrarily by agents as they tried things out-- sometimes not realizing they would be better to adopt standard academic terms even if from a different field where the concepts are better known. More importantly was the *loss of clarity on what is being theorized* -- a lot of it is there, it's just not part of the overall arc and narrative of the theory. For example, there is a lot of work on Strategic DAG, including no-goes and theorems. It is unclear (to me at least) what it's saying universally about agents (and, if so, what classification of agents and what things fall under that classification that can be tested empirically), what is one of several possible models-- and if so, how does it map to concrete examples, what is being theorized as *optimal* for an agent with the abililty to do so, etc. etc. etc.--- the whole "bigger picture." Don't get caught up on the strategy-dag thing-- that's just a somewhat recent example where I realized "I don't even know what this theory is *trying* to illuminate..."

**On the process itself:**

> Agents *love* to leave breadcrumbs and history everywhere. It feels productive and it feels like it's a safe thing to do that doesn't do harm. It does. That's what changelog is for. If your own process here isn't more disciplined (at *least*) and thoughtful as the normal canon (which forbids finished work-items from being in the segments, for example, and forbids unnecessary breadcrumbs except in changelog, etc.) -- you will drown before ever making a dent toward the real goal.
