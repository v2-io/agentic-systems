## 5. What the tool got wrong, or made hard

- **A recheck's `--why` cannot be amended.** I needed to amend twice. In A3's `--why` I repeated the pilot's figure that one Part I segment carries a formal citation command, but seven do at `cfc7b529`. In the LEXICON item's `--why` I called step 3 open, and it is answered in canon. The only remedy was to say so here. Something like `vsect amend <event> --why` that appends a correcting event would keep the record honest without editing events.
- **A held item cannot be re-judged.** It can only be closed as done, moot or superseded. When the coordinator later agreed a cross-lane survivor for CL-1, I could not record `duplicate --of`, the outcome that keeps the link, and had to close it as `superseded` with the survivor named in prose. `close` could take `duplicate --of` too.
- **Verbatim imports break link checks when their tracker is deleted** (section 4). Rendering the import's links as plain text, or fencing the verbatim block so check-links skips it, would fix this at the source. Then the ignore line can go.
- **Closed imported items stay in `items/imported/`.** They look the same as unjudged ones to `grep`. To tell which of my source files' items were still open I had to use `vsect open`, not the directory. Moving closed items out, or adding a `closed:` line to the file, would make the directory honest.
- **Owner checking.** `--owner I.2` was refused because I.2 is met and has left INTENT. That is fair, but a met KR's residue has nowhere obvious to go. I used I.6 for the md-press `\lt` gap, since a tool check is what lets that prose rule leave the always-loaded surface. Free text ("no KR: monograph build") is accepted silently. That served to make the gap visible, but it means owners can drift from INTENT unnoticed.
- **The outcome words fit well.** `superseded` was the right word thirteen times, mostly for briefs and items that a KR in INTENT now restates. I used `superseded` rather than `holds` when a KR fully restates the item, and `holds` with that KR as owner when the item names specific work inside a KR.

One process failure was mine, for the record. I ran `git commit --amend --only` to fix my own commit's message. Another lane had committed in between, so the amend rewrote that lane's commit (t2-todo, `3e51b2f5`). I restored its message, tree and author at once, as `e60e5d3b`, and told the coordinator. The lead has since put "no --amend in the shared tree" into agents.sop.md (`73ac4e7b`).
