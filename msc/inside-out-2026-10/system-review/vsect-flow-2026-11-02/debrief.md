# Debrief: what vsect's record says about the sprint, and what to change

You asked what the kinds are now, how things flow, what measures of signal against noise would hold up, what has drifted, how cruft gets retired, what questions nobody is asking, and whether vsect could draw the whole thing as a state machine. I read the design, the tool, the sprint's accounts and the trail itself, and computed everything below from the events. The lead and I did this independently; the lead has not shown me its own reading.

**The record is sound; the files around it drifted at two seams.** Every number the dashboard prints reproduces from the events. But 189 imported items are closed in the record and still say "not yet re-checked" in their files, because the re-check lanes committed the event files vsect wrote and not the item files it rewrote; `vsect check` could not see that because it never compares a file's `closed:` line to the trail. And 17 of the 18 items closed "by the commit that did it" cite commits that no longer exist on any branch: they were worktree commits later rebased, and `close` checks only that a commit exists locally. Both are a day's tool work (`01-flows.md` §4, `06-recommendations.md` §1–2).

**The clearest signal in the store is who did the checking.** When an integrator recorded a re-derivation of its own landing, it confirmed every time: 116 confirmed, none broken, none narrowed. When a different mind re-derived the same kind of thing: 88 confirmed, 22 broken, 43 narrowed. The dashboard adds the two together, and 59% of what it shows as "confirmed" is the author's own word. The fix is one column: the actor is already on every event (`02-measures.md`).

**On identity, which the lead asked me to check from my own shell.** You assumed `--by` referenced the harness session id, inferred rather than typed. It is typed today, in 74 spellings, and 5 of 2,259 events carry a session. From a subagent's shell: the session id is visible and it is the parent's; the subagent's own id is visible nowhere in the environment, only as its transcript's filename under the parent session, which is a guess when lanes run in parallel. So vsect can infer session-grain identity now with a default, subagent-grain needs one exported variable from the harness (a `/feedback` ask), and the event should say which it got (`06-recommendations.md` §4a).

**Your loop is real, and its gain was above one.** You suspected that almost half the "fixed" claims were claims the same sprint had written. Measured at the version level, at least 86% of findings were placed on text written during the sprint. Independent checks kept finding something at about one in four, round after round, in three of four lanes. And the four most-checked segments grew between 1.5x and 2.5x across their rounds, each round adding 300 to 5,000 words and removing a tenth of that. A loop converges when each round settles more than it adds; these rounds added more than the next round could clear. Part I's mathematics did converge (its checks went to zero by round three); its restatements elsewhere did not, which is the correction the retrospective already names. The one test nobody ran: a round that lands only the findings and adds nothing, then one more check. If that check still finds a quarter, the defects were there all along; if not, the rounds were making their own work (`03-loops.md`).

**On `kind: item`: your worry is right, and the model you already have says why.** The verisectorium's own rule is that a kind earns its place when it fails differently and is repaired differently. A random 45 of the held items contains at least six repairs behind one word: a defect in a claim, a segment to author, a derivation to attempt, a citation to land, a tool to build, and judgments that are not work at all. It did not cost anything during the sprint because nothing closed: 18 of 858 items are done. It costs at `close`, where a defect needs a revision and a second mind, an authoring task needs a file, a measurement needs a run. Add the closing kind now, before the next phase starts closing (`06-recommendations.md` §3).

**Retiring cruft: half built.** Items and findings have their exits and the words are close to right (`landed`, `stale`, `superseded`, `duplicate`, `not-an-item`; `done`, `moot`). Sources have nothing. The design's influx crossing (integrated / rejected / needs-review / skipped) was never built, so the 23 drain files each invented it in prose, in their own words, and I.10 found that 60% of what the ledger knows about claims is written only there. The states you listed, already superseded, an initial finding not yet a change to a claim, the final disposition, are exactly the states a `source` record needs, and the delete-test you stated in August is the rule that lets "retired" be computed instead of typed. The seed is already written: 2,700 dispositions in the drains, importable the way the trackers were (`04-sunset.md`).

**Yes, vsect should draw the machine, and it already can.** I drew it from the trail in 150 lines of Python; `01-flows.md` §2 is the result. What it shows is stocks and one cycle: 467 noted items with no act since they were opened, 182 findings with a single exit, 336 lapsed checks, a two-percent `done` sink, a drain edge where half of what was found was already there, and the check → finding → revise → lapse → check cycle with its gain measured by edit mass per round. A `vsect flows` verb is a read verb over events, not standing, so it does not conflict with the design's rule that standing waits on declared projections. The probabilities are honest only as frequencies over one day's regime, and the diagram should say so on its face (`06-recommendations.md` §7).

**Questions the system is not asking**, the first four: who recorded this check relative to who wrote the text; what did the check read; what kind of work is this item and what act closes it; what has and has not been read for integration. The rest are in `05-questions.md`.

**The second coordinator.** The lead's 113 sprint commits were mostly relaying, merging and committing, and both drifts above happened inside those acts, so the role has content. Define it as the list of things the tools cannot yet do, and shrink it as verbs land, so a missing verb does not become a habit; and keep it from becoming the integrator of last resort, which is how "a new integrator each round" started.

**What I would leave alone.** The strict lapse rule (its alarms are 96% false and the three true ones were the ones that mattered; scope it with read sets, do not loosen it). The bare-brief independent check, which is where all the information came from. The append-only events, including the 135 self-corrections.

**Where I could be wrong.** The sprint was one day under one regime, so every rate is a frequency, not a law. "Sprint-written" is measured at the version level, so a finding about an old sentence counts when a new section was inserted above it; the floor is a floor in both directions. The flat yield per round may measure checker diversity rather than churn; the one-round test above separates them. I did not read the lane accounts, the regional influx files or the other drain files beyond their vocabulary, and I launched no agents. The item sample is 45 of 661.
