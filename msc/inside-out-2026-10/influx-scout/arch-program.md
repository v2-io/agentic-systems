**What the 10-19 middle-folding note gives the refactor.**

- *Part I, the (a)/(b)/(c)/(d) stratification.* Joseph asked whether AAT's strategy formalism says (a) agents *can* be modeled thus, (b) *can only* be modeled thus, (c) this is the most advantageous model, or (d) this model allows $x, y, z$. The note answers joint by joint. (b) forced: decomposition canonicity under goal interventions, acyclicity from time-indexed tokens, Cox probability, the four forced coordinates, and the orient-cascade ordering. (a) sufficient with necessity open: DAG plus Markov factorization, quoting `#deriv-graph-structure-uniqueness`'s own "must-if-sufficient-via-this-route". (c)+(d) a convergent representational choice: AND/OR with single-parameter edges, and the five pieces of machinery that choice unlocks. Canon carries each piece, but **no canon or front-door text gives a reader this map**. It is the clearest answer I found in arch to "what is ASF claiming about strategy?". The middle-fold audit (§K) calls this "the most reliable part of the note" and confirms the tiers, with two corrections. Corollary 4's compatibility condition (C) is omitted. - *Part II, a safe-middle-folding argument* (**misapplied, per the audit; recast below**). As the note puts it: once a subgoal $K$ is verified done and every path from its interior runs through $K$, the Causal Markov Condition screens off the interior. Under the strategy IB objective, keeping the interior adds description length for zero reduction in $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi^\ast \Vert Q_{\Sigma_t})$. Three conditions bound this: causal sufficiency, meaning constraints and common causes discovered inside $K$ must be hoisted into $M_t$, $O_t$ or an L1 node first; extraction of the calibration residual and the pruned OR-branches; and a recall-cost-gradient pointer to the archive. The argument assembles existing canon (`#deriv-graph-structure-uniqueness`, `#form-strategy-complexity-cost`'s "stop tracking what you already know", `#disc-credit-assignment-boundary`'s OKR passage, `#disc-deferred-observation-processing`). The assembly is new and canon-shaped. It is also the formal content of Joseph's founding prediction in `REFLECTIONS-2026-08-28.md`: "in a well-run hierarchical strategy, the 'middle' being forgotten is a *good* thing". The dossier (§3, see §1.3) frames the discriminator the same way: "a screening-off condition, not a decay-shape condition." **The audit's correction, which I checked against the cited lines and accept.** CMC is about $\Sigma_t$, whose nodes are *future* event tokens ($\tau_i \gt t$). A finished subgoal's interior is *past* history in the append-only $\mathcal C_t$, carried for an LLM in context, which canon treats as $M_t$ (`#obs-context-turnover`). So a context fold is a compression of $M_t$ relative to $\mathcal C_t$, judged by model sufficiency $S(M_t)$ (`#def-model-sufficiency`, `#disc-compression-operations`), and CMC says nothing about it. Joseph's prediction survives the recasting, and it is cleaner: a discharged interior should lose salience because it stops contributing to the sufficiency the forward horizon needs. Whatever *does* still contribute (constraints, common causes, calibration counts) is what a fold must keep. The audit's §3 adds canon-native points the note missed. On-policy evaluation is structurally blind to a dropped constraint, so evaluate with probes that force the governed decision. And the audit's §7 question, "which canon object is a context fold an operation on?", would catch this whole class of misapplication if asked of any proposed application.
- *The "AAT Theorem of Peak-End Middle-Folding"* is **contradicted by canon, not merely overclaimed** (audit §C, which goes further than my first reading). In canon's own edge model a routine success *is* an update: Beta-Bernoulli pseudo-counts increment, so the count of uneventful successes is part of the sufficient statistic. Duration neglect discards exactly what calibration runs on, and the note's own Nuance 2 then recommends recording attempt counts. The weakest-link result is a minimum across edges, not a supremum over time. The psychology's "peak" is affect intensity, and the peak-end literature shows it leading people to prefer dominated options, which is evidence of what such a summary loses. What survives is a design heuristic consistent with canon: keep the outcome, the surprises and dead ends, and attempt counts. Do not land the theorem.
