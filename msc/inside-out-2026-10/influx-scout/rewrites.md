## 2. What the rewrites found wrong in canon, confirmed at HEAD

Each item names the canon target, what is wrong, the source, what kind of material it is, its state in canon, how I checked, and how sure I am. "Recorded, not landed" means the defect is named somewhere in asf (usually a segment's Working Notes or an audit file) but the body still carries it.

### 2.1 Sufficiency and fitness are infinite-horizon ratios, which makes the structural-adaptation trigger fire for every bounded agent

- **Target:** `01-aat-core/src/def-model-sufficiency.md:26` defines $S(M_t)$ with $o_{t+1:\infty}$ and future actions $a_{t:\infty}$ conditioned on. `def-model-class-fitness.md` takes $\mathcal F(\mathcal M) = \sup_{M \in \mathcal M} S(M)$ and sets the trigger $\mathcal F(\mathcal M) \lt 1 - \varepsilon$ for `result-structural-adaptation-necessity`.
- **Finding:** in any world whose law has a continuous identifiable parameter, $I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1:\infty})$ grows like $\tfrac d2 \log t$. Any store with at most $N$ states has $I(M_t; Y) \leq \log N$. So $\mathcal F_t \to 0$ for **every** finite-state class, however large $N$ is. The trigger therefore fires eventually for every bounded agent, a fixed context window included, **regardless of predictive adequacy**. The verifier's $N = 64$ Beta–Bernoulli agent is within $10^{-4}$ nats per step of optimal, and it still receives "structural adaptation is necessary". The infinite-horizon ratio also lets law learning swamp the ratio: a store that keeps the law and drops all state tends to $S = 1$.
- **Source:** `aat-refactored/spikes/verify-ch2-retention-2026-09-25/report.md` §1–§2 (lines 39–71), with a numerical check in `checks/finite_state_floor.py`. The de novo review's F-R2 (`spikes/review-ch1-2-2026-09-25/phase1-de-novo.md:138`) found the swamping independently, with a Gaussian log-determinant check. Both were written against the drafts, which used the same $Y = o_{t+1:\infty}$ object as canon.
- **Kind:** a defect in a canon definition that a canon result consumes; a strengthening is available (§3.1).
- **Canon state:** not recorded anywhere I found. Canon's Discussion says only that "the infinite-horizon formulation … is the most demanding". **Checked:** read the canon definitions at HEAD; grep of all four volumes' `src/` and `FINDINGS.md` for `finite-horizon`, `readout`, `retention`, `sandwich` finds no relevant hit.
- **Confidence:** high on the finite-state collapse (elementary, as the verifier says, plus numbers). Two readers reached the swamping half independently. The consequence for the trigger is my reading of canon's text against the verifier's §2, and it matches what the verifier itself states.

### 2.3 Fitness's residual discriminator and its "exact" bias–variance parallel

### 2.5 Canon's sequential-ignorability claim may be a conditioning-set confusion, or the correction may be wrong; it needs a verifier

- **Target:** `der-loop-interventional-access.md:41` says (C2) sequential ignorability fails "generic for any agent whose action depends on internal state, plans, or memory that also bear on the outcome". `def-value-object.md:50` says directed separation "*delivers* (C2) on the epistemic-processing leg".
- **Finding:** if the store is formed from the history plus private randomness independent of the world, and the action is drawn from the store, then $a_t \perp \Omega_t \mid \mathcal C_t$ holds **by construction**. From the agent's own conditioning, its actions are unconfounded. What can fail is positivity, not ignorability. Confounding enters for an outside analyst who conditions on less than $\mathcal C_t$.
- **Source:** de novo review F-AG4 (`phase1-de-novo.md:106`) and look-ahead A1 (`phase2-heads-up.md`, Group A), both from the same reviewer instance. The reviewer rated its confidence high on the derivation but only moderate that canon's (C2) uses the same conditioning set.
- **Kind:** a possible correction to a load-bearing Part II segment. The look-ahead names the stakes as Part II identification and the NeurIPS Paper 2 bridge.
- **Canon state:** the canon text is as quoted (read at HEAD); the correction is absent from canon.
- **Confidence:** I did not adjudicate it. One honest reading is that canon means the outside analyst's conditioning, or that goal state $G_t$ is not a function of the history. Then the fix is a sentence that names the conditioning set, and the one-line lemma becomes a canon result. This is a natural first item for an independent verifier.

### 2.6 The adaptive scope: the predicate's typing, and a no-go that sharpens SP-30

- **Target:** `scope-adaptive-system.md`, whose predicate is $H(\Omega_t \mid \mathcal C_t) \gt 0$. That is residual uncertainty about the *state* only, so fully observed RL with an unknown law is excluded while the rationale reasons about known dynamics. This is asf's own SP-30 (`PROPOSALS.md:389`), packaged in `asf/spikes/epistemic-target-ontology/` and waiting in `JOSEPH-TODO.md:16` as "SP-30 typed epistemic target (package ready)".
- **What the rewrites add beyond SP-30** (`aat-refactored/spikes/spike-adaptive-predicate-2026-09-25/`, and its verifier `de-novo-feedback-1.md`):
  - **The right predicate is the stream form.** It is a supremum over deterministic continuation policies of $I(Z_t; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t)$, with $Z_t = (\Omega_t, \theta)$ and future actions interleaved in $F_t$. The observation-only, own-policy form fails: an XOR counterexample learns its whole target in one step yet is classed out (F1, `:24–42`, exact, enumerated).
  - **A no-go: scope membership of known-law trackers is not determined by behavior.** The belief-state twin (the classical POMDP-to-belief-MDP reduction) re-houses any known-law loop, passive *or acting*, as out of scope, with an identical interaction law under every policy. It was checked numerically on a two-sensor Kalman case matching asf's own `example-kalman` (F2, `:46–87`). This refutes the spike's "rescue for actors".
  - **The resulting fork is a steward-purpose decision.** (a) A behavior-level scope, "something about the law is still learnable", is canonical but excludes known-law tracking and the persistence race. (b) A scope relative to a declared physical world keeps the race. Canon's own Kalman example and Part I's architecture presuppose (b). The verifier's sharper candidate, that the behavior-determined content of the predicate is exactly its law-learning part, is conditional on SP-30's §3.1 canonicity of $\theta$, which is itself single-agent.
  - **"Stuck" as a coordinate-free diagnostic inside the scope:** some $g(Z_t)$ has zero information under the agent's own policy but positive information under some policy (F5, `:121–135`, sketch). This candidate formal object would sit under `der-observability-dominance` and `hyp-solicitable-escape`.
- **Kind:** new theory (one no-go, one predicate form), plus a decision item for Joseph that SP-30's package does not yet frame this sharply.
- **Canon state:** absent; SP-30 is undecided. **Checked:** read `scope-adaptive-system`, PROPOSALS SP-30 and JOSEPH-TODO at HEAD; grep for "stream form", "belief state" and "typed target" finds nothing relevant.
- **Confidence:** F1 is exact and enumerated. F2's twin is checked numerically and rests on a classical reduction cited from working knowledge. Everything is single-agent.

### 3.1 Chapter 2: retention, readout and sufficiency (the best-checked material in either repo)

- **The retention/readout split** [exact; re-derived by two independent readers]. The one-step Bayes regret splits exactly into a **retention** leg, $I(\mathcal C_{t-1}; o_t \mid M_{t-1}, a_{t-1})$ (the store didn't keep it), and a **readout** leg, $\mathbb E\, D(p^M \Vert q)$ (the agent reads what it kept wrongly). The de novo reviewer calls it "the best idea in Chapter 2". Home in the rewrite: `aat-refactored/src/retention.md`. Proofs: `verify-ch2-retention-2026-09-25/report.md` §12; `phase1-de-novo.md:148`. In canon, Proposition 3's estimation divergence (i′) is the sum of the two legs, so canon already has the total but not the split.
- **Three ways to be wrong, not two** [single-agent]. Not kept (retention); kept but misread (readout); and right about everything observable but wrong about what lies behind it (latent non-identifiability, invisible to every predictive metric in Part I and fixed only by a channel or an action whose outcome differs). A deceiver's channel with no honest alternative is the paradigm of the third (`report.md` §4). This directly reframes the liar cases in asf's `spike-honest-activation-2026-09-23`.
- **The sandwich lemma** [exact as proved; single-agent; numerically checked]. For a recursive store under exogenous future actions, $R_1(t) \leq I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1:t+K} \mid M_t) \leq \sum_{j=0}^{K-1} R_1(t+j)$, where $R_1$ is the one-step retention leg. Corollaries: one-step sufficiency at every step is equivalent to full sufficiency, the information-state condition of stochastic control (Striebel; Kumar and Varaiya; Subramanian et al., cited from recall); fitness bounds the class's best one-step leg; and $\mathcal F_t \lt 1$ means every rule in the class has a positive one-step leg at *some* later step, the rigorous form of canon's step 2→3 in `result-structural-adaptation-necessity` (`report.md` §3).
- **Finite-horizon sufficiency $S_k$ under an explicit reference action law $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$** [single-agent; recommended repair for §2.1]. The infinite-horizon target form is $\infty - \infty$ in every continuous-parameter learning problem; finite $k$ removes that. The verifier also found **four different conventions for future actions** in five segments, and three cross-segment identifications that equate objects defined under different conventions (`report.md` §5). Canon has the same exposure: it conditions observationally on $a_{t:\infty}$ and defers to Part II's $\pi_{\text{cont}}$. The verifier's reading is that the choice of $\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is exactly where goals can enter the compression criterion, a precise location for the IB-versus-directed-separation tension.
- **Integrity as a Markov condition** [single-agent]. Replace "$M_t$ is a function of $\mathcal C_t$" with "$M_t - \mathcal C_t - (Z_t, Y)$ is a Markov chain" under exogenous future actions. Private store randomness is then harmless, and $S \in [0,1]$, the regret identity, the target form, the split and the IB chain all survive (`report.md` §6). One caution about its landing in aat-refactored: the qualifier "under exogenous future actions" was dropped there, and the landed clause then contradicted itself (T1 in `spikes/fresh-view-2026-09-29/theory-findings-to-verify.md`, with a one-step check). Bears on canon `form-agent-model` and on the "priors have no place" gap.
- **Two further results from the same report** [single-agent]. The pointwise information curve is only an *upper bound* for recursive stores: an XOR-parity counterexample shows a horizon-1-sufficient recursive store needs two bits where the pointwise optimum needs one (`report.md` §7). And Still 2009's with-actions limit gives policy-relative equivalence classes; only the passive limit recovers causal states, which the verifier checked at the primary (§5). Canon does not cite Still, so the second matters only if Still is adopted.

### 3.2 Chapter 1: the loop, history and scope

- **Housing rules H1–H3 (later H4)** [formulation; aat-refactored `src/the-loop.md:54–58`]. H1: memory goes into the state, so the step kernel is Markov given the law. H2: persistence goes into the law. H3: boundary and grain are the modeler's, and **every classification must be invariant under redescription at the same boundary and grain**. - **The interior-action loophole in directed separation** [single-agent; mechanism high-confidence]. Goal-dependent processing can be rewritten as a goal-chosen *interior action*, recorded in the history and fed to a goal-blind update. `der-directed-separation`'s test then passes trivially, so a redescription moves Class 3 to Class 1. World-acting actions yield causal learning about dynamics; probes yield information about the target and interventional knowledge of the instrument only; interior actions change the agent's own computation. - **The history/retention/record trichotomy** [definition; `src/history.md:19–31`]. The **history** is the run: a fact, monotone and identity-bearing. **Retention** is what the store keeps: bounded and lossy. A **record** is a stored, copyable artifact. Every claim in canon's `def-chronica` is true of exactly one of the three (v3 reached the same reading independently but after reading about the trichotomy, so this is coherence, not corroboration). - **"The inside view can't certify the outside"** [the lemma is exact but tautological; its content is in its hypotheses]. - **Clock as a channel** [framing that resolves a canon open question; `scratch/14-form-event-driven-dynamics.md`]. The ordinal and metric horns of canon's open question (`def-chronica` WN; audit 731548's null-event dilemma) become two kinds of agent. The applied consequence: an LLM agent without timestamps cannot tell a one-second pause from a one-year pause. - **The chain-rule learning budget** [single-agent derivation, done "in my head" by the reviewer; `phase1-de-novo.md:76–80`]. $\sum_s I(Z^{\text{inv}}; o_{s+1} \mid \mathcal C_s, a_s) \leq H(Z^{\text{inv}})$: the total an agent can ever learn about what holds still is at most its entropy, with no identifiability or Borel condition. I did not search canon for such uses.
