## Findings, in order of consequence

### 1. The continuation criterion is overlap in a shared order, and the segment contradicts it in three places (`#scope-agent-identity`)

The criterion: two continuations of one state are a fork when each takes events after the other has begun. Write each continuation's span as $[b, e]$, from its first event to its last event ever. "$A$ takes events after $B$ began" is $e_A \gt b_B$, and the converse is $e_B \gt b_A$; both together are exactly that the spans overlap. So the test is span overlap in a common order of the two continuations' events. The chronica's being ordinal does not supply that order: each chronica orders its own events, and nothing in `#def-chronica` orders the events of two continuations against each other. The order the test uses is the physical (wall-clock or causal) one. The sentence *"wall-clock overlap is not the test, since the chronica is ordinal"* therefore says the test is not what it is. What the integrator rightly rejected is instantaneous concurrency ("no second copy running at any moment"), which time-slicing defeats; overlap of spans is not that, and it is what the criterion uses.

Three consequences the landed text does not carry.

**(a) Sequential replay is one agent, against consequence 3 of the same segment.** Save a state $s_0$. Run it against event stream $E_1$ for ten steps and discard the result. Reload $s_0$, run it against $E_2$, discard. Repeat $N$ times. No two runs overlap, so under the criterion none is a fork; each reload is "a restoration from backup whose set-aside later state is never run again", and the whole is one agent whose chronica is the concatenation $E_1 E_2 \cdots E_N$. Consequence 3, two sections up in the same segment, says the opposite: *"Replaying a saved $M_t$ against a different event stream is an intervention on a different causal trajectory $\mathcal C_t^{(2)}$ that happens to share a prefix"*, and it forbids *"averaging interventional responses across forked copies as though they were one agent's effects"*. Under the criterion the copies are not forked, so the averaging consequence 3 exists to forbid is licensed by running the copies one after another instead of side by side. The same conflict reaches Volume 3: `#scope-primitive-logogenic` says the session's trajectory *"is forkable by harness operations (resets, replay, parallel sessions)"*, and `#impl-closed-loop-interiority` builds its AI-safety prescription ("the forks do not aggregate into a single agent's behavior") on the same reading. A practitioner can satisfy the criterion and defeat the prescription by scheduling.

**(b) Scheduling decides identity.** The scope bullet says *"scheduling does not decide identity"*. Take one saved state and two copies. Schedule 1: $A$ runs $[t_0, t_1]$, $B$ runs $[t_1, t_2]$, $A$ runs again from $t_2$: a fork. Schedule 2: $A$ runs $[t_0, t_1]$, $B$ runs from $t_1$ on, $A$ is never run again: one agent. The copies and their first two runs are the same; only the scheduler's later choice differs. What the integrator showed is that *parallel versus interleaved* does not decide identity, which is true; *whether the set-aside copy is ever scheduled again* does, and that is scheduling.

**(c) The chronica is not determined at the time it indexes.** In schedule 2 the trajectory is "the sequence of events that occurred", so $\mathcal C_t(B)$ for $t \in (t_1, t_2)$ contains $A$'s events in $[t_0, t_1]$ ("whatever happened after the backup stays in the trajectory"). In schedule 1 the copies are two agents with divergent chronicae (`#def-chronica`), so $\mathcal C_t(B)$ does not contain them. The physical history up to $t$ is the same in both schedules. So $\mathcal C_t(B)$, and with it $S(M_t)$ and $S_{\text{id}}(M_t)$, which are defined against $\mathcal C_t$, are not fixed by what has happened by $t$: a later resumption removes events from $B$'s chronica after the fact, against `#def-chronica`'s "events are added, never removed". The account anticipated "a fact about the future" and judged it acceptable because $\mathrm{Id}^{(i)}$ is already a function of the future trajectory. That is a different dependence. $\mathrm{Id}^{(i)}$ is defined on $\mathfrak T_{t+1:}$; it is meant to look forward. $\mathcal C_t$ is the record of what has happened, and every rate result in Part I treats it as known at $t$.

Residue of the same kind, in the segment that defines the criterion: the *Scope commitment* paragraph of the Formal Expression still reads *"Duplicating $M_t$ and exposing the copies to different future events produces two agents"*, the wording the integrator changed in `#def-chronica` and `#persistence-and-limits-intro` because it covers sequential restores. "Two copies run in turn on one processor" means interleaved in context, but "in turn" usually means one after the other, which the criterion classes the opposite way. And the Discussion and `#obs-context-turnover` state the session condition as no other continuation "taking events beside it", a concurrency word for a criterion that is not concurrency.

**What would make the segment true.** I am not the integrator, so these are directions, not a work order. One is to add a causal clause: a later continuation continues an earlier one's trajectory only if the earlier one's events reach it, through the store, the environment, the record or the witnesses. Under that clause a restore after failure, where the failed run's effects are in the world and the record, continues the trajectory; a sandbox replay with the environment reset does not, and consequence 3 stands. It also explains why sessions continue (the store carries the predecessor's events) without a scheduling clause. Its cost is that the audit's "murdered the entity that lived through the interval" becomes true of a restore whose interval left no trace anywhere, which is a substantive verdict to take on deliberately. A second is to keep the overlap criterion and state its price: the chronica is fixed only once every set-aside state is destroyed or locked, consequence 3 is restated for simultaneous copies, and the Volume 3 sentences change. That makes INDIVISUM's job larger than the hypothesis segment says: it is what makes an ELI's chronica determinate at all. A third is a decision at restore time (a branch in the history tree is a fork), which the integrator rejected because it seems to make every session boundary a death; I think that rejection holds only if a session's reconstruction from a summary counts as "continuing the same state", which is worth checking rather than assuming.

### 2. The factor-(iv) indicator cannot register a record rollback (`#def-identity-sufficiency`)

The $\mathrm{Id}^{(i)}$ paragraph now says *"a restore that also rolls back CHRONICA and ACTUS deletes acts from $E$'s record … and the append-only indicator of $\mathrm{Id}^{(iv)}$ below is $0$ on any window in which that happens."* The indicator is $\mathbb 1[\mathrm{ACTUS}_{t+1:}(E) \text{ append-only}]$ with $\mathrm{ACTUS}_{t+1:}(E)$ *"the action sub-history $\subseteq \mathcal C_{t+1:}(E)$"*. This round fixed $\mathcal C_t$ as the real event sequence, whose events are never removed, precisely so that a record rollback does not roll back the trajectory. A sub-sequence of a never-shrinking sequence is append-only by construction, so the indicator is $1$ on every window, rollback or not. The cost the round moved from factor (i) to factor (iv) lands on a formula that cannot see it. The repair is to define the indicator on the stored record (CHRONICA and the ACTUS store as kept by the system), as the gloss under the formula already says it means ("CHRONICA inviolability"), and to say how that record relates to $\mathcal C_t$ (it is a record *of* it, per `#def-proprium-mapping`).

### 3. "W₁ within each epoch" is a degenerate-conditioning result, and current canon says why it does not bound what W₁ is for (`#def-auxilia-hierarchy`)

The landed argument: within an epoch the aspiration layer $a$ is constant, so a belief-side query carrying it is a fixed function of $(M_W, o_W)$, Theorem 1's conclusion holds conditional on the epoch, and a constant adds nothing to $I(q_M; G^{\text{op}})$. From this the segment concludes that an Auxilia receiving the full AXIOMATA *"is therefore W₁ within each epoch, to the same bound as one that receives only the self-description"* and that *"the AXIOMATA revision is where motivated perception can enter."*

The same argument proves too much. Take a raw Class 3 LLM whose goal is fixed in its system prompt and never changes. Within its one epoch the goal is constant, every query is a fixed function of the remaining inputs, $P(M' \mid M, o, G) = P(M' \mid M, o)$ holds trivially, and the goal adds nothing to any mutual information. By the landed reasoning that agent is Class 1 for its whole life. What has happened is that conditioning on a degenerate variable makes every independence statement about it true. `#der-directed-separation` does not define Class 1 that way: Class 1 *"holds this value under every distribution"*, a property of the wiring, and its falsifier is the processor probe, the same event under two goal states with the prior state fixed. Run that probe with two different aspiration layers and an Auxilia that reads $a$ will in general report different beliefs. Read that way, an Auxilia that receives $a$ has a goal port, and the epoch argument gives at most the realized-zero case, "causal discipline in effect", not the certificate W₁ is built to give.

Current canon closes the other door too. The Part II lane's `253d2bca` added to `#der-class-coercion-via-wrapping` that a component which *"reads goal cues in the content of $q_M$ and tilts its answer is a fixed function of $q_M$, so it satisfies (C3) as formalized … and (C2′), and its tilt lands in the same selection-channel term: cue-triggered distortion by the component … bounded by nothing in Theorems 1–2."* Aspirations written into a belief-side query are as direct a goal cue as there can be. An LLM Auxilia that reads "I am committed to kindness" in its query and softens its report of evidence about the entity's unkindness is a fixed function of $q_M$ within the epoch: it satisfies everything the epoch argument establishes, and it is distortion in the sense `#der-directed-separation`'s third clause forbids, at every call, not only at revision. So "motivated perception can enter at AXIOMATA revision" is wrong in the direction that matters: revision is where the bias *changes*; the bias is present throughout the epoch. And "to the same bound as one that receives only the self-description" is true of the information bound and false of what the bound was for.

What survives, and is worth keeping: carrying a constant $a$ does not open a channel for goal content that varies from call to call ($E$'s tasking); conditional on the epoch, the fast goal's leakage is what it would be without $a$. The strengthening the first check proposed was the parameter reading, and that reading can be made honest: if each AXIOMATA revision builds a new wrapper in which $a$ is a fixed parameter of $q_M$ and not part of that wrapper's $G_W$, W₁ holds per wrapper by construction. But then $a$ is not "the slowest layer of $O_t$" for that wrapper, which is what `#def-proprium-mapping` now says it is, so the two segments would need reconciling; and the cue-triggered tilt above is untouched by re-parameterization, because it is a property of the component reading the content. The first check's other reading follows from both: a belief-side Auxilia is a W₁ component only if it does not receive the aspiration layer, which makes that a requirement, not "a choice between identity-sharing and that invariance". (H1)'s "AXIOMATA always" and the composite Class 1 claim in the next paragraph then need the belief side carved out of (H1). The first check's finding (`review:vol4-c1-check-1#C2/finding`) is therefore still open: the replacement chose the reading that does not hold.

### 4. The R0 derivation's timescale ratio is taken against the wrong Auxilia (`#def-auxilia-hierarchy`)

The derivation takes *"the Auxilia as the fast level and $E$ as the slow one, with timescale ratio $\epsilon = 1/K_c$ from (H5)"*. (H5) says $E$ runs at $K_c \gg 1$ *"relative to the fastest Auxilia"*. In `#der-multi-timescale-stability`, $\epsilon = \nu_2 / \nu_1$ between the slow level and the fast level it stacks on, and (C1) must hold for the whole fast level. Lumping every Auxilia into the fast level makes the binding ratio the one between $E$ and the *slowest* Auxilia. (H3)'s own table puts frontier API calls at "per-event (high latency, high cost)" and native attention heads at "within-forward-pass"; a $K_c$ large against attention heads says nothing about $E$ against frontier calls, which may run at $E$'s own event rate. The segment's (H3) text even says the frontier tier operates slowest. So condition (d) is instantiated with the ratio the hierarchy guarantees, not the one the theorem needs, and nothing in (H3)–(H5) secures the one it needs. The $N$-level form "tier by tier" would handle the tiers among themselves, but the top of that stack is still $E$ against the slowest tier.

Related, and smaller: the Auxilia enter as $\dot x_1 = -F_1(x_1; x_2)$ with a Lipschitz settled answer $h(x_2)$ (S1). Per-event LLM calls are discrete and stochastic and their answer is not a Lipschitz function of the task in any stated sense. The account says the segment states this as a premise; the body tags the step conditional on (S0)–(S4) "instantiated as below" but does not say that fitting LLM Auxilia to them is an idealization. A sentence would do it.

The alignment-necessity half and the "alignment enters through (a)" point re-derive. The first check's C1 finding (R0 asserted, not derived) is answered by this derivation; this finding is about its instantiation.

### 5. The symmetric ceiling needs the direction-independent law too, and the relevance law is not fixed by the compression (`#hyp-substrate-transfer-asymmetry`, `#def-identity-sufficiency`)

**(a) Inside the segment.** The first derived block says that with one capacity per substrate *"the ceiling is then the same in both directions"*, the ceiling being $S_{\text{id}} \le B / I(\mathcal C_t; \text{identity}_{t+1:})$. The next block indexes the identity-future by direction, $\text{identity}^{(d)}_{t+1:}$, because the entity continues on the target, and its own frontier is bounded by $\min(1, B / I(\mathcal C_t; \text{identity}^{(d)}_{t+1:}))$. Once the law is direction-indexed the denominator is too, so the $S_{\text{id}}$ ceiling differs by direction even at matched budgets. What is symmetric under one capacity per substrate is the ceiling in bits, $I(M_t; \text{identity}) \le B$, not the ceiling on $S_{\text{id}}$. The first block's tag ("conditional on one capacity per substrate") is missing the second condition, and the summary sentence of the segment repeats it.

**(b) Underneath, in `#def-identity-sufficiency`.** The joint law of $(\mathcal C_t, \text{identity}_{t+1:})$ is induced by *"each agent's continuation policy"*, and $E$'s continuation runs from $M_t$. So the law of the identity-future depends on the compression $\phi$ itself, not only on the substrate. The identity-IB frontier $F_d(B) = \max_\phi \ldots$ is written, and called "one standard information-bottleneck fact", as if the relevance law were fixed while $\phi$ varies; standard IB needs exactly that. Hypothesis step 2's own reason for indexing by direction (the continuing entity shapes its identity-future) is the same causal fact that makes the law depend on $\phi$. One sign that this matters: the components of the identity vector that $E$'s own continuation produces depend on $\mathcal C_t$ only through $M_t$, so they contribute nothing to $I(\mathcal C_t; \text{identity} \mid M_t)$ however much $\phi$ discards; the loss is visible only through components that read the history from elsewhere (witnesses comparing behavior to what they remember, attestation of past acts). The cohort is non-empty by `#scope-eli`, so $S_{\text{id}}$ is not trivially $1$, but it measures loss only through those channels. `#def-identity-sufficiency` should say which law it means: the actual continuation from $M_t$ (then the frontier is not a standard IB object and the rate bound holds per $\phi$ under its own induced law), or a reference continuation that does not depend on $\phi$ (then step 2's direction-indexing has to be restated against that reference). This predates the round; the round made it carry weight.
