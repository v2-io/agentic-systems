## Findings

**F1 (required): `#impl-cooperative-adversarial`:45 still says the question this lane settled is unsettled.** It reads: "Whether a defender can *exploit* this by adding noise to its own channel is not settled by either result … the net is an unmodeled trade, and the defender-side prescription is a hypothesis, not a framework prediction." P1 settles it: the prescription is false even at fixed gain. Line 84 of the same file, which the lane edited, now says "False as a defense … The derivation is `#deriv-observation-noise-placement`", so the file contradicts itself. The same paragraph also gives the event-rate mechanism ("a faster attacker makes more corrections, each of them noisy"), which the lane showed is not what Variant E did, and it frames the collapse through the recipient-side Regime-III reading that the lane removed from `#der-interaction-channel-classification`. This is the softened ghost that integration-is-replacement deletes. The paragraph's last sentence still stands: the superlinear exponent belongs to the coupling-dominant, low-observation-noise regime.

**F2 (required): `#result-adversarial-tempo-advantage`, scope paragraph: "Tempo raised by event rate lowers $\sigma_\nu^2 \propto 1/\nu$ and keeps the coupling dominant."** This is false for the attacker, and it contradicts the new segment's P2(c). With $r$ events per unit time at per-event gain $h$ (so $\mathcal T = rh$ and channel intensity $s^2/r$), the injected term is $\mathcal T^2\sigma_\nu^2 = \mathcal T h s^2$, linear in tempo, while the attacker's lag term $\sigma_{\text{env},A}^2/(2\mathcal T_A)$ falls. Equivalently, $\mathcal T_A\sigma_{\nu,A} = s\sqrt{h\mathcal T_A}$ grows, so the condition $\gamma_B\mathcal T_B \gg \mathcal T_A\sigma_{\nu,A}$ that this paragraph states still fails, at $\mathcal T_A \sim \sigma_{\text{env},A}^2/(hs^2)$ instead of the gain-raised point. Past it the attacker's error tends to the floor $nhs^2/2$ and the exponent goes from $3/2$ to $1$, not to $1/2$ (script §4: local exponent $1.000$). Event rate delays the loss of coupling dominance and limits it to $3/2 \to 1$; it does not prevent it. The sentence was there before this lane and is outside both lapsed criteria, but it sits in the paragraph the lane edited and against the segment the lane wrote. The integrator's open question on `#def-adaptive-tempo`:52 ("You cannot outrun a bad observation channel by iterating faster") is related. By the same algebra, whether that sentence is true depends on what is held fixed as the event rate rises. At a fixed per-event gain the floor $nhs^2/2$ does not move, so iterating faster does not get below it. At a fixed total gain $\sigma_\nu^2$ falls, and the floor falls with it.

**F3 (register; strengthening proposed): the normative claim is judged by the ratio, which P1 says not to do.** The Claim Record's third claim ("degrade the attacker's observation and protect its own") gives as its support that "the attacker-channel lever lowers the ratio". The Discussion says attacker-channel noise helps the defender "through the attacker's error directly". In this Setup the attacker's error does not enter the defender's: P2(a) itself says $V_B$ does not change with $\sigma_{\nu,A}$ at a fixed attacker gain. So in the segment's own terms ("the cost of each lever is in the defender's own error and persistence margin, which the exponent alone does not show"), attacker-channel noise does nothing for the defender unless the attacker lowers its gain. The Working Note leaves that response open. In this model the attacker has no reason to lower its gain under either obvious objective: $V_B$ increases without bound in $\alpha_A$, and $V_B/V_A$ still grows as $\alpha_A^{1/2}$ (script §5).

What does cap the attacker is its own persistence. By `#result-persistence-condition`, an attacker that persists in mean square has $\alpha_A \lt \alpha_+(A) = \big(R_A^2 + \sqrt{R_A^4 - n^2\sigma_{\text{env},A}^2\sigma_{\nu,A}^2}\big)/(n\sigma_{\nu,A}^2) \leq 2R_A^2/(n\sigma_{\nu,A}^2)$, whatever $\sigma_{\text{env},A}$ is. With P1(c), the defender can persist at some gain against every attacker that itself persists if

$$\frac{2\gamma_A R_A^2}{n\,\sigma_{\nu,A}^2} \;\leq\; \frac{R_B^2}{n\,\sigma_{\nu,B}} - \sigma_{\text{base}} .$$

This mirrors P1(c). The attacker's persistent tempo is capped as $1/\sigma_{\nu,A}^2$, while the defender's survivable attacker tempo falls with $1/\sigma_{\nu,B}$, and "degrade the attacker's observation, protect your own" then holds for the defender's own persistence, under the named condition that the attacker must stay in its region. I checked the cap numerically on 11,306 random parameter draws (script §5), and the algebra by hand. My recommendation is to land it as a P4 in `#deriv-observation-noise-placement`, and to restate the normative claim's "True of" and the Discussion to rest on P4 rather than the ratio. If the integrator judges otherwise, the normative claim's register should say it is about the contest ratio, not the defender's persistence. This is offered as a strengthening for the integrator to judge, not edited by me.

**F4 (strengthening, small): P2(c) can state its exponent.** In continuous time, event-rate tempo gives an attacker floor $nhs^2/2$ that does not grow with tempo, so past the crossover in F2 the ratio grows as $\gamma_A\mathcal T_A/(s\sqrt{h\mathcal T_B})$ and the exponent is $1$. The Claim Record's "False of … the exponent there stays above one half" is true but weaker than what is derivable. The script's §3 discrete form uses the per-step contraction $1 - (1 - \eta)^r$, which saturates at $1$, so its slopes over $r \in [0.5, 4]$ are local and are not this asymptote. The results file and the segment use them only as local slopes, which is right, but a reader can take $0.71$ for the event-rate exponent.

It is inversely proportional to $\sigma_{\nu,B}$ only when $\sigma_{\text{base}} = 0$. That covers P1(c)'s prose ("falls as $1/\sigma_{\nu,B}$"), the Discussion ("inversely proportional"), the `#obs-gated-tempo-advantage` lead ("falls in inverse proportion to that noise"), `#impl-strategic-composition`:63 and the OUTLINE row.
