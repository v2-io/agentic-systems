- **The Copernican correction (`#der-change-expectation-baseline`).** Conditioning $1/T$ on $T \gt t_0$ alone leaves $\int_{t_0}^\infty T^{-1}\,dT = \infty$. With $p(t_0 \mid T) = 1/T$ the posterior is $t_0/T^2$ and $P(T \gt t_0 + u \mid t_0) = t_0/(t_0+u)$, so the median remaining life is $t_0$. The hazard route's "needs no Copernican likelihood" is also right, and the two routes agree for a structural reason. With $T \mid k \sim \text{Exp}(k)$ and $0 \lt f(0) \lt \infty$, the marginal lifetime density is $\int k e^{-kT} f(k)\,dk \sim f(0)/T^2$. That is a proper prior whose tail equals Jeffreys' prior times the Copernican likelihood, so conditioning it on survival alone gives the same Pareto(1) tail.

## Measurement: what does not hold as written

All numbers in this section are printed by `c1-lanes/tst-check-2.py` (fixed seeds). Section labels (A)–(F) refer to its output.

**What the window-free test rests on, checked first.** The integrator's intervals come from an i.i.d. bootstrap over commit pairs. Pairs cluster: consecutive gaps from one author on one day share a session. The script's (A) resamples author-days instead. ### M1. The exponent's "below 1" depends on how time away is modelled

`#hyp-exponential-cognitive-load` concludes: "Where these data discriminate, then, the count form's cost grows as a power of $(1+d)$ below 1". Its Epistemic Status says the exponential is "rejected for a power of $(1+d)$ with exponent about 0.7". The integrator found that a second idle component moves the obsidian-linter exponent to 0.85 and cited no number for it. With that model (B), the exponent's interval is $[0.63, 1.00]$, and the dominant author alone gives 0.83 $[0.73, 0.97]$ under the block bootstrap. The pure exponential stays rejected in every variant ($b$ $[0.20, 1.25]$ in (B); quantile regressions give $b$ 1.0–1.4 with $a \le 0$). What does not survive is a clean "below 1". The Limits paragraph's "the exponent moves with it" is true, but the conclusion and the Epistemic Status do not carry it.

*Suggested, Discussion:* "Where these data discriminate, then, the count form's cost grows as a power of $(1+d)$ that is at most about proportional: 0.72 $[0.56, 0.87]$ with one idle component, 0.85 $[0.63, 1.00]$ with two." *Epistemic Status:* "…is rejected for a power of $(1+d)$ with exponent 0.7–0.85, depending on how time away is modelled…". ### M2. "Without compounding" asserts more than "shows no compounding"

### M3. Strengthen-first: the capped test discriminates in obsidian-linter after all

Round 2 replaced the checker's simulation with a better null: a pure exponential working component plus the fitted idle component, passed through each cap. It then judged the capped test on $b$ alone ("the simulated $b$ reaches the observed one in between 0.7% and 6% of draws"). The first checker had named a second statistic: obsidian-linter's $a$ is negative at every cap. Round 2 did not test it against the new null. (E) does. In obsidian-linter the observed $a$, from $-0.056$ to $-0.075$, is reached by the null in 0.3%, 1.0%, 0.3% and 2.0% of draws at caps of 24 h, 8 h, 4 h and 1 h. The null's own $a$ has median $+0.005$ to $+0.018$ and 2.5th percentile $-0.035$ to $-0.059$. In ink, $a$ is reached in 2–13% of draws; in the corpus, where $a$ is positive, in 5–9%.

There is a reason the null misses on $a$. The best-fitting exponential mixture in obsidian-linter puts the working component's median at 61 minutes at $d = 10$ and 688 minutes at $d = 20$, longer than its own idle component's median of 606 minutes. The exponential fits only by turning high-$d$ working time into something longer than time away. That is a second, qualitative sign against the form, which this check observed and did not test.

So in the largest history the capped fits are not weak evidence. Against the integrator's own null they reject a pure exponential at the 0.3–3% level per cap, on $b$ (0.7–3%) and separately on $a$ (0.3–2%); the four caps reuse the same gaps, so these are not independent tests. In the other two histories they lean the same way at 1–13% and do not decide. The segment's item 3 understates this. *Suggested replacement for item 3's last two sentences:* "But a pure exponential in working time, mixed with idle gaps as fitted to each history and then capped, also gives $b$ up to about 1, because the cap attenuates an exponential rate toward zero; it reaches the observed $b$ in 0.7–6% of draws. It rarely gives a negative $a$: in obsidian-linter, whose $a$ is negative at every cap, the null reaches it in 0.3–2% of draws. There the capped fits reject a pure exponential too; in ink and the corpus they lean against it and do not decide." The (E) block belongs in `elapsed_time.py` section (7), so the claim stays printed by the toolkit.

## Other narrowings

### N6. `#hyp-communication-gain`: summed gains overshoot when terms coincide

The Formulation adds one term per source, each with gain $\eta_{ji}^\ast = U_{M_i}/(U_{M_i} + \dots)$ computed against the same prior uncertainty $U_{M_i}$, and adds them to $i$'s own-observation term. Applied at one instant, separately computed gains double-count. With $U_{M_i} = U_{o,i} = 1$ and one perfect-channel source of residual variance 1, each gain is $1/2$ and they sum to 1. The joint Gaussian update gives each $1/3$, for a total of $2/3$: the summed update throws away the prior. Read event by event, it is consistent with `#deriv-tempo-additivity`: independent Poisson streams almost never coincide, and $U_{M_i}$ shrinks between events. The segment does not say which reading it means. *Suggested clause after the update:* "The terms apply as their events arrive, with $U_{M_i}$ updated between them; terms that arrive together need a joint gain, as in #deriv-tempo-additivity's synchronized batch." This is discussion-grade, and it matters to falsifier (i): "any fixed trust weight $w_{ji}$" competes against whichever reading the experiment implements.

## Observations, no finding

- **Gott and Buch.** `#der-change-expectation-baseline`'s Lineage says "The Jeffreys derivation is Gott's Copernican argument (Gott 1993)". Gott 1993 argued from the uniform distribution of $t_0/T$ without a prior. The Bayesian form, prior $dT/T$ with the Copernican likelihood, is Buch's (1994), which Gott then endorsed (Caves 2000, arXiv:astro-ph/0001414, which also argues that the Copernican premise often fails). The segment now names that premise, and Caves's critique bears on it directly.
- **"whatever the staffing limit".** The compression bound shows a Putnam-form law must fail beyond $E = (\text{LOC}/C)^3\tau^{-4}$. Where Putnam's own gradient limit binds first ($t_{\min} \gt \tau$), that region is never reached. The Hypothesis paragraph, which asks which limit binds, carries this; the sentence alone could be read as saying the failure is always observable.
- **`#detail-operationalization`: "Two procedures follow from the same segments."** The quorum is one implementation of risk-asymmetric trust, not a consequence of it; "two procedures fit the same segments" would be the register the rest of that section uses.
- **`#emp-changeset-size-principle` reads its corollary two ways.** This predates round 2. By `#def-comprehension-time`, $t_{\text{comp}}$ is the implementer's time from assignment to first surviving change. The measurement paragraph reads the corollary that way, and the measurement bears on it. The Discussion's "Connection to AAT" reads it as the *next* agent's time to comprehend a changeset, which no inter-commit gap measures.
