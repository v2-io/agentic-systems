### 1. The negotiated stance is now death-apt in one sentence and excluded by the definition three sentences earlier, and the derivation it leans on says otherwise (`#def-death-as-factor-loss`, `#disc-continuity-stance`)

The repair cannot be the one-word change of the iff, because canon's grounding segment disagrees about what negotiated is. `#deriv-self-actuation-grounding` line 106: negotiated is the bare persistence floor as terminal invariant, with continuity "tradeable down to that floor"; morally continuous is floor plus a continuity clause. `#disc-continuity-stance` line 20, unchanged by this round, says the same. ### 2. A resumption after the restored copy has stopped is a fork by one clause and a continuation by the criterion (`#scope-agent-identity`, and the three places that repeat it)

The criterion's first sentence: a continuation that starts from a reconstruction of the agent's state (the text includes a snapshot of it) continues the trajectory up to the latest of its events that reaches it. A fork is two continuations taking events at once.

Case. $A$ runs past backup $S_0$ to state $S_1$ and stops. $B$ is restored from $S_0$ into a world that keeps what happens, takes events, and is retired for good. Later $S_1$ is resumed as $A'$. $A'$ starts from a snapshot of the agent's state, and $B$'s events reach it through the world, so by the first sentence $A'$ continues the trajectory through $B$'s events, with a state stale on $B$'s interval and fresh on $A$'s last one. $B$ and $A'$ never take events at once, so the fork clause does not fire. The bullet says otherwise: "a set-aside state resumed after the trajectory has run on without it: the resumption is a fork at the moment it begins". `#hyp-checkpoint-forking-failure-modes` ("at once with the restored one or after it has taken events"), factor (i) of `#def-five-constitutive-factors` ("after the restored one has taken events") and $\mathrm{Id}^{(i)}$ ("once the restored state has taken events") say the same. Read literally, the bullet's clause also covers the plain restore: the backup $S_0$ is a set-aside state that the trajectory ran on without.

The integrator's reason for the fork verdict was determinacy of $\mathcal C_t$ (C1 of its record). The reach reading keeps it: nothing in $B$'s past is reassigned when $A'$ starts, and $A'$'s chronica holds what happened. So a direction (not a work order) is that a resumed later state is a fork only while the restored copy is still a running continuation, and is otherwise a restore with a differently stale store, which is what the first sentence already says. Whether a retired-but-restartable $B$ counts as running is the question that direction has to answer.

### 6. Is the readable record part of the state? Three segments now answer differently (`#def-identity-sufficiency`, `#der-identity-continuity-threshold`, `#der-compensation-channel-uniqueness`)

A continuation that retrieves from the record depends on $\mathcal C_t$ through the record, outside $M_t$, so its self-produced components can carry $I(\mathcal C_t; \text{identity} \mid M_t) \gt 0$ and $S_{\text{id}}$ falls below $1$ for an entity whose state discarded what it can read back. One statement fixes all three: either $M_t$ includes the readable record (then $\phi$ cannot discard what the record holds, and *Which law*'s "however much $\phi$ discards" needs that qualifier), or read-back is a session-time channel and enters the walk beside $\varrho_{\text{rg}}$.

The integrator's F1, (IS-A2) under a stochastic $\phi$, is real: its one-bit example gives $I(M_t; \text{identity}) \gt I(\mathcal C_t; \text{identity})$. ### 7. (REC) leaves out the world the entity's actions changed (`#der-compensation-channel-uniqueness`)

The Derivation's last step: a process that conditioned on $E$'s actual trajectory otherwise than through the record is a cohort member. (REC) defines the record as the system's: transcripts, CHRONICA, logs, memory stores. A trace of $E$'s actions left in the world outside that record (code it committed, a document it helped write, a message a recipient kept) is conditioned on $E$'s trajectory otherwise than through $\mathcal R_k$, is not a cohort member, and can carry $I(\cdot; Y \mid \mathcal R_k) \gt 0$ for the components of $Y$ that $E$'s history determines whenever $\mathcal R_k$ is incomplete, which a curated store always is. `#scope-agent-identity`, in the same merge, names "the world the agent's actions changed" as a route distinct from the record. The conclusion very likely survives with (REC) widened to the entity's whole exteriorized trace, so that the record channel is everything $E$'s actions wrote anywhere; but then "a function of $\mathcal C_{\tau_k}(E)$ and of nothing else" fails for traces others have since edited, and the edit is where cohort-side information can enter. Worth stating rather than assuming.

### 8. "Not at any one restore" is stronger than the walk (`#hyp-checkpoint-forking-failure-modes`)

It then says no single restore is a death, and the Discussion says the first harm is a death "only in the limit …, not at any one restore". The bullet can say that a single restore is a death only when it carries the gap to the barrier, and that the threshold theorem says nothing about one restore.
