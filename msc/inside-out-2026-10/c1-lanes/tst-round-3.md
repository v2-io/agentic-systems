## Where I departed from the check

**M3, the strengthening, does not survive M4's filter.** On all pairs, the check found obsidian-linter's observed $a$ reached by the pure-exponential null in 0.3–2% of draws, enough to call the capped test a rejection there. Once pairs touching a squash merge are left out (81 of 1,264), the same null, now with 1,000 replicates, reaches the observed $a$ in 1.3–7.9% of draws and $b$ in 3.9–9.5%. The cause is the null itself. Dropping those pairs moves the fitted exponential (log-scale rate 0.243 to 0.261, idle median 606 to 509 minutes), and the null moves with it. Item 3 now says the capped fits lean against an exponential in every history and reject it in none. The check's qualitative sign is real and is now printed by section (7): obsidian-linter's best exponential puts working time at $d = 20$ (976 minutes) above its own idle median (509). It is in the README, not in canon, because a null built from that fit is the thing in doubt.

**M1's two-idle-component number came from a likelihood with several optima.** The check's 0.85 $[0.63, 1.00]$ was one EM start. From 30 random starts, the power form's best optimum on all pairs is 0.847, so that number was right. But the check's nested fit under the same model was not at its optimum ($b = 0.988$ at $-2934.9$; the best is $b = 0.749$ at $-2930.9$). On the filtered pairs a single start gave 0.74 with the exponential ahead on likelihood, which would have reversed the reading. The toolkit now takes the best of 30 starts and starts each bootstrap draw from it: obsidian-linter 0.86 $[0.69, 1.05]$, power ahead by 4.3. In ink and the corpus the best optimum is degenerate (ink: a working spike of s.d. 0.17 beside an 8-minute "idle" component; the corpus: an idle median of 141,060 minutes). So canon cites the variant for obsidian-linter only. The check's suggested wording ("at most about proportional", 0.7–0.85) survives with 0.86.

**The Gott/Buch observation became a correction of the derivation's premise.** I read Caves (2000) in full to verify the attribution before citing it. The attribution is right: the Bayesian form is Buch's (1994), and Gott endorsed it. But Caves's main argument applies to `#der-change-expectation-baseline` directly. The uniform-age likelihood $p(t_0 \mid T) = 1/T$ describes per-system sampling: each system observed once, at a uniform point of its own life. A system observed because it is alive at the observer's time has a likelihood flat in $T$; longer lives are more likely to be found in progress. The posterior is then the prior conditioned on survival (his Eq. 13), and Gott's rule needs a prior density $1/T^2$ (his Eq. 30). A developer meets the system in front of them the second way. Under that sampling, Jeffreys' prior gives no baseline at all, and round 2's own remark ("conditioning the improper prior on survival alone … does not normalize") is that failure. The hazard-mixture route was already a survival-conditioning derivation. With a prior finite and positive at zero hazard, it supplies exactly the $1/t$ survival tail Gott's rule needs, from a proper prior. So the segment now states the baseline as survival conditioning under a $1/t$ tail, gives the hazard mixture as where the tail comes from, and keeps the Jeffreys route with its sampling premise named. The headline no longer says the baseline is the consequence of Jeffreys ignorance. `#der-dual-optimization`'s prior-art row and `#der-principled-decision-integration`'s two mentions followed. I read only Caves; the Buch and Gott 1994 citations are as his bibliography gives them.

**The squash filter drops pairs with a squash merge at either end; the check's D dropped only pairs ending in one.** A gap that starts at a merge timestamp is merge cadence as much as one that ends there. Both rules, and removing the commits before pairing, give ink exponents 0.26–0.30 and obsidian-linter 0.72–0.73 (C2). The classifier is the "(#N)" subject. In ink, 70 of the 261 such commits were committed locally rather than through the web interface. They carry pull-request titles too, and counting them as direct leaves ink's exponent at 0.30.

**Appendix F §F.4's downward half is reassigned, not carried.** It mapped directive translation onto $U_{\text{align},ji}$. By the segment's own definition, that term is uncertainty about whether $j$'s messages serve $i$'s interests, so translation fidelity is not alignment. On the merits the downward half is a resolution matter: the upper layer is a broad but coarse source on local quantities, so its $U_{\text{src},j}$ is high there. The objective and strategy content of a directive is a different transmission, which `#hyp-auftragstaktik-principle` treats. The landed paragraph says the four terms are variances over *the quantity being updated*, which is the general point both halves are instances of.

## Decisions on the merits

- **Filter squash merges in `elapsed_time.py`, by default.** The method's own rule is that merge timestamps are not work, and in ink they are a fifth of the pairs. The squash-merged method check keeps running under `--keep-squash` (gemini-cli and codex $\rho$ 0.07–0.12 reproduce, C7). Sections (1)–(4) no longer print what they printed at `0fe3432c`; the old numbers are in git.
- **Resample author-days in every bootstrap**, not only the window-free ones, so the toolkit has one resampling unit. The corpus's intervals widen most (exponent $[0.17, 0.84]$).
- **The pairing loop is one function**, where it had been written three times. The filter and the author-day key then live in one place.
- **The overhead ratio is printed, and guarded.** Canon keeps obsidian-linter's 2.5 (it was 2.8 before filtering). In ink the 201–1000-line bin's median (8.4 minutes, 22 pairs) is below the 11–50-line bin's (10.9), so the ratio is not defined, and canon says so instead of 1.6.

- **N6 strengthened into a derivation.** The check offered a reading clause; the exact statement is short. Applied one event at a time, the terms reproduce the joint Bayesian update; terms arriving together take gain $U^+_{M_i}/V_k$. This is checked algebraically and numerically to $10^{-12}$ (C8), and falsifier (i) now names the event-by-event reading.
- **M2, M5 and the reading notes landed as the check proposed, re-derived first.** M2: "shows no compounding" in the segment's Epistemic Status and in `#impl-code-structure`. The OUTLINE and `#der-principled-decision-integration` already said it, and the README §3 now does. ## What I would look at, if I were the verifier

1. **Section (7)'s null as an instrument.** Eighty-one pairs moved its p-values by a factor of three to five, and in obsidian-linter its fitted exponential calls the long high-$d$ gaps work. Is a pure exponential plus the fitted idle component the right null, or should the null's exponential be constrained to stay below its idle component?
2. **`#der-change-expectation-baseline`.** Is "a developer meets the system in front of them as time-first sampling" right, and is per-system sampling ever the developer's situation (a portfolio reviewed system by system)? Caves's own setting needs a finite prior mean; a $1/t$ survival tail has a logarithmically divergent one. I believe the posterior $w(T)/Q(t_0)$ does not depend on it, because only the normalizing constant $P(I)$ does, but that deserves a second derivation.
3. **ink.** After filtering it is mostly one person's 365 pairs, weakly identified by the mixture: a label-swapped fit, calling a 7-minute component time away, has slightly higher likelihood (C3). Canon still calls it one of three histories. Whether it earns that place, or should be reported as a history where the method cannot separate work from time away, is a fair question.
4. **The communication-gain derivation's regime.** It rests on independent errors across terms. `#deriv-tempo-additivity` has the correlated case for a batch; the event-by-event reading with a persistent shared bias is its saturation regime, which the paragraph names only by pointer.
5. **The §F.4 reassignment** to $U_{\text{src}}$, judged against the segment's definitions.
