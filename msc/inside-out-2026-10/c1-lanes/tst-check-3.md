## Findings

### F1. `#der-change-expectation-baseline`: scale ignorance does give the baseline, placed on the system in progress

The landing replaced "the mathematical consequence of maximum ignorance under Jeffreys' prior" with "whenever the prior over its lifetime has a survival function falling as $1/t$". The new subsection ends: "Under that sampling, scale ignorance alone gives no baseline, and the baseline rests on the $1/t$ tail." Epistemic Status says the Jeffreys derivation "does not apply to a system observed because it is alive now". Read with Jeffreys' prior placed on the population density $w$, as the subsection's opening sentence places it, both statements are true. Read as statements about ignorance of scale, which is how "scale ignorance alone" and the headline it replaced read, both are false, and Caves's paper, which the landing read to make this correction, contains the result that shows it. The correction was right about Jeffreys' prior on $w$, and its wording went past that.

The derivation, in Caves's notation, under exactly the observation the segment now assumes (a system found in progress, with births equally likely at any time):

1. A system found in progress has its lifetime weighted by its length, $p(T \mid I) = T\,w(T)/\bar T$ (Eq. 20), and given its lifetime its age is uniform, $p(t_0 \mid T, I) = 1/T$ (Eq. 23). So the uniform-age likelihood is not peculiar to per-system sampling. It holds under the segment's own time-first sampling; what changes is the prior it multiplies.
2. Ignorance of time scale for a system known to be in progress is invariance of $p(t_0, t_f \mid I) \propto w(t_0 + t_f)$ under rescaling past and future together. That forces $w(\alpha T) = w(T)/\alpha^2$, so $w \propto T^{-2}$ (Eqs. 31–32). Caves: "knowing that a phenomenon is in progress, you cannot identify any time scales associated with the phenomenon either into the past or into the future", and "$w_g(T)$ has a scale-free status similar to that found by Jaynes for the Jeffreys prior."
3. Equivalently, $p(T \mid I) \propto T \cdot T^{-2} = 1/T$, which has the form of Jeffreys' prior, placed on the duration of the system in progress rather than on $w$. (The naming is mine; Caves says only that $w_g$ has a scale-free status "similar to" Jeffreys'.) The population density $w = T^{-2}$ has survival function $Q(t) = 1/t$ exactly, the segment's assumed tail with $c = 1$. The posterior $w(T)/Q(t_0) = t_0/T^2$ holds at every age, not only asymptotically, and the median remaining life is $t_0$.

So Buch's computation, Jeffreys' prior times the uniform-age likelihood, is exact under time-first sampling when its prior is read as belonging to the system in progress. Caves calls it flawed because he reads the prior as $w$, which pairs a likelihood conditioned on the system being in progress with a prior that is not. Both statements hold, each about its own reading. What fails is scale ignorance about lifetimes in general. Scale ignorance about the system in front of you gives the baseline exactly. That is the strengthening: under the segment's own observation premise, the baseline is again a consequence of ignorance, exact at every age, and the hazard mixture is a second, independent source of the same tail. One difference matters: $w = T^{-2}$ is improper (it does not integrate near zero), as Jeffreys' prior is, while the hazard mixture is a proper prior with the same tail. Gott's reply, which Caves endorses (p. 7), is that an unnormalizable prior is acceptable when the posterior normalizes, and $t_0/T^2$ does; the segment can keep both routes for that reason.

Two smaller points on the same segment. (a) The observation Assumption says the observer's time "is uniform over a span much longer than lifetimes". Under a $1/t$ tail the mean lifetime is infinite, so no span is longer than lifetimes, and Caves's Eqs. 16–17 assume a finite mean. The derivation needs only that births occur at a constant rate up to the time the system was born. With births at rate $r$, the expected number of systems alive now with age in $[t_0, t_0 + dt_0]$ and lifetime in $[T, T + dT]$ is $r\,w(T)\,dT\,dt_0$ for $T \geq t_0$. That is finite whatever the mean, and conditioning on one found at age $t_0$ gives $w(T)/Q(t_0)$. This is the second derivation the integrator asked for (item 2): $\bar T$ enters only $P(I)$ and the normalization of $p(T \mid I)$, never the posterior. (b) The subsection title "Jeffreys' prior, and which observation it needs" and its tag "conditional on per-system sampling" locate the condition in the sampling. Point 1 above shows the condition is in where the prior is placed.

*Suggested, for the integrator to judge.* Summary: "…It follows exactly, at every age, from ignorance of the time scale of the system found in progress, and at large age from any unknown constant termination hazard whose prior density at zero hazard is finite and positive; each gives the prior survival function the $1/t$ tail the derivation uses." Replace the last subsection with one titled for scale ignorance about a system in progress: points 1–3 above, then one sentence that Jeffreys' prior placed on $w$, conditioned on survival, does not normalize and gives no baseline. Epistemic Status: the scale-ignorance route is *exact* at every age, with an improper prior whose posterior is proper, given that the developer's ignorance concerns the system in front of them (the segment's reading, like the observation premise); the hazard mixture is the proper source of the same tail. Lineage: add that Caves gives $w_g$ the scale-free status for a phenomenon known to be in progress that Jeffreys' prior has before any observation (his Eqs. 31–32). Assumption: "systems begin at a constant rate over the past" instead of the span clause. The record took this as a broken check (`review:tst-lane-check-3#C8`). On reflection that overstates it: the derivation stands, and the two sentences are true read narrowly and false read broadly. I annotated C8 to say the outcome should be read as narrowed, an incompleteness with a strengthening available, not a broken derivation.

### F2. `#hyp-communication-gain`: a source's miscalibration is persistent, and then the event-by-event reading over-trusts

The new Derived block puts $U_{\text{src},j}$ into $V_k$ and applies the terms "as their events arrive", with errors "independent across terms". Read across events from one source, that treats $j$'s miscalibration as fresh noise on each message. The segment defines $U_{\text{src},j}$ as uncertainty about $j$'s "model calibration and domain competence", a property of the source that persists across its messages. Persistent error shared by successive events is the regime of `#deriv-tempo-additivity`'s saturation theorem ("[Derived (saturation-under-persistent-bias)]"). No number of messages buys precision about the quantity past $1/U_M^{(0)} + 1/\sigma_s^2$. An update that charges $U_{\text{src},j}$ afresh at each message shrinks $U_{M_i}$ at the additive rate while the true error stalls at the bias floor, which is the gain collapse that segment describes. The block's closing pointer names the synchronized batch, the within-instant case. *Suggested, after that sentence:* "Read event by event, the terms count each message's noise as fresh. That fits channel noise. A source's miscalibration is usually shared by all its messages; then repeated messages from $j$ are the persistent-bias regime of #deriv-tempo-additivity, whose information about the quantity saturates at precision $1/U_{\text{src},j}$, and $U_{\text{src},j}$ belongs in the trust meta-model as a persistent state, not in each event's noise." This is the integrator's own item 4, and I think it deserves the sentence.

### F4. Item 3's "reject it in none" states no criterion

Against section (7)'s null, each history reaches a tail share near 2% or below on at least one statistic at one cap: obsidian-linter $a$ 1.3% at 240 minutes, ink $b$ 2.1% and $a$ 2.0% at 480, the corpus $b$ 1.5% at 60. Round 2's check called 0.3–2% a rejection; round 3 calls 1.3–9.5% "lean". Both may be defensible. The four caps reuse the same gaps and two statistics are read, so a per-cap 5% is not the right threshold, and a Bonferroni bound over eight correlated readings (0.6%) is too strict. But the reader is given neither the threshold nor the reason. Say which criterion makes "reject in none" true, for example: "no history's smallest tail share survives a correction for the eight correlated readings (four caps, two statistics)". Which criterion is right is the integrator's call.

### F5. Ink's one-idle fit is not the likelihood maximum, and canon does not say so

From 30 random starts (block B), each form's best fit in ink swaps the labels. Its "idle" component has a median of 7 minutes, and the component carrying $d$ is the broad one (s.d. 1.76). Its log-likelihood exceeds the toolkit's by 2.0 (power), 2.1 (exponential) and 1.4 (nested). The integrator found this (C3), and the README's "the one-idle fit starts with its idle component above the working one, which is the reading the method needs" alludes to it. But neither the README nor the segment's Limits says that ink's reported numbers (0.30 $[-0.04, 0.76]$, the medians, $b$) come from a local optimum chosen for its reading. The integrator's C3 asks "Is the one-idle mixture's fit the maximum of its likelihood … in each history?" and records `confirmed`, though for ink the answer is no; `narrowed` fits. The conclusion "not distinguished" is unaffected, and is if anything strengthened. On the integrator's item 3, whether ink earns its place: I think it does, as a history in which the method cannot separate work from time away, provided Limits says so in one clause. *Suggested, Limits:* "In ink the one-idle mixture is weakly identified: its likelihood is highest with the labels swapped (a 7-minute 'idle' component), and the reported fit is the one with time away above working time."
