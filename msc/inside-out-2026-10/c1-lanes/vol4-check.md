## Findings, in order of consequence

### 1. The restore case rests on two different criteria, and they disagree (A14)

`#scope-agent-identity` decides continuity in two places by two tests. Lines 19 and 42 (and `#def-identity-sufficiency` line 60) use concurrency: a restore "with no second copy running" is a state discontinuity on one trajectory. Line 64 (and `#obs-context-turnover` lines 15 and 40) uses the store: a session continues the agent when it is "that store's single continuation".

Take the ordinary restore. A backup of the store is made at $t_0$. The original keeps running to $t_1$, writing to its store, and then fails. The backup is loaded and runs from $t_1$. No second copy is running at any moment, so the concurrency test says one trajectory. But the $t_0$ store now has two continuations, one after the other: the original's run over $(t_0, t_1]$ and the restored run. The store test says the restored run is not the single continuation of anything the $t_1$ entity wrote. `#def-chronica` (lines 16 and 36) states the clone problem as "duplicating an agent's state and exposing the copies to different futures", which needs no simultaneity; the backup state has been exposed to a second future.

This verdict depends on which $\mathcal C_t$ is meant. The integrator uses $\mathcal C_t$ as the real sequence of events ("the dropped interval stays in $\mathcal C_t(E)$"); under that reading the prefix condition holds across any restore, and the record matters only for evidence. The carve below reads $\mathcal C_t$ through CHRONICA, as A12's "identity by construction" does, and finding 3 says that identity is itself unsettled. The two-criteria inconsistency stands under either reading, and so does the `#def-chronica` clone criterion (one state exposed to two futures). What decides it, under the record reading, is what happens to the record. Since A12, `#def-proprium-mapping` lists "CHRONICA is a record of $\mathcal C_t$" among its identities by construction, and $\mathrm{Id}^{(i)}$ reads "$\mathcal C_t(E)$ is a prefix of $\mathcal C_{t+1:}(E)$". If the restore rolls CHRONICA back to $t_0$, the record after the restore does not extend the record at $t_1$, and the prefix condition fails. If CHRONICA is append-only outside the backup and only the state is restored, the record runs on, the restore is logged in it, and the integrator's account is exactly right: a boundary with a stale state, costing identity sufficiency.

So the carve that makes both sides true is a strengthening, not a softening:

- a **state restore**, where the append-only record continues and only the state is reloaded, is a state discontinuity on one trajectory ($\mathrm{Id}^{(i)} = 1$, with the cost in the ratio);
- a **record rollback**, where the record itself is reset to the backup, is a sequential fork: the restored run is a second continuation of the $t_0$ record ($\mathrm{Id}^{(i)} = 0$ for the continuation of the $t_1$ entity).

That carve also gives INDIVISUM and append-only CHRONICA a precise job, which `#hyp-checkpoint-forking-failure-modes` asks for.

Two smaller points in the same text. `#def-identity-sufficiency` line 60 still says $\mathrm{Id}^{(i)}$ "takes value $1$ except on substrate-failure or fork" in the sentence before "a restoration from backup … also leaves it at $1$"; restores usually follow a substrate failure, so the reader cannot tell which clause governs. And the store test, read literally, makes concurrent sessions that read and write one store a fork (see the question at the end).

Resolver: a C.1 integrator for A14 (not the original integrator, who also wrote the concurrency test). The change is to one criterion in `#scope-agent-identity` lines 19/42/64, `#obs-context-turnover` lines 15/40 and `#def-identity-sufficiency` line 60, then finding 2 follows.

### 4. Whether full AXIOMATA in a belief-side query loosens W₁ or leaves it depends on an unstated premise (A11)

The third consequence in the new `#def-auxilia-hierarchy` paragraph reads: *"Whatever is in a belief-side Auxilia's context is part of $q_M$, so the objective layer of AXIOMATA enters $I(q_M; G^{\text{op}})$. Theorem 1 closes the processing channel *because* $q_M$ has no $G_W$ argument (line 75, "D-pathway-2 closed"). After A12, AXIOMATA holds the slowest layer of $O_t$. If that layer is read as part of $G_W$, a belief-side query that carries full AXIOMATA takes a $G_W$ argument, Theorem 1's premise fails, and the update is no longer Class-1-by-structure. If it is read as a fixed parameter of the wrapper on loop timescales, it is context like any other, line 105 applies, and the integrator's "structural but loosened" is right. The paragraph needs to say which reading it relies on; nothing in it or in `#def-proprium-mapping` decides. `#der-directed-separation` line 128 states the test in one line: "does the belief-update query to the underlying component carry $G_W$ in its input or not?"

Under the first reading, a belief-side Auxilia is a W₁ component only if it receives the parts of AXIOMATA that are not $O_t$ content, which makes that a requirement of W₁ rather than "a design choice the construction leaves open". Under the second, the design-choice wording stands, conditional on the parameter reading. It is also the second place (after finding 3) where the unplaced "self-description" matters: "passing only AXIOMATA's self-description keeps the bound tight" has no referent until self-description is placed. Even placed in $M_t$, self-description can carry goal information about $G^{\text{op}}$ (what an entity says it is correlates with what it wants), so "tight" should be "the selection-channel bound applies, at whatever $I(q_M; G^{\text{op}})$ the self-description carries".

The second reading is the strengthening, and it is worth a spike rather than a sentence. AXIOMATA is quasi-static at loop timescales (`#der-temporal-nesting`). One could try to show *epoch-conditional* separation: within an AXIOMATA epoch the slow $O_t$ layer is a fixed parameter of the wrapper, so $f_M$ is separated conditional on it, and the coupling appears only at the developmental timescale, when AXIOMATA is revised. If that holds, it licenses the integrator's sentence with its condition stated, and it names where motivated perception could enter an ELI: at AXIOMATA revision. The integrator's own open question (is $G^{\text{op}}$ only the fast per-step goal?) is on the other axis and does not decide this.

### 8. The strengthened step in `#hyp-substrate-transfer-asymmetry` needs two premises it does not have

The new derived step: *"If, in both directions, the source's compression and the target's reconstruction can realize a rate-distortion-optimal channel through the bottleneck, then each direction attains the shared ceiling, and $S_{\text{id}}(X_1 \to X_2) = S_{\text{id}}(X_2 \to X_1)$."* It is tagged conditional on "the bound of #deriv-identity-sufficiency-rate-bound being attained in its matched-channel regime".

(a) That segment does not say its bound is attained. Its "exact in the matched-channel regime" (line 57) is the validity of the functional form; tightness is listed as open three times (lines 69, 88, 90: "whether the rate-distortion floor is achieved by any realizable compression schedule or is strictly loose"). And in general it is not attained: the achievable set is the information-bottleneck frontier, $\max_{I(M;\mathcal C) \le B} I(M; \text{identity})$, which lies strictly below the line $I(M;\text{identity}) = B$ unless the identity-relevant content of $\mathcal C_t$ can be extracted bit for bit. The step can be repaired without attainment: with unrestricted optimal compression at the same budget in both directions, each direction achieves the same IB-frontier value, which is symmetric for the same reason the ceiling is.

(b) That repair, and the original, need the joint law of $(\mathcal C_t, \text{identity}_{t+1:})$ to be the same in both directions. `#def-identity-sufficiency` fixes $S_{\text{id}}$ relative to a continuation policy $\pi_E^{\text{cont}}$ (its conditioning conventions). After a transfer, $E$ continues on the target substrate, so the continuation policy, and with it the law of $\text{identity}_{t+1:}$ and the denominator $I(\mathcal C_t; \text{identity}_{t+1:})$, differ by direction. With different laws, matched channels in both directions give different achieved values. So an asymmetry can sit inside $S_{\text{id}}$'s own policy-relativity, which cuts against the segment's headline ("not derivable from $S_{\text{id}}$ alone") and against "the asymmetry's origin lies in mechanisms outside the $S_{\text{id}}$ formalism". It is close to the first candidate origin (substrate inductive biases), but it lives in the definition's conditioning, not outside it.

(c) The $\min(C_1, C_2)$ model gives each substrate one capacity for both roles. The segment's own localization ("which substrate's restriction binds, in which role") is a role-dependent capacity. With $C_i^{\text{enc}} \ne C_i^{\text{dec}}$ the bottleneck is $\min(C_1^{\text{enc}}, C_2^{\text{dec}})$ one way and $\min(C_2^{\text{enc}}, C_1^{\text{dec}})$ the other, and the ceiling itself is asymmetric. The integrator flagged the model as unexamined; this is where it binds.

Resolver: a C.1 integrator or a short spike: restate the step on the IB frontier, add the direction-independence premise, and say what $S_{\text{id}}$'s policy-relativity does to the headline.

### 9. The $\Delta S_{\text{turnover}}$ lower bound is false (adjacent; `#obs-context-turnover`)

The displayed bound is $\Delta S_{\text{turnover}} \ge 1 - I(M_{\tau_k^-}; M_{\tau_{k+1}}) / H(M_{\tau_k^-})$. Counterexample: let $M_{\tau_k^-} = (Y, N)$, where $Y$ is one bit that is a sufficient statistic of $\mathcal C_t$ for the future, and $N$ is 99 bits independent of everything predictive. Let the reconstruction keep $Y$ exactly and drop $N$. Then $S(M_{\tau_k^-}) = S(M_{\tau_{k+1}}) = 1$, so $\Delta S_{\text{turnover}} = 0$, while the right-hand side is $1 - 1/100 = 0.99$. The bound normalizes by all of $H(M^-)$, but $S$ counts only predictive information. The same mismatch makes the segment's "when externalization captures everything relevant, $\Delta S \approx 0$" inconsistent with its own bound. The Epistemic Status says the bound "follows from information-theoretic definitions given that fact". `TODO.md` line 454 already says the bound "needs formal repair before exact status". The A14 argument does not need the bound (see *What holds*), so this finding lands on the bound and not on A14.

Resolver: a C.1 integrator or the Vol-3 lane: replace with a bound in predictive terms (for example, on $I(\mathcal C_t; o_{t+1:} \mid M_{\tau_{k+1}}, a)$ via what the store carries), or delete it.
