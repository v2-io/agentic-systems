### From `reality_monitoring.md`

**RM-1. Johnson's source-monitoring framework is the human prior art for `disc-structural-imagination`'s level-confusion diagnosis of hallucination, and it bears on the tag hypothesis itself.** Canon (`disc-structural-imagination`, discussion-grade, draft) proposes $X_t=(M_t,G_t,W_t)$ with $W_t$ "explicitly tagged as ungrounded", and diagnoses hallucination as a $W_t$ element promoted to $M_t$ without grounding. ### From `event_cognition.md`

**EC-1. The surprise-measure taxonomy, and which measures human boundary data favour.** The note separates seven quantities: Shannon surprisal, Bayesian surprise (KL posterior‖prior over hypotheses; Itti & Baldi 2009, DOI 10.1016/j.visres.2008.09.007, abstract-verified, the KL form [K]), KL between successive *predictive* distributions (which Kumar et al. 2023 call "Bayesian surprise" but is formally different), predictive entropy, epistemic variance, change-point posterior $P(r_t=0\mid x_{1:t})$, and belief-consistent vs belief-inconsistent surprise (Antony et al. 2021). Across Kumar 2023, Antony 2021 and Nguyen 2024/2025, raw surprisal predicts human event boundaries poorly and belief-change and uncertainty measures do better, with small absolute effects (cross-validated $r\approx 0.05$–$0.12$ for text; about 20% of variance against a 36% ceiling for video). Canon at HEAD: `form-event-driven-dynamics` Working Note (line 102) still records that $\mathcal I(e)$ is a mutual information used in prose as a per-event surprise (the scout's A2); canon has no hit for Itti, Baldi, "Bayesian surprise" or Kumar. Disposition: supporting sources for the A2 fix the lead is routing; specifically, if canon introduces a per-event quantity it should say *which* of these it is, and the predictive-KL vs latent-KL distinction is the one most often conflated in the literature. Feeds C.1 (A2) and F.2.

### From `consolidation_schemas_maps.md`

**CS-1. A concrete stability upper bound for `form-consolidation-dynamics`, from McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995 eqs. (3)–(5).** The open ledger item `item:todo-stability-upper-bound-for-form-consolida-be13` (owner C.1) says the bound $(1-\lambda)\lt\phi(\nu_{\text{consol}},\text{budget})$ is stated with $\phi$ undefined while the window's existence is labelled Derived, and asks someone to "derive $\phi$ for one concrete consolidation mechanism" before relabelling. MMO 1995's simplified consolidation model is such a mechanism, and the note transcribes it from the full text [V-ft]: fast-store strength $S_h(t)=S_h(0)e^{-D_h t}$; reinstatement probability per unit time $r_h S_h(t)$; slow-store growth $dS_c/dt=C\,S_h(t)\,[1-S_c]-D_c S_c$ with $C=\epsilon\, r_h$ (slow-store learning rate times reinstatement rate, i.e. consolidation cadence). With $D_c=0$ this integrates exactly: $1-S_c(\infty)=(1-S_c(0))\exp(-C S_h(0)/D_h)$. So the slow store reaches a required strength $s^\ast$ iff $D_h\le \epsilon\, r_h\, S_h(0)/\ln\!\big((1-S_c(0))/(1-s^\ast)\big)$: an upper bound on the fast store's forgetting rate that is linear in consolidation cadence $r_h$, which is the monotone form the segment asserts. I checked the closed form numerically against MMO's own fitted parameter sets (agreement to six decimals) and that with $D_c\gt 0$ the peak slow-store strength still falls monotonically in $D_h$ (scratch script, not committed). Tier: the integration is exact; the identification of MMO's $D_h$ with the segment's $(1-\lambda)$ on $M^{\text{fast}}$, and of $s^\ast$ with "enough retained signal for the slow sub-state", is a modelling step the integrator has to state. Disposition: added as a source to the existing C.1 item (strengthen route), not a new item. The segment already cites MMO 1995 but only for the factorization, not this model.

**CS-2. A second, different upper bound: replay-driven transfer can overfit (go-CLS).** Sun, Advani, Spruston, Saxe & Fitzgerald 2023 (Nature Neuroscience, read in full text by the note's author; Methods equations [V-ft]): in a linear teacher–student–notebook model, unlimited replay into the student with a noisy teacher drives generalization error *up*; the remedy is to stop replay-driven learning when held-out error stops falling ("memories only consolidate when it aids generalization"). This bounds the *amount* of consolidation into the slow store given the environment's signal-to-noise ratio, which is a different object from CS-1's bound on fast-store forgetting given cadence. `form-consolidation-dynamics` defines consolidation by its objective (IB-gap reduction on replayed pseudo-events) and its "Predictive statement" scales needed machinery with structure, budget gap and regularity rate; nothing in it says more consolidation can hurt. Canon has 0 hits for go-CLS or Sun et al. The scout's (d) item "replay regulation as the missing stability upper bound" conflated the two bounds; they are separate and both real. Disposition: source added to the same C.1 item with the distinction stated; F.2 for the citation. The go-CLS model is analytically tractable, so it is a candidate for direct adoption under prior-art integration if the integrator wants a derived rather than cited statement.

**CS-3. Canon labels the CLS factorization an "External theorem"; the evidence supports that only for artificial networks.** `form-consolidation-dynamics`'s derivation table (line 97) lists "CLS factorization (hippocampal fast / neocortical slow) as canonical (N1) instance" with justification "External theorem (CLS literature)", and the Discussion (line 133) states "CLS's core claim is that hippocampal replay during sleep supports interleaved neocortical learning". So the honest restatement is stronger where it matters, not weaker: the row becomes "(N1) instance, established for distributed artificial networks (McCloskey & Cohen 1989; MMO 1995); proposed for hippocampus–neocortex by CLS, with offline reactivation supported (Hu et al. 2020) and its interleaving role contested", and "theorem" goes. Disposition: C.1 item (label tracks truth), F.2 for the added citations. Low effort, but it is in the derivation table that readers take as the claim register.
