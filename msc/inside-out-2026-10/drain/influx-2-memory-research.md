### From `reality_monitoring.md`

**RM-1. Johnson's source-monitoring framework is the human prior art for `disc-structural-imagination`'s level-confusion diagnosis of hallucination, and it bears on the tag hypothesis itself.** Canon (`disc-structural-imagination`, discussion-grade, draft) proposes $X_t=(M_t,G_t,W_t)$ with $W_t$ "explicitly tagged as ungrounded", and diagnoses hallucination as a $W_t$ element promoted to $M_t$ without grounding. Its Working Notes already owe a relata pass on "the mental-simulation/prospection literature". The note supplies the matching literature, verified at citation level: Johnson & Raye 1981 (reality monitoring: telling perceived from imagined memories), Johnson, Hashtroudi & Lindsay 1993 (source monitoring: source is *not* read from a stored tag but attributed at retrieval by judging a memory's qualities), Garry et al. 1996 (imagination inflation: imagining a childhood event raises confidence that it happened), Foley & Johnson 1985 (children confuse did-vs-imagined but not did-vs-watched), Woolley & Wellman 1993 (3-year-olds often say imagination reflects reality), Henkel et al. 1998 (older adults' perceived-vs-imagined confusion is specific to similar items). What this adds beyond a citation: canon's hypothesis is that an *explicit* tag prevents mis-typing; the human evidence is that a system with *no* tag, attributing source by features, shows exactly the predicted failure (imagination inflation), and fails most where perceived and imagined contents are similar. That is an existing empirical instance of the segment's falsification handle seen from the untagged side, and a design note: a tag that is inferred from content rather than stored will fail on similar items. Disposition: absent from canon; worth landing at discussion-grade in that segment's Discussion as prior art and as an instance (F.2). The DLPFC-in-REM material (§9 of the note) is a hypothesis per the note's own verdict; set aside for ASF.

### From `event_cognition.md`

**EC-1. The surprise-measure taxonomy, and which measures human boundary data favour.** The note separates seven quantities: Shannon surprisal, Bayesian surprise (KL posterior‖prior over hypotheses; Itti & Baldi 2009, DOI 10.1016/j.visres.2008.09.007, abstract-verified, the KL form [K]), KL between successive *predictive* distributions (which Kumar et al. 2023 call "Bayesian surprise" but is formally different), predictive entropy, epistemic variance, change-point posterior $P(r_t=0\mid x_{1:t})$, and belief-consistent vs belief-inconsistent surprise (Antony et al. 2021). Across Kumar 2023, Antony 2021 and Nguyen 2024/2025, raw surprisal predicts human event boundaries poorly and belief-change and uncertainty measures do better, with small absolute effects (cross-validated $r\approx 0.05$–$0.12$ for text; about 20% of variance against a 36% ceiling for video). Canon at HEAD: `form-event-driven-dynamics` Working Note (line 102) still records that $\mathcal I(e)$ is a mutual information used in prose as a per-event surprise (the scout's A2); canon has no hit for Itti, Baldi, "Bayesian surprise" or Kumar. Disposition: supporting sources for the A2 fix the lead is routing; specifically, if canon introduces a per-event quantity it should say *which* of these it is, and the predictive-KL vs latent-KL distinction is the one most often conflated in the literature. Feeds C.1 (A2) and F.2.

**EC-2. Prediction error has two roles in latent-cause learning, which maps onto AAT's parametric-vs-structural split.** Gershman, Monfils, Norman & Niv 2017 (eLife, equations [V]): the same prediction error is an associative learning signal *and* evidence for a new latent cause; small errors modify the existing cause's parameters, large ones create a new cause. SEM (Franklin et al. 2020, equations [V] from PDF and code) scores repeat / restart / switch at every step as Bayesian model comparison with log-likelihood $-\lVert x-f(x)\rVert^2/2\beta$. BOCPD (Adams & MacKay 2007) gives the run-length recursion and hazard. Canon has no event-segmentation or boundary operation; `deriv-update-detection-latency` concedes a CUSUM/GLR sidecar beats the log-odds accumulator, and `deriv-adaptive-gain-dynamics` carries the IMM case (the scout's C5). Disposition: this is the formal prior art the scout's C5 proposal (promote IMM to a boundary operation) would need; the Gershman 2017 "two roles of one error" statement is the cleanest bridge to `result-structural-adaptation-necessity` (when parametric update gives way to structural change). Absent from canon; at most `sketch`/`discussion-grade` as a scope note on what AAT's mismatch machinery does *not* yet decide (when to open a new model rather than update the old). Feeds G.2 and F.2.

**EC-3. Endogenous goal or task-set switches are sufficient for memory boundaries; boundaries are retirement operations, not only error spikes.** Wang & Egner 2022 (Cognition, abstract-level): cued and voluntary task-set changes create event boundaries in order and distance memory with stimulus, response, cue and difficulty shifts ruled out. Shim et al. 2024 (preprint): identical sequences produce boundaries only when the working-memory task is tied to the segments. Güler et al. 2025: stable context changes segment strongly, transient prediction-error-only changes barely do. The note's verdict, at its own ~70%: the surviving core of event segmentation theory is model *retirement* and its memory consequences; prediction error is one trigger among goal change, schedule, context stability and uncertainty. For ASF this bears on two places. (a) Any boundary or archiving operation AAT adds cannot be triggered only by $M_t$-side mismatch; a $G_t$-side trigger is needed, and `der-directed-separation`'s selection-vs-processing distinction is where it can legitimately sit (the scout's (d) "goal-structured archiving" item already argues this from lesson 09b). (b) Solway et al. 2014 (PLoS CB, [V]) show the evidence-maximizing behavioural hierarchy on Schapiro's community graph is exactly the community partition, and that the optimal hierarchy minimizes codelength; Sutton, Precup & Singh 1999 (options, equations [V] from the TR) define option termination $\beta(s)$. `form-strategy-complexity-cost` Working Note (line 202) already asks whether node merging is "the Options framework / hierarchical RL"; canon has 0 hits for Sutton–Precup–Singh, Solway or Botvinick. Disposition: (b) is a citation-and-instance item for that Working Note (F.2); (a) joins the read-the-record / archive cluster below rather than standing alone.

### From `retrieved_context_and_classical.md`

**CR-1. LLM attention is a published instance of cue-driven reinstatement of the raw record.** Li, Zhou, Benna & Mattar 2024 (NeurIPS; arXiv 2405.14992): induction heads are behaviourally and mechanistically CMR-like (temporal clustering, forward asymmetry), and ablating them hurts in-context learning more than random heads. Salvatore & Zhang 2025 (Communications Psychology, DOI 10.1038/s44271-025-00322-6): seq2seq attention over encoder states corresponds in expectation to CMR context reinstatement. Canon at HEAD: `the-reality-model-intro` Working Note (line 61) already says LLMs "literally *do* carry raw history (the context window $\approx\mathcal C_t$)" and suggests a hybrid $M_t$; `scope-channel-collapse` line 50 says the forward pass updates the model "via attention over the prompt's prior context". Neither names the operation. This bears directly on the scout's structural question (B4/B5: AAT's core has no operation for reading the record). In logogenic agents the read operation exists, is attention, and has a published retrieved-context characterization. Disposition: joins the read-the-record cluster as a concrete instance (G.2, F.2); it does not by itself change any derivation.

**CR-2. A bank of exponential forgetters with log-spaced rates is an established construction with neural evidence.** Howard et al. 2014 (J Neurosci, equations [R]), Shankar & Howard 2012: $dF(s,t)/dt=-sF+f$ over a spectrum of $s$ is the real Laplace transform of the input history; TCM is the single-$s$ case (the note's inference); Bright et al. 2020 and Cao et al. 2022/2024 report a spectrum of time constants and log-compressed time in entorhinal, hippocampal and mPFC cells. MCM (Mozer et al. 2009; form [BK]) uses the same kind of bank for spacing. Canon has 0 hits for Howard, Shankar, Laplace-in-this-sense. Disposition: the external instance the scout's (d) "power-law forgetting as a spectrum of $\lambda$" proposal needs. The form of human forgetting is itself contested (Averell & Heathcote 2011: exponential fits individuals best, Bayesian selection favours power), so this supports "a spectrum is possible and is what brains appear to build", not "forgetting is a power law". Whether `schema-strategy-persistence`'s inequality survives a spectrum is the open question; no item yet exists for it (grep of items/ for "power-law" is empty). Feeds G.2 at `conjecture`/`sketch` tier if anyone takes it; F.2 for the citations.
