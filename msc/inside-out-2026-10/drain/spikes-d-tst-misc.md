- *Weakest channel for weakest direction.* Three spikes read `#deriv-matrix-persistence-condition` as "the weakest channel is the bottleneck". The segment sums channels into one matrix tempo, $\mathcal T = \sum_k \nu^{(k)} K^{(k)}$, and binds on the weakest direction of mismatch space. That makes it a present defect, not a stranded addition: **`item:tst-canon-reads-the-matrix-loewner-persistence-c` (C.1)**.
- *The bifurcation without two basins.* The unmaintainability spike's own assumptions (an unbounded $\psi$, no finite steady-state $W$ below $\rho^\ast$) make its persistence inequality fail at every $\rho \in (0, \rho^\ast)$ in steady state. Its low-$\rho$ basin exists only under the $W$ ceiling it says to drop. - *"Theorem-grade" by substitution.* The headline wrapper persistence inequality defines tempo as $1/T_\text{restart}$ and disturbance as failure rate times damage. It never shows the sector premise for reset dynamics, so it is a heuristic analogy. The crash-early "must" overclaims: recovery inside a component need not consult the goal. **`item:otp-supervision-as-a-worked-instance-of-der-clas` (G.2)** carries both corrections, together with what is landable at discussion grade (restart strategies as widths of one commitment, the stash partition, the ETS resolution, BEAM's zero instantiation cost).
