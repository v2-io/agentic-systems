## 1. The one picture

Four bodies of work converged on the same sentences, and the shape that came out is simpler than any of them stated it. Three quantities had been run together across Part II and Volume 3, and each claim is now about exactly one of them:

- **Identification** (#der-loop-interventional-access, #def-value-object): whether an agent's own action–outcome data identifies the effect of its actions. Ignorability is free at the policy's own inputs $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$ — the agent's state, plans and memory are the *adjustment set*, not the confounder — and what a deterministic policy lacks there is positivity; without a restriction on the environment class nothing off-policy is identified at a visited decision state, and the restriction (outcome-sufficiency of a coarsening, with positivity inside it) cannot be tested on-policy under determinism. The three conditions carry their standard names: positivity, sequential ignorability, consistency.
- **Goal-dependence** of a belief (#deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound, $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ in #der-directed-separation): how much the belief varies with the goal. Bounded by transferred goal information $I(G; M_{\tau^+} \mid e, M^-) = \kappa\, H(G \mid e, M^-)$; saturates for any noiseless coupling; produced by calibrated allocation as much as by distortion.
- **Distortion** of a belief (#der-directed-separation Scope Condition, #scope-observation-ambiguity-modulation): its departure from the conditional law of the world given the agent's information — the reliability term. This is what "goal-blind" was reaching for and what the degradation norm is about. Bounded model-free only by the size of the processor's reweighting; its dependence on observation sharpness is a hypothesis (fixed-gain tilt), with a regression test.
