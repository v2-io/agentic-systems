### Part I: the persistence and sector-condition cluster (re-derived)

This is the largest change to the theory and the one I spent the most time on. Six commits across `deriv-sector-condition`, `result-persistence-condition`, `form-sector-condition`, `deriv-persistence-cost`, `deriv-stochastic-non-exit`, `def-mismatch-signal` and their dependents.

**What changed.** (1) The state of the persistence dynamics is now the estimation error, not the innovation, and its disturbance includes the channel noise the correction itself injects: for linear correction at gain $\alpha$ on a channel with noise intensity $\sigma_\nu$, $\sigma_w^2 = \sigma_{\text{env}}^2 + \alpha^2\sigma_\nu^2$. (2) Prop A.1S's bound is restated on the *killed* second moment $\mathbb E[\lVert\delta_t\rVert^2;\ \tau_R \gt t]$, not the stopped moment. (3) A new Prop A.1S-$\tau$: the first-exit time of any sector corrector from $\mathcal B_R$ is bounded below by the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck benchmark with the same floor, exponentially in $\kappa_R = \alpha R^2/\sigma_w^2$, with a three-term expansion at the mean-square threshold. (4) The exit exponent, as a function of gain on a fixed white-noise channel, peaks at the Kalman–Bucy gain. (5) The Model S persistence condition becomes a *window* of gains about that optimum, and no gain satisfies it unless the Kalman–Bucy error fits inside the region. (6) The information floor is restated from a universal $n\alpha/2$ to $n(\sigma_{\text{env}}^2/(2D) - \lambda_s)$, which equals $n\alpha/2$ for the Kalman-optimal agent.

**What I re-derived.**

- The gain-injected noise and the Kalman cap. With $\kappa_R(\alpha) = \alpha R^2/(\sigma_{\text{env}}^2 + \alpha^2\sigma_\nu^2)$, $d\kappa_R/d\alpha = 0$ at $\alpha^2 = \sigma_{\text{env}}^2/\sigma_\nu^2$, so the maximizer is $\alpha^\ast = \sigma_{\text{env}}/\sigma_\nu$ with value $R^2/(2\sigma_{\text{env}}\sigma_\nu)$. For the scalar continuous-time steady-state Kalman–Bucy filter with process noise $\sigma_{\text{env}}^2$ and measurement noise $\sigma_\nu^2$ the Riccati equation gives $P = \sigma_{\text{env}}\sigma_\nu$ and gain $K = P/\sigma_\nu^2 = \sigma_{\text{env}}/\sigma_\nu$. Same number. The claim holds.
- The window. The mean-square condition $\alpha \gt n\sigma_w^2/(2R^2)$ with the gain-dependent $\sigma_w^2$ is $n\sigma_\nu^2\alpha^2 - 2R^2\alpha + n\sigma_{\text{env}}^2 \lt 0$. The roots have product $\sigma_{\text{env}}^2/\sigma_\nu^2 = K^2$ (geometric about $K$) and a real pair exists iff $R^2 \gt n\sigma_{\text{env}}\sigma_\nu$, the Kalman–Bucy error fitting inside the region. Holds.
- Killed versus stopped moment. The tag's Prop A.1S bounded $\mathbb E[\lVert\delta_{t\wedge\tau_R}\rVert^2]$ by the OU scale $n\sigma_w^2/(2\alpha)$. That is false under Model S: Dynkin on the stopped process gives $\mathbb E[V(\delta_{t\wedge\tau})] = \mathbb E[V(\delta_t); t \lt \tau] + \tfrac{R^2}{2}P(\tau \leq t)$, and since exit is almost sure (Corollary A.1S.1, which the tag already had), the stopped moment tends to $R^2$, not to the OU scale. The killed moment is what obeys the differential inequality. The correction is right, and it was a real error in the tag's `exact` appendix.
- The OU exit-time benchmark. I integrated the 1-D mean exit time $T(0) = \tfrac{2}{\sigma^2}\int_0^R e^{\alpha y^2/\sigma^2}\int_0^y e^{-\alpha z^2/\sigma^2}\,dz\,dy$ numerically at $\kappa_R = n/2 = 1/2$ and got $\alpha\Phi(0) = 0.5957$, which is the number the new Claim Record states. The comparison argument itself (radial $v = \Phi\circ\lVert\cdot\rVert$ with $\Phi' \leq 0$, so $-F\cdot\nabla v \geq -\alpha r\Phi'$ under the sector condition, then Dynkin) is a standard comparison and holds as written. I did not verify the three-term threshold expansion beyond this one number.
- The Kalman one-step information in `scope-adaptive-system` item (b): $\tfrac12\log(1 + P_t/2) = 0.1347$ nats for the scalar random walk with unit noises and posterior variance $P_t = (\sqrt5-1)/2$. Matches.
- F72, the hard ceiling (next section).

**Judgment.** This cluster is the best thing the day did to the theory. The tag's Part I treated the disturbance as exogenous and gain-independent, so "more gain is always better" was a silent consequence; now the theory says what every filtering engineer knows and what AAT needed to say: under white channel noise (Model S), gain and information are different levers, and raising gain past the optimum on the same observations injects noise. ### F72: the strategic-persistence hard ceiling, deleted (re-derived)

The tag's canon carried `#deriv-strategic-persistence-hard-ceiling` at `status: exact`, with a ceiling $\rho_\Sigma \lt R_\Sigma/2$ on reachable strategic persistence under exponential forgetting. It is an algebra error. Under the discounted Beta-Bernoulli recurrence $n_{t+1} = \lambda n_t + 1$ the stationary count is $n_{\text{eff}} = 1/(1-\lambda)$, and the posterior mean updates as $\hat p_{t+1} = (\lambda n_{\text{eff}}\hat p_t + y)/(\lambda n_{\text{eff}} + 1)$. Since $\lambda n_{\text{eff}} + 1 = n_{\text{eff}}$, the gain is $y$'s coefficient $1/n_{\text{eff}} = 1-\lambda$ exactly, not $1/(n_{\text{eff}}+1)$. With gain $1-\lambda$ the reachable region is $\rho_\Sigma \lt R_\Sigma$ and the factor of two disappears. I did this on paper in two lines.
