Source: `spikes/tst-mining-2026-05-21/02-forensic-mining.md`. Checked at cbd2f345 by grep of `02-tst-core/src/` and `TST-IDEAS.md` (no hits for "repositor", "omission", "Nagappan", "author-count").

1. **F13, the repo boundary.** P5 (the committed chronicle is exteriorized with cryptographic immutability) holds per repository. Across repositories there is no joint hash-chain, so cross-repo causal claims (microservices, multi-repo systems) fall back to temporal-proximity inference, with the identifiability cost the other domains pay. One Discussion sentence in #obs-software-epistemic-properties scoping P5 to a single history; the "virtual root" stitching technique is itself an extra assumption that would need naming.
2. **F17, bugs by omission.** A commit touching $A$ but not $B$ when the chronicle's estimate of their coupling is high is a forward prediction of the estimate that later commits can check (does a follow-up touch $B$?). This is the operational form of "validate the causal estimate" for #hyp-causal-discovery-from-git's research program. Modest; refactoring legitimately breaks historical coupling, so the test needs that confounder named.
3. **F7, organizational metrics.** The mining relays, through Tornhill, that organizational metrics out-predict code metrics for defects. The primary is Nagappan, Murphy & Basili, "The Influence of Organizational Structure on Software Quality", ICSE 2008, which reports precision and recall of failure-proneness prediction (about 0.85), not the correlations $r \approx 0.7$ vs $0.2$ the mining quotes. Read the primary before citing; F.2's lane if it lands as a citation in #meas-coherence-coupling :41–45, which currently cites Conway 1968 and MacCormack, Baldwin & Rusnak 2006.
