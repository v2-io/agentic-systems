# #der-bounded-objective-as-sanity-criterion rests its whole claim on the step from an unbounded satisfaction threshold to instrumental convergence (:33, 'lifted from a behavioral observation to a derived consequence'), which no segment derives; its Max-attainable (:39) names that derivation as owed. A published result bears on its named softener: Turner et al. 2021 ('Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power', NeurIPS) show power-seeking for most reward functions, and Turner & Tadepalli 2022 extend the tendency to broader decision rules (both from training, unread here). If that covers satisficers, bounded thresholds do not by themselves prevent instrumental convergence. Strengthen first: derive the cascade-runaway to resource-acquisition step under named conditions, then test the bounded case against those results. The Working Notes' open questions belong to the same derivation: is a bounded threshold necessary for cascade closure or only sufficient; how do implicit asymptotic thresholds behave; does 'permanent dissatisfaction is slow-onset truth death' compose formally with #def-death-as-factor-loss; and does active inference's preferences-as-priors avoid the problem (the dark-room critique). Their 'canonical source' is AUDIT-WORKING-193847 file 38 §14, now in `.integrated/`.
