# Cor A.1S.1 is selected by the disturbance's support, and the bodies elsewhere say 'genuinely stochastic environment' as if randomness alone decided it. Cor A.1S.1's own wording is right: which of {0,1} obtains is fixed by 'the disturbance model's support structure (bounded vs. unbounded)'. But the hand-off sentences say 'in a genuinely stochastic environment region-exit is a certain eventual event … loss of the parametric certificate is generic' and treat stochastic and unbounded-support as one thing. Sites: #deriv-sector-condition summary paragraph and Discussion 'Kind of guarantee'; #deriv-stochastic-non-exit opening and Discussion; #result-persistence-condition Discussion 'Model S containment is a different kind of guarantee'. A random disturbance with bounded support satisfies Model D, so its region is positively invariant past threshold; certain exit belongs to additive non-degenerate (unbounded) forcing. A second boundary: state-dependent noise that vanishes at the boundary can make exit impossible. The likely repair is 'under additive unbounded noise' in place of 'genuinely stochastic', plus a sentence on bounded random disturbance as Model D. Whether real environments are better modelled one way or the other is the descriptive question the records carry.
