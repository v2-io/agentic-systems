# Reflection: disc-strategic-self-coupling

**1. Predictions vs evidence.**
I predicted the segment would use Schelling's game theory to explain why an agent would deliberately increase its own coupling (tying its own hands) to gain access to credibility-dependent actions. The segment does exactly this, explicitly adopting Schelling (1960), Ainslie (1992), Akerlof & Kranton (2000), and Frank (1988) to cover external, intertemporal, identity, and emotional commitment mechanisms.

**2. Cross-segment consistency.**
It perfectly instantiates the second operation of the M4 meta-pattern (`disc-modularity-state-dynamics`). The structural extensions (M1-M3) provide the formal hooks necessary to attach these classical economic/game-theoretic concepts onto AAT's existing mathematical skeleton without breaking the definitions of $X_t$, $\Sigma_t$, or $\mathcal{A}$.

**3. Math verification.**
The formalization of the "Credibility Paradox" (a commitment's value equals its uncoupling cost) via the strict inequality $c_{\text{couple}} \ll c_{\text{uncouple}}$ (M3) is mathematically elegant. Without this asymmetry, the "enabling edge" (M2) in the strategy DAG provides no actual expansion to the action space $\mathcal{A}(\kappa_t)$ (M1). The acknowledgment that $\mathcal{A}(\kappa_t)$ has an inverted-U shape (too much coupling eventually destroys reality-dependent actions, leading to cult-like delusion) is an excellent example of the framework finding the natural limits of its own operations.
