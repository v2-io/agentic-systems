**3. Math verification.** One genuine soft spot spotted at the framing level, worth carrying as a *candidate finding* until I reach `#deriv-sector-condition` / `#deriv-stochastic-non-exit`:

> "against genuinely stochastic disturbance no correction strength buys that — over an unbounded horizon the system *will* leave any fixed region, with probability one … as an exact dichotomy in the stochastic case"

The exit-with-probability-one result needs **unbounded-support noise** (Gaussian/Brownian forcing), not merely "stochastic." NOTATION.md's GA-2S defines Model S as zero-mean with finite second moment — a *bounded* zero-mean disturbance satisfies GA-2S **and** GA-2 (Model D, with $\rho$ = its bound), and for such disturbance the deterministic containment result applies: no exit, ever. So "Model D vs Model S" as axiomatized in the global assumptions is **not** an exact dichotomy — the two assumption classes overlap, and on the overlap the "stochastic ⇒ certain exit" reading is false. The intro's hedge word is "genuinely stochastic," which may be carrying "unbounded support" informally. To resolve at the appendix: does the actual derivation assume Brownian/Gaussian forcing (then the intro is compressed-but-fair and GA-2S's statement is the imprecise one), or does it claim exit under GA-2S as stated (then it's a real error)? Either way there is likely a *statement-level* imprecision somewhere in the chain — the dichotomy as popularly stated (bounded vs stochastic) doesn't partition, since boundedness and stochasticity are orthogonal properties. Epistemic ladder position: Hypothesis, from first principles; not yet checked against the derivation.

*Second*: the Containment Dichotomy, if it survives my §3 concern, has an under-advertised converse reading. The theorem says stochastic forcing guarantees eventual exit from any fixed region — but "fixed region" is doing the work. An agent that *grows* its region (learns, expands capacity) or *moves* it (structural adaptation) isn't bound by it. So the theorem is really a forcing argument for one of: unbounded growth, periodic re-architecture, or death. That trilemma — grow, transform, or eventually fail, pick one — deserves to be stated somewhere as the corollary a general reader would carry away; it's more portable than the exit result itself. If no segment states it, that's a gap worth suggesting.
