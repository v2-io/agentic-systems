**2. Cross-segment consistency — the Kalman tension [mine; candidate finding, medium-high].** The Formal Expression atomizes *"The agent does not know $T$ exactly"* as a definition, and Epistemic Status calls it "constitutive of the uncertainty setting." But the framework's flagship exact instantiation is the Kalman filter (`#example-kalman`, the cross-domain table, the persistence condition's canonical reading) — and a Kalman filter **knows its transition model exactly** ($A$, $Q$ given); its uncertainty is *state* uncertainty from partial observation plus stochastic realization, not kernel ignorance. Under the natural reading of "does not know $T$" (kernel unknown), the Kalman filter falls outside the constitutive setting this segment declares — the theory's own exact worked example would violate the root definitions. The rescue readings: (i) "know $T$ exactly" means "predict the realized transition exactly," which a stochastic kernel forbids even when known; (ii) opacity is *disjunctive* — unknown kernel OR unobserved state OR stochastic realization, any one sufficing. Either rescue works, but the segment states neither, and the summary sharpens the wrong way: "If $T$ were known, action selection would collapse to plain optimization over a known function" — false for known-$T$ POMDPs, where belief maintenance (the entire mismatch/gain machinery, i.e., filtering) is still required. Adaptation-as-learning and estimation-as-filtering are being conflated in the vacuity argument, and the theory *needs* the filtering case in scope because its exact results live there. This is the same *shape* as my segment-02 concern (vacuity claims proving too much), now with a concrete internal contradiction attached. The gold block's nearest items (learnability assumption; "unknown-but-fixed $T$ requires optimization, not adaptation") circle it but nobody named the Kalman collision. **To verify at `#example-kalman` and `#emp-update-gain` before certifying.**

**14. Wandering thoughts.** *One:* the segment's non-triviality argument ("if $T$ were known, action collapses to planning") implicitly ranks kernel-knowledge as the master variable, but the more interesting boundary for the coming LLM volumes is *whose* $T$ is unknown. An LLM knows the micro-transition of its own context-append with certainty; the opacity is entirely concentrated in the *other agents* inside $\Omega$. So opacity isn't a scalar property of the coupling — it has a *locus*, and the locus migrates as you move up the volumes (physical dynamics → other minds). If Part III's agent-opacity ($H_b$) is the formal home for locus-of-opacity, the root definitions could seed it with one sentence; right now opacity is locus-free.

*Two:* there's a nice unstated duality forming: perception channel = information flows in, lossily; action channel = influence flows out, opaquely. Loss and opacity are almost adjoint — the agent can't fully *read* the world and can't fully *write* it either, and the mismatch signal is the round-trip residual of a read-after-write. That read-after-write framing might be the single most legible way to introduce the mismatch signal to software-native readers, and I haven't seen it in the framing prose yet. Candidate gloss for Ch.3.
