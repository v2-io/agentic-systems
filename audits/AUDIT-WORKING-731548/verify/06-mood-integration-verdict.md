### The "likely a corollary" conjecture is right — leading-order closure sketch (verifier-grade, not landed)

The two-layer claim closes affirmatively under nameable conditions. Setup: fast-loop steady performance with gain $K$ in regime $\theta$ is $\ell(K,\theta)$, minimized at the regime-optimal gain $K^\circ(\theta)$ (this is `#emp-update-gain`'s object). Mood supplies the estimate $m_t$ of $\theta_t$ and the designer sets the modulation by certainty equivalence, $K_0\, g(m) = K^\circ(m)$. Then the downstream objective is $\mathbb{E}[\ell(K^\circ(m_t),\theta_t)]$ and, Taylor-expanding about $K^\circ(\theta_t)$:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[\ell(K^\circ(m),\theta)\big] - \mathbb{E}\big[\ell(K^\circ(\theta),\theta)\big] \;\approx\; \tfrac12\, \mathbb{E}\Big[\ell_{KK}\big(K^\circ(\theta),\theta\big)\, K^{\circ\prime}(\theta)^2\, (m-\theta)^2\Big] \;=\; \mathbb{E}\big[c(\theta)\,(m-\theta)^2\big],$$

a $\theta$-weighted MSE with weight $c(\theta) = \tfrac12 \ell_{KK} K^{\circ\prime 2} \gt 0$. In the segment's own linear-Gaussian model, $(\theta_t, e_t)$ are jointly Gaussian and zero-mean, so all third moments vanish: $\operatorname{Cov}(\theta, e^2) = \mathbb{E}[\theta e^2] = 0$, and the first correction to $\mathbb{E}[c(\theta)e^2] = \mathbb{E}[c(\theta)]\,\mathbb{E}[e^2]$ enters only through $\operatorname{Cov}(\theta,e)^2$ — which the spike already established is the higher-order mean-reversion term, $O(\tau_{\mathrm{env}}^{-1/2})$ relative. Hence the weighted objective is $\bar c \cdot J(\lambda)\,(1+o(1))$ with $\bar c = \mathbb{E}[c(\theta)] \gt 0$ constant in $\lambda$, and the minimizer is the *same* $\lambda^\ast = \sqrt{q/r}$ to leading order. **The estimator-role $\tau^\ast$ governs the modulator role**, conditional on: (i) certainty-equivalent calibration of $g$ to $K^\circ$; (ii) $K^\circ \in C^1$, $\ell$ locally $C^2$ in $K$ with positive curvature; (iii) the fast loop equilibrating between mood movements (the quasi-static premise — the same condition Gap 2 concerns); (iv) *symmetric* local-quadratic downstream loss.

Where it genuinely fails is (iv): if the downstream objective is persistence-margin protection (threshold/asymmetric — too-low gain risks overflow, too-high gain costs only variance), the induced loss on $m$ is asymmetric and $\lambda^\ast$ shifts (toward faster response to adverse surprise). That failure mode is *exactly* the already-flagged asymmetric-loss open item (der-mood-timescale WN; TODO item (iv); the memo's F-asymmetry) — so the conjecture and its known boundary are both already in the tracking surface. This sketch should get an independent re-derivation before landing as the corollary segment/appendix (standing verify-before-landing practice), but it settles the direction: item (ii) is a short positive derivation, not a hole under the headline result.

## Gap 2 — persistence compatibility: REAL-AND-OPEN, with the $\epsilon_{\max}$ half refuted

**The open part is confirmed, and it is genuinely unlogged.** Neither mood segment invokes (MG-1)–(MG-4) or any quantitative slow-variation condition; `#def-mood` line 32 places mood "one rung above the endogenous single-step gain dynamics of `deriv-adaptive-gain-dynamics`" without discharging that segment's composition conditions, and line 46 answers the directed-separation worry with timescale separation *asserted* ("It does not, by **timescale separation**"). I searched TODO.md (the mood-arc items (i)–(v) at line 150), both 2026-06-17 spikes' open remainders, both segments' WNs, the sovereignty-carve memo's Open threads, CHANGELOG 2026-06-17, and `spikes/INDEX.md`: the MG-discharge appears nowhere as a stated open item. Unlike Gap 1, this one is an oversight in the tracking surface, not a deferral. (Mitigating tier note: `#def-mood` is `discussion-grade`/`draft` and is a *definition* — it claims no persistence theorem. The one sentence where assertion outruns tier is the flat "It does not" at line 46; a discussion-grade segment may carry the qualitative argument, but the unhedged declarative is a notch strong for asserted-not-derived.)

**But the challenge's quantitative demand is half-vacuous, by the cited machinery's own statement.** The `#der-multi-timescale-stability` $\epsilon_{\max}$ threshold is $\epsilon_{\max} = \Delta\rho_1^\ast/(L_h v_2^{\max})$, where $L_h$ is the Lipschitz constant of the fast level's quasi-steady-state manifold $h(x_2)$ — how much the slow variable *moves the fast level's target*. Mood modulates the gain, not the target: the mismatch equilibrium is $\delta = 0$ for every $K$ ($F(0;K) = 0$), so $h(m) \equiv 0$, $L_h = 0$, and the theorem itself says "$\epsilon_{\max} = \infty$ when $L_h = 0$: **a fixed target imposes no separation requirement**." There is no "how slow is slow enough" question of the (C1)/moving-target kind for mood's shape — arbitrary mood speed cannot make the fast loop chase a moving target, because mood moves the *drain*, not the water line the drain aims at. The Lyapunov argument for the primary channel ($V = \tfrac12\lVert\delta\rVert^2$, gain-independent) is pointwise in time: $\dot V \le -\alpha_t \lVert\delta\rVert^2 + \rho\lVert\delta\rVert$ holds for time-varying $\alpha_t$ with **no slow-variation hypothesis at all**, provided only $\alpha_t \ge \alpha_{\min} \gt \rho/R$ uniformly.

**Which localizes the real missing condition — and it is one the segments do not state.** The uniform floor is (MG-1) in `#deriv-adaptive-gain-dynamics`'s vocabulary, and via `#der-gain-sector-bridge` ($\alpha_{\mathrm{time}} = \nu\,\eta^\ast c_{\min}$, gain-monotone) it requires the modulation range to stay inside the sector-validity band: `#def-mood` gives $g$ only as "a monotone modulation," unbounded in both directions. Both directions bite: (down) "sustained easy tracking relaxes them" with $g \to 0$ drives $\alpha_t \to 0$ and persistence fails against the next regime shift — mood-induced complacency is a genuine failure mode the current definition permits; (up) in discrete time the contraction needs the step-size ceiling $\eta^\ast \lt 2c_{\min}/c_{\max}^2$ (`#der-gain-sector-bridge` line 141), so unbounded adverse-surprise gain-raising can overshoot. The fix is one clause in the definition: $g : \mathbb{R} \to [g_{\min}, g_{\max}]$, $0 \lt g_{\min}$, with the band chosen inside the sector/step-size validity region.

**The rest of the discharge is near-mechanical, so strengthen-not-soften applies.** Instantiating (MG-1)–(MG-4) for mood: MG-1 = the $g$-range clause above; MG-2 = trivial — the mood channel is a *linear* leaky integrator, sector constant $\alpha_K = \lambda$ exactly; MG-3 = $\lambda \ll \underline\alpha$ — the quantitative content of "quasi-static," directly available from def-mood's $\lambda \ll 1$ premise once $\underline\alpha$ is order-1 (this is the honest answer to "how slow is slow enough": Lyapunov-rate separation, not $\epsilon_{\max}$); MG-4 = the surprise summary $a_t$ has $\delta$-bounded second moment ($\mathbb{E}[a_t^2 \mid \delta] \le \sigma_0^2 + c_a\lVert\delta\rVert^2$) — plausible for any reasonable $a_t$ but needs stating since $a_t$'s functional form is deliberately unpinned. With those four, `#deriv-adaptive-gain-dynamics`'s composed augmented-state persistence result applies as-is and mood lands in sub-scope $\alpha_2$ — arguably the *cleanest* $\alpha_2$ instance in the corpus (linear meta-channel; compare its WN on deterministic-meta-gain special cases, which mood sits just above). The same MG-3 rate gap is what makes the directed-separation Discussion sentence quantitative. Estimated size: a short derivation section or appendix segment; no new mathematics.
