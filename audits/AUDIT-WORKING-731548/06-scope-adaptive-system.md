**2. Cross-segment consistency — the exclusion equivalence fails on the natural reading [mine; strongest finding so far].** The Discussion asserts: *"Closed-form systems ($H(\Omega_t \mid \mathcal C_t) = 0$): When the agent has complete knowledge of the environment, there is no uncertainty to adapt to. There $H(\Omega_t \mid \mathcal C_t) = 0$ — so the predicate as written **excludes it from adaptive scope** — yet it needs the entire model-learning machinery and is the bread-and-butter case of the RL column in the cross-domain table. The rescue is the totality reading: if $\Omega$'s "totality of external state" *includes the environment's law of evolution*, then unknown $T$ ⇒ $H(\Omega_t\mid\mathcal C_t) \gt 0$ and everything works. But (a) no segment states that $T \subseteq \Omega$; (b) `#def-action-transition` writes $\Omega_{t+1} \sim T(\cdot\mid\Omega_t, a_t)$ with $T$ *outside* $\Omega$ notationally, which pulls against the rescue (putting $T$ inside $\Omega_t$ is the Bayes-adaptive hyperstate move, standard but never invoked); (c) the exclusion prose commits to the wrong equivalence explicitly. One sentence fixes it — either adopt the hyperstate reading in `#def-agent-environment`, or restate the exclusion as "state fully determined *and* dynamics known." This is the segment-02 question come due, now with a concrete broken case, in a **claims-verified** segment (it survived Gate 2).
