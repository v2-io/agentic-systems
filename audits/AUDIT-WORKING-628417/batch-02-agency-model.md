## What I hadn't anticipated

**Observable contrast vs Ω-contrast.** Agency is stated on $P(o \mid do(a))$, not $P(\Omega \mid do(a))$ — then carefully refined: pure active-perception (re-aim sensor, leave Ω alone) is *not* the operative contrast for causal learning about the world. Observation-mediated in both directions. The placebo-button problem (proprioception makes every action "contrastive") is real; the segment answers it. I hadn't predicted that precision at the scope layer.

**Causal postulate is pre-Pearl and weaker.** Temporal precedence only — structure of *possible* influence. Pearl hierarchy is later machinery, not the foundation. This avoids loading SEM metaphysics into Part I and keeps zero-coupling systems inside adaptive scope. Elegant restraint.

**"Nominal" collision.** Nominal *agent* (out of agency) vs nominal *coupling* (query-only, in agency) — same word, opposite scope membership. The text itself sometimes says "query-only" later. Pedagogy tax for a fresh reader.

**Completeness of $M_t$ is tautological-relative-to-retention.** Not "enough for the task" — just "everything retained." Sufficiency is separate. Discharging by definition and *naming where the cost went* is a recurring AAT move; I'm starting to feel it as style, not accident.

**β vs ρ as first anti-collapse instance.** Volatility does not force lowering β; it degrades $I(\mathcal{C}; \text{future})$ natively so optimal φ discards old history at constant β. β is internal memory cost. Double-counting trap "obvious once seen." Also: forgetting as joint-distribution necessity even at free memory — not hardware flaw. That lands hard.

**IB policy-relativity already tensions directed separation.** Optimal epistemic compression depends on future actions/policy. Seed of a crack that the orient cascade / continuation convention must manage. Surprising this early.
