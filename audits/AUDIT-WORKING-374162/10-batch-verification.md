## Supplementary Q&As

### Q b10-v.1 [mental-model] — `form-complete-agent-state`
The complete-agent-state formulation's Discussion says "action is the single point where the two substates interact," but its Formal Expression also gives a between-event dynamic $\dot G = g_G(G,M)$ and a general coupled update $f_X$. Is "action is the single point of interaction" strictly true as stated, or does it need a qualifier — and what qualifier does the segment's own audit-gold layer suggest?

### A b10-v.1 [mental-model]
Not strictly true as bare prose: the segment defines a general full-state update $X_{\tau^+} = f_X(X_{\tau^-}, e_\tau)$ and explicitly allows between-event purposeful dynamics $\dot G = g_G(G,M)$ (deliberation, strategy revision, "an epiphany in the shower with no new external evidence") — so $M$ and $G$ can interact internally, outside action, unless directed separation plus extra factorization is imposed. The audit-gold layer flags this as a contested overclaim and suggests narrowing to "action is the *outward* coupling point to the environment" or "under directed separation, policy is the point where both substates jointly determine action." The claim is accurate about the *external/behavioral* channel, not about all internal coupling.

### Q b10-v.2 [math] — `form-complete-agent-state`
State the complete-agent-state formulation's uniqueness conjecture about the $(M_t, G_t)$ decomposition precisely: what does it claim, on what does it hinge structurally, and what is its explicit epistemic status?

### A b10-v.2 [math]
Conjecture (explicitly *not* proved): any alternative decomposition of the complete agent state into epistemic and purposeful components — *if it preserves directed separation* — will be structurally isomorphic to $(M_t, G_t)$, because directed separation forces a component whose update function does not reference purpose. Status: a "plausible structural claim," not derived; alternative decompositions that cross-cut the boundary (e.g. a "relevance-weighted model" mixing $M_t$ and $O_t$) might be practically useful without being isomorphic to the clean split. The $(M_t,G_t)$ factorization is chosen for analytical utility, not proved unique.

### Q b10-v.3 [implications] — `def-agent-spectrum`
Richens, Abel, Bellot & Everitt (2025) and Virgo, Biehl, Baltieri & Capucci (2025) both bear on the "+model" side of the agent spectrum but give structurally different kinds of grounding. State what each proves and how the two readings are "dual."

### A b10-v.3 [implications]
Richens et al. (Thm 1, arXiv:2506.01622): any *bounded* goal-conditioned agent — regret bound $\delta$ on goals up to depth $n$ — admits extraction of a predictive world model from its policy alone, model-error scaling with $\delta$ and $n$; Thm 2 shows the bound is tight at $n=1$ (truly myopic agents need no such model). Consequence: any "model-free" agent competent at multi-step ($n \geq 2$) goal-directed tasks *must* have learned an implicit world model — the +model side is *structurally forced*, not a definitional posture, under bounded-goal-conditioned scope. Virgo et al. (arXiv:2508.06326) instead refine the Conant-Ashby Good Regulator Theorem: a regulating agent may be *interpreted by an observer* as having beliefs whose update is consistent with an environment model — an observer-relative attribution, not an intrinsic forcing. The two are dual: Richens et al. force the model *intrinsically from policy + goal*; Virgo et al. license *observer-relative* attribution. Together they sharpen "+model" from "the agent explicitly maintains $M_t$" to a two-perspective characterization — but the original definitional posture remains the useful AAT-internal working notion.

### Q b10-v.4 [mental-model] — `def-agent-spectrum`
Miller (2022)'s one-state vs. two-state Moore-machine result is used as the "minimal AAT agent" grounding instance. State the empirical finding precisely (what is proved about one-state machines, not just what two-state machines can do), and name the composite claim the corpus flags as worth decomposing before the worked example is built.

### A b10-v.4 [mental-model]
Precise finding: a one-state Moore machine (fixed output regardless of input, cannot condition on observations) is *provably incapable of social behavior in any game-theoretic setting* — no game, payoff structure, or interaction can produce cooperation, coordination, or exchange with one-state machines. A two-state machine crosses a "quantum leap" threshold: it can branch, remember one bit of history, and implement strategies like Tit-For-Tat. The composite claim flagged for decomposition (per the segment's audit-gold Working Notes): (a) minimum AAT agent = two-state machine, (b) social-behavior threshold = two-state, (c) one-state machines categorically cannot exhibit cooperation/coordination/exchange — where (a)+(b) is claimed to imply (c), a logical step worth verifying piece-by-piece once `#worked-example-cam` is built rather than assumed.

### Q b10-v.5 [math] — `der-directed-separation`
The "form axis" from `#disc-partial-coupling-pathways`, cross-referenced in the directed-separation segment's Working Notes, gates *wrappability* differently from the *stage* and *source* axes. State what the form axis distinguishes and which wrapping regime (W₁ or W₂) each side of it admits.

### A b10-v.5 [math]
The (stage × source × form) parameterization decomposes Class 2 (Partial) along three structurally-independent axes. The *form* axis distinguishes **content** coupling (an additive bias on the epistemic output) from **process** coupling (a functional-form change to how the update itself runs). Content-form coupling ↔ W₂ (partial wrapping — parse/patch the output); process-form coupling ↔ W₁ (strict wrapping, requires pipeline access to intercept the mechanism itself, not just its output). The form axis is what "unifies the Class-1-by-structure vs. Class-1-by-behavior refinement at the Class 1 cell with the same distinction read at Class 2" — the same content/process split explains both refinements.

### Q b10-v.6 [implications] — `der-directed-separation`
The bounded-signaling assumption's failure feeds "adversarial-coupling-pressure saturation." Name the two competences the directed-separation segment distinguishes as "class-orthogonal vs. κ-defining," and state why they pull the structural leakage bound $\kappa_{W_1}$ in *opposite* directions as component capability increases.

### A b10-v.6 [implications]
The architectural classification is *blind to* world-simulation fidelity (accuracy of a component's response to the explicit content of a query) and *defined by* input-structure extraction (competence at inferring latent context — including the operator's goal — from a query's surface form). These correlate empirically in pretrained components but are conceptually distinct, and pull $\kappa_{W_1}$ oppositely at high capability: the **accuracy effect** lowers $\kappa$ (more faithful goal-blind posteriors — the component does its stated job better, without inferring more than asked); the **inference effect** raises $\kappa$ (more effective implicit goal-inference from query patterns — the component reads more into the query than its formal input). Named at discussion-grade, kept open as a place to pull on rather than a closed result.
