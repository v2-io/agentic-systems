## §B — Findings Under Burden of Proof

### Finding 1: Prop B.4 Optimal Exploration Rate — Subscript Transposition

**Headline:** The optimal exploration rate formula in `deriv-edge-credence-dynamics` Proposition B.4 has a subscript transposition: the numerator should be $(n_2+1)$ (the experience count on the exploratory arm) but the segment states $(n_1+1)$ (the experience count on the greedy arm).

**Anchor:** `01-aad-core/src/deriv-edge-credence-dynamics.md` §"Proof of (b): ε-greedy recovery." The formula for "Optimal exploration rate" reads: $\varepsilon^\ast = (n_1+1)/(n_1+n_2+2)$ where arm 1 is the greedy choice.

**Problematic passage (verbatim):**
> "Optimal exploration rate." $\varepsilon^\ast = (n_1+1)/(n_1+n_2+2)$

**Counter-evidence search:** I verified the derivation from first principles by solving $\max_\varepsilon \min((1-\varepsilon)/(n_1+1), \varepsilon/(n_2+1))$. Setting the two terms equal: $(1-\varepsilon)(n_2+1) = \varepsilon(n_1+1)$ → $n_2+1 = (n_1+n_2+2)\varepsilon$ → $\varepsilon^\ast = (n_2+1)/(n_1+n_2+2)$. The segment's formula gives the wrong answer; the derivation gives the correct one. Checked for prior known issues in `audits/` files — not found. The equal-experience case ($n_1 = n_2$, $\varepsilon^\ast = 1/2$) is satisfied by both formulas and cannot distinguish them; the error is only visible for unequal experience.

**Numerical verification:**
- $n_1 = 100$ (greedy), $n_2 = 10$ (exploratory)
- Correct formula: $\varepsilon^\ast = 11/112 \approx 0.098$. Check: $(1-0.098)/101 \approx 0.00893$; $0.098/11 \approx 0.00891$ ≈ equal ✓
- Segment's formula: $\varepsilon^\ast = 101/112 \approx 0.902$. Check: $(1-0.902)/101 \approx 0.00097$; $0.902/11 \approx 0.082$ ≠ equal ✗

The segment's verbal description is correct: "For unequal experience, $\varepsilon^\ast$ allocates more trials to the arm with higher $n$ (lower gain) to equalize correction rates." When $n_1 > n_2$: $\varepsilon^\ast = (n_2+1)/(n_1+n_2+2) < 1/2$, so $(1-\varepsilon) > 1/2$ → arm 1 (greedy, higher $n$) gets more trials. The verbal description is right; the formula is wrong.

**Status:** Still real.

**Confidence:** High. The derivation is algebraic, the numerical check is deterministic, and the equal-experience case passes both formulas (concealing the error without catching it).

**Why it still stands:** The error is a subscript transposition, not a conceptual misunderstanding. The verbal description and the derivation both correctly characterize the behavior; only the formula has the transposition.

**Severity:** Medium. The equal-experience result ($\varepsilon^\ast = 1/2$ at $n_1 = n_2$) and the qualitative direction are correct. The error only affects the quantitative formula for the unequal-experience case. Downstream: Props B.6 and B.7 cite B.4's formula structure for optimal exploration within conditional branches; if they inherit the subscript convention without re-deriving, those formulas may have the same transposition.

**Type:** Math error / subscript transposition

**Suggested disposition:** Direct fix — correct the formula in `deriv-edge-credence-dynamics` §"Optimal exploration rate." Also audit Props B.6 and B.7 for any inherited transposition in their conditional-branch exploration-rate formulas.

**Effort:** Trivial in the segment itself. The audit of B.6/B.7 for inherited transposition is editorial (a few lines each).

## §F — Bigger-Picture Observations

**F.1 (Hypothesis): The framework's most practically useful contributions are not its most mathematically novel ones.** The two-condition decomposition of persistence (structural vs. task adequacy), the 2×2 diagnostic (satisfaction gap × control regret), the forgetting prerequisite for strategic persistence, and the adversarial squared law are all practically actionable and clearly derived. They're more valuable to practitioners than the additive-coordinate-forcing meta-pattern (M3) or the identifiability-floor meta-pattern (M1), which are primarily structurally interesting.

Suggestion: The README and OUTLINE preambles could prioritize these diagnostic contributions more prominently. The current framing leads with integration/synthesis; the practical utility of the satisfaction-gap/control-regret orthogonality and the forgetting prerequisite deserves equal billing.

**F.2 (Hypothesis): The Correlation Hierarchy (L0/L1/L1'/L2) is underutilized as a pedagogical tool.** The cascade from "AND/OR gives correct probabilities iff causally sufficient" → "correlated failure is the dominant case" → "L1 augmentation is the practical default" → "L1' for soft facilitators with observable C" → "identifiability obstruction for unobservable C" is one of the framework's most sophisticated practical contributions. The derivation in `def-strategy-dag` is dense; a standalone exposition would increase accessibility.

**F.3 (Hypothesis): The `form-consolidation-dynamics` introduces an important failure mode (the stability-plasticity window's upper bound is open) that practitioners of the framework should know about.** The plasticity lower bound (from the forgetting prerequisite) is derived; the stability upper bound is not. Without both bounds, the "feasibility window" is only half a window. This is honestly labeled as open, but it means any agent designed to "forget at the right rate" is only satisfying one of two structural requirements.

**F.4 (Observation, not hypothesis): The three meta-segments (M1 identifiability floor, M2 separability, M3 additive-coordinate-forcing) are among the most mature Discussion segments in the corpus but I could not read them in this pass due to context constraints.** `disc-separability-pattern`, `disc-identifiability-floor`, `disc-additive-coordinate-forcing` are the cross-sectional synthesizing segments that the OUTLINE preamble describes as reading every segment through. They were not read. A future pass should prioritize these.

## §G — Process Feedback on the Instructions

**G.1: The 5-segment batch cadence worked well for mathematical segments but created tension with the appendix-back-pointer convention.** Reading 5 segments, then immediately jumping to an appendix (when first encountered in `depends:`), then returning to the batch sometimes meant the "batch" was 6 or 7 segments before the next reflection file. The instructions could be clarified: does the appendix count as one of the 5, or is it additional? I treated it as additional, which worked but felt like a minor protocol ambiguity.

**G.2: The triage mode transition (§4.6) needs a clearer signal for the 1M-context case.** The instructions say "switch when you sense your context budget tightening — somewhere around 80% utilization in spirit." At 1M context, the "spirit" of 80% is genuinely hard to estimate — I had read ~95 segments and still felt like I had more capacity than a 200k-context agent would at a comparable fraction. The instructions could acknowledge that at 1M, the triage transition may appropriately come later than it would at 200k.

**G.3: The "five core elements" for findings (§7.6) are excellent.** Having the explicit requirement for (1) problematic passage, (2) counter-evidence search, (3) status determination, (4) confidence, (5) why-it-still-stands when real was the most useful structural element for the audit. It prevented me from reporting a "finding" that was actually a misread on my part (the adversarial exponents were initially flagged as unverified; the explicit counter-evidence-search requirement kept me from reporting them as errors).

**G.4: The "Wandering Thoughts" prompt (14) was consistently the most generative reflection.** The formal checks (prompts 1-9) are important discipline; the wandering thoughts produced insights that I wouldn't have generated if I were just mechanically checking. The explicit requirement to write 3-10 paragraphs of diffuse thinking prevented me from treating the reflection as a form to fill out rather than a space to think in.
