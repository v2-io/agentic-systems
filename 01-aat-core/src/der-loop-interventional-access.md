# Derived: Loop Provides Interventional Data Access

The chapter's central architectural result. By the temporal-ordering postulate ( #post-causal-structure), the agent's action $a_t$ causally precedes the next observation $o_{t+1}$; the agent *chose* the action, the environment responded. The feedback loop therefore generates **intervention-produced data** — data whose causal character differs from passive observation because the agent's action was a genuine cause of the subsequent observation. The mismatch signal conditioned on the agent's action carries interventional information: how the environment responded to *this specific intervention*, relative to what the model predicted. This is how agents within AAT's **agency scope** ($\mathcal S_{\text{agency}}$, which requires $\lvert\mathcal{A}\rvert \geq 2$ and at least one action with causal effect) gain Level-2 access — not through internal architecture but through the loop itself; agents in the adaptive scope but outside agency scope (passive observers) lack the action contrasts needed.

The framework is *unusually careful* to distinguish two claims that look alike. "Action-generated data" is **not** the same as "cleanly identified do-estimates." The action-observation pair is produced *under intervention* (interventional in character) — but between intervention-produced data and a usable estimate of the interventional outcome distribution stand four obstacles: coverage (the agent must have tried diverse actions), within-step confounding, delayed consequences, and partial observability. The strength of causal identification varies by regime — strong in intervention-rich domains (software, laboratory science), moderate with partial intervention (organizational), weak under observation-only constraints (see #scope-ciy-observational-proxy). The claim is about the *character of the data* (interventional, not observational), not about the agent's ability to extract clean causal estimates from it. The result is *exact* as a logical consequence of temporal ordering and the feedback-loop structure; it is about *data availability*, not reasoning capacity — the loop *provides* interventional data whether or not the agent exploits it for Level-2 reasoning.

The interventional character of loop data is a property of the action being the agent's *own* causally-efficacious move, not of any architectural feature and not of the trajectory's forkability. The $do$-operator's semantics is population-level by construction: the interventional distribution sums over the exogenous distribution $P(\mathbf U)$, and its canonical physical realization — randomized controlled trials and A/B experiments — is forkable and repeatable \citep[Def.~5 and fn.~19]{bareinboim-correa-ibeling-icard-2022-pearl-hierarchy}. What the singular-trajectory scope ( #scope-agent-identity) supplies is narrower and still essential: it grounds *whose* effect a loop datum is the effect of, and forbids averaging interventional responses across forked copies as though they were one agent's effects. The *interventional character* is real; *identification* is a further step: action-generated data identifies the interventional distribution at a conditioning set under positivity, sequential ignorability and consistency there. Ignorability is free at the agent's own decision inputs, which are the adjustment set and not the confounder; positivity is what a policy deterministic in what the environment can know lacks there, and without a restriction on the environment class nothing off-policy is identified at a visited decision state; coarsening the set recovers contrast at the price of a sufficiency condition the agent can refute on-policy but cannot confirm under determinism. The exact content of this segment is the *availability of the interventional-character channel*, plus the free-ignorability and deterministic-no-go statements; identification is gated. The Discussion below treats: the precision conditions on "interventional" (low confounding / short delay / varied action); even-without-explicit-causal-model benefits (Q-learning in the tabular case); honest credit to the action-perception-loop framing in active inference and cybernetics (Wiener 1948, Conant–Ashby 1970, Friston et al. 2017, Parr–Pezzulo 2022) together with the three specific moves AAT makes that the implicit treatments do not (the Bareinboim-hierarchy connection, regime-indexed identification strength, explicit scope honesty as the conservative-form posture documented in the Bruineberg et al. 2022 Pearl-vs-Friston critique); the connection to the identifiability-floor pattern as a load-bearing escape with two semantically distinct deployment modes (agent-self-intervention at the causal-structure layer, observer-on-sub-agent at the composition layer); and the singular-trajectory ground for whose effect the loop datum is — distinct from the $do$-semantics, which is forkability-agnostic.

## Formal Expression

*[Derived (loop-interventional-access, from causal-structure + recursive-update)]*

By #post-causal-structure, the temporal ordering is constitutive: $a_t$ causally precedes $o_{t+1}$. The agent chose $a_t$; the environment responded with $o_{t+1}$. The feedback loop therefore generates **intervention-produced data** — data whose causal character differs from passive observation because the agent's action was a genuine cause of the subsequent observation.

The critical distinction: **"action-generated data" is not the same as "cleanly identified do-estimates."** The pair $(a_t, o_{t+1})$ is produced under intervention — the agent executed $a_t$, making it interventional in character rather than a passively observed association. But between intervention-produced data and a usable estimate of $P(o \mid do(a_t), \Omega_t)$ stand: (1) coverage — the agent must have tried diverse actions, not just one policy; (2) confounding within a time step — unobserved state variables that affect both action choice and outcome; (3) delay — consequences may appear much later than $t+1$; (4) partial observability — $o_{t+1}$ reveals only part of the outcome. The strength of causal identification from this data depends on the regime ( #scope-edge-update-causal-validity): strong in Regime A (intervention-rich domains like software and laboratory science), moderate in Regime B (partial intervention), weak in Regime C (observation-only). The claim here is about the *character* of the data (interventional, not observational), not about the agent's ability to extract clean causal estimates from it.

The mismatch signal conditioned on the agent's action:

$$\delta_t \mid a_t = o_{t+1} - \hat{o}_{t+1}(M_t, a_t)$$

carries interventional information: it tells the agent how the environment responded to its specific intervention $a_t$, relative to what the model predicted.

**The exact claim is the availability of the channel; identification is gated.** What is exact, as a logical consequence of temporal ordering and the loop structure, is that an agent in agency scope that executes at least one action with causal effect *generates action-coupled data that is interventional in character* — sampled under the agent's own action rather than passive observation. This is the substrate from which Level-2 quantities $P(o \mid do(a), \cdot)$ can be *identified*, but identification is not automatic. Identification is a statement about a **conditioning set** $Z_t$ — the variables the learner stratifies its action–outcome data on — and from the agent's own data it holds under the three standard conditions of causal inference (Hernán & Robins 2020, ch. 3; the sequential form is Robins 1986[^recall-2026-10-30]). Write $Y_t^{(a)}$ for the potential outcome of action $a$ at step $t$, so that $o_{t+1} = Y_t^{(a_t)}$.

- **Positivity at $Z_t$** — every action under comparison has positive probability inside each $Z_t$-cell, so the interventional distribution there is estimated rather than extrapolated;
- **Sequential ignorability at $Z_t$** — $a_t \perp Y_t^{(a)} \mid Z_t$ for every $a$; graphically, $a_t$ is $d$-separated from $o_{t+1}$ given $Z_t$ in the graph with the edges *out of* $a_t$ removed — the back-door criterion (Pearl 2009, *Causality* §3.3.1);
- **Consistency** — the executed action is the decided one, or the execution map is known, so the outcome observed after deciding $a$ is $Y_t^{(a)}$.

Under these, for every $a$ with $P(a_t = a \mid Z_t) \gt 0$,

$$P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a_t = a),\, Z_t) \;=\; P(o_{t+1} \mid a_t = a,\, Z_t).$$

**Ignorability is free at the policy's own inputs; what it costs there is positivity.** Write the agent's policy with its randomization explicit, $a_t = \pi(X_t, U_t)$, with $X_t = (M_t, G_t)$ the complete state ( #form-complete-agent-state) and the randomization source $U$ independent of the environment's exogenous inputs, reaching the world only through the actions it helps pick. This is a premise — **(P) environment-independent randomization** — and not a consequence of canon's $a_t = \pi(M_t, G_t)$, which is deterministic: a retained pseudo-random state is part of $M_t$ under state completeness ( #der-recursive-update, C3), and an external coin reaches the agent as an event. Under (P), every parent of $a_t$ other than $U_t$ lies in $X_t$, so conditioning on $X_t$ blocks every back-door path from $a_t$ to $o_{t+1}$: ignorability holds at $Z_t = X_t$ when the coins are fresh across steps (given $X_t$ the action is a function of $U_t$ alone, and $U_t$ is independent of everything that produced $X_t$ and of the environment's exogenous inputs — mediated access is not needed for this case), and at the full chronica $\mathcal C_t$ for any environment-independent coin source and environment-independent initial state $X_0$ (here mediated access, #der-recursive-update C2, is what makes every past decision state a function of what the chronica records; a goal set before the trajectory by a principal who knows something about the world opens $a_t \leftarrow X_t \leftarrow G_0 \leftarrow \Omega \rightarrow o_{t+1}$, which $\mathcal C_t$ does not block). With a shared or scheduled source — one seed, a fixed exploration schedule — ignorability at $X_t$ needs $X_t$ to retain the past *coins*, or enough to determine every past decision state; retaining only the past *actions* does not suffice, because a past action is a collider between the coin source and the environment, and conditioning on it opens the path (exact two-step enumeration). The statement is Pearl's adjustment for the direct causes of the treatment (2009, Thm 3.2.2); on the inference side it is Ortega & Braun 2008's remark (§3.2) that the agent's own action "does not carry any information that could be used for inference" beyond its knowledge state; Dawid & Constantinou 2014 make it definitional for a *control strategy* (Def. 5.1), and Bareinboim, Zhang & Lee 2026 state it for policy classes as *experimental* no-unmeasured-confounding (Lemma 1): it holds in the submodel induced by any deployed policy, because the policy's inputs are by construction all the endogenous parents of its action[^recall-2026-10-30]. An agent's internal state, plans and memory are therefore the *adjustment set*, not the confounder.

What is not free at $X_t$ is positivity. A policy whose action is determined by **what the environment can know** — the actions it has received and its own state, so that no component the action varies with is hidden from it — has on-policy data for exactly one action per such cell, and **without a restriction on the environment class nothing off-policy is identified there**: two environments, one of which records the interaction history in $\Omega_t$ (which mediated access permits) and reconstructs the agent's decision from it, agree on every on-policy quantity and differ on $P(o_{t+1} \mid do(a'), X_t = x)$ for every action $a'$ not taken at $x$ (exact enumeration over the sixteen deterministic one-step environments $g(\theta, a)$ on a world bit $\theta$: with the policy $\pi(\theta) = \theta$ the environments fall into four on-policy classes and untaken-action effects differ within a class; this is the Causal Hierarchy Theorem of #def-pearl-causal-hierarchy in miniature, and nothing new). The boundary is determinism *in what the environment can know*, not determinism in $X_t$: canon keeps an agent's randomness inside $M_t$, so a canonical randomizing agent is deterministic in $X_t$, and it is not caught by the no-go. Take a fresh, environment-independent coin $u$ drawn and stored in $X_t$, with $\pi$ deterministic in $X_t$ and varying with $u$. Under canon's coupling the environment receives the agent only through executed actions ( #def-agent-environment), so the coarsening that drops $u$ is outcome-sufficient *unconditionally* — $Y_t^{(a)} \perp X_t \mid \mathcal C_t$, a property of the agent's construction and not an assumption about the environment — and it supplies positivity at $\mathcal C_t$ as long as the coin is fresh, not yet pinned by executed actions; ignorability there then gives $P(Y^{(a')} \mid X_t = x) = P(o \mid a', \mathcal C_t = c(x))$, identifying the untaken action's effect at a visited decision state with no class restriction (in the same enumeration, with the coin stored in $X_1$ and $\pi(\theta, u) = \theta \oplus u$, all sixteen environments have distinct on-policy laws and every untaken effect is identified). A shared or scheduled seed is the boundary case: once past actions reveal it, the current action is determined given the chronica, an unrestricted environment can compute the agent's future coins, positivity fails and the no-go returns — the mirror of the coin-retention clause above. Premise (P) can accordingly be read as "the policy varies with a fresh, environment-independent component, stored in $M_t$ or arriving from a source the outcome-generating environment does not see," and whether the coin sits inside or outside $X_t$ stops mattering. Identification of an untaken action's effect at a visited decision state therefore rests on **positivity at the environment-visible coarsening**: either a fresh private component supplies it by the agent's own construction, or some coarsening $Z_t = \psi(X_t)$ is *outcome-sufficient* — $Y_t^{(a)} \perp X_t \mid Z_t$, the dropped part of the decision state carries no information about what the actions would produce — as a restriction on the environment class, and has positivity inside its cells. A sufficient condition for adjustment at a coarsening that includes both sufficiencies: let $R = r(X_t)$ carry everything the policy reads and $S = s(X_t)$ everything the outcome depends on; then $R \perp S \mid Z_t$ gives $a_t \perp Y_t^{(a)} \mid Z_t$ (weak union and contraction), with outcome-sufficiency ($S$ a function of $Z_t$) and policy-sufficiency ($R$ a function of $Z_t$: the policy read only $Z_t$) as its two special cases. The weakest of the stated forms is the direct condition $Y_t^{(a)} \perp R \mid Z_t$ for each $a$, which $R \perp S \mid Z_t$ implies by contraction and which is the condition #def-value-object states at $Z_t = M_t$; the converse fails when the outcome laws indexed by $S$ are linearly dependent mixtures. The marginal — $Z_t$ trivial, the learner conditioning on nothing — is the limiting case in which every decision input is a confounder, and on-policy $(a_t, o_{t+1})$ converges to $\mathbb{E}[o \mid A = a]$ rather than to the effect. So coarsening re-opens ignorability unless the coarse set is sufficient in this sense, and the policy's own inputs foreclose contrast; the two are one trade-off, and an agent that wants its own data to identify its action effects needs both levers: a retained decision state whose dropped part is outcome-irrelevant, and outcome-irrelevant variation inside its cells. Randomization is the case where the varying input is a fresh coin; a tie-break rule, a round-robin schedule, a CI pipeline that runs regardless of the developer's hunch do the same work under the same condition ( #scope-edge-update-causal-validity, (E3)).

**Two limits of what the agent can know from inside.** First, outcome-sufficiency of a coarsening is **refutable on-policy but not confirmable** under a deterministic policy. The on-policy test, $I(X_t; o_{t+1} \mid Z_t, a_t) = 0$, is implied by the needed interventional condition, $I(X_t; Y_t^{(a)} \mid Z_t) = 0$ for every $a$, so a failure refutes sufficiency under any policy — and the test does fail where sufficiency fails: in canon's own edge-credence learner at $\varepsilon = 0$ with ties broken deterministically, inside a single $M_t$-stratum with $a = 1$ the on-policy $\mathbb E[o \mid X_t, a = 1]$ ranges from $0.19$ to $0.81$ over cells with more than $200$ samples. The converse needs every action to have positive probability at every $X_t$ in the cell: under determinism a pass constrains only $Y^{(\pi(x))}$ at each $x$ and says nothing about untaken actions, and it is vacuous in the cells where $(Z_t, a_t)$ determines $X_t$ — for instance when the policy is injective in the dropped component (Suau, Spaan & Oliehoek 2024 call the gap *policy confounding*[^recall-2026-10-30]). The restriction identification rests on, when it is a class restriction, is therefore an assumption about the environment that the agent's own data can refute but not certify: tabular Q-learning's state $s$ is outcome-sufficient *by stationarity* of the kernel $T(\cdot \mid s, a)$, which is a property of the environment the agent assumes; Kalman + LQR's untaken-action effects are supplied by the known model, not identified from its data. Second, consistency is separate from both. When the executed action $\tilde a_t = \chi(a_t, \Omega_t, W_t)$ can differ from the decided one, adjustment at the decision state identifies the effect of *deciding* $a$ through whatever execution follows — the intention-to-treat effect — and the effect of the executed action needs $\chi$ known and its noise $W_t$ unconfounded with the outcome given $(X_t, a_t)$; a slippery floor that both perturbs the step and causes the fall violates it. For planning with $Q_O$ the intention-to-treat effect is usually the one wanted, since the agent can only ever decide; for attributing *why* an action failed ( #scope-edge-update-causal-validity) the executed-action effect is the one needed.

The channel is still present throughout; what is gated is the move from action-generated data to an identified $do$-estimate.

## Epistemic Status

*Exact.* The exact content is the **availability of the interventional-character channel**: a logical consequence of temporal ordering ( #post-causal-structure) and the feedback-loop structure ( #scope-agency), an agent in agency scope generates action-coupled data that is interventional in character rather than passively observed. The claim is about **data availability**, not reasoning capacity — the loop *provides* this channel whether or not the agent *exploits* it for Level-2 reasoning, and whether or not the data yields identified estimates.

The precision is load-bearing and first-class in the Formal Expression: the agent has *access to* interventional-character data, but *identification* of $P(o \mid do(a), \cdot)$ from it is gated on positivity, sequential ignorability and consistency at a named conditioning set. Three statements there carry their own tiers. That ignorability holds at the policy's own inputs is *exact* under the premise (P) of environment-independent randomization (fresh coins), and at the chronica under mediated access with an environment-independent coin source and initial state, with the coin-retention clause for shared sources; (P) is a premise about the agent, and the clock-seeded "randomizer" in an environment whose regime also depends on the clock is the case where it fails silently. That a policy deterministic in what the environment can know identifies nothing off-policy at its own decision state in an unrestricted environment class is *exact* (the sixteen-environment enumeration), and so is its complement: a fresh environment-independent private component identifies untaken effects with no class restriction. Identification at a coarsening is otherwise *conditional* on that coarsening's sufficiency, which is a restriction on the environment class the agent assumes and can refute but not confirm on-policy under determinism. The reading of consistency's failure as intention-to-treat is exact. Confounding within a single time step (a decision input outside the conditioning set), insufficient action coverage (positivity), delayed outcomes, and partial observability all bear on whether clean causal estimates can be extracted. The gated form is what keeps the *exact* label honest: the availability of the channel is unconditional; the identified $do$-estimate is the conditional consequence, and the segment claims only the former, and the two exact statements above, as exact.

## Claim Record

### The feedback loop makes action-coupled data that is interventional in character

**Register:** formal — exact; a consequence of temporal ordering ( #post-causal-structure) and the loop structure (tag `loop-interventional-access`). It is about the data's character, not about what the agent extracts from it.

**Population:** systems in agency scope ( #scope-agency): at least two actions, at least one pair with distinct interventional outcome distributions routed through $\Omega$.

**True of:** every such system, by the derivation; a tool-using LLM agent is the body's standing instance.

**False of:** passive observers and nominal agents (adaptive scope, outside agency). An active-perception agent whose actions change only what it sees of an unchanged $\Omega$ sits at the boundary ( #scope-agency, "Which contrast counts").

**Falsifier:** none at the level of agents.

**Instantiated:** not applicable.

### An agent's own action–outcome data identifies the effect of its actions at a conditioning set under positivity, ignorability and consistency there; ignorability is free at the policy's own inputs

**Register:** formal — standard identification theory applied to the loop (the conditions by their standard names, Formal Expression). Exact: ignorability at $X_t$ under (P), and at the chronica under mediated access with environment-independent coins and initial state; the intention-to-treat reading of consistency's failure. Conditional: identification at a coarsening $Z_t$, on the sufficiency of $Z_t$ (a restriction on the environment class, unless the dropped component is a fresh private coin, in which case the sufficiency is the agent's own construction). Which real learners' conditioning sets are sufficient is a descriptive question each instance must answer for itself.

**Population:** representations of learners in agency scope whose action is a function of a decision state plus environment-independent randomization, estimating action effects by stratifying their own data on a conditioning set that is a function of that state.

**True of:** tabular $\varepsilon$-greedy Q-learning in a stationary, fully observed MDP (Discussion): the policy reads $(s, \text{Q-table})$, stratifying on $s$ alone is valid because stationarity of $T(\cdot \mid s, a)$ makes $s$ outcome-sufficient, exploration supplies positivity inside each $s$-cell, and the Q-values converge to $Q^\ast$ given that every state is visited infinitely often and the step sizes are Robbins–Monro (Watkins & Dayan 1992[^recall-2026-10-30]). An agent whose policy varies with a fresh coin stored in $M_t$, at the environment-visible coarsening.

**False of:** at a coarsening, any learner whose dropped component is outcome-relevant and read by the policy: canon's own edge-credence learner stratifying on $M_t$ while its policy reads outcome statistics held in $\Sigma_t$ ( #def-value-object; simulated at $\kappa_{\text{processing}} = 0$). The "already tired" agent of #causal-access-intro is not a counter-instance but a premise violation: fatigue is a body state that modulates the *executed* action (consistency), a component of the actual decision state absent from the *recorded* one, or a scope exit from mediated access, and which one tells the agent what to log, randomize or declare.

**Falsifier:** for the exact parts, a derivation error. For the conditional part, a learner stratifying on a coarsening that fails the $R \perp S \mid Z_t$ condition whose estimates are nonetheless unconfounded on-policy, without a further class restriction.

**Instantiated:** not by this project. Logged-propensity off-policy evaluation is the applied practice that lives on this claim (uncited in the body); the constructed instances in the Formal Expression are not instantiations.

### A policy deterministic in what the environment can know identifies nothing off-policy at a visited decision state in an unrestricted environment class

**Register:** formal — exact (the sixteen-environment enumeration, Formal Expression), with its complement exact too: a fresh, environment-independent private component the action varies with restores positivity at the environment-visible coarsening and identifies untaken effects with no class restriction.

**Population:** learners in agency scope identifying from their own data, by whether any component their action varies with is hidden from the outcome-generating environment.

**True of:** Kalman + LQR, whose untaken-action effects are supplied by the known model and not identified from its data ( #def-value-object's by-construction record); any policy that is a function of the chronica alone in an unrestricted class.

**False of:** a policy that varies with a fresh coin stored in $M_t$ (deterministic in $X_t$, not in what the environment can know): every untaken effect identified at the visited decision state. The boundary case is a shared or scheduled seed once past actions have revealed it.

**Falsifier:** a learner in an unrestricted class, with a policy deterministic in the chronica, whose untaken-action estimates at a visited decision state match the interventional truth across environments agreeing on-policy; or a learner varying with a fresh private coin whose untaken effects are not identified at the environment-visible coarsening.

**Instantiated:** not applicable beyond the enumeration.

## Discussion

**The loop as a Level 2 engine.** This is one of AAT's load-bearing results. The causal hierarchy theorem ( #der-causal-hierarchy-requirement) says Level 2 knowledge requires more than correlational data. This result says: the adaptive loop *is* the "more." An agent that acts and observes the consequences is generating interventional data — the same kind of data that a scientist generates through experiments. The loop is a perpetual experiment.

**Two levers, both the agent's.** The identification gate reads, for an agent, as two things it controls. It chooses what it *retains*: a conditioning set whose dropped part is outcome-irrelevant keeps ignorability; the marginal — remembering nothing about why it acted — makes every decision input a confounder. And it chooses how much outcome-irrelevant *variation* it injects inside that set: a fresh coin, a round-robin, a tie-break, a pipeline that runs regardless, each giving the contrast a deterministic policy forecloses. Exploration is the identification-layer form of what #der-causal-insufficiency-detection establishes at the structural-detection layer — a structural prerequisite, not a heuristic. Delays and partial observability lengthen the path from action to attributable outcome without changing this structure; #def-causal-information-yield measures how much structure a given action reveals.

**Even agents without explicit causal models benefit.** A tabular $\varepsilon$-greedy Q-learner maintains no causal model, yet its Q-values converge to $Q^\ast(s, a) = \mathbb{E}[r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q^\ast(s', a') \mid s, do(a)]$ — the interventional quantity, not its on-policy conditional — and the reason is worth stating in the gate's terms, because the illustration is right for a less obvious reason than "its data comes from its own interventions." The policy reads both $s$ and the Q-table, and the Q-table is a statistic of past outcomes; stratifying on $s$ alone drops it. That is valid in a *stationary, fully observed* MDP because the kernel $T(\cdot \mid s, a)$ is fixed, so no statistic of past outcomes carries information about the next one beyond $s$ — the dropped component is outcome-irrelevant by stationarity — and $\varepsilon$-greedy supplies positivity over actions inside each $s$-cell, while every state being visited infinitely often is an ergodicity assumption the convergence theorem also needs. Both hypotheses are doing work: in a partially observed environment, or one with a hidden regime that the agent's outcome statistics track but $s$ does not, the same learner's estimates are confounded by its own strategy state ( #def-value-object), and at $\varepsilon = 0$ nothing off-policy is identified. The loop provides Level 2 data; whether that data yields *identified* quantities depends on what the learner retains, what it varies, and what it is entitled to assume about the environment.

**Honest credit to the action-perception-loop framing.** The substantive observation that the agent's actions cause its observations — and therefore that loop data is interventional in character — is implicit in any framework built around an action-perception loop, including active inference (Friston, FitzGerald, Rigoli, Schwartenbeck & Pezzulo 2017, "Active inference: a process theory," *Neural Computation* 29; Parr & Pezzulo 2022, *Active Inference*, MIT Press, ch. 3) and the broader cybernetic-and-control lineage (Wiener 1948, *Cybernetics*; Conant & Ashby 1970, "Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system," *Int. J. Systems Sci.* 1). AAT's distinctive contribution is not the observation that loop data is interventional but the *explicit lift* of this observation to a load-bearing theorem connected to Pearl's causal hierarchy ( #def-pearl-causal-hierarchy) via Bareinboim, Correa, Ibeling & Icard (2022). Three specific moves AAT makes that the implicit treatments do not:

1. **The Bareinboim-hierarchy connection.** Active inference and the cybernetic lineage rest on Bayesian-network generative models (Pearl Level 1, associational). They do not invoke the causal-hierarchy theorem to argue that the loop's action-generated data is the substrate Level-2 queries require. AAT does — and the consequence is that $\Sigma_t$ is positioned as a *causal* DAG rather than a *Bayesian-network* DAG.
2. **Regime-indexed strength of causal identification.** Even granting that loop data is interventional in character, the strength of usable causal identification varies by domain. AAT partitions this into Regime A (intervention-rich, software/laboratory), B (partial intervention, organizational), C (observation-only — see #scope-edge-update-causal-validity). The AI literature treats causal identifiability uniformly within its modeling assumptions and does not surface the regime distinction at the segment level.
3. **Explicit scope honesty.** The Formal Expression above carefully distinguishes "data generated under intervention" from "cleanly identified do-estimates." The AI literature, as Bruineberg, Dolega, Dewhurst & Baltieri (2022) document in their Pearl-vs-Friston critique, sometimes elides this distinction — using the action-perception loop language to support stronger causal claims than the formal apparatus delivers. AAT's careful split is the conservative form.

The identification half of the segment is older than any of this and is credited where it is used: that an agent's own action is as good as randomized given its decision inputs is Pearl's adjustment for direct causes, the "actions as interventions, observations as conditions" prescription of Ortega & Braun 2008, Dawid & Constantinou 2014's control strategy, and the experimental no-unmeasured-confounding of Bareinboim, Zhang & Lee 2026; the two routes to ignorability at a coarsening (sequential randomization and sequential irrelevance) are Dawid & Constantinou's; the deterministic case of the positivity requirement is in Dawid & Didelez 2010 §5.2.2, and the one well-understood exception — a deterministic policy switching at a threshold of a continuous decision state identifies a *local* contrast at the threshold under continuity (regression discontinuity, Hahn, Todd & Van der Klaauw 2001) — makes precise what "extrapolation" means. Ortega et al. 2021's *delusion* (Remark 3) is the mirror image of the under-conditioning failure here: an agent whose model of its own action mechanism carries a dependence on the environment that its real mechanism lacks over-reads its action as evidence; an agent that conditions on less than its policy read under-conditions the outcome. What AAT adds is the identity of the adjustment set — the agent's own $(M_t, G_t)$, which it holds and can condition on without an external analyst, grounded in whose data it is by #scope-agent-identity — and the consequence, carried to #def-value-object, that canon's own strategy state can be the dropped outcome-relevant component.[^recall-2026-10-30]

The reframing here is rhetorical, not substantive: the headline result (the loop is a Level-2 engine) stays; what changes is the explicit acknowledgment that the observation about loop data being action-generated is shared with the broader literature. **Connection to the identifiability-floor pattern.** This segment is structurally load-bearing for the L0-causal-insufficiency-detection no-go ( #der-causal-insufficiency-detection): without the loop's interventional access, the no-go forbids detection entirely; the covariance test under joint sibling observability is the unique broadly-available violation of the no-go's "purely on-policy" scope. See #disc-identifiability-floor for the meta-pattern.

**Composite-layer extension.** `#disc-identifiability-floor` Instance 3 (the composition-layer no-go anchored in Liberzon 2003's common-Lyapunov nonexistence) establishes that composite contraction cannot in general be certified from component-level data alone — the coupling-topology bit (broadened 2026-05-21 from the original coupling-sign bit alone to include the potential-existence bit and the game-structure-class bit distinguishing R0 / R1 / R2 dynamic regimes per `#disc-dynamic-regime-axis`) is unidentifiable from component marginals. One of the five structural escapes in Instance 3 is composite-extended loop-interventional-access: **interventions on sub-agent $A_j$ reveal $A_i$'s cross-coupling response**, which is a $do(\cdot)$-data distinction between the topologically-distinct coupled constructions. This is the composite-layer analog of the single-agent interventional-access-escape the present segment delivers for Instance 1; in the composite setting, the agent performs interventions on one sub-agent's action space and observes the effect on another sub-agent's mismatch trajectory, extracting the coupling-topology object (sign and structure) that component marginals cannot. The load-bearing role of `#der-loop-interventional-access` therefore extends across two identifiability-floor instances (agent-internal and composite-layer), not one.

**Modes of deployment across `#disc-identifiability-floor` instances.** The shared load-bearing role — Level-2 data from interventional action under `#der-causal-hierarchy-requirement` supplying, for Instances 1, 3 and 4, an escape from an observational-equivalence no-go (for Instance 1 by generating the joint sibling observability the covariance test needs; for Instance 4 as one of three escapes beside instrumentation and off-policy summaries) — manifests through **semantically distinct interventional mechanisms** at different layers. Instance 2, the L1′ mixture under an unobservable common cause, is the exception: its escapes are observational — observing the latent, or joint observation of $K \geq 2$ children under one realization ( #deriv-edge-credence-dynamics, repair routes (i)–(ii)) — and no intervention supplies them. The modes share the Pearl-$do$ structure but differ in who performs the intervention and on what:

- **Mode 1 — agent-self-intervention (Instance 1, causal-structure layer).** The agent performs $do$-actions on its own action space as part of its ordinary adaptive loop. The intervention is *on the agent's own action*; the target is the environment's response, which reveals the latent common cause that Instance 1's on-policy L0-insufficiency-detection no-go (Bareinboim et al. 2022 CHT) makes unidentifiable. The agent-as-Level-2-data-generator property is intrinsic to the loop structure, not a capability that must be added.

- **Mode 2 — observer-on-sub-agent (Instance 3, composition layer).** An observer external to a composite performs $do$-interventions on one sub-agent $A_j$'s action space; the target is another sub-agent $A_i$'s mismatch-trajectory response, which reveals the cross-coupling sign that Instance 3's Liberzon-anchored no-go makes unidentifiable from component-marginal observation. The same Pearl-$do$ structure, but the intervening agent and the measured agent are distinct members of the composite.

- **Mode 3 — observer-on-agent-input (Instance 4, agent-internal architecture layer).** A $do$-perturbation on the agent's input excites similarity-fiber directions that on-policy summary data never excites, breaking the architecture degeneracy (Kalman–Ho canonical-form non-uniqueness) that `#der-architecture-noidentifiability` shows is unidentifiable from on-policy summaries — exact in the linear-Gaussian sub-scope, robust-qualitative in general via the Causal Hierarchy Theorem at the agent-as-SCM layer; landed at #der-architecture-noidentifiability / `#disc-identifiability-floor` Instance 4.

Further instances of `#disc-identifiability-floor` at new layers may add further modes (a fourth — declaring or varying the agent's purposeful substate — is proposed as a sketch at `#hyp-truth-serving-loophole`). The three-mode pattern is a structural regularity worth naming; the load-bearing content — Level-2 escape from observational-equivalence no-goes via loop-interventional access — is shared across the three deployment modes even when the specific interventional quantity varies, and is not the whole escape menu of the floor pattern, since Instance 2 escapes by observation. The unification is at the pattern level; the mechanism is semantically distinct per layer.

**What the singular-trajectory scope grounds — and what it does not.** The interventional *character* of loop data does not rest on the trajectory being non-forkable: the $do$-operator's semantics is population-level by construction (Bareinboim, Correa, Ibeling & Icard 2022, Def 5 — the interventional distribution sums over the exogenous distribution $P(\mathbf U)$), and its canonical physical realizations, randomized controlled trials and A/B experiments, are forkable and repeatable (CHT footnote 19). The $\mathcal L_1/\mathcal L_2$ discriminator is whether the action's generating mechanism was the system's own intact mechanism (observational) or an override / unconfounded-and-known mechanism (interventional) — not whether the trajectory can be replayed. What the scope commitment in #scope-agent-identity supplies is narrower and still essential: each agent is instantiated on a singular, non-forkable causal trajectory $\mathcal C_t$, and this grounds *whose* effect a loop datum is the effect of. Replaying $a_t$ from a checkpointed $M_t$ against a different event stream is an intervention on a *different* trajectory $\mathcal C_t^{(2)}$ that happens to share a prefix; the singularity forbids averaging interventional responses across such forked copies as though they were one agent's effects, because each fork is a distinct trajectory-SCM with its own $P(\mathbf U \mid \mathcal C_t)$. Agents whose ontology is *type-like* (equivalence classes of copies) rather than *token-like* (singular trajectories) are outside AAT's formal scope; aggregate claims about "the model" across copies require additional machinery not provided here. So the singular-trajectory scope grounds the *identity* of the effect (and forbids copy-averaging and lossy cross-trajectory merging), while the *interventional character* itself comes from the action being the agent's own causally-efficacious move, and identification from the gate above.
