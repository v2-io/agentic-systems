# Scope: Adaptive System

This segment names AAT's broadest scope. A system is an **adaptive system** at time $t$ when its world can still show it something it does not yet know, either about *where things are* (the environment's state $\Omega_t$) or about *how things work* (the environment's law $\theta$: the rules by which the state moves and is observed). Part I's machinery (mismatch, gain, tempo, persistence) is the theory of correcting a model against what the world shows, so this is the condition under which there is something to correct toward.

Two familiar systems sit inside for opposite reasons. A Kalman filter tracking a moving signal knows the law exactly and is uncertain about the state. A tabular reinforcement learner reads its state exactly and is uncertain about the law, because it does not know the transition probabilities. Passive Bayesian learners and biological sensory systems are inside as well; none of these needs to *act* on its environment for Part I to apply.

A system sits outside when its world can show it nothing more, and that happens in three ways, which can overlap and combine. The first already has, in its history and its design, everything its world could reveal: a fully observed process whose transition probabilities it was given, deterministic or stochastic. Nothing about that world remains to be learned; what remains is planning under chance. The second has an observation channel that carries nothing about state or law, which includes having no channel at all (formally, a channel with a single symbol). The third does not know something about its world, but nothing it will ever see bears on it: a hidden state drawn afresh at every step, or the rules at places no conduct can reach. Limited computing power never brings a system inside: an agent that has everything the world could reveal but cannot work out the consequences fast enough is outside, and its errors, which are its own, are priced by tempo. Nor does it put a system outside: a slow Kalman filter is still tracking a hidden state. A theorem prover built knowing its rules and its checker, reading its proof state exactly, is outside on the first count, and its errors are computation; it is inside where its declared world holds something its design did not fix and later observations can show, such as the behavior of a library or a collaborator it was not given.

The scope classifies situations, not competent adapters: a system is inside when its world has something left to show it, whether or not it uses what it is shown well. And the classification rests on two declarations, which this segment makes explicit because no observation can make them. The first is *what the world is*: which variables it comprises. Every loop has a re-description that gives every observation sequence the same probability, averaged over the worlds it allows, and is outside, so membership belongs to the world as declared, not to the agent's experience (the housing result below). AAT declares the physical world. Which of the declared variables are state and which are law then follows from the invariance rule below; that cut labels each uncertainty as about where things are or about how things work, but it does not change membership. The second is *which worlds were possible for the agent*: the class of worlds the analysis ranges over, cut down by what the agent knows by design, not by what its designer knows or what it believes. When the world's state, observations and actions are finite, that set of possible worlds alone fixes membership (item (h′)); otherwise a reference measure on it has to be declared as well, and only its null sets matter (item (h)).

## Formal Expression

*[Definition (environment-law; typed-epistemic-target)]*

The environment's **law** is $\theta = (\theta_T, \theta_h)$: the transition kernel $T$ of #def-action-transition and the observation kernel $p_h(\cdot \mid \Omega_t, a_{t-1})$, the conditional law of $o_t$ that $h$ and $\varepsilon_t$ jointly induce ( #def-observation-function). The initial state and the law are random under a reference measure $\pi_0$ on $(\Omega_1, \theta)$, declared on the set of worlds possible for the agent: the declared class of worlds, less those that what the agent holds by design rules out (its knowledge, as opposed to its beliefs). What the agent was built knowing is fixed under $\pi_0$; what the modeler or a designer knows beyond that is not. A Kalman filter declared to be built with its exact model has a point mass on the law; a tabular learner not given its transition probabilities has a non-degenerate measure over them; a false belief is not knowledge, rules out no world, and does not shrink $\pi_0$. By item (h) below, membership depends on $\pi_0$ only through its null sets, which the declaration fixes. When the state, observation and action spaces are finite, the kernel entries carry the Euclidean topology, and $\pi_0$ is declared to charge every open set that meets the set of possible worlds, so its support is that set's closure. A measure that only gives the set probability one can have a smaller support, and would then treat as ruled out worlds the agent's design leaves possible. By item (h′) membership then depends only on that support, so the declared set of possible worlds fixes membership with no further choice of measure. The **typed epistemic target** at time $t$ is

$$S_t = (\Omega_t, \theta).$$

The cut between the components is **invariance**, applied to the declared world's variables. Each declared variable that, under every policy over $\mathcal A$, stays the same along the run almost surely (under the law of the run that $\pi_0$ and that policy induce) is named into $\theta$: it becomes an index of the other variables' kernels, so $\theta$ remains a pair of kernels. Each variable that varies, however slowly, on only some runs, or only under some conduct, is state. The quantifier over conduct is needed because the predicate ranges over every continuation: a lever the agent's own policy never touches, but some other policy would reset, is state, or else the Markov commitment below would fail under the continuations that press it. A drifting regime is therefore slow state, and $\theta$ holds the law of its drift. Given $\theta$, the world is generated by its kernels. Given $(S_t, a_t)$, the next state and the next observation are drawn independently of everything earlier in the run: this is the fresh transition of #def-action-transition (its Markov commitment, stated for the whole run) and the fresh noise of #def-observation-function, each read with the law held fixed. Items (c), (g), (g′), (h) and (h′) and the housing result's compressed state use this property; the non-vacuity statement in the Epistemic Status uses only its observation half, fresh noise. The property does not follow from a world that is Markov in its own past together with noise independent of the chronica. A transition that remembers something outside $\Omega_t$ satisfies both and breaks (g′) and (h) (the example in #def-action-transition); a channel that does breaks the non-vacuity statement as well. When the law is fully known, $\pi_0$ is a point mass in $\theta$ and $S_t$ carries no more uncertainty than $\Omega_t$. Membership does not depend on the cut. Moving an invariant coordinate between $\Omega_t$ and $\theta$ changes no mutual information below, because the future stream depends on the target, under either cut, only through the same variables of the declared world. The cut fixes labels: which uncertainty is about the law and which about the state, and the split of the mismatch floor into a state part and a law part. It does not sort warrants by how they behave in time. Item (g′) gives the property that does, renewal, and the law falls on one side of it by construction.

*[Scope (scope-adaptive-system)]*

Let $F_t = (a_t, o_{t+1}, a_{t+1}, o_{t+2}, \ldots)$ be the loop's future stream after $t$, and let $\pi'$ range over the ways of continuing the agent's actions from $t$ on: any policy over $\mathcal A$ that may depend on the history and on independent randomness, with its actions recorded in the chronica ( #def-chronica). The loop is in the **adaptive scope at time $t$** if it belongs to

$$\mathcal{S}_\text{adaptive}(t) = \left\{(\text{Agent}, \Omega) \;:\; \lvert\mathcal{O}\rvert \geq 2, \;\; \sup_{\pi'} \, I_{\pi'}\big(S_t \,;\, F_t \,\big\vert\, \mathcal C_t\big) \gt 0 \right\}$$

with $\Omega$ read as the declared world: its state process, its law, and $\pi_0$. Two conditions:

1. **The world can still reveal something about state or law**: under some continuation, the future stream carries information about $S_t$ beyond the history.
2. **The perceptual channel can vary**: $\lvert\mathcal{O}\rvert \geq 2$ ( #def-observation-function). Every loop receives an observation at every step, so $\mathcal O$ is never empty; having no channel is, formally, a channel with a single symbol. Condition 1 implies this one rather than adding to it (with a single symbol, the future stream holds only actions and a constant, which carry nothing about $S_t$ beyond the history); it is kept as the visible gate.

The mutual information averages over histories, and this averaged predicate is the scope. On a single run, the **pointwise** predicate conditions on the realized history instead: $\sup_{\pi'} I_{\pi'}(S_t ; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t = c_t) \gt 0$. It is a different predicate. With discrete histories, a loop is in at $t$ exactly when it is pointwise-in at some history of positive probability, so a run can be pointwise-out while the loop is in. With continuous observations the pointwise predicate is defined only up to histories of probability zero. The predicate is dated, so membership is assessed at each $t$, and a result that cites this scope uses it at the times the result is applied.

*[Derived (adaptive-scope-membership), exact]* Each item is a short computation from the definitions above.

- **(a) Residual uncertainty is necessary, not sufficient.** If the conditional law of $S_t$ given $\mathcal C_t$ is a point mass, every mutual information with $S_t$ is zero, so condition 1 fails. Membership therefore requires residual uncertainty about the typed target, written $H(S_t \mid \mathcal C_t) \gt 0$ in the discrete case (for continuous targets, read it as "the conditional law is not a point mass"). Items (b), (e) and (f) show residual uncertainty that does not suffice.
- **(b) Hidden state, known law (a Kalman filter).** Take $x_{t+1} = A x_t + w_t$ and $o_t = C x_t + v_t$ with independent Gaussian noises, $A, C$ and the noise covariances $Q, R$ known, and no coordinate of $x$ constant along the run (for example $Q \succ 0$), so $\theta$ is fixed and $S_t = x_t$ with posterior $\mathcal N(\hat x_t, P_t)$. A coordinate that does stay constant is named into $\theta$ by the cut, and the criterion below is unchanged, because membership does not depend on the cut. Each later observation is $o_{t+j} = C A^j x_t$ plus noise terms independent of $(x_t, \mathcal C_t)$, so the two are jointly Gaussian given $\mathcal C_t$ with cross-covariance $P_t (A^j)^\top C^\top$. Hence $I(x_t; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t) \gt 0$ if and only if $C A^j P_t \neq 0$ for some $j \geq 1$. The filter is in exactly when the uncertain state persists into what is later observed. The random-walk tracker of #example-kalman is in at every $t$ (under sensor choice, membership needs one continuation, and always using the better sensor is Gaussian), and stays in because $P_t$ converges to a positive steady state. With $R \succ 0$ and $N = C Q C^\top + R$, the predicate's one-step part is $I(x_t; o_{t+1} \mid \mathcal C_t) = \tfrac12 \log\det\big(I + C A P_t A^\top C^\top N^{-1}\big)$. When $P_t$ converges to a $P_\infty$ with $C A P_\infty \neq 0$, this part converges to a positive limit, and the predicate's value, which is at least its one-step part, stays bounded away from zero. For the scalar random walk with unit noise variances, the one-step part settles at $0.135$ nats and the whole-future value at $0.162$ nats. A state drawn afresh at every step ($A = 0$) is out: nothing seen later bears on it.
- **(c) Observed state, unknown law (reinforcement learning).** Take $o_t = \Omega_t$, a channel the agent was given. The state is read off the history, so all the information is about $\theta_T$, and membership has an exact test, under any $\pi_0$: *the loop is in at $t$ exactly when some continuation, with positive probability, takes at some time $k \geq t$, at the current state $s = \Omega_k$, an action $a$ whose row $T(\cdot \mid s, a)$ the history $\mathcal C_k$ does not fix* (its posterior is not a point mass). By the chain rule over the stream, with the action terms zero, the value is the sum over $k \geq t$ of the expected information that $o_{k+1}$ carries about $\theta$ given $(\mathcal C_k, a_k)$, each term non-negative at every history. Given the law and the history, $o_{k+1}$ is a fresh draw from the row at $(\Omega_k, a_k)$, so each term is what that draw shows about its row. A draw $X$ from a random law $K$ shows nothing about $K$ exactly when $K$ is almost surely constant: if $I(K; X) = 0$, then $K(B) = P(X \in B \mid K) = P(X \in B)$ almost surely for each $B$ in a countable $\pi$-system (a class closed under finite intersections) that generates the $\sigma$-field, so by Dynkin's $\pi$–$\lambda$ theorem $K$ is almost surely the marginal law of $X$. The same test, with the row replaced by the law of the next observation given the law and the history, holds whenever the history fixes the state at every time; for a state that moves under a known deterministic law, seen through a channel whose law the agent was not given, that law is the channel's kernel at the next state, and the loop is an instance of the passive learner of (e). On a finite state space with the rows under independent Dirichlet laws, no row's posterior is ever a point mass, so the learner is in at every finite $t$ and never leaves. If the rows are put instead on deterministic successor maps, one visit fixes a row: the learner is in exactly while some continuation can, with positive probability, reach a pair it has not visited. Once it has visited every pair it can still reach, it has fixed every part of its law that any continuation could show, and it leaves the scope, having become a planner. That exit is pointwise: it holds at the histories on which every pair the agent can still reach has been visited. Rows at pairs no continuation reaches never enter the test, as (f) says. What decays is the *one-step* information. Under Dirichlet rows, what a draw shows about its row is the entropy of the predictive (the posterior-mean row) minus the expected entropy of the row, and for a row with $K$ possible successors it falls like $(K-1)/(2n)$ after $n$ visits to the pair (the Clarke–Barron rate). The predicate's own value does not decay. Suppose some continuation visits the pair infinitely often with positive probability. Given the row, the draws at successive visits are independent draws from it, so on that event their frequencies converge to the row. The joint law of the row and the stream then gives positive probability to the graph of that limit, which the product of the two marginals, under the continuous prior, gives probability zero. So the information is $+\infty$, at every finite $t$. Under full-support Dirichlet rows every pair qualifies: every transition then has positive probability a.s., so the continuation that always takes $a$ at $s$ returns to $s$ infinitely often. Two things a reinforcement-learning reader brings are covered. Where the world emits a reward, the transition draws it into the observed state, $\Omega_t = (s_t, \text{reward}_t)$, so the row at $(s, a)$ is a joint law of successor and reward, and the test applies to that row. A bandit, with one state and unknown reward laws, is therefore in while some arm's reward law is not fixed by the history. And the Dirichlet is a choice of $\pi_0$, not the learner's prior. A learner not given its transition probabilities has every row possible, and by (h′), with finitely many states, actions and reward values, every $\pi_0$ with that support gives the same membership. A Q-learner, which holds no prior at all, is in. With real-valued rewards or states (h′) does not apply, and the declaration does work that the set of possible worlds cannot. Take a one-armed bandit whose observed state is its latest reward, starting from $\Omega_1 = 0$. If its reward law is drawn from a Dirichlet process whose base law has full support, the posterior after any history is again a Dirichlet process, never a point mass, so the loop is in at every $t$. If instead the finitely supported laws with rational atoms and rational weights are enumerated so that each recurs infinitely often, the $m$-th is shifted by $\sqrt 2 / m$ to give $Q_m$, supported on $\mathbb Q + \sqrt 2 / m$ (these sets are disjoint for distinct $m$), and the reward law is $Q_m$ with probability $2^{-m}$, then the first reward lies in exactly one of these sets and identifies the law: the loop is in at $t = 1$ and out from $t = 2$ on, at every history. Both measures have every law on $\mathbb R$ in their weak support (the Dirichlet process because its finite-dimensional marginals are Dirichlet with positive parameters, Ferguson 1973; the second because the $Q_m$ are weakly dense), and they are mutually singular, which is why (h) lets them disagree. This is the law-side twin of (h′)'s counterexample, whose finiteness hypothesis fails on the state side. By (h), membership is the same for every $\pi_0$ with the declared one's null sets.
- **(d) Observed state, known law.** $S_t$ is determined by $\mathcal C_t$, so condition 1 fails: out, whether the world is deterministic or stochastic.
- **(e) A channel that carries nothing.** If, under every continuation, the future observations are independent of $S_t$ given the history (a constant channel, or one that emits noise whose law is known and independent of the state), condition 1 fails: out. A channel with a single symbol, the formal form of having none, is the extreme case. A noise channel whose law the agent was not given is different: its observations carry information about $\theta_h$, and the loop is in, as a passive learner of its own noise.
- **(f) Unreachable uncertainty.** Uncertainty confined to coordinates of $S_t$ on which no continuation's observations depend, such as the transition law at state–action pairs that no policy can reach, does not count toward membership.
- **(g) Leaving is final where nothing renews.** For the law, $I_{\pi'}(\theta; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t) = I_{\pi'}(\theta; a_t, o_{t+1}, \ldots, o_s \mid \mathcal C_t) + I_{\pi'}(\theta; F_s \mid \mathcal C_s)$ for $s \gt t$ by the chain rule, and because $\theta$ is invariant the last term is the law part at $s$. The agent's conduct from $t$ to $s$ followed by any continuation from $s$ is itself a continuation from $t$, so if no continuation from $t$ reveals anything about $\theta$, none from any later time does, for almost every history reached by $s$. For the whole target the same holds wherever nothing renews after $t$. With $r_k$ the renewal of (g′), the chain rule and data processing give $I_{\pi'}(S_s; F_s \mid \mathcal C_s) \leq I_{\pi'}(S_t; F_s \mid \mathcal C_s) + \sum_{k \geq s} r_k$, and the first term is at most $I_{\pi'}(S_t; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t)$. So a target that is out at $t$, and renews nothing under any continuation, is out at every later time. Without that condition, being out need not be final: a state drawn afresh at every step, carrying a clock and the previous draw, whose previous draw the channel reports from time $K$ on, is out before $K - 1$ and in from $K - 1$, the step whose draw the first report shows.
- **(g′) The value is a stock, refilled only by renewal.** Fix a continuation and, for $k \geq t$, let $u_k = I_{\pi'}(S_k; o_{k+1} \mid \mathcal C_k, a_k)$ be the one-step part at $k$, and let

  $$r_k = I_{\pi'}\big(S_k ; o_{k+1} \,\big\vert\, S_t, \mathcal C_k, a_k\big) = I_{\pi'}\big(\Omega_k ; o_{k+1} \,\big\vert\, \Omega_t, \theta, \mathcal C_k, a_k\big)$$

  be its **renewal** since $t$: what the next observation shows about the target beyond what the target at $t$ and the history fix. Given $(S_k, \mathcal C_k, a_k)$, $o_{k+1}$ is independent of $S_t$ (the world is generated by its kernels: fresh transitions and fresh noise), so expanding $I_{\pi'}(S_t, S_k; o_{k+1} \mid \mathcal C_k, a_k)$ by the chain rule in both orders gives $u_k = I_{\pi'}(S_t; o_{k+1} \mid \mathcal C_k, a_k) + r_k$. The chain rule over the stream gives $I_{\pi'}(S_t; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t) = \sum_{k \geq t} I_{\pi'}(S_t; o_{k+1} \mid \mathcal C_k, a_k)$, the action terms vanishing because actions depend only on the history and independent randomness. Together, in $[0, \infty]$,

  $$I_{\pi'}(S_t; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t) + \sum_{k \geq t} r_k = \sum_{k \geq t} u_k .$$

  Three consequences follow. The law never renews: it sits in both $S_t$ and $S_k$, so renewal is fresh randomness in the state that the next observation sees. Where nothing renews, for instance when every later $S_k$ is a function of $S_t$ and $\mathcal C_k$, the value is the sum of the later one-step parts, so a finite value forces them to zero. And whenever the value is finite, the part of $u_k$ that is not renewal, $I_{\pi'}(S_t; o_{k+1} \mid \mathcal C_k, a_k)$, tends to zero, so a one-step part that stays bounded away from zero while the value stays finite is exactly a renewal that does. In the linear-Gaussian case of (b), $r_k = \tfrac12 \log\det\big(I + C A \tilde P_k A^\top C^\top N^{-1}\big)$, where $\tilde P_k$ is the filter covariance restarted at $t$ from $\tilde P_t = 0$: the uncertainty that process noise has put in since $t$. So $r_k \gt 0$ if and only if $C A \tilde P_k \neq 0$. For the scalar random walk, $r_k$ rises to the steady one-step part, $0.135$ nats, and the differences $u_k - r_k$ sum to the value, $0.162$.
- **(h) Only the prior's null sets matter.** Given $(S_t, \mathcal C_t)$, the future stream is independent of $\Omega_1$ (the world is generated by its kernels, and actions are functions of the history and independent randomness). Replacing $\pi_0$ by a measure with a positive density with respect to $\pi_0$ therefore reweights only the law of $(S_t, \mathcal C_t)$, to an equivalent one, and leaves the conditional law of $F_t$ given $(S_t, \mathcal C_t)$ unchanged. Conditional independence of $S_t$ and $F_t$ given $\mathcal C_t$ is preserved, so membership is the same under any prior with the same null sets as $\pi_0$.
- **(h′) In finite worlds, only the possible worlds matter.** *Hypotheses: $\Omega$, $\mathcal O$ and $\mathcal A$ are finite, and $\theta$, the tuple of kernel entries (with any invariant variables absorbed into the kernels as indices), carries the Euclidean topology.* Under them, membership, averaged or pointwise at a history of positive probability, depends on $\pi_0$ only through its support. Fix a continuation and a block of the next $m$ steps. Two quantities are polynomials in the kernel entries, and so continuous: the probability of a history $c$ jointly with $\Omega_t = s$, given $(\Omega_1, \theta)$; and the law of the block given $(s, \theta, c)$. The predicate fails exactly when, for every continuation, block and history of positive probability, that block law is constant almost everywhere under the posterior given the history. Four facts make each part of this condition a function of $\operatorname{supp} \pi_0$ alone. First, for a continuous nonnegative likelihood $L$, the support of $L \cdot \mu$ is the closure of $\operatorname{supp} \mu \cap \{L \gt 0\}$. Second, a continuous map into a finite-dimensional simplex is constant almost everywhere under a measure exactly when it is constant on that measure's support, because in a second-countable space an open set that misses the support is null. Third, a history has positive probability exactly when the open set $\{L \gt 0\}$ meets $\operatorname{supp} \pi_0$. Fourth, the posterior over $(\Omega_t, \theta)$ is the image of $L \cdot \pi_0$, normalized, under the projection that drops $\Omega_1$; the space is compact, so the projection of the support is closed, and it is the support of the image. The hypotheses are needed. Take a known law and a state $(t, x_t)$ that carries a clock: $x_1 \in [0, 1]$ is hidden, $x_2 = x_1$, and $x_t$ is drawn afresh and uniformly for $t \geq 3$, so no coordinate is invariant and the law is a point mass. Observations are constant except $o_2 = 1\{x_2 \in \mathbb Q\}$. Under the Lebesgue prior on $x_1$, $o_2 = 0$ almost surely, so the loop is out at $t = 1$. Under half Lebesgue plus half a dense atomic measure on the rationals, which has the same support, $o_2$ is a fair coin that depends on $x_1$, so the loop is in.

*[Derived (adaptive-scope-housing), exact]* **Membership is a property of the declared world, not of the interaction law.** Take any loop and re-describe its world. Let the new state $\Omega'_t$ be the pair (Bayes posterior over $S_t$ given $\mathcal C_t$ under $\pi_0$, latest observation), let the new world emit $o_{t+1}$ from the Bayes predictive $p(o_{t+1} \mid \mathcal C_t, a_t)$, and let it update the posterior by Bayes' rule. When the law is known this is the belief-state reduction of a partially observed process (Åström 1965; Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra 1998). When the law is unknown it is the Bayes-adaptive reduction: for tabular reinforcement learning the new state is the current state together with the transition counts (Martin 1967; Duff 2002). Four facts follow:

- the new world is Markov;
- its kernel is one fixed function of state and action, because the Bayes posterior given a history does not depend on the policy that produced it;
- its channel reads the stored observation without noise;
- it gives every action-observation sequence the same probability as the original world does, with $(\Omega_1, \theta)$ drawn from $\pi_0$, under every policy, because the two agree on the one-step predictive given any history.

The agreement is with the original as a mixture over the worlds $\pi_0$ allows. For a law left open under a continuous $\pi_0$, and pairs visited without bound, the twin and the original under any one fixed law are mutually singular: the original's long-run frequencies recover that law, and the twin's are random (de Finetti's representation, read backwards). But $\Omega'_t$ is a function of $\mathcal C_t$ and its law is known, so the re-described loop is a case-(d) loop and condition 1 fails: it is out, while the original may be in. Every loop therefore has an observationally identical twin that is out, and a condition that depends only on the interaction law cannot both include anything and exclude every case-(d) loop. Conditions stated on the declared target, including $H(S_t \mid \mathcal C_t) \gt 0$ and the state-only $H(\Omega_t \mid \mathcal C_t) \gt 0$, can draw the line, but only relative to the declaration. AAT reads the world as the declared physical world ( #def-agent-environment): its physical state and its physical law, each known or open as the agent's design makes it. Under that declaration, both known-law tracking and law learning are in.

## Epistemic Status

*Axiomatic* for the scope itself. The definition of the typed target, the invariance cut, and the predicate are not derived; they are the condition under which the world has something left to show the agent. The rule fixing the worlds $\pi_0$ ranges over by the agent's design-time knowledge is likewise definitional, and so is the generation of the declared world by its kernels ( #def-action-transition, #def-observation-function). The membership consequences (a)–(h′) and the housing result are *exact*: each is a short computation from the definitions, given above, and (h′) holds under its stated finiteness hypotheses, which its counterexample shows cannot simply be dropped. The renewal identity in (g′) is the chain rule taken in two orders together with the generation of the world by its kernels. It holds for the whole target and can fail for a part of it taken alone. Take an invariant $w$, standard normal and seen through unit Gaussian noise, and a state coordinate $z$ with $z_1 = w$ and $z_k = 0$ for $k \geq 2$. Then $I(z_1; F_1 \mid \mathcal C_1) = \infty$, while the one-step parts of $z$ sum to $\tfrac12 \log \tfrac32$. The Kalman criterion in (b) is standard Gaussian conditioning, the decay rate in (c) is the Clarke–Barron asymptotic, the full weak support of the Dirichlet process in (c) is Ferguson's, and the housing result rests on the classical belief-state and Bayes-adaptive reductions. Not claimed: that the declared world, or the set of worlds possible for the agent, can be read off the observations. The housing result shows the first cannot, and the chronica does not record what the agent was built knowing, so both are the modeler's declaration. Once the world is declared, the cut between $\Omega_t$ and $\theta$ is fixed by the invariance rule, and it does not affect membership. What the conditions supply is non-vacuity in one exact sense. If the loop is in at $t$, then under some continuation the state-and-law information terms (ii′) of #deriv-mismatch-budget-attribution at the observations after $t$ sum to at least that continuation's value, which is positive (Discussion, "Why the future stream"). So at some later observation, the state-and-law term of the budget computed under that continuation is positive. The budget the agent runs is computed under its own policy $\pi$. There, the same sum is at least $I_\pi(S_t; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t)$ plus the renewals, so the running budget has a positive state-and-law term after $t$ whenever the agent's own conduct can still reveal something. Membership alone does not give this. An invariant hidden bit that is shown only after an action the agent never takes keeps the loop in at every $t$, while every (ii′) under the agent's policy is zero. Membership does not make the mismatch at a given step about the world: a loop whose only revealing observation comes late is in at every earlier time, while every earlier mismatch is chance or the agent's own error. Nor do the conditions by themselves deliver Part I's results, which carry their own assumptions (the global assumptions in NOTATION.md).

## Discussion

**Two kinds of warrant.** Compare, at each time, the predicate's value with its one-step part: what the next step says about the target as it is now. By item (g′), the value at $t$, plus everything later observations show that is new since $t$, equals the sum of the later one-step parts. So a warrant can rest on two things, a stock and renewal, and one target can have both (a Kalman filter that does not know its noise level draws on a stock about the law and on renewal of the state). Where nothing renews, the value is a *stock* that the one-step parts draw down. A finite stock runs out: its one-step parts go to zero. An infinite stock never runs out, and it can be drawn at a falling rate or at a steady one. The tabular learner of (c) draws at a falling rate: whenever some continuation returns to a pair without bound, each return shows less, like $(K-1)/(2n)$, and the infinite value is the sum of those terms. A learner whose every step enters a part of a static world it has not seen draws at a steady rate: if each step reaches a fresh row of the transition law, whose parameter is independent and uniform, every one-step part is $\log 2 - \tfrac12 \approx 0.193$ nats. Where something renews, the warrant can be *standing* on the renewal, and a finite value with a one-step part bounded away from zero is possible only there. The Kalman random walk is the instance: its value settles at $0.162$ nats, its one-step part at $0.135$, and its renewal rises to meet the one-step part (items (b) and (g′)).

The state/law cut does not sort warrants into these kinds; it puts the law on one side of them. The law never renews, so a law learner's warrant is a stock, finite or not. A state can be either. A hidden state that moves deterministically under a known law, as an orbit does under known gravity with no process noise, renews nothing. For $x_{t+1} = -x_t$, started from a standard normal and seen through unit Gaussian noise, the one-step part falls like $1/(2t)$ while the value is infinite: the law learner's pattern, though the state never stops moving. A state's stock can also be drawn at a steady rate: the doubling map $x_{t+1} = 2 x_t \bmod 1$ from a uniform start, read through the first binary digit of $x_t$, shows at every step a digit of $x_t$ that no earlier observation showed, $\log 2$ nats, with nothing renewed. A state renews when fresh randomness keeps entering it and later observations see it, as in the random walk. By (g), a target that renews nothing stays out once it is out, and so does the law part taken alone. The law part reaches zero only when the history has fixed every part of the law that any continuation could still show; a learner of a fixed world whose state it observes has then become a planner, while one with a hidden state may still be in, as a tracker. A drifting law is slow state, and its warrant stands on renewal only if the drift carries fresh randomness that observations see. A law that drifts as a Gaussian random walk, seen through noise, keeps a standing warrant as the Kalman random walk does; one that follows a known schedule from an unknown starting point renews nothing, and its warrant is a stock.

**The collapse map.** Each kind of knowledge removes one kind of work. A known state leaves nothing to *filter*; a known law leaves nothing to *learn*; both together leave nothing to *adapt* to, which is case (d). The four ways to be behind, and their remedies, follow the same carve:

| Behind on | Example | Remedy | In this scope? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| state that later observations still bear on: where things are | where the lane edge is right now | observe, probe | yes |
| law that some conduct can still show: how things work | how this car brakes on this ice | accumulate experience, intervene | yes |
| chance: what is not yet decided | the next gust | accept it, or change the channel | no; it is what remains when state and law are known, and a hidden state drawn afresh and never seen again is the same case under another name (item (b), $A = 0$) |
| speed: knowing enough but not computing in time | the skid you could compute, but not before the curve | think faster, or act earlier | no; priced by tempo ( #def-adaptive-tempo, #der-deliberation-cost) |

**Full knowledge of the environment, as a boundary.** "Full knowledge of the environment" correctly excludes a world with nothing left to learn, but full knowledge means knowledge of the state *and* the law. Reading the state exactly is not enough: a learner that sees its state perfectly and does not know the transition probabilities faces live prediction error, which is precisely the setting of tabular reinforcement learning. Item (c) puts it inside. Item (d) keeps the true core of the exclusion, a known world fully observed, which is planning under chance rather than adaptation.

**Why the future stream, not residual entropy.** Residual uncertainty about $S_t$ is necessary (item (a)) but counts uncertainty the world can never show. It counts a channel that carries nothing (e), law at unreachable pairs (f), and a state drawn afresh each step and seen through noise (b with $A = 0$). What separates the two predicates exactly is this. Take a coordinate of the target on which, given the rest of the target and the history, no continuation's future depends. Adding or removing it leaves the stream predicate unchanged, because by the chain rule its contribution is zero; it can flip the entropy predicate. The fresh state is such a coordinate, and re-describing it as channel noise removes it. Neither predicate survives every re-description: the housing result flips both. The mutual information is also well defined for continuous states and kernel-valued laws, where a differential entropy would depend on units or need a reference measure. The quantity is the retrospective, whole-future counterpart of the state-and-law information term (ii′) in the information-form mismatch decomposition of #deriv-mismatch-budget-attribution. Term (ii′) for $o_{t+1}$ measures what that observation says about the target it is emitted from, $S_{t+1}$; the predicate's one-step part measures what it says about the target now, $S_t$, and by data processing is at most the first, so the predicate's one-step part is positive only where (ii′) is. Summed, the same comparison says more. Along any continuation, fresh noise makes $o_{k+1}$ independent of $(S_t, S_k)$ given $(S_{k+1}, \mathcal C_k, a_k)$, so by data processing (ii′) at $o_{k+1}$ is at least $I_{\pi'}(S_t, S_k; o_{k+1} \mid \mathcal C_k, a_k)$, which by the chain rule is $I_{\pi'}(S_t; o_{k+1} \mid \mathcal C_k, a_k) + r_k$, with $r_k$ the renewal of (g′). The first terms sum over $k \geq t$ to the value $I_{\pi'}(S_t; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t)$, by the chain rule over the stream with the action terms zero. So the (ii′) terms after $t$, computed under that continuation, sum to at least its value plus the renewals. Fresh transitions are not needed for this. A loop in the scope therefore has, under some continuation, a positive state-and-law term at some later observation. Not necessarily at the next one: with an invariant hidden bit that only the observation at some step $K$ reports, the loop is in at every $t \lt K$, and (ii′) is zero before $K$. The converse fails when what $o_{t+1}$ shows about $S_{t+1}$ was drawn afresh: with $A = 0$, (ii′) is positive and the predicate is zero, so the mismatch decomposition books a positive floor for a loop outside the scope. That floor's label is the declaration-relative one (see #result-mismatch-decomposition's Epistemic Status).

**Whose uncertainty.** The predicate is computed by the modeler, over the true $S_t$, but under $\pi_0$, the law of the world given what the agent knows by design. Two jobs that "the modeler's viewpoint" would run together are thereby separated: whose probability (the modeler's), and whose knowledge (the agent's). An agent that is certain it has its world figured out, and is wrong, is classified as if it held no such belief: a false belief is not knowledge and rules out no world. Whether it is in on the law therefore rests on the declared class of worlds, which has to say what else was possible. A benchmark whose transition law its designer knows stays in scope for the learner that was not given it, and a planner handed its true law, with its state observed, is out. In finite worlds, by (h′), $\pi_0$ matters only through which worlds it allows. In general, by (h), it matters through its null sets, which a set of possible worlds does not fix. The chronica does not record what the agent was built knowing, so this rule is part of the declaration, not something the history settles. The predicate is also evaluated over every continuation, not only the agent's own conduct. An agent whose own policy reveals nothing it does not already know, while some other policy would, is in scope; that is a fact about the agent's conduct, not about the world (the absorbing regions of #hyp-solicitable-escape are the case where such conduct locks in).

**The declared world.** The housing result says that whether an agent is tracking a hidden world, learning an unknown law, or watching a known random process cannot be settled from the probabilities of the agent's data, averaged over the worlds it allows. Nothing in AAT is lost by saying so: the result is the same in kind as the observation that fated and genuine randomness cannot be told apart from inside ( #disc-law-discovery-ceiling), and #def-agent-environment already treats the agent–environment decomposition as a modeling choice. What the declaration buys is that Part I's persistence race, keeping up with a world that moves, is a statement about the physical state the modeler names. The magnitude of the mismatch an agent experiences does not depend on the declaration; its attribution to a moving world, as against irreducible noise, does.

**Narrowing to agency.** Adding causal action unlocks the interventional and purposeful results of Parts II and III. The agency scope ( #scope-agency) is the intersection of $\mathcal{S}_\text{adaptive}$ with the condition that actions carry Pearl-level-2 contrast: distinct actions produce distinct interventional outcome distributions. Adaptive-scope systems that remain outside agency are *passive observers* (no choice) or *nominal agents* (choices with no causal effect). For both, Part I's machinery applies but the causal-information and purposeful-agent results do not.
