# Scope: Adaptive System

This segment names AAT's broadest scope. A system is an **adaptive system** at time $t$ when its world can still show it something it does not yet know, either about *where things are* (the environment's state $\Omega_t$) or about *how things work* (the environment's law $\theta$: the rules by which the state moves and is observed). Part I's machinery (mismatch, gain, tempo, persistence) is the theory of correcting a model against what the world shows, so this is the condition under which there is something to correct toward.

Two familiar systems sit inside for opposite reasons. A Kalman filter tracking a moving signal knows the law exactly and is uncertain about the state. A tabular reinforcement learner reads its state exactly and is uncertain about the law, because it does not know the transition probabilities. Passive Bayesian learners and biological sensory systems are inside as well; none of these needs to *act* on its environment for Part I to apply.

Three kinds of system sit outside. The first is a system that knows both the state and the law, such as a fully observed process with known transition probabilities, deterministic or stochastic. Nothing about that world remains to be learned; what remains is planning under chance. The second is a system whose observation channel carries nothing about state or law, which includes having no channel at all. The third, excluded by design, is an agent that knows everything the world will show but cannot compute its consequences fast enough; its errors are its own, and they are priced by tempo rather than by this scope.

One fact about the boundary must be said at the outset. Whether a system is inside is a property of the world as the modeler *declares* it, not of anything the agent's experience can settle: a known-law tracker and a re-description of the same loop in which the "state" is the observer's own posterior produce identical observations, and only the first is inside. AAT declares the physical world. The derivation is below.

## Formal Expression

*[Definition (environment-law; typed-epistemic-target)]*

The environment's **law** is $\theta = (\theta_T, \theta_h)$: the transition kernel $T$ of #def-action-transition and the observation kernel $p_h(\cdot \mid \Omega_t, a_{t-1})$, the conditional law of $o_t$ that $h$ and $\varepsilon_t$ jointly induce ( #def-observation-function). Whatever part of the law the analysis does not fix is random under a reference measure $\pi_0$ on $(\Omega_1, \theta)$ that the modeler supplies; $\pi_0$ names the class of worlds the analysis ranges over. The **typed epistemic target** at time $t$ is

$$S_t = (\Omega_t, \theta).$$

The cut between the components is **invariance**. Whatever about the world stays the same along the whole run is named into $\theta$, and whatever varies, however slowly, is state. A drifting regime is therefore slow state, and $\theta$ holds the law of its drift. Given $\theta$, $\Omega_t$ is sufficient for its own evolution (the Markov commitment of #def-action-transition, read with the law held fixed). When the law is fully known, $\pi_0$ is a point mass in $\theta$ and $S_t$ carries no more uncertainty than $\Omega_t$.

*[Scope (scope-adaptive-system)]*

Let $F_t = (a_t, o_{t+1}, a_{t+1}, o_{t+2}, \ldots)$ be the loop's future stream after $t$, and let $\pi'$ range over the ways of continuing the agent's actions from $t$ on: any policy over $\mathcal A$ that may depend on the history and on independent randomness, with its actions recorded in the chronica ( #def-chronica). The loop is in the **adaptive scope at time $t$** if

$$\mathcal{S}_\text{adaptive}(t) = \left\{(\text{Agent}, \Omega) \;:\; \mathcal{O} \neq \emptyset, \;\; \sup_{\pi'} \, I_{\pi'}\big(S_t \,;\, F_t \,\big\vert\, \mathcal C_t\big) \gt 0 \right\}$$

with $\Omega$ read as the declared world: its state process, its law, and $\pi_0$. Two conditions:

1. **A perceptual channel exists**: $\mathcal{O} \neq \emptyset$ ( #def-observation-function).
2. **The world can still reveal something about state or law**: under some continuation, the future stream carries information about $S_t$ beyond the history.

The mutual information averages over histories; on a single run, condition on the realized history $\mathcal C_t = c_t$ instead. The predicate is dated, so membership is assessed at each $t$, and a result that cites this scope uses it at the times the result is applied.

*[Derived (adaptive-scope-membership), exact]* Each item is a short computation from the definitions above.

- **(a) Residual uncertainty is necessary, not sufficient.** If the conditional law of $S_t$ given $\mathcal C_t$ is a point mass, every mutual information with $S_t$ is zero, so condition 2 fails. Membership therefore requires residual uncertainty about the typed target, written $H(S_t \mid \mathcal C_t) \gt 0$ in the discrete case (for continuous targets, read it as "the conditional law is not a point mass"). Items (b), (e) and (f) show residual uncertainty that does not suffice.
- **(b) Hidden state, known law (a Kalman filter).** Take $x_{t+1} = A x_t + w_t$ and $o_t = C x_t + v_t$ with $A, C$ and the noise covariances known, so $\theta$ is fixed and $S_t = x_t$ with posterior $\mathcal N(\hat x_t, P_t)$. Each later observation is $o_{t+j} = C A^j x_t$ plus terms independent of $x_t$ given the history, so the two are jointly Gaussian given $\mathcal C_t$ with cross-covariance $P_t (A^j)^\top C^\top$. Hence $I(x_t; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t) \gt 0$ if and only if $C A^j P_t \neq 0$ for some $j \geq 1$. The filter is in exactly when the uncertain state persists into what is later observed. The random-walk tracker of #example-kalman is in at every $t$, and stays in because $P_t$ converges to a positive steady state. A state drawn afresh at every step ($A = 0$) is out: nothing seen later bears on it.
- **(c) Observed state, unknown law (tabular reinforcement learning).** Take $o_t = \Omega_t$ on a finite state space, with the rows $T(\cdot \mid s, a)$ unknown under independent Dirichlet priors. The state is read off the history, so all the information is about $\theta_T$. At the current state $s$, taking action $a$ makes the next observation a draw from $T(\cdot \mid s, a)$, whose posterior is not a point mass after finitely many visits; when the row has at least two possible successors, that draw carries positive information about the row. So the learner is in at every finite $t$. Its information decays as the posterior concentrates on the pairs it visits. If the rows are deterministic and the agent identifies all reachable ones exactly, it leaves the scope and has become a planner.
- **(d) Observed state, known law.** $S_t$ is determined by $\mathcal C_t$, so condition 2 fails: out, whether the world is deterministic or stochastic.
- **(e) A channel that carries nothing.** If, under every continuation, the future observations are independent of $S_t$ given the history (a constant channel, or one that emits pure noise), condition 2 fails: out. The empty channel of condition 1 is the extreme case.
- **(f) Unreachable uncertainty.** Uncertainty confined to coordinates of $S_t$ on which no continuation's observations depend, such as the transition law at state–action pairs that no policy can reach, does not count toward membership.
- **(g) Leaving the law part is final.** Because $\theta$ is invariant, $I_{\pi'}(\theta; F_t \mid \mathcal C_t) = I_{\pi'}(\theta; a_t, o_{t+1}, \ldots, o_s \mid \mathcal C_t) + I_{\pi'}(\theta; F_s \mid \mathcal C_s)$ for $s \gt t$ by the chain rule. The agent's conduct from $t$ to $s$ followed by any continuation from $s$ is itself a continuation from $t$, so if no continuation from $t$ reveals anything about $\theta$, none from any later time does. The state part has no such property: a state that keeps moving keeps renewing what can be revealed about it.
- **(h) Only the possible worlds matter.** Given $(S_t, \mathcal C_t)$, the future stream is independent of $\Omega_1$ (the Markov commitment, with actions functions of the history and independent randomness). Replacing $\pi_0$ by a measure with a positive density on $(\Omega_1, \theta)$ therefore reweights only the law of $(S_t, \mathcal C_t)$ and leaves the conditional law of $F_t$ given $(S_t, \mathcal C_t)$ unchanged. Conditional independence of $S_t$ and $F_t$ given $\mathcal C_t$ is preserved, so membership is the same under any prior with the same null sets as $\pi_0$.

*[Derived (adaptive-scope-housing), exact]* **Membership is a property of the declared world, not of the interaction law.** Take any loop whose law is known, and re-describe its world. Let the new state $\Omega'_t$ be the pair (Bayes posterior over the physical state given $\mathcal C_t$, latest observation), let the new world emit $o_{t+1}$ from the Bayes predictive $p(o_{t+1} \mid \mathcal C_t, a_t)$, and let it update the posterior by Bayes' rule. This is the belief-state reduction of a partially observed process (Åström 1965; Kaelbling, Littman & Cassandra 1998). Four facts follow:

- the new world is Markov;
- its kernel is one fixed function of state and action, because the Bayes posterior given a history does not depend on the policy that produced it;
- its channel reads the stored observation without noise;
- it gives every action-observation sequence the same probability as the original world does, under every policy, because the two agree on the one-step predictive given any history.

But $\Omega'_t$ is a function of $\mathcal C_t$ and its law is known, so the re-described loop is a case-(d) loop and condition 2 fails: it is out while the original tracker may be in. A condition that depends only on the interaction law must give the two the same verdict, so it cannot both include known-law tracking and exclude every case-(d) loop. The same holds for any condition stated on the state, including $H(S_t \mid \mathcal C_t) \gt 0$ and the state-only predicate it replaces. AAT reads $\Omega$ as the declared physical world ( #def-agent-environment), under which known-law tracking is in.

## Epistemic Status

*Axiomatic* for the scope itself. The definition of the typed target, the invariance cut, and the predicate are not derived; they are the condition under which the world has something left to show the agent. The membership consequences (a)–(h) and the housing result are *exact*: each is a short computation from the definitions, given in full above. The Kalman criterion in (b) is standard Gaussian conditioning, and the housing result rests on the classical belief-state reduction. Not claimed: that the cut between $\Omega_t$ and $\theta$, or the choice of $\Omega$, can be read off the observations. The housing result shows it cannot, and the declaration is the modeler's. What the conditions supply is non-vacuity: they make mismatch, gain, tempo and persistence statements about adapting to a world rather than about chance or the agent's own errors. They do not by themselves deliver Part I's results, which carry their own assumptions (the global assumptions in NOTATION.md).

## Discussion

**Two kinds of warrant.** A system can be in scope because the state is unknown, or because the law is unknown, and the two behave differently in time. Uncertainty about a moving state is renewed every step, so a Kalman filter's membership is *standing*: it never ends while the signal moves. Uncertainty about an invariant law is spent as it is learned, so a learner's membership is *transient*. By (g), once nothing about the law can be revealed, nothing ever can be again, and the learner of a fixed world eventually becomes a planner. A drifting law is slow state, which is why the realistic case, where the rules themselves shift, keeps a standing warrant.

**The collapse map.** Each kind of knowledge removes one kind of work. A known state leaves nothing to *filter*; a known law leaves nothing to *learn*; both together leave nothing to *adapt* to, which is case (d). The four ways to be behind, and their remedies, follow the same carve:

| Behind on | Example | Remedy | In this scope? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| state: where things are | where the lane edge is right now | observe, probe | yes |
| law: how things work | how this car brakes on this ice | accumulate experience, intervene | yes |
| chance: what is not yet decided | the next gust | accept it, or change the channel | no; it is what remains when state and law are known |
| speed: knowing enough but not computing in time | the skid you could compute, but not before the curve | think faster, or act earlier | no; priced by tempo ( #def-adaptive-tempo, #der-deliberation-cost) |

**What the old exclusion got right and wrong.** "Full knowledge of the environment" correctly excludes a world with nothing left to learn, but full knowledge means knowledge of the state *and* the law. Reading the state exactly is not enough: a learner that sees its state perfectly and does not know the transition probabilities faces live prediction error, which is precisely the setting of tabular reinforcement learning. Item (c) puts it inside. Item (d) keeps the true core of the exclusion, a known world fully observed, which is planning under chance rather than adaptation.

**Why the future stream, not residual entropy.** Residual uncertainty about $S_t$ is necessary (item (a)) but counts uncertainty the world can never show. It counts a channel that carries nothing (e), law at unreachable pairs (f), and a state drawn afresh each step and seen through noise (b with $A = 0$). The last case is indistinguishable from a world with no state and more channel noise: re-describing it that way changes no observable and flips the entropy verdict, while the future-stream verdict is "out" both ways. The mutual information is also well defined for continuous states and kernel-valued laws, where a differential entropy would depend on units or need a reference measure. The quantity is a relative of the state-and-law information term (ii′) in the information-form mismatch decomposition of #deriv-mismatch-budget-attribution: that term measures what the target tells about the next observation; this one measures what the whole future can tell about the target, dated at $t$.

**Whose uncertainty.** The predicate is evaluated from the modeler's viewpoint, over the true $S_t$ under $\pi_0$, not from the agent's beliefs. An agent that is certain it has its world figured out, and is wrong, is still in scope, and is about to be surprised. By (h), $\pi_0$ matters only through which worlds it allows, so "whose prior" becomes "which class of worlds is the agent being analyzed over". This keeps a designer-known benchmark in scope for the agent that does not know it. The predicate is also evaluated over every continuation, not only the agent's own conduct. An agent whose own policy reveals nothing it does not already know, while some other policy would, is in scope; that is a fact about the agent's conduct, not about the world (the absorbing regions of #hyp-solicitable-escape are the case where such conduct locks in).

**The declared world.** The housing result says that whether an agent is tracking a hidden world or watching a known random process cannot be settled from the agent's data. Nothing in AAT is lost by saying so: the result is the same in kind as the observation that fated and genuine randomness cannot be told apart from inside ( #disc-law-discovery-ceiling), and #def-agent-environment already treats the agent–environment decomposition as a modeling choice. What the declaration buys is that Part I's persistence race, keeping up with a world that moves, is a statement about the physical state the modeler names. The magnitude of the mismatch an agent experiences does not depend on the declaration; its attribution to a moving world, as against irreducible noise, does.

**Narrowing to agency.** Adding causal action unlocks the interventional and purposeful results of Parts II and III. The agency scope ( #scope-agency) is the intersection of $\mathcal{S}_\text{adaptive}$ with the condition that actions carry Pearl-level-2 contrast: distinct actions produce distinct interventional outcome distributions. Adaptive-scope systems that remain outside agency are *passive observers* (no choice) or *nominal agents* (choices with no causal effect). For both, Part I's machinery applies but the causal-information and purposeful-agent results do not.
