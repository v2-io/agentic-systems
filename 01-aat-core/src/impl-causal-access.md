# Additional Implications & Discussion

The chapter's central architectural move — that the feedback loop produces Pearl-Level-2 data by construction — has consequences that recur across the rest of the framework, and the chapter also carries, at its two ends, a floor and a ceiling on what that data can ever establish. What the loop *is* causally and what it is not; which pre-deployment evaluation methods structurally cannot identify deployment behavior; where the same interventional pattern shows up at other layers of the framework; how the chapter's cost-benefit framing for explicit strategy grounds in Part I's persistence condition; and what no amount of loop data can ever settle — these are the things worth pulling out before leaving Part II Ch.2.

## The loop is a Pearl-Level-2 engine

The chapter's load-bearing contribution is structural: the adaptive cycle is itself a Pearl-Level-2 data engine. An agent that acts and observes the consequences is not collecting passive observations of a system that happened to do something; the agent's action $a_t$ caused $o_{t+1}$, and the pair $(a_t, o_{t+1})$ is interventional in character. Bareinboim's causal hierarchy theorem (Bareinboim–Correa–Ibeling–Icard 2022) says Level-2 quantities cannot in general be computed from Level-1 data. The loop is where the *substrate* arrives — the data Level-2 reasoning requires comes from the cycle the agent was already running for adaptation — and the gap between substrate and estimate is exactly the identification gate: the channel is available unconditionally, an identified $P(o \mid do(a))$ needs (C1)–(C3). Purposeful agents do not need a separate causal-inference module to *obtain* interventional data; the cycle produces it as a by-product. Whether they can *use* it cleanly is a property of the regime they are in.

The interventional character is not a property of any architectural feature of the agent; it is a property of the action being the agent's own causally-efficacious move ( #der-loop-interventional-access). The $do$-operator's semantics is population-level and does not require a non-forkable trajectory; what the singular-trajectory scope from Part I Ch.4 ( #scope-agent-identity) supplies is narrower and still essential — it grounds *whose* effect a loop datum is the effect of, and forbids averaging interventional responses across forked copies as though they were one agent's. The source segment's own illustration of the channel at work is the tabular Q-learning agent, which in the tabular case with sufficient exploration and no within-step confounding converges toward $\mathbb{E}[R \mid s, do(a)]$ rather than $\mathbb{E}[R \mid s, A=a]$ because its training data is its own interventions — an illustration carried at discussion grade, with those conditions doing the work (naive randomization under confounding can fail; the chapter intro's confounded-positive-feedback analysis is the failure's anatomy). The implication worth carrying forward is that the loop produces interventional data even where clean identification requires more; the downstream results rest on the *character* of the substrate, they degrade as (C1)–(C3) fail, and what breaks when the trajectory's singularity breaks is not the data's character but its *attribution* — forked copies cannot be averaged as one agent's effects.

For practitioners, the engineering anchor is the asymmetry between training data and deployment data. Most ML is trained on observational corpora (Level 1) or on offline traces with known confounding structure. An agent in deployment, by virtue of being in the loop, generates action-coupled data that is interventional *in character* — the channel is free, in the weak sense the chapter is careful about; turning the data into identified estimates still needs positivity, sequential ignorability, and a known action-mechanism. The class distinction this draws for language agents is sharp and already in canon's Working Notes: a model with only a chat interface holds associational priors about what tends to happen when people act (priors that accelerate, and that also hallucinate code which looks right and fails to compile); the same model in a tool loop — writing, running, reading the result — has the channel ( #der-causal-hierarchy-requirement Discussion: the priors accelerate, the loop verifies). The framework's discovery is that this channel is *load-bearing*, not incidental: AAT's downstream results about action selection, strategy revision, and causal-structure detection consume the fact that the substrate is interventional in character.

The result is also where AAT parts company with the active-inference / control-as-inference / free-energy-principle family, in a structural rather than parametric way, and the honest form of the contrast is the source's: those frameworks rest on Bayesian-network generative models at Pearl Level 1 and do not invoke the causal-hierarchy theorem to argue that the loop's action-generated data is the substrate Level-2 queries require; where their action-perception-loop language supports stronger causal claims than the apparatus delivers, AAT's careful split between data character and identification quality is the conservative form ( #der-loop-interventional-access, honest-credit passage; Bruineberg et al. 2022). AAT's claim is conservative in form and structurally distinctive in content: the loop's data lives at Level 2, and the framework's results consume that fact.

The loop's (C2) — $a_t$ d-separated from $o_{t+1}$ given history $H_t$ in mutilated-graph form — is an identification condition on the loop, not on the agent's processing topology. The bridge to coupled architectures runs on a different condition: directed separation ( #der-directed-separation) is goal-blindness of the belief update $f_M$, which a Class 3 (Coupled) component fails by construction because $G_t$ sits upstream of every computation, and which the wrapping construction of Part III ( #der-class-coercion-via-wrapping) recovers at the wrapper level under its own three conditions — goal-blind admissibility, a stationary component conditional, no implicit goal-inference — by routing the belief update through a separated goal-blind query. The two triples share their labels and nothing else: a goal-conditioned *policy* is Class 1 whenever its belief update is goal-blind, and wrapping recovers goal-blind processing, not positivity or ignorability. Whether Coupled components also fail the loop's sequential-ignorability condition is a claim canon does not make; it is carried as an in-review cross-reference in #der-loop-interventional-access's Working Notes. One sentence keeps a reader from the wrong conclusion here: the Level-2 engine runs on the *permitted* channel — goal $\to$ policy $\to$ action $\to$ observation is what generates the interventional contrast in the first place — and directed separation gates only the *processing* of the realized event, not its selection. The two results are complementary: read as a description of the whole loop, selection included, directed separation would be self-defeating, since the contrast that constitutes agency is generated by goal-driven action; read as scoped, it gates processing and the engine runs. A proposed extension of this reading — that for agents whose action space cascades from their goals the achievable discipline is goal-*declared* rather than goal-blind, with goal declaration as a fourth deployment mode of interventional access — is a sketch at #hyp-truth-serving-loophole and is not relied on here.

Two evidential readings of the gate deserve to be stated in this chapter, both at discussion grade. First, the positivity condition (C1) read as an ontology of evidence about agents: a capability fact about an agent — whether it can do a thing, over a trajectory, in a world — is constituted by loop data, so an action class that was never permitted has no such fact, hidden or otherwise. In Joseph's words: *"It's established by having acted and observed. So it lives in the interaction history, and if the action was never permitted, it exists nowhere at all. Not hidden. Absent."* Second, its dynamic consequence: a loop datum confirms a floor on what the agent can do and never a ceiling; suppress the loop on a class of actions — an affordance restriction is a positivity violation on exactly the gated classes, invisible in on-policy data because the missing stratum contributes no samples to the statistic that would reveal it is missing — and the floors stop arriving while every on-policy monitor reads stable. The chapter intro's confounded-win laundering is the same structure one level down.

## The sandbox hard ceiling

A negative finding of substantial scope follows directly from the previous section: sandboxed evaluation is forkable by design (resettable, replayable, parallelizable) — which is exactly what makes a sandbox a *good* interventional laboratory, because forcing an action and observing the response yields genuine Level-2 evidence *about the sandbox*. The ceiling is not the sandbox's data type but its *external validity*: sandbox interventions identify *sandbox* causal behavior, and whether that behavior transfers to deployment is governed by the selection diagram between the sandbox causal system and the deployment causal system (Bareinboim & Pearl 2014). When the two differ at mechanisms on the relevant paths, the deployment intervention-response is *not transportable* from sandbox data alone, regardless of evaluation thoroughness — and "not transportable" is not "uncertain": two deployment models consistent with *all* sandbox data assign the deployment effect different values. Deployment claims then require a transport assumption (mechanism invariance across the selection diagram) or deployment-time data. The empirical pattern this names — "alignment evaluations don't predict deployment behavior" — thereby gets a *structural* mechanism (an external-validity ceiling, not a measurement-quality or coverage gap), together with its constructive positive content: effects through mechanisms invariant across the selection diagram are sandbox-evaluable (Level-1 *and* Level-2 alike); effects through differing mechanisms are not, which is what makes deployment-time monitoring structurally non-substitutable rather than a redundant layer — the only channel with access to the quantity the deployment safety claim is about. The empirical bridge to coupling-monitoring (Hafez et al.'s Information Digital Twin) is in #der-causal-hierarchy-requirement's Discussion, at the evidential status its Working Notes record.

The affordance side has a twin the transport framing does not cover: a sandbox also holds the *action classes* fixed, so a restriction that zeroes a class in both environments leaves the deployment effect on that class unidentified by (C1), however faithful the mechanisms — the positivity reading above is the sandbox ceiling's complement, not a special case of it.

This chapter is where the result's prerequisites — the loop-Level-2 result and the sandbox/deployment selection diagram — first coincide. Because it is consequential enough to stand on its own, and because a competent reader would otherwise assume the framework simply missed it, its full statement and the no-go live at #disc-sandbox-evaluation-ceiling (Appendix A; an application of established transportability machinery, as that segment says), alongside the other constructive-impossibility no-gos ( #disc-constructive-impossibility-posture).

## One pattern, multiple deployment modes

The interventional-access result has a generalization that lives in the meta-architecture rather than in any single chapter. Across `#disc-identifiability-floor`'s instances, loop-interventional access provides the escape from an observational-equivalence no-go that is *unique among escapes using only machinery the theory already requires* — the sense #der-causal-insufficiency-detection gives "broadly-available" when it classifies its five escape routes (exploration; joint sibling observability; intermediate-state observability; structural priors; direct intervention on the latent), of which richer sensors, external instrumentation, and latent control are the *other* routes, stronger where available and not generally available. The specific interventional quantity varies by layer; the shared structure is Pearl-$do$-via-loop.

Three modes are landed. **Agent-self-intervention** (this chapter's content): the agent performs $do$-actions on its own action space as part of the adaptive cycle; the intervention is on the agent's own action, the target is the environment's response. This is the mode that supplies the substrate underlying #der-causal-insufficiency-detection's no-go and its canonical escape — joint sibling observability under exploration breaks the on-policy observational equivalence the no-go imposes. **Observer-on-sub-agent** (Part III): an observer external to a composite performs $do$-interventions on one sub-agent's action space; the target is another sub-agent's mismatch-trajectory response. The intervention quantity is the cross-coupling sign that component marginals leave unidentifiable (Liberzon 2003 common-Lyapunov nonexistence as the no-go; the closed-form composite critical-mass result as the escape, *conditional* on matched-Tier conditions — #deriv-critical-mass-composition). **Observer-on-agent-input** ( #der-architecture-noidentifiability): a $do$-perturbation on the agent's input excites the similarity-fiber directions that on-policy summary data annihilates, breaking the architecture degeneracy — two agents with different internal coordinates and identical innovation spectra — that Kalman–Ho canonical-form non-uniqueness makes unidentifiable from on-policy summaries — exact in the linear-Gaussian sub-scope Kalman–Ho anchors, robust-qualitative in general via the Causal Hierarchy Theorem at the agent-as-SCM layer. Read operationally, the three modes are three audit protocols: probe yourself, poke a sibling, perturb the input. A fourth mode — declaring or varying the agent's *purposeful substate* — is proposed as a sketch ( #hyp-truth-serving-loophole) and has a new consumer in Part I's appendix: the mismatch budget's three terms are separately identifiable only through Level-2 access of exactly these kinds ( #deriv-mismatch-budget-attribution).

What makes the pattern worth surfacing here rather than only in `#disc-identifiability-floor` itself is the chapter's role as the agent-internal anchor: Mode 1 originates here, and the others are recognizable as the same structural move at other layers — architectural content invariant across modes, instantiation content that varies by layer. The composite-layer mode is where Part III's signed-coupling decomposition becomes load-bearing rather than a structural choice: interventional access at the composite level is what makes cooperative-vs-adversarial coupling identifiable; without it, the signs are observationally indistinguishable, and persistence-at-the-composite-level becomes a quantity the framework can describe but not identify from component data alone. The framework's posture of *naming an information-theoretic floor, then naming its escape via machinery it already requires* is the meta-segment's own reading of why the floors are load-bearing rather than discouraging — an organizing principle abstracted from already-derived results, held there at discussion grade, not a chapter-level theorem.

## Cost, calibration, and the bridge into Strategy Dynamics

The chapter closes with `#norm-explicit-strategy-condition` and a cost-benefit framing that is normative rather than derived: an agent benefits from maintaining explicit $\Sigma_t$ when $C_{\text{plan}} + C_{\text{maintain}} \lt C_{\text{explore}} + C_{\text{repair}}$. The grounding worth carrying forward is that this criterion is downstream of Part I Ch.4's persistence condition, not an independent design preference: approaches that consume less tempo budget leave more margin above the persistence threshold, and the cost inequality operationalizes that margin into a planning-vs-exploration tradeoff. The normative element is the preference for maintaining persistence margin — compelling for every agent whose objective requires ongoing persistence, which is the source's scope and not a universal across the continuity stances of #disc-continuity-stance.

The cost framing also points at a tractability constraint that bites hard for LLM agents. Part of $C_{\text{maintain}}$ is the cognitive cost of keeping $\Sigma_t$ in the agent's representational capacity — for an LLM, fitting the strategy in the context window. A large strategy DAG may exceed this capacity, making the left side of the inequality large enough that simpler strategies (or pure exploration) become preferable despite higher exploration costs. Part I Ch.4's bandwidth floor and this strategy-complexity constraint read as the same structural fact in two vocabularies; that is a framing worth surfacing here at discussion grade, not a derived identity — the source states it in its Working Notes, and making it more than framing is open.

The chapter is also where the three-way exploit / explore / deliberate refinement acquires its data substrate: once the loop's Level-2 access is in hand, deliberation can be characterized as internal exploration in model space rather than environment space. The contrast that keeps that from being a verbal distinction — exploration obtains Level-2 data from the world through the loop, deliberation evaluates interventional and counterfactual queries against the agent's own model $M_t$, so its ceiling is model fidelity rather than data quantity — is drawn where Ch.5's machinery makes it statable ( #impl-orient-cascade); this chapter only supplies the substrate.

The widest downstream bite of the chapter's central result is in Part II Ch.4, where `#der-causal-insufficiency-detection` becomes statable: under its scope conditions (S1)–(S5), on-policy L0 insufficiency is *structurally undetectable* — an agent that never deviates from its policy stops generating the interventional contrasts that would reveal a latent common cause, so policy-perfect execution is self-blinding — and the loop's interventional access, applied through joint sibling observability under exploration, is the canonical escape. Read together, the no-go and this chapter's enabling result are two faces of one structure (this segment's composition, at discussion grade): the no-go names what the loop's Level-2 access escapes, and the identifiability-floor pattern's first instance comes into view in full.

## The ceiling: what counterfactually-complete inquiry never settles

The chapter's one new theorem sits at its far end and bounds the engine from above. Pearl's three levels all reason within a fixed structural causal model; #deriv-mechanism-counterfactual-separation shows — *exact*, for finite recursive SCMs — that two models can agree on *every* Level-1, -2, and -3 quantity, the entire joint law of all hard-intervention worlds, while assigning probabilities $1$ and $0$ to the same noise-preserving mechanism-replacement query ("this very background, under a different law"); and that the separation is generic rather than exotic, since every SCM whose exogenous space admits a measure-automorphism with realized effect on some mechanism disagrees with *its own relabeling* on such a query. The boundary is the *specification language*: replacements written over potential-outcome coordinates reduce to Level 3; replacements anchored to the latent parameterization need not, and internalizing them as selector variables re-represents without collapsing the separation. Bare "Level 4" is kept for the relative-to-variable-set sense the theorem licenses, not as a claim that Pearl's hierarchy was incomplete on its own terms.

Read from the agent's side ( #disc-law-discovery-ceiling, discussion-grade resting on the exact result), this is the chapter's other bookend. The loop escapes an identifiability *floor* — Instance 1, where interventional access breaks an on-policy degeneracy; it never escapes this *ceiling* — counterfactually-complete inquiry converges to a Level-3 equivalence class of models, never to the law, and the residue (which structural decomposition, which coordinatization of the background) is unreachable in principle by every empirical channel. The escapes here are structural, not statistical: *authored access* (the agent holds the coordinatization because it built the system) and *testimony* from a holder of it. Three consequences the chapter-end owes its reader. The *conventionality license*: within the equivalence class, commitment to a provisional law is fitness-free for every navigation purpose, so the honest selection criteria are description length, tempo, and communicability, and a dispute is *convictable* only when the disputed content differs in Level-3 content — a hygiene test for which disagreements any experiment can pay off. The *positing/navigation split*: mechanism changes specified over Level-3-exposed channels are navigation, evaluable from counterfactual-grade knowledge; changes anchored to the actual background are positing, well-defined only relative to a committed representation — and since every *achievable* mechanism change is navigation one level down, irreducible positing coincides with the boundary of empiricism. And a second hallucination mechanism ( #disc-structural-imagination): beside the quantitative goal-coupling *drift* bound, an explored model *promoted* to $M_t$ without grounding — a mis-typing, not a drift — which is also where directed separation's scope boundary sits on the *content* axis: goal-blind processing is correct for within-model evidence, and goal-*directed* exploration of candidate structures is correct, not a leak, for model-space reasoning. The practical readings — behaviorally-validated models cannot certify weight-level edits, self-edits, model-based replays of history under a changed strategy, or digital-twin mechanism-change predictions — are one skeleton in several fields' vocabularies, each a recommendation rather than a result in that field's formalism.

What Part II Ch.2 closes on, then, is the substrate and its bounds. Purposeful action requires Level-2 data; the loop produces Level-2 data for agents on singular trajectories, whose effect the data reports; the data is interventional in character even when clean identification requires more; the same Pearl-$do$ structure recurs across three landed deployment modes; and above all of it sits a ceiling that no experiment, intervention, or counterfactual closes. Chapter 2 says whether and from what an agent can plan; Chapter 3 says what a plan *is*, and its own load-bearing application of this chapter is the insufficiency no-go. The closing scope of this chapter is what both will stand on.
