# Derivation: Convention Monotonicity — the Receding-Horizon Rung

The convention hierarchy ( #def-value-object) orders three continuation conventions by their best-achievable value: $A_O^{(1)} \leq A_O^{\text{RH}} \leq A_O^{\text{B}}$, for fixed model $M_t$, fixed policy class $\Pi$, fixed evaluated horizon $N_h$. This appendix carries the derivation of the two rungs, which differ in character. The **right rung** $A_O^{\text{RH}} \leq A_O^{\text{B}}$ is exact under three named hypotheses, read with $A_O^{\text{B}}$ as the best first action under the $\Pi$-optimum as continuation: (M) the receding-horizon policy, stitched state by state from the window optima, is itself a member of $\Pi$; and either (PC) $\Pi$ contains, with any member, the policy that takes an arbitrary first action and then follows that member, or (SO) the $\Pi$-optimal continuation is optimal over $\Pi$ from every state a first action can reach. A *rectangular* class — every policy whose action at each state lies in a state-wise admissible set, the class of all policies among them — has all three. A restricted plan library generally has none, and each hypothesis is needed: on the instance $(x, H, D)$ a two-policy class with (M) but neither closure property has $A_O^{\text{RH}} = 100$ against $A_O^{\text{B}} = 10$, because the best first action under the receding-horizon continuation composes into a policy the class does not contain; a prefix-closed two-policy class with (SO) has $A_O^{\text{RH}} = 15$ against $A_O^{\text{B}} = 6$, because the stitched replanner is not a member. The **left rung** $A_O^{(1)} \leq A_O^{\text{RH}}$ is *false in general*: an unguarded receding-horizon replanner over a window $N_r \lt N_h$ optimizes a *truncated* objective whose preference order can invert relative to the full evaluated objective, and it can then underperform the frozen current policy. The left rung holds **exactly** when the replanning objective is an *order-consistent surrogate* for the full-horizon objective; three structurally-checkable conditions each force this. The negative half is the standard receding-horizon / model-predictive-control fact that a short-horizon controller without a terminal cost or terminal constraint is not guaranteed to be monotone (or stabilizing); the positive half is rollout / one-step policy improvement and the control-Lyapunov terminal-cost construction.

## Formal Expression

Fix $M_t$, policy class $\Pi$, and evaluated horizon $N_h$. Recall the static-evaluation forms ( #def-value-object): $A_O^{(1)}$ scores the best first action under continuation $\pi_{\text{current}}$; $A_O^{\text{RH}}$ scores it under the receding-horizon continuation $\pi_{\text{RH}}$ with replanning window $N_r$; $A_O^{\text{B}}$ scores it under the Bellman-optimal continuation $\pi^\ast$, a maximizer of $V_O(M_t, \cdot; N_h)$ over $\Pi$. Stage values accrue over the evaluated horizon and $V_O$ is their expectation. Throughout, $Q_O(M_t, a; \pi, N_h) = \mathbb{E}\big[r(a) + V_O(s', \pi; N_h - 1)\big]$ with $s'$ the state the first action $a$ reaches, and for a history-dependent $\pi$ the state $s'$ is read as the history through $a$.

### The right rung holds when the replanner's policy is a member of a closed class

*[Derived (convention-monotonicity-right-rung, Conditional on (M) membership of the stitched replanner in $\Pi$, and (PC) prefix-closure of $\Pi$ or (SO) state-wise optimality of $\pi^\ast$; on the first-action reading of $A_O^{\text{B}}$)]*

$$A_O^{\text{RH}}(M_t;\, \Pi, N_r, N_h) \;\leq\; A_O^{\text{B}}(M_t;\, \Pi, N_h).$$

Write $A_O^{\text{RH}} = \sup_a Q_O(M_t, a; \pi_{\text{RH}}, N_h)$ and $A_O^{\text{B}} = \sup_a Q_O(M_t, a; \pi^\ast, N_h)$ ( #def-value-object). Two readings of $A_O^{\text{B}}$ are in use: this **first-action reading**, and the **class-supremum reading** $V_O(M_t, \pi^\ast; N_h) = \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O(M_t, \pi; N_h)$. The first is never below the second, for every $\Pi$: $\pi^\ast$'s own first action is an admissible $a$, and $Q_O(\pi^\ast(x); \pi^\ast) = V_O(\pi^\ast)$. The quantities the rung compares are action values under a fixed continuation, not the values of the continuations themselves, and $Q_O(M_t, a; \pi, N_h) \neq V_O(M_t, \pi; N_h)$ in general: the first scores "do $a$, then follow $\pi$", the second scores $\pi$ from the start. Two things are therefore needed and neither is given by C2's construction. The receding-horizon policy is stitched, state by state, from the first actions of different window-optimal members of $\Pi$ ( #def-value-object, C2), so it is a composite of members and for a restricted class need not be one. And even granting membership, "$\pi_{\text{RH}} \in \Pi$, so $V_O(M_t, \pi_{\text{RH}}; N_h) \leq \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O$" bounds the wrong quantity: it says nothing about $Q_O(M_t, a; \pi_{\text{RH}}, N_h)$ for a first action $a$ that $\pi_{\text{RH}}$ would not itself take.

**(M) Membership.** The stitched policy $\pi_{\text{RH}}$ is a member of $\Pi$. With it, $V_O(M_t, \pi_{\text{RH}}; N_h) \leq \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O$, and each of the following two hypotheses carries that bound from the policy's own value to its action values.

**(PC) Prefix-closure.** For every admissible first action $a$ and every $\pi \in \Pi$, the policy $a \oplus \pi$ ("do $a$ now, then follow $\pi$") is in $\Pi$. Then for every $a$, $Q_O(M_t, a; \pi_{\text{RH}}, N_h) = V_O(M_t, a \oplus \pi_{\text{RH}}; N_h) \leq \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O(M_t, \pi; N_h) = V_O(M_t, \pi^\ast; N_h) \leq A_O^{\text{B}}$, and the supremum over $a$ preserves it. This uses nothing about the form of $V_{O_t}$, gives the rung on both readings, and makes the two readings coincide: the same step applied to $\pi^\ast$ gives $\sup_a Q_O(M_t, a; \pi^\ast, N_h) \leq \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O$.

**(SO) State-wise optimality.** $\pi^\ast$ attains $\sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O(s, \pi; N_h - 1)$ at every state $s$ that an admissible first action can reach. Then for every $a$, by (M), $Q_O(M_t, a; \pi_{\text{RH}}, N_h) = \mathbb{E}\big[r(a) + V_O(s', \pi_{\text{RH}}; N_h - 1)\big] \leq \mathbb{E}\big[r(a) + V_O(s', \pi^\ast; N_h - 1)\big] = Q_O(M_t, a; \pi^\ast, N_h)$, and the supremum over $a$ gives the rung on the first-action reading. This uses the stage-value form of $V_{O_t}$. (SO) does **not** give the class-supremum reading: it constrains $\pi^\ast$ at the successor states only and says nothing about whether $\pi^\ast$'s own first action is the best first action with $\pi^\ast$ as continuation, so $\sup_a Q_O(M_t, a; \pi^\ast, N_h)$ can strictly exceed $V_O(M_t, \pi^\ast; N_h)$ whenever the better composite $a \oplus \pi^\ast$ lies outside $\Pi$ — the case (PC) excludes and (SO) does not (third instance below: $51$ against $12$). The two readings coincide under (SO) when also **(G)** $\pi^\ast$ is greedy at the decision point, $\pi^\ast(x) \in \arg\sup_a Q_O(M_t, a; \pi^\ast, N_h)$; then $\sup_a Q_O(M_t, a; \pi^\ast, N_h) = Q_O(M_t, \pi^\ast(x); \pi^\ast, N_h) = V_O(M_t, \pi^\ast; N_h)$.

*Which maximizer.* When $\Pi$ has several maximizers of $V_O$ from $x$, they can differ at states their own first action does not reach, and the first-action reading of $A_O^{\text{B}}$ then depends on which one serves as $\pi^\ast$. Under (PC) it does not: every maximizer gives $\sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O$. Under (SO) it does not either: every maximizer satisfying (SO) gives $\sup_a \mathbb{E}\big[r(a) + \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O(s', \pi; N_h - 1)\big]$. Without both the reading is a property of the chosen maximizer, and the rung fails anyway.

**Rectangular classes have all of it.** Call $\Pi$ *rectangular* when it is the set of all history-dependent policies, or of all time-indexed Markov policies, with $\pi(s) \in A_\Pi(s)$ for given state-wise admissible action sets $A_\Pi(s)$, and the first-action supremum ranges over $A_\Pi(x)$. Then (M): each window argmax over $\Pi$ picks an action from $A_\Pi(s)$, so the stitched policy has an admissible action at every state and is a member. (PC): $a \oplus \pi$ has admissible actions throughout for $a \in A_\Pi(x)$ and $\pi \in \Pi$. (SO) and (G): the backward-induction optimum of the restricted decision process is optimal from every state and greedy at $x$; an arbitrary maximizer of $V_O$ from $x$ need not be, since it is unconstrained at states its own first action does not reach, so (SO) names the backward-induction maximizer. The rung then holds on both readings for every maximizer: with $V^\ast$ the restricted optimal value, $A_O^{\text{RH}} \leq \sup_{a \in A_\Pi(x)} \mathbb{E}[r(a) + V^\ast(s')] = V^\ast(x) = \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O \leq A_O^{\text{B}}$, the first inequality by (M), the equality by Bellman's equation on the restricted process. The class of all history-dependent policies and the class of all time-indexed Markov policies over a finite horizon are rectangular with $A_\Pi(s)$ the whole action set. A class of *stationary* Markov policies with admissible sets is not prefix-closed in general, since $a \oplus \pi$ is time-dependent; and a DAG-representable plan library ( #def-satisfaction-gap) is generally not rectangular, which is why for the AAT case the rung stays a hypothesis-bearing statement.

No condition on $N_r$ is needed. None of the three hypotheses can be dropped: see the right-rung counterexamples below. $\square$

### The left rung is conditional

*[Derived (Conditional on order-consistency of the replanning objective)]*

$$A_O^{(1)}(M_t;\, \Pi, N_h) \;\leq\; A_O^{\text{RH}}(M_t;\, \Pi, N_r, N_h)$$

holds exactly when the receding-horizon replanning objective is an **order-consistent surrogate** for the full-horizon objective: for every reachable state, the action $\pi_{\text{RH}}$ commits has full-$N_h$-horizon value at least that of the action $\pi_{\text{current}}$ would take. Under order-consistency, $\pi_{\text{RH}}$ weakly dominates $\pi_{\text{current}}$ pointwise on the full objective, so $V_O(M_t, \pi_{\text{RH}}; N_h) \geq V_O(M_t, \pi_{\text{current}}; N_h)$ at every reachable state; taking the supremum over the first action gives the rung. The naive justification — "$\pi_{\text{RH}} \succeq \pi_{\text{current}}$ because C2 optimizes where C1 holds fixed" — fails without order-consistency: C2 with window $N_r \lt N_h$ optimizes the *truncated* $N_r$-step objective, and "optimal for the truncated objective" does not imply "$\succeq \pi_{\text{current}}$ on the full objective."

Three named conditions each force order-consistency and are therefore each sufficient:

**(RH-1) Horizon alignment: $N_r \geq N_h$.** The replanning window covers the whole evaluated horizon, so C2's per-step optimization *is* full-horizon optimization from each reached state. By Bellman's principle of optimality the receding-horizon continuation then equals the optimal continuation, giving $A_O^{\text{RH}} = A_O^{\text{B}} \geq A_O^{(1)}$. This condition is degenerate by design: when $N_r \geq N_h$ the C2/C3 distinction collapses on the evaluated horizon, so it serves as a boundary marker, not as the operative condition for genuine $N_r \lt N_h$ replanning.

**(RH-2) Value-compared (improvement-checked) replanning — the operative condition.** C2 commits the replanned action only if its full-horizon value is at least that of continuing $\pi_{\text{current}}$; otherwise it keeps $\pi_{\text{current}}$. At each reachable state $s$ with $k$ steps remaining,

$$\pi_{\text{RH}}(s) \;:=\; \arg\max_{a \in \{a_{\text{replan}}(s),\; \pi_{\text{current}}(s)\}} \Big[\, r(s, a) + V_O\big(s'(s,a),\, \pi_{\text{current}};\, k-1\big) \,\Big],$$

i.e., each candidate first action is scored by *rollout under the base policy $\pi_{\text{current}}$* over the remaining full horizon. By construction $\pi_{\text{RH}}$ weakly dominates $\pi_{\text{current}}$ pointwise on the full objective, so $A_O^{(1)} \leq A_O^{\text{RH}}$. This is one-step policy improvement (rollout with base policy $\pi_{\text{current}}$), whose defining property is that the improved policy never underperforms its base policy. It is the natural AAT reading of C2 for strategy revision, since the orient cascade's purpose at step 4 ( #der-orient-cascade) is *improvement over the current strategy*: the monotonicity then follows from the improvement guard rather than from replanning per se.

**(RH-3) Terminal-cost / cost-to-go consistency — the general condition.** Equip the $N_r$-step replanning objective with a terminal value function $V_f$ at the truncation boundary satisfying both

$$V_f(s) \;\leq\; \max_{a} \big[\, r(s,a) + V_f(s') \,\big] \qquad \text{(control-Lyapunov / value-underestimator decrease inequality)},$$

$$V_f(s) \;\leq\; V_O\big(s,\, \pi_{\text{current}};\, \text{remaining horizon}\big) \qquad \text{(terminal cost lower-bounds the baseline tail)}.$$

The first inequality is the standard terminal-cost ingredient that makes the truncated-with-terminal-cost optimization a valid surrogate for the full-horizon objective. The second ensures the surrogate never undervalues the baseline tail, so the replanner never trades the baseline away for a truncation artifact. Together they force order-consistency, giving $A_O^{(1)} \leq A_O^{\text{RH}}$. Conditions (RH-1) and (RH-2) are special cases: (RH-1) is $V_f \equiv$ optimal tail with $N_r = N_h$; (RH-2) is $V_f = V_O(\cdot,\, \pi_{\text{current}};\, \cdot)$, the baseline cost-to-go itself.

### The left-rung counterexample

Absent any order-consistency condition the left rung fails. A deterministic two-state, two-action, two-step instance suffices. Evaluate continuations from a post-first-action state $x$ with evaluated horizon $N_h = 2$ continuation steps and replanning window $N_r = 1$; stage rewards lie on transitions and $V_O$ is their sum.

- At $x$, two admissible actions: $g$ ("greedy") gives immediate value $+1$ and transitions to a dead state $D$ (value $0$ thereafter); $p$ ("patient") gives immediate value $0$ and transitions to a high-value state $H$.
- At $H$: value $+10$ per step. At $D$: value $0$.
- $\pi_{\text{current}}$ is "patient at $x$" (action $p$).

Then:

- **C1 (frozen $\pi_{\text{current}}$):** $0$ at step 1 via $p$, then $+10$ at step 2 in $H$, so $A_O^{(1)} = 10$.
- **C2 ($N_r = 1$ replanning):** the one-step lookahead at $x$ prefers $g$ ($+1 \gt 0$), lands in $D$, collects $0$ at step 2, so $A_O^{\text{RH}} = 1$.
- **C3 (Bellman over $N_h = 2$):** picks $p$, value $10$, so $A_O^{\text{B}} = 10$.

Hence $A_O^{(1)} = 10 \gt A_O^{\text{RH}} = 1$: the left rung is violated, while the right rung ($1 \leq 10$) holds as derived. The myopic window optimizes a truncated objective whose order is *inverted* relative to the full objective — $+1$ now beats $+0$ now within the window, but $+0$ now then $+10$ later beats $+1$ now then $0$ later on the full horizon. This is precisely the situation C2 is reaching for ("a goal unattainable under frozen continuation may be reachable with replanning") and shows the reaching cuts both ways unless the replanning objective is order-consistent with the evaluated objective. The control case $N_r = N_h$ restores the rung (then $A_O^{\text{RH}} = A_O^{\text{B}} = 10$, condition (RH-1)); the value-compared guard (RH-2) and a baseline-lower-bounding terminal cost (RH-3) each likewise restore it to value $10$.

### The right-rung counterexamples

Each of (M), (PC) and (SO) is needed; three deterministic instances show it, one per hypothesis. In all three $x$ is the current state, the action at $x$ is the first action the attainability supremum ranges over, stage rewards lie on transitions, and $V_O$ is their sum. This frame differs from the left-rung instance above, which fixed the first action and ran two continuation steps from $x$, so that its action at $x$ was the continuation policy's; the two frames are not the same evaluation and neither instance transfers to the other.

**(M) holds, (PC) and (SO) fail — the instance $(x, H, D)$.** One continuation step. At $x$: $g$ pays $+1$ to $D$, $p$ pays $0$ to $H$. At $H$: $u$ pays $+10$, $w$ pays $+100$. At $D$: $0$. Fix $\Pi = \{\pi_1, \pi_2\}$ with $\pi_1 = (x \mapsto g,\; H \mapsto w)$ and $\pi_2 = (x \mapsto p,\; H \mapsto u)$. Then $V_O(\pi_1) = 1 + 0 = 1$ and $V_O(\pi_2) = 0 + 10 = 10$, so $\pi^\ast = \pi_2$ and $A_O^{\text{B}} = 10$ on both readings ($\max_a Q_O(a; \pi_2) = \max(1 + 0,\; 0 + 10) = 10$). The $N_r = 1$ replanner takes $g$ at $x$ ($+1 \gt 0$) and $w$ at $H$ ($+100 \gt +10$), so $\pi_{\text{RH}} = \pi_1 \in \Pi$ and (M) holds. But

$$A_O^{\text{RH}} = \max\big(Q_O(g; \pi_1),\; Q_O(p; \pi_1)\big) = \max(1 + 0,\; 0 + 100) = 100 \;\gt\; 10 = A_O^{\text{B}}.$$

The best first action under the receding-horizon continuation composes into $p \oplus \pi_1$, worth $100$, which $\Pi$ does not contain, so (PC) fails; and $\pi^\ast = \pi_2$ takes $u$ at $H$ where $w$ is available, so $\pi^\ast$ is not optimal from $H$ and (SO) fails. Restoring either restores the rung: over the class of all policies, $\pi^\ast$ takes $w$ at $H$ and $A_O^{\text{B}} = 100$. The one-line argument "any continuation drawn from $\Pi$ has value at most $A_O^{\text{B}}$" is true here ($V_O(\pi_1) = 1 \leq 10$) and silent about $Q_O(p; \pi_1) = 100$, which is what $A_O^{\text{RH}}$ takes its supremum over. The same mechanism reaches every comparison against $A_O^{\text{B}}$, including C1 against C3: with $\pi_{\text{current}} = \pi_1$ here, $A_O^{(1)} = 100 \gt A_O^{\text{B}}$ too.

**(PC) and (SO) hold, (M) fails — the stitched replanner leaves the class.** $N_h = 3$, $N_r = 1$. At $x$ the single action $z$ pays $0$ to $s$. At $s$: $c$ pays $+5$ to $s_1$, $d$ pays $0$ to $s_2$. At $s_1$: $e_1$ pays $0$, $e_2$ pays $+10$. At $s_2$: $f$ pays $+6$. Fix $\Pi = \{\pi_A, \pi_B\}$ with $\pi_A = (z, c, e_1, f)$ and $\pi_B = (z, d, e_2, f)$, listing the action at $x, s, s_1, s_2$. Then $V_O(\pi_A) = 5$ and $V_O(\pi_B) = 6$, so $\pi^\ast = \pi_B$. (PC) holds: the only first action is $z$, and $z \oplus \pi_i = \pi_i$. (SO) holds: $s$ is the only state a first action reaches, and from $s$ with two steps left $\pi_B$ attains the class supremum $6$ against $\pi_A$'s $5$. The replanner, each one-step window's argmax taken over $\Pi$, takes $c$ at $s$ ($5 \gt 0$) and $e_2$ at $s_1$ ($10 \gt 0$), so the stitched $\pi_{\text{RH}} = (z, c, e_2, \cdot)$ is not a member of $\Pi$, and

$$A_O^{\text{RH}} = Q_O(z; \pi_{\text{RH}}) = 0 + 5 + 10 = 15 \;\gt\; 6 = A_O^{\text{B}}$$

on both readings. The prefix-closure here is trivial; it need not be: add a second first action $z'$ paying $0$ to $s$ and take $\Pi$ as the four policies $\{z, z'\} \times \{\pi_A\text{'s tail}, \pi_B\text{'s tail}\}$. Then (PC) holds non-trivially, (SO) holds, the stitched replanner is still outside $\Pi$, and $A_O^{\text{RH}} = 15 \gt 6 = A_O^{\text{B}}$ again. What fails is membership: the replanner stitches the first actions of two different window-optimal members, and a two- or four-member library does not contain the patchwork.

**(M) and (SO) hold, (PC) fails — the two readings part.** One continuation step. At $x$: $p$ pays $+2$ to $H$, $g$ pays $+1$ to $D$. At $H$: only $u$, paying $+10$. At $D$: $d_1$ pays $0$, $d_2$ pays $+50$. Fix $\Pi = \{\pi_A, \pi_B\}$ with $\pi_A = (x \mapsto p,\; H \mapsto u,\; D \mapsto d_2)$ and $\pi_B = (x \mapsto g,\; H \mapsto u,\; D \mapsto d_1)$. Then $V_O(\pi_A) = 12$ and $V_O(\pi_B) = 1$, so $\pi^\ast = \pi_A$. (SO) holds: at $H$ the class supremum $10$ and at $D$ the class supremum $50$ are both attained by $\pi_A$. (PC) fails: $g \oplus \pi_A \notin \Pi$. The $N_r = 1$ replanner takes $p$ at $x$ ($2 \gt 1$), $u$ at $H$ and $d_2$ at $D$, so $\pi_{\text{RH}} = \pi_A \in \Pi$ and (M) holds. Then $A_O^{\text{RH}} = \max(2 + 10,\; 1 + 50) = 51$, and on the first-action reading $A_O^{\text{B}} = \sup_a Q_O(a; \pi_A) = 51$ as well, so the rung holds as derived; on the class-supremum reading $A_O^{\text{B}} = V_O(\pi_A) = 12 \lt 51$. The composite $g \oplus \pi_A$, worth $51$, is what the first action can reach with $\pi^\ast$ as continuation and what $\Pi$ does not contain; (G) fails, since $\pi^\ast$'s own first action $p$ is not the best first action under $\pi^\ast$.

A restricted $\Pi$ is the AAT case, not a curiosity: #def-satisfaction-gap takes $\Pi$ to be the strategies representable in the agent's DAG formalism, which carries no closure guarantee and no guarantee that a replanner's patchwork of its members is itself representable.

### Corollary orderings

The corollary $\delta$-orderings inherit the same split. With $\delta_{\text{sat}} = V_{O_t}^{\min} - A_O$ ( #def-satisfaction-gap) and $\delta_{\text{regret}}$ a difference of $A_O$ values ( #def-control-regret):

$$\delta_{\text{sat}}^{\text{B}} \leq \delta_{\text{sat}}^{\text{RH}} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \delta_{\text{regret}}^{\text{RH}} \leq \delta_{\text{regret}}^{\text{B}} \qquad \text{(from the right rung, under (M) and (PC) or (SO), on the first-action reading)};$$

$$\delta_{\text{sat}}^{\text{RH}} \leq \delta_{\text{sat}}^{(1)} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \delta_{\text{regret}}^{(1)} \leq \delta_{\text{regret}}^{\text{RH}} \qquad \text{(conditional on order-consistency, RH-1/2/3)}.$$

Three further statements fall out of the same comparison and are what the diagnostic reading of #def-value-object actually uses.

*The unconditional half.* On the first-action reading $A_O^{\text{B}} \geq \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O$ for every $\Pi$, so $\delta_{\text{sat}}^{\text{B}} \gt 0$ says that no member of $\Pi$ reaches the threshold, whatever the class: C3 gives no false "unattainable" diagnosis, with no closure hypothesis. The converse — that $\delta_{\text{sat}}^{\text{B}} \leq 0$ certifies a member of $\Pi$ reaching the threshold — holds under (PC) or (G); under (SO) alone it certifies a first action followed by $\pi^\ast$, which the agent can take and $\Pi$ need not contain.

*C1 against C3 directly.* $A_O^{(1)} \leq A_O^{\text{B}}$ holds under $\pi_{\text{current}} \in \Pi$ and (PC) on both readings, or (SO) on the first-action reading, with no order-consistency and no (M): under (PC), $Q_O(a; \pi_{\text{current}}) = V_O(a \oplus \pi_{\text{current}}) \leq \sup_{\pi \in \Pi} V_O$; under (SO), $Q_O(a; \pi_{\text{current}}) = \mathbb{E}[r(a) + V_O(s', \pi_{\text{current}})] \leq \mathbb{E}[r(a) + V_O(s', \pi^\ast)] = Q_O(a; \pi^\ast)$. The comparison does not pass through C2, so the "locally stuck under C1, infeasible under C3" reading needs only membership of the current policy and one closure property. Without them it fails in the same way as the rung: $\pi_{\text{current}} = \pi_1$ on the instance $(x, H, D)$ gives $A_O^{(1)} = 100 \gt 10$; and on the third instance with $\pi_{\text{current}} = \pi_A$, $A_O^{(1)} = 51 \gt 12 = V_O(\pi^\ast)$ on the class-supremum reading, under (SO) alone.

*Non-negativity of control regret.* $\delta_{\text{regret}} = A_O - V_O(M_t, \pi_{\text{current}}; N_h) \geq 0$ holds under C1 for every class, since $\pi_{\text{current}}$'s own first action is one of those $A_O^{(1)}$ ranges over; under C3 given $\pi_{\text{current}} \in \Pi$, on either reading; and under C2 only given order-consistency (RH-1/2/3), since an unguarded replanner can fall below the frozen policy — the left-rung instance has $A_O^{\text{RH}} = 1$ against $V_O(\pi_{\text{current}}) = 10$, so $\delta_{\text{regret}}^{\text{RH}} = -9$.

## Epistemic Status

*Exact, under named hypotheses on each rung.* The right rung is exact under membership of the stitched replanner in $\Pi$ (M) together with prefix-closure of $\Pi$ (PC) or state-wise optimality of $\pi^\ast$ (SO), on the first-action reading of $A_O^{\text{B}}$; under (PC) the two readings coincide, under (SO) they coincide only with (G). The three instances are exact demonstrations that each hypothesis is needed: without (PC) and (SO) the first-action supremum reaches composites with $\pi_{\text{RH}}$ that lie outside $\Pi$, so "the $\Pi$-optimum bounds every member of $\Pi$" does not reach them; without (M) the replanner's own policy is such a composite. A rectangular class has all of (M), (PC), (SO) and (G), and the rung holds there on both readings for every maximizer. The left rung is exact under any one of the order-consistency conditions (RH-1), (RH-2), (RH-3) — each a closed-form sufficient condition — and the two-state counterexample is an exact demonstration that no unconditional left rung exists for genuine $N_r \lt N_h$ replanning. That failure is structural: it is objective-mismatch between the truncated $N_r$-step replanning objective and the evaluated $N_h$-horizon objective, not a pathology of a contrived instance. The conditions are standard receding-horizon results — (RH-1) is Bellman's principle of optimality; (RH-2) is rollout / one-step policy improvement; (RH-3) is the control-Lyapunov terminal-cost construction — applied to AAT's value-object setting; the elementary derivations above are self-contained and do not depend on any external citation. The corollary $\delta$-orderings inherit the hypotheses rung by rung; the unconditional half and the direct C1-against-C3 comparison are exact as stated.

## Discussion

**Why the left rung is the interesting one.** The hierarchy is usually read as "more planning is weakly better," and the right rung makes that precise for the step from receding-horizon to Bellman, provided the replanner's patchwork of members is itself a member (M) and the class can absorb any first action (PC) or its optimum is optimal from wherever a first action lands (SO); for a short plan library none of these need hold, and the "larger set" the Bellman supremum ranges over is then not larger than the set the replanner's first-action supremum ranges over. The left rung — from frozen continuation to receding-horizon — is where the reading breaks: replanning is *not* automatically an improvement over standing pat. The reason is that a finite replanning window optimizes a different objective than the one the value is scored against, and a short window can be actively misled by near-term value. The standard remedy in receding-horizon control is exactly (RH-2)/(RH-3): guard the replanned action against the baseline, or carry a terminal cost that keeps the truncated objective honest about the tail. AAT's C2-for-strategy-revision reading inherits this: the orient cascade escalates to C2 *to improve on* $\pi_{\text{current}}$, and the improvement guard (RH-2) is what makes "escalate to C2" a monotone move rather than a gamble.

**Relation to the diagnostic direction.** The split does not disturb the C1-default diagnostic logic ( #def-value-object). C1 remains the most conservative diagnostic, and the locally-stuck-vs-genuinely-infeasible reading is unaffected by the conditional left rung: it rests on the C1/C3 endpoints, compared directly, which needs membership of the current policy and (PC) or (SO) and nothing about C2. One half of it needs nothing at all: a positive C3 gap says no member of $\Pi$ reaches the threshold, for every class. The other half is where the class matters: for a policy class that is not prefix-closed, a C1 or C2 attainability can exceed the $\Pi$-optimum, and "C3 is the strongest convention" is then a statement about closure of the class, which the DAG-representable $\Pi$ of #def-satisfaction-gap does not guarantee. What the split adds is a caution for deployments that escalate to C2 — unguarded short-horizon replanning is not guaranteed to dominate the frozen baseline, so a C2 diagnostic should use a window covering the horizon, a value-compared guard, or a control-Lyapunov terminal cost — and a second caution for anyone reading a C2 attainability against C3: unless the replanner's stitched policy is itself in the library, the comparison is between a patchwork and a member, and the patchwork can win.

## Findings

### Convention monotonicity holds rung by rung under named hypotheses

**Brief:** More planning is not automatically better, in either direction of the hierarchy. Looking one move ahead and grabbing the action that looks best *right now* can be worse than just sticking with your current plan — the classic short-sighted trap (take the quick $+1$ into a dead end, miss the $+0$-now-then-$+10$-later path). It becomes reliably better only if you check the replanned move against simply continuing as you were (or carry an honest estimate of the long-run value past your planning window). And solving the whole problem optimally over your plan library beats the limited replanner only when the library is the right shape: it has to let you bolt any first move onto any plan, or its best plan has to be best from wherever a first move lands, and the replanner's move-by-move patchwork of library plans has to itself be in the library. If your library is a short list, the patchwork usually is not, and the replanner can then score higher than the "optimal" plan — not because it found a better plan in the library, but because it left the library.

**Impact:** Sharpens the convention-hierarchy monotonicity ( #def-value-object) from an unconditional claim into named-hypothesis guarantees. The receding-horizon-to-Bellman rung ($A_O^{\text{RH}} \leq A_O^{\text{B}}$) holds when the replanner's stitched policy is a member of the class and the class is closed under prefixing a first action or its optimum is optimal from every state a first action reaches, read with $A_O^{\text{B}}$ as the best first action under the optimum; a rectangular class has all of this, and each hypothesis is needed (three two-policy instances: $100$ against $10$, $15$ against $6$, $51$ against $12$). The one-step-to-receding-horizon rung ($A_O^{(1)} \leq A_O^{\text{RH}}$) is false in general and holds exactly under order-consistency of the replanning objective, forced by any of (RH-1) horizon alignment, (RH-2) a value-compared/rollout guard, or (RH-3) a baseline-lower-bounding control-Lyapunov terminal cost. Three consequences travel with it: C3's positive gap means no member of the class reaches the threshold, unconditionally; the C1-against-C3 diagnostic needs only membership of the current policy and one closure property; and control regret is non-negative under C2 only when the replanner is guarded. Two cautions follow for deployments: guard the replanning objective or the monotone-improvement reading of C2 does not hold, and read "C3 is strongest" as a statement about a closed policy class, which a restricted plan library (the DAG-representable $\Pi$) need not be.

**Novelty Claim:** Claim transfer of rollout / one-step policy improvement and terminal-cost receding-horizon results into AAT's value-object setting for the left rung; claim novelty, at not-searched depth, on the right rung's characterization for restricted policy classes — that the stitched replanner's membership is a hypothesis of its own, that prefix-closure and state-wise optimality split across the two readings of attainability, and that a rectangular class is the sufficient shape.
