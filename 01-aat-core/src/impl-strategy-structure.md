# Additional Implications & Discussion

The chapter delivered three things that work together at the chapter-end better than they do separately at chapter-middle: a formal strategy representation whose graphical structure is theorem-backed under a named condition and whose parameterization is principled but chosen; an orthogonal diagnostic split that distinguishes goal-attainability from policy-quality and whose meaning is convention-indexed rather than absolute; and an honest scope statement about correlated failure that is the chapter's in-situ encounter with an identifiability floor — Instance 2 of the pattern Part II's opening meta-segment already introduced ( #disc-identifiability-floor), met here on chapter content with the escape AAT's own machinery supplies. The chain-confidence-decay identity at the head of the chapter does work three layers deeper than its elementary form suggests. None of these are findings the intro doesn't preview; what the chapter-end can do that the intro can't is read them together, point at the bridges to the rest of the framework, and say — claim by claim — at what strength each is held.

**Proof provenance, up front.** Results below are surfaced here; their proofs live at the cited homes, and several homes are later in the reading order. *Exact:* acyclicity of $\Sigma_t$; the chain-rule identity; the two diagnostic definitions and the sum identity they satisfy; the Cramér-Rao refutation under an unobservable common cause (Instance 2); the right rung of the convention-monotonicity chain. *Conditional:* the Markov factorization (on causal sufficiency); the L0/L1/L1' transfer results (on the Beta-Bernoulli edge model); the left rung of the monotonicity chain (on order-consistency, RH-1/2/3); the three forced coordinates (each on its own additivity or invariance axiom); the on-policy detection no-go (on (S1)–(S5), exact only for shallow strict-prerequisite cases). *Robust-qualitative:* the AND/OR scope itself; the no-go under general topology. *Discussion-grade:* the convergent-choice category, the dual reading of the two floors, and every positioning claim in this segment. ## Theorem-backed structure, chosen parameterization

The chapter is careful about a distinction that downstream segments rely on without restating: what falls out of theorems, and what is a parameterization choice within the theorem-backed structure. Acyclicity of $\Sigma_t$ is proved — directed temporal order over a finite horizon forces it ( #post-causal-structure plus standard order theory), for time-indexed event *tokens*; a retry loop is a linear chain in the unrolled view ( #def-strategy-dag §"Acyclicity is Derived"). The Markov factorization is proved under causal sufficiency via the Causal Markov Condition theorem (Spirtes-Glymour-Scheines 2000, Pearl 2009) — `#deriv-graph-structure-uniqueness` does the work. Together they constitute a *sufficient* condition for the DAG-with-Markov-property representation. The argument parallels Cox's theorem in form (operational desiderata force a representation) but not yet in strength (Cox is necessary-and-sufficient; this is sufficient only — whether a non-DAG structure could also satisfy the postulates is an open stronger result).

Three layers are worth keeping apart, because the chapter's sources occasionally let them merge. First, causal sufficiency buys the *Markov factorization* — conditional independence over variables. Second, AND/OR combination with single-parameter edges is a *chosen* local parameterization inside that structure; correct status propagation needs both the factorization and the parameterization to hold. Third, the Correlation Hierarchy repairs violations of edge independence *within* that parameterization. The parameterization is chosen but not arbitrary, and the reason it was chosen is sharper than "the alternatives were unidentifiable": noisy-OR everywhere was rejected because it *demonstrably overestimates conjunctive structure* — three required results at $0.95$, $0.90$, $0.99$ score $0.99995$ under noisy-OR and $0.846$ under AND ( #scope-and-or) — and weighted combination because it reintroduces a second per-edge parameter. The single-parameter edge replaced an earlier two-parameter form when the AND/OR rules at nodes turned out to absorb the second parameter's role. Three independent formalism attempts converged on the same representation; the chapter calls this a *convergent representational choice* — a category between *derived* and *arbitrary* that this segment names but no source derives, so it is held here as framing, not as a result.

The chapter-level reason planning needs both combinators where diagnosis gets by with one is worth stating plainly, because it is what the noisy-OR table shows: the logic of causation in a *plan* is more brittle than the logic of evidence in a *diagnosis*. If one piece of evidence is missing, a diagnosis can still be highly probable; if one required step is missing, a plan fails with probability one. Strategy DAGs are not belief networks turned sideways — which is also why AAT represents strategy as a DAG at all rather than as a reactive policy: a Moore machine says *what* to do, a DAG says *why*, and strategy revision under a changed $M_t$ needs the why ( #def-strategy-dag §"Relationship to Moore machines").

For practitioners, the implication worth carrying is methodological: when AAT's strategy machinery shows up downstream in regret bounds, persistence schemas, or composition arguments, the load-bearing structural commitments are theorem-backed under causal sufficiency, not modeling conveniences. The conditioning on causal sufficiency is the right level of conditionality — a property of *strategy quality* (did the agent represent the relevant common causes as nodes?), not of the proof. It is repairable in practice (add the common-cause node) and testable — but only under exploration: the on-policy residual cannot reveal it, as the no-go below states, so the test is joint sibling covariance, route (b) of `#der-causal-insufficiency-detection`. Under causal sufficiency and in Regime A ( #scope-edge-update-causal-validity), Pearl's do-calculus applies to status propagation and the strategy-layer results inherit the strength of the Pearl-Bareinboim machinery; in Regimes B and C the edge credences carry their identifiability coefficients $\iota_{ij}$ and $\hat P_\Sigma$ is a working heuristic, not a calibrated probability ( #def-strategy-dag §"Connection to Pearl's framework").

## The diagnostic split, lifted by the convention hierarchy

The chapter's orthogonal diagnostic — satisfaction gap $\delta_{\text{sat}} = V_{O_t}^{\min} - A_O$ and control regret $\delta_{\text{regret}} = A_O - V_O(\pi_{\text{current}})$ — separates "the world doesn't permit it" from "you're not doing it well enough." The orthogonality is a one-line fact the two definitions jointly entail and neither states: with both gaps evaluated under the same $(M_t, \Pi, N_h)$ and continuation convention, so that the two $A_O$'s are the same object,

$$\delta_{\text{sat}} + \delta_{\text{regret}} \;=\; V_{O_t}^{\min} - V_O(M_t, \pi_{\text{current}};\, N_h),$$

*[Derived (two-gap-sum), exact — $A_O$ cancels]* — the two diagnostics partition the total shortfall of the current policy against the satisfaction threshold, and they are the two additive pieces of the one quantity a single goal-distance signal used to conflate. The right-hand side is the value of a *specified* policy, so it is convention-invariant: the continuation convention moves only the partition point $A_O$ between the two gaps, never the total. (Non-negativity of $\delta_{\text{regret}}$ is the separate condition $\pi_{\text{current}} \in \Pi$: a current policy outside the class the supremum ranges over could outperform $A_O$.) The 2×2 cell map (goal × policy) routes four regimes to four corrective actions, and the orient cascade in Part II Ch.5 will use this routing to direct revision pressure to the right substate. The implications-side framing: the diagnostic is not a measurement but a *routing* device, and the narrative under the routing is the difference between thrashing and deliberating — a standard RL learner that fails perturbs its policy and retries; an agent that reads $\delta_{\text{regret}} \approx 0$ first knows that thrashing cannot help, because it is *optimally failing*, and escalates instead: improve $M_t$, extend $N_h$, expand $\Pi$, and only last revise $O_t$. The disambiguation table in `#def-satisfaction-gap` encodes that order; read as a posture it is the mathematics of grit versus acceptance — when to keep pushing and when to accept that the goal, not the plan, is the problem.

The lift the chapter delivers but does not entirely surface is that the diagnostic is *convention-indexed*, not absolute. The value object's three conventions — C1 (one-step, canonical), C2 (receding-horizon), C3 (Bellman) — order the satisfaction gap $\delta_{\text{sat}}^{\text{B}} \leq \delta_{\text{sat}}^{\text{RH}} \leq \delta_{\text{sat}}^{(1)}$, and the two rungs differ in character: the $\text{B} \leq \text{RH}$ half is exact and unconditional; the $\text{RH} \leq (1)$ half holds under order-consistency of C2's replanning objective (RH-1/2/3 in `#def-value-object`) and fails for unguarded short-horizon replanning ( #deriv-convention-monotonicity exhibits the two-state counterexample). The regret ordering runs the other way — $\delta_{\text{regret}}^{(1)} \leq \delta_{\text{regret}}^{\text{RH}} \leq \delta_{\text{regret}}^{\text{B}}$, with the same rung structure — and the two orderings are one movement seen twice: a deeper search raises $A_O$, which *lowers* the satisfaction gap and *raises* regret. You regret more when you know how much better you could have done. C1 gives the most false "unattainable" diagnoses; C3 gives none modulo model error. The 2×2 map is therefore a convention-indexed family, and the practical reading is an escalation ladder: if C1 says stuck, C2 may say recoverable under replanning; if C2 says stuck, C3 — where computable — is the last resort. The C1→C2 move is the formal shape of stepping back to think: an agent optimally failing under C1 (continuation frozen at $\pi_{\text{current}}$) re-optimizes over a replanning window at the same $N_h$, a path becomes visible, $A_O$ jumps, and regret that read zero now reads large — a *convention* change, distinct from extending the horizon $N_h$, which is its own row of the disambiguation table. Convention choice is thus a deliberation lever, not only a measurement caveat — and the honest form of that claim is measurement-theoretic: two analyses under different conventions are not disagreeing, they are measuring different things, so the convention is part of the measurement and cross-convention comparison is a category error.

One relativity the chapter names only implicitly deserves the chapter-end's attention. $A_O$ is a supremum over $\Pi$, *the available policy class* — and nothing in the diagnostic asks who set $\Pi$. A restriction imposed from outside the agent shrinks $\Pi$: the satisfaction gap rises and the regret the *true* $A_O$ defines can fall, but the disambiguation table's other rungs — improve $M_t$, extend $N_h$, expand $\Pi$ — stay live, so nothing about an ordinary restriction lands the agent in the capability-limit cell by itself. Volume 4 works the limiting case where those rungs are exhausted: under complete severing of the external sphere no model improvement, horizon extension, or policy-class expansion restores contrast the kernel does not have, the true external regret is exactly zero, $\delta_{\text{sat}} \gt 0$ permanently, and the C1/C2/C3 split collapses — the infeasibility verdict is convention-invariant ( #der-severed-actuation-dynamics Result 5a; a forward reference). That landing is *truth-relative*; the agent's own computed diagnostic is $M_t$-relative, and until its efficacy credences have converged it reads $\delta_{\text{regret}} \gt 0$ from stale credences and self-diagnoses *strategy-problem* — churning against a wall, and freezing that wrong self-diagnosis permanently if it withdraws before convergence. *[Discussion]* The chapter-end consequence, held at discussion-grade: the split is policy-class-relative in a way the convention indexing above does not capture, the two relativities are independent, and the gap between the truth-relative cell and the agent's $M_t$-relative reading of it is exactly where a regret-based monitor reports "converged" on an agent the world has boxed in. The partial, sub-sphere case is unwritten in canon. A related blind spot has the same shape: the map reads a change in attainability but not its origin — a self-denial attack and legitimate self-protection (admission control, load-shedding) present the same signature, an elevated $\delta_{\text{sat}}$ with refusals.

The split's positive content against active inference's preferences-as-priors form — expected free energy ranks policies but cannot separate "the goal is unattainable from here" from "the current policy is not the best available" — is developed in `#def-satisfaction-gap` §"Diagnostic content" and not repeated here; the one-line version is that the diagnostic exists because $V_{O_t}$ is a value functional on trajectories rather than a log-prior on outcomes, so wanting $o$ and expecting $o$ stay formally distinct.

## The Correlation Hierarchy and identifiability-floor Instance 2

The chapter's `#def-strategy-dag` carries an honest scope statement about correlated failure that is more consequential than it reads on first encounter. The Correlation Hierarchy (L0 / L1 / L1' / L2) names what AAT's formal strategy-layer results actually claim and where they degrade. L0 (independence — the tractable baseline under causal sufficiency) is where the persistence schema, gradient-based credit assignment, and sector-condition transfer are proved. L1 (augmented DAG with strict-prerequisite common-cause nodes factored above the correlation they create) is where those results transfer exactly. L1' (mixture form for soft facilitators with $\theta_{\text{child} \mid \neg C} \gt 0$, when $C$ is observable per trial) transfers with five-way gating via Prop B.7 in `#deriv-edge-credence-dynamics`. All three transfers are *conditional on the Beta-Bernoulli edge model* those propositions assume. L2 (full joint distribution) is mathematically ideal but exponential in parameter count and incompatible with the bounded-cognition constraint that motivates the DAG in the first place. The canonical story is project-management overconfidence: three independent backup teams at $0.7$ each score $0.973$ under L0, but they share a staging server — a latent common cause — and the OR-gate is optimistic by exactly the shared covariance; the fix is mechanical, `Plan = AND(staging works, OR(team 1, team 2, team 3))`, and the lesson is the general one: highly redundant architectures are more fragile than their designers believe, because an unmodeled common cause collapses an OR-gate that looked like safe redundancy.

The substantive finding lives at the boundary between L1' with observable $C$ and L1' with unobservable $C$. When the common cause is unobservable, the per-conditional decomposition is not "open" — it is *structurally refuted*: the per-trial Fisher information is rank one, the Cramér-Rao bound is infinite along the indeterminacy manifold, and no unbiased online estimator on the joint conditional vector admits a sector parameter $\alpha \gt 0$. This is *exact* — by an external theorem, not a modeling judgment — and it is Instance 2 of `#disc-identifiability-floor`. The repair routes at the home are three, and not peers: *instrument* secondary signals that identify $C$ per trial (the route that turns refuted into globally derived); run $K \geq 2$ children jointly under the same $C$-realization, which is sketch-grade and narrow ( #deriv-edge-credence-dynamics WN); or fall back to L0-on-marginals, losing the per-conditional diagnostics. The framework's recurring posture is in view: each floor is a *constructive use of impossibility* — the no-go elevates the specific machinery that supplies the escape from useful to structurally required.

The chapter is also where Instance 1 becomes statable: the on-policy L0-insufficiency-detection no-go. An agent at L0 cannot detect its own causal insufficiency from purely on-policy traces — the worlds with and without a latent common cause emit identical on-policy distributions under sequential short-circuit execution (the Bareinboim Causal Hierarchy Theorem applied at the L0/L1 distinction). The no-go is conditional on (S1)–(S5) — pure on-policy execution, short-circuit evaluation, censored siblings, no interventional access to the latent, no structural priors — and is *exact* for shallow strict-prerequisite cases and *robust-qualitative* for general topology. The escape language the chapter inherits from its sources is a defined term and should be read as one: `#der-causal-insufficiency-detection` names five routes, each violating one scope condition — (a) $\varepsilon$-exploration, (b) joint sibling observability under exploration, (c) intermediate-state observability, (d) structural priors, (e) direct intervention on the latent — and calls (b) the *unique broadly-available* violation because it is the only one that uses *nothing the theory does not already require*: exploration via SA3, interventional data via the loop ( #der-loop-interventional-access). Richer sensors are (c); external instrumentation or oracle labels are (e) or (d); randomized audits are (a). "Broadly available" is a property, not an intensifier: the route needs no capability the theory does not already require. The full treatment is in Part II Ch.4; the Correlation Hierarchy here is where the scope condition is named and the no-go becomes anticipatable — and the no-go reads outward as the innovator's dilemma with a mechanism under it: an organization that never deviates from its proven playbook cannot detect when its causal model of the business is wrong, because pure on-policy experience is self-reinforcing; the failure is structural, not motivational.

Read Ch.3's L1'-unobservable boundary and Ch.4's on-policy no-go together and the first two floor instances come into view as a pair — but their escapes are *different*: Instance 1 escapes by loop-interventional access; Instance 2 by observability of the latent. What they share is not a common escape but a common mechanism, stated at the meta-segment's discussion-grade: both are *rank-collapse* floors, and the reason no change of coordinates escapes either is Sylvester's law of inertia — every reparameterization of the observation model acts on the information operator by congruence, congruence preserves inertia, so a rank-deficient operator is rank-deficient in every coordinate and the only escape is *rank augmentation* — a genuinely new score component, never a reweighting ( #disc-identifiability-floor §Discussion). You cannot survey your way out of a blind spot by changing the units on the ruler. One practical gap the hierarchy leaves open at the L1' rung: estimating the mixing weight $\theta_C$ for a soft facilitator has the identification problems of Regime B, and the sources say nothing about where that weight comes from.

## Chain-confidence decay, coordinate forcing, and the bridge into Strategy Dynamics

The chapter opens with `#der-chain-confidence-decay` — the chain rule of probability in log-space, $\log P(\text{chain}) = \sum_i \log P(E_i \mid E_{\lt i})$, exact as an identity, with chain confidence non-increasing in depth (strictly decreasing whenever each added step is uncertain). The identity is elementary; what the chapter intro flags and the implications can develop is that it is the *anchor* of a meta-pattern AAT deploys at three further representational layers. The three are theorems, each forcing a coordinate by a uniqueness result on an AAT-internal axiom, and each *conditional* on its axiom: reverse-KL at the divergence layer (Cauchy's functional equation on chain-rule additivity over conditional factorizations), log-odds at the update layer (Cauchy-FE on evidential additivity), the Fisher metric at the metric layer (Čencov invariance under the parameterization-invariance commitment (PI)). The chain layer is not a fourth theorem — it is the mathematical identity the three axioms cite as their analog — and the substance is that the three axioms, independently posited, converge on one exponential-family Legendre-Fenchel geometry. `#disc-additive-coordinate-forcing` carries the catalog and the three-way vocabulary that keeps it honest: a coordinate is *forced* when a uniqueness theorem admits exactly one, *matched* when chosen to fit a structure, *adopted* when imported with citation. Part I's Lyapunov quadratic is matched (a converse-Lyapunov choice to fit the sector form; the chapter has no Lyapunov argument of its own), and the IB Lagrangian is adopted. The posture: where a coordinate matters, the framework can usually point at the axiom that forces it, and where it cannot, it can say what was matched or adopted.

The downstream bite is wide, and each instance carries its source's tier. The chain identity anchors the depth-fragility result ("deep plans are structurally fragile, at a rate exponential in depth") — and, with evidence starvation and cognitive cost, the *triple depth penalty* — three co-pressures on depth, of which `#form-strategy-complexity-cost` derives the maximum useful depth $d^\ast$ from the starvation/persistence bound (conditional on Beta-Bernoulli dynamics) and the other two ride alongside as an observation, not a min-formula. *[Discussion]* That compound is the chapter's cleanest instance of emergent normativity: nobody tells the agent "make short plans with observable milestones"; confidence decay, starvation, and representational cost jointly force it. Reverse-KL anchors the Bretagnolle-Huber regret bound in Part II Ch.4 (conditional). Log-odds anchors the detection-latency no-go ( #deriv-update-detection-latency, conditional) — but not the forgetting prerequisite of `#schema-strategy-persistence`, which rests on $\alpha_\Sigma = 1/(n+1)$ in the raw Beta-Bernoulli coordinate and makes no use of log-odds. The Fisher metric anchors the Class 3 (Coupled) bias bound — Track 2, $C_{FR} = \sqrt{2}$ under (PI) plus Čencov, dimension-free, conditional on the small-information statistical-manifold regime ( #deriv-observation-ambiguity-bias-bound) — and the survival-LMI directional exploration bonus ( #deriv-causal-ib-lmi, conditional). A small practical corollary of the identity itself: an agent's *stated* plan confidence can be checked against the confidence *implied* by its per-step credences, and a mismatch is a calibration or candor flag — exact arithmetic, cheap to run.

The widest downstream bite is in Part II Ch.4 (Strategy Dynamics), which opens with strategic calibration ( #def-strategic-calibration) and then states the no-go anticipated above in full ( #der-causal-insufficiency-detection) — forward references, both. There the strategy-edge persistence schema instantiates this chapter's DAG with Beta-Bernoulli update dynamics, and one scope fact carries across the bridge: an OR-arm that is *never* taken has no sector parameter — strategic persistence on it is not small, it is undefined (Prop B.4; exploration gates $\alpha_\Sigma$ rather than spending a budget).

What Part II Ch.3 closes on, then, is the formal substrate Strategy Dynamics will operate on: a theorem-backed graphical structure with a chosen and defended parameterization, a convention-indexed — and policy-class-relative — diagnostic that routes revision pressure to the right substate, a scope-honest treatment of correlated failure with one Cramér-Rao floor named exactly and one CHT no-go anticipated at its stated tier, and the anchor of a coordinate-forcing meta-pattern whose three theorems reach across the rest of the framework. The chapter is small in segment count and wide in downstream reach.
