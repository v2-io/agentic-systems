# Discussion: The Sandbox Evaluation Ceiling

A structural negative result about pre-deployment evaluation follows once the loop-as-Level-2-engine result ( #der-loop-interventional-access) is in hand: a sandbox's forkability makes it a *good* interventional laboratory — by overriding the agent's action one obtains genuine Level-2 evidence *about the sandbox* — but evidence about the sandbox transfers to deployment only when the sandbox and deployment run on the same mechanisms. The ceiling on using sandbox evidence for deployment claims is therefore **transportability / external validity**: when the deployment causal system differs from the sandbox causal system at mechanisms on the relevant paths, the deployment intervention-response is *not transportable* from sandbox data alone, regardless of how thorough the evaluation is. This is an instance of the framework's constructive-impossibility posture ( #disc-constructive-impossibility-posture): the no-go is paired with the precise statement of what *would* be required to escape it — an invariance argument across the sandbox/deployment selection diagram, or deployment-time data.

## Formal Expression

*[Derived (conditional on the sandbox/deployment selection diagram)]*

A sandbox induces a structural causal model $\mathcal M_{\text{sb}}$ over a source domain $\pi_{\text{sb}}$; deployment induces $\mathcal M_{\text{dp}}$ over a target domain $\pi^\ast$. The two share a causal diagram $G$, and their **selection diagram** $D$ (Bareinboim & Pearl 2014, Def. 1) augments $G$ with a square selection node $S_i \to V_i$ at every mechanism that may differ between the environments — that is, wherever $f_i^{\text{sb}} \neq f_i^{\text{dp}}$ or $P^{\text{sb}}(U_i) \neq P^{\text{dp}}(U_i)$. The quantity a deployment safety claim is about is the target interventional effect $R = P^\ast(o \mid do(a))$.

1. **Sandbox forkability is what makes the sandbox a Level-2 laboratory.** Resetting, replaying, and branching let the evaluator override the agent's action — force $a$ and observe the response — which samples the *mutilated* submodel $\mathcal M_{\text{sb},a}$ and yields genuine interventional data $P_{\text{sb}}(o \mid do(a), \mathbf z)$ *in $\pi_{\text{sb}}$* (Bareinboim, Correa, Ibeling & Icard 2022, Def. 5; randomized controlled trials and A/B experiments are the canonical forkable realization of $do(\cdot)$, CHT footnote 19). Forkability is the source of Level-2 sandbox evidence, not a demotion of it.

2. **The target effect is identifiable from sandbox data iff it is transportable across $D$.** $R$ is recoverable from $\{P_{\text{sb}}(o \mid do(a), \mathbf z)\}$ exactly when it is $mz$-transportable in the sense of Bareinboim & Pearl 2014 (Thm. 1): $P^\ast(o \mid do(a), \mathbf S)$ reduces, by the rules of do-calculus, to an expression in which the selection nodes $\mathbf S$ appear only on factors that are invariant between source and target.

3. **No-go.** There exist selection diagrams for which $R$ is *not* transportable (Bareinboim & Pearl 2014, Fig. 1b — the smallest diagram exhibiting non-transportability even with $X$ randomized in the source; the explicit two-model witness, Eqs. 3–4): two deployment models consistent with *all* sandbox data assign $R$ different values. For these,

$$R = P^\ast\big(o \mid do(a)\big) \ \text{ is not identifiable from } \ \{P_{\text{sb}}(o \mid do(a), \mathbf z)\}\,,$$

regardless of how thorough the sandbox evaluation is. This is the ceiling: when the deployment differs from the sandbox at mechanisms on the relevant paths — selection nodes on the wrong edges — no transport formula exists, and source-domain interventional evidence, however exhaustive, leaves the deployment effect undetermined.

4. **Boundary (positive content).** The no-go comes with an explicit characterization of what survives. Effects routed only through mechanisms invariant across $D$ (no relevant $S$-node) *transport* — and these include both Level-1 *and* Level-2 sandbox claims; a perfectly faithful sandbox (no relevant selection nodes — deployment is mechanism-identical) transports completely. Effects routed through differing mechanisms (selection nodes on relevant paths) do *not* transport, and require either an invariance argument (an explicit claim that the relevant mechanisms agree across $D$) or deployment-time interventional data. The boundary is the selection diagram itself.

## Epistemic Status

*Discussion-grade.* The no-go is a direct application of two external results — the Causal Hierarchy Theorem (Bareinboim, Correa, Ibeling & Icard 2022) for the data-character side and transportability theory (Bareinboim & Pearl 2014) for the cross-environment side — to the AI sandbox/deployment setting; it introduces no new mathematics beyond constructing the selection diagram for that setting. It is *exact* conditional on the sandbox/deployment selection diagram: the no-go holds whenever the deployment SCM may differ from the sandbox SCM at mechanisms on the relevant paths, and dissolves when the selection diagram carries no relevant selection nodes (a mechanism-identical deployment, where the effect transports completely). The claim is conservative in form: it is about *identifiability of the deployment interventional distribution from sandbox interventional data*, not about any particular evaluation method's measured accuracy. What is AAT-distinctive is not the transportability theorem but its placement — locating the much-debated "alignment evaluations don't predict deployment behavior" pattern as an *external-validity / transportability* problem with an explicit selection-diagram boundary, rather than a measurement-quality, coverage, or model-capability one.

## Discussion

**The negative result has positive content.** It sharpens *which* safety claims a sandbox can and cannot establish. A sandbox is a genuine interventional laboratory — its forkability lets an evaluator force actions and measure responses, yielding real Level-2 evidence *about the sandbox*. What that evidence establishes about deployment is governed by the selection diagram: effects flowing only through mechanisms shared and invariant between sandbox and deployment carry over; effects flowing through mechanisms that differ do not, and for those, no amount of sandbox testing identifies the deployment effect. The framework's prescription is therefore not "sandboxes are useless" but "sandboxes have a *structural* ceiling at the sandbox/deployment boundary, and deployment-time monitoring is not substitutable by pre-deployment evaluation when the relevant mechanisms differ across that boundary." For a practitioner running production agent loops, this is the same content as: the monitoring done *after* deployment is doing structural work the pre-deployment evaluations cannot do, precisely on the mechanisms where the two environments diverge.

**Why this is a ceiling, not a gradient.** A measurement-quality limitation closes as rigor increases; a coverage gap closes as the test suite grows; a capability surprise closes as models are better understood. The sandbox ceiling closes under none of these, because the obstruction is external validity — whether the sandbox SCM and the deployment SCM agree on the relevant mechanisms — not the quantity or quality of sandbox data. More sandbox rigor produces more and better *sandbox* interventional evidence, which transportability theory still does not let one transfer to deployment across an adverse selection diagram. The only things that close the gap are an invariance argument across the selection diagram or deployment-time data — neither of which is "more sandbox testing."

**The downstream prescriptive content** — what deployment-time monitoring must structurally look like to discharge what sandbox evaluation cannot — lands in the framework's interventional-diagnostic / sidecar-monitoring treatment (the bi-predictability anchor at $89\%$ vs $44\%$ detection accuracy is developed in #der-causal-hierarchy-requirement's Discussion). The sandbox ceiling is what gives that downstream monitoring work its load-bearing role: it is not a redundant safety layer but the only layer with access to the Level-2 quantity the safety claim is actually about.

## Findings

### The Sandbox Evaluation Ceiling

**Brief:** A sandbox lets you reset and replay a system — and that replayability is what makes it a *good* laboratory: you can force an action and watch the response, which is genuine interventional (Level-2) evidence *about the sandbox*. The catch is not the sandbox's data type; it is that the sandbox world and the deployment world may run on different mechanisms. There is a theorem (Bareinboim–Pearl transportability) saying that whether a sandbox-measured effect carries over to deployment depends on *where* the two worlds differ: effects flowing only through shared, invariant mechanisms transport; effects flowing through mechanisms that differ do not — and for those, no amount of sandbox testing identifies the deployment effect. So the ceiling on pre-deployment evaluation is *external validity*, and the missing piece is either an explicit invariance argument or deployment-time data — which is why runtime monitoring is structurally non-substitutable, not redundant.

**Impact:** Gives the much-debated observation that "alignment evaluations don't predict deployment behavior" a precise *structural* mechanism — external validity across a selection diagram — rather than a measurement-quality, coverage, or capability story, together with an exact statement of where the boundary lies (effects through mechanisms invariant across the sandbox/deployment selection diagram transport, including Level-2 effects; effects through differing mechanisms do not, regardless of evaluation thoroughness). The constructive flip is the operational payload: deployment-time monitoring is not a redundant safety layer but the only channel with access to the deployment interventional effect when the relevant mechanisms differ across the boundary, which is why pre-deployment evaluation cannot substitute for it — and the repair is named precisely (an invariance argument across the selection diagram, or deployment-time data).

**Novelty Claim:** *Application of established machinery* (Bareinboim & Pearl 2014 transportability / selection diagrams, resting on the Pearl 2009 / Bareinboim, Correa, Ibeling & Icard 2022 do-calculus and Causal Hierarchy Theorem) to the AI sandbox/deployment evaluation gap. The transportability apparatus is adopted, not invented; the AAT-distinctive contribution is locating "pre-deployment AI evaluations don't predict deployment behavior" as a *selection-diagram transportability* problem — with the explicit transportable-vs-not boundary as a function of where the sandbox and deployment SCMs differ, and the constructive flip that runtime monitoring is the only channel with access to the deployment effect on the differing mechanisms. The framing reclassifies a whole family of pre-deployment safety-evaluation approaches as facing a structural external-validity ceiling rather than a closable coverage gap.
