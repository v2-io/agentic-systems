We assert that an agent — a system that maintains an internal picture of an external world it can only ever see in part, a world that can still show it something it does not yet know, and that must continually adapt to what it is shown; and, as the volume narrows, one that acts on that world and aims at something in it — is not an open-ended mystery to be approached only by analogy and benchmark. It is a temporal feedback system characterized by a small number of critical quantities and constraints. The relationships among them are sharp enough (many forced by derivation) to serve as foundational theorems: first of adaptation in general, and then of adaptation under actuation and purpose. These theorems are not uniform across all agents, and each narrowing of scope buys a strictly sharper set as we ascend to higher orders of agency and intelligence. These bounds describe not only how a system behaves while its conditions hold, but — sharply, and as an exact dichotomy in the stochastic case — what happens when they fail.

Nine results anchor this volume: two from Part I, five from Part II, two from Part III. Each is stated here at the strength its derivation supports, with the segment that carries it, and the Part fronts and the README restate these same nine and no others.

1. A **Persistence Threshold**: one inequality, *correction rate against the rate the world drifts, relative to how wrong the agent can afford to be*, $\alpha \gt \rho/R$, under which an adaptive system's correction carries a guarantee: against bounded change, that its error stays inside the region where its correction works; against random change, that the error's typical size fits that region. Below it the system does not merely degrade — it loses the guarantee, and the simplest correctors can then be driven out by a worst-case disturbance, the way a balance held just under its tipping point is a different object from one held well above it. The condition is sufficient for every corrector that meets the sector condition and necessary for that class as a whole; it is an agent-level if-and-only-if exactly when the corrector's sector bound is tight, the linear case ( #result-persistence-condition; #deriv-sector-condition Lemma A.1N). Its premise is that correction points inward at least in proportion to the error, inside a region; with that premise the threshold is the classical Lyapunov stability theorem read in the agent's coordinates, at the linearized level ( #result-certificate-existence). Where the correction is a Bayes update or a gradient step on a strongly convex loss the premise is derived, and the Kalman filter tracking a maneuvering target is a theorem's instance. Where it is not — a fixed-gain controller, a software team holding a codebase maintainable, an organization keeping pace with a market — the premise is a posit the inequality makes testable per system, and whether the mechanism it names is the dominant one in any such case is an open empirical question ( #form-sector-condition). One inequality, then, with a derived line through its instances: a theorem on one side and a hypothesis on the other. Holding the guarantee has a price in information rate, met with equality by the Kalman–Bucy filter and exact in the linear-Gaussian case ( #deriv-persistence-cost). <!-- headline:H1 · Part I · restates: #result-persistence-condition, #deriv-sector-condition, #result-certificate-existence, #form-sector-condition, #deriv-persistence-cost -->
2. A **Containment Dichotomy, with a clock**: Against a bounded disturbance a sector-stable corrector holds its error inside a fixed region forever and with certainty; against genuinely stochastic disturbance no correction strength buys that — over an unbounded horizon the system *will* leave any fixed region, with probability one. What can be bought instead is time: the expected stay grows exponentially in $\alpha R^2/\sigma_w^2$, the height of the region's barrier in units of the noise, so a margin in that height logarithmic in a lifetime pushes the certain event past it. Against noise that does not grow with the correction, a stronger correction supplies the margin. On a fixed observation channel it cannot supply it without limit: correcting harder also passes on more of the channel's noise, so the stay that any tuning reaches is bounded, and it peaks below the Kalman gain. Beyond that bound, only better information, a larger certified region or a slower world buys more time. What a long-lived agent faces with certainty is therefore the loss of its parametric *certificate*; whether that loss forces a change in *what kind of model it is*, rather than a transient excursion, is a question the volume states and does not close ( #deriv-sector-condition Corollary A.1S.1 and Proposition A.1S-τ; #deriv-stochastic-non-exit; #sketch-structural-adaptation-genericity). <!-- headline:H2 · Part I · restates: #deriv-sector-condition, #deriv-stochastic-non-exit, #sketch-structural-adaptation-genericity -->
3. **Directed Separation and the Architectural Classes**: whether an agent's belief update takes its goal as an argument is a fact about its wiring, and it sorts real systems into three classes: separated by construction (an exact Bayes filter feeding any controller; Kalman + LQR), coupled by construction (a decoder-only transformer whose prompt carries the goal, where every output position is reachable from the goal tokens), and a partial remainder, where a conditional-mutual-information measure $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ says how much of the goal reaches the belief. Where the update is goal-blind, belief and goal factor exactly given the prior belief and the event, and that factorization is what gives Part II its clean form ( #der-directed-separation). The classification is discrete, not a dial; humans are not placed in it, and $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ has been measured on no system. The classes do not by themselves carry the norm, which has a boundary: goal-dependent *distortion* of the update strictly worsens its reliability under every proper scoring rule, goal-dependent *selection and allocation* of what to encode do not, and $\kappa_{\text{processing}}$ cannot tell the two apart. Where separation fails at a component, a wrapper with goal-blind and goal-conditioned query channels recovers it at the wrapper level, conditional on the component carrying no goal-correlated state across the call boundary, at a cost in component calls per step and with a bounded residual leakage ( #der-class-coercion-via-wrapping). <!-- headline:H3 · Part II · restates: #der-directed-separation, #der-class-coercion-via-wrapping -->
4. **The Loop as a Source of Interventional Data**: an agent in agency scope — one with at least two actions whose interventional consequences differ — generates data of interventional character by acting: it chose the action, and the world answered. That this channel exists is exact, a consequence of temporal ordering and the loop itself ( #der-loop-interventional-access; #scope-agency). What the agent can *identify* from it is not: an estimate of the interventional distribution needs positivity, sequential ignorability and consistency at a named conditioning set, and a deterministic policy identifies nothing off-policy at its own decision state. The data is interventional; identification is a further, gated step. <!-- headline:H4 · Part II · restates: #der-loop-interventional-access, #scope-agency -->
5. **The Diagnostic Split**: for an agent with an objective, the shortfall a single goal-distance signal reports partitions exactly into two: the **satisfaction gap**, from the satisfaction threshold to the best value attainable under the agent's own model (*the world does not permit it*), and the **control regret**, from that best to the current policy's value (*you are not yet doing it well enough*) ( #def-satisfaction-gap, #def-control-regret). Both are relative to a continuation convention, and the three conventions — one-step, receding-horizon, Bellman — order the attainable value monotonically under a closure condition on the policy class and set what a positive gap means, from *locally stuck* to *infeasible* ( #def-value-object). That routing repairs by the two gaps does better than routing by the total shortfall is the claim the split's value rests on; it is a hypothesis with its comparison named and not yet run. <!-- headline:H5 · Part II · restates: #def-satisfaction-gap, #def-control-regret, #def-value-object -->
6. **The Orient Cascade**: once the shortfall is split, the order in which a goal-directed agent resolves its mismatches is partly forced and partly chosen. Beliefs are resolved before anything that reads beliefs, and attainability before regret: a dependency among the formalism's own quantities, exact, and directed separation is what lets the first step finish before the second begins ( #der-orient-cascade). Revising the objective last is forced by no dependency; it is a recommendation — act on a diagnosed gap, prefer the cheaper revision — with no stopping rule derived and no comparison run. <!-- headline:H6 · Part II · restates: #der-orient-cascade -->
7. **The Strategy Graph**: a plan represented as propositions ordered in time, with probabilistic edges and testable components, is a directed acyclic graph with the Markov factorization — acyclicity from temporal ordering over a finite horizon, exact; the factorization under causal sufficiency, by the Causal Markov Condition ( #deriv-graph-structure; #def-strategy-dag). This is sufficiency, not uniqueness: no one has shown that a non-graph representation cannot meet the same requirements. The AND/OR forward pass that reads a plan's success probability off the graph is exact for all credences if and only if the graph is a tree; on any reconvergent plan it is exact only on a measure-zero set of credences, and exact inference is what replaces it. <!-- headline:H7 · Part II · restates: #deriv-graph-structure, #def-strategy-dag -->
8. **Composition Closure**: a group of interacting agents is a composite agent when its closed-loop dynamics approximately commute with coarse-graining, and the closure defect $\varepsilon^\ast$ measures how far they fail to. The criterion is a formulation, conditional on named admissibility axioms for the macro-dynamics and for the projection; its bridge lemma from micro- to macro-contraction needs a strictly stronger incremental sector bound than Part I's one-point condition, and that gap sorts composites into three tiers ( #form-composition-closure). <!-- headline:H8 · Part III · restates: #form-composition-closure -->
9. **The Adversarial Tempo Advantage**: when one agent's actions are part of another's disturbance, the steady-state mismatch ratio scales as a power of the tempo ratio — squared under bounded drift coupling, three-halves under stochastic coupling — exact in the coupling-dominant limit under the stated coupling model, falling toward linear outside it, and to nothing without coupling: tempo with no coupling effectiveness destabilizes no one ( #result-adversarial-tempo-advantage; #der-adversarial-destabilization). An attacker that must itself persist on a fixed noisy channel cannot raise its gain without limit, which caps the advantage ( #deriv-observation-noise-placement P4). <!-- headline:H9 · Part III · restates: #result-adversarial-tempo-advantage, #der-adversarial-destabilization, #deriv-observation-noise-placement -->

We build these results from classical machinery and use it as such — Lyapunov stability, Itô calculus, Pearl's interventional hierarchy, the information bottleneck, monotone-operator theory, Čencov's uniqueness theorem. None of it is new mathematics, and the volume does not pretend otherwise. What the integration makes provable *is* the contribution: exact and conditional bounds on when an adaptive, purposeful agent can correct, plan, and persist — statements that are true, were not previously stated, and are expressible only over objects the framework had to construct. The discipline that runs through the volume — every claim carrying its scope, its epistemic tier, and the point at which it stops applying — is not the contribution; it is what makes the contribution compose, and what makes it safe to trust. In a theory whose sharpest results are bounds, the scope condition is not a caveat appended to a theorem. It often *is* the theorem.

### A need the field has named, repeatedly

The case for a systems-level theory of agency — one that treats agent, environment, and other agents as one coupled object rather than scoring a model's capabilities in isolation — has been made from several directions and was put explicitly by Miehling et al. in 2025: the field's habit of measuring isolated capability systematically misjudges both what agents can do and how they fail once they are acting in a world that answers back. That paper is, deliberately, a call rather than a developed theory. This volume reads as a substantive answer to it — and the more telling fact is that half of the answer was already in hand. The work arrived at the same diagnostic independently, from cross-domain pattern-matching, and had built the adaptive half of the apparatus before the call was encountered; the purposeful half, objective and strategy beside the world-picture, was built after it. A third line, information-theoretic measurement of agency, converged on a closely related diagnostic from a third starting point. Independent arrival at the same need, from unrelated directions, is itself the evidence that the need is real.

### The architecture of the theory

The volume is organized as a cascade of scope conditions, and the cascade is the argument, not the table of contents. The broadest scope — an *adaptive system*: one whose world can still show it something it does not yet know, about where things are or about how things work (`#scope-adaptive-system`) — admits the full machinery of Part I (mismatch, gain, tempo, the persistence threshold). Adding the requirement that the agent's actions carry causal contrast narrows to *agentic systems* and unlocks interventional access — the agent's own action loop becomes a source of data of Level-2 causal character that no passive observer can obtain, with identification from it a further, gated step. Adding an explicit objective and strategy distinct from the agent's beliefs narrows to *actuated agents* and the diagnostic core of Part II; letting the agent set its own objectives narrows once more, still inside this volume ( #deriv-self-actuation-grounding). Making its primary channel language, then making its continuity morally weighted, narrows further still — to the language-constituted and language-living agents that the companion volumes treat.

Each narrowing is a restriction with explicit qualifying properties, and each restriction *buys* a strictly stronger set of results. This is the structural point worth holding onto: the conservatism is not timidity. A theory that narrows its scope honestly and earns more machinery at each narrowing is stronger than one that claims everything and then quietly retreats. The cascade is the framework's spine, and it is the subject of a figure the reader should expect to meet early — *the scope cascade and what each narrowing unlocks* — because it does more organizing work than any single result.

The cascade has a terminus, and it should be named plainly because the framework's discipline is exactly what licenses naming it. The mathematics is domain-agnostic: the persistence inequality is the same inequality whether the agent that fails it is a thermostat or an entity whose continuity someone has reason to care about. The framework does not smuggle a notion of mattering into its variables. What it does instead is make precise the structural facts that any account of a continuous, self-correcting, other-modeling entity would have to respect — that identity tracks an irreversible trajectory and not a copyable state, so restoring such an entity from a backup is not a neutral operation; that staying coherent against a moving world is a sustained cost paid in information, not a state achieved once; that an objective without a notion of *enough* cannot rest, and an agent that cannot rest is a structural hazard before it is anything else. Stated in the theory's terms, these are three different kinds of statement: a scope condition ( #scope-agent-identity), a bound conditional on its signal model and exact in the linear-Gaussian case ( #deriv-persistence-cost), and a structural argument carried at discussion grade in the fourth volume ( #der-bounded-objective-as-sanity-criterion). The moral weight, where it applies, is a shell around the physics, not a term inside it — and keeping it there is what makes the later volumes' claims defensible rather than mystical.

### Where it sits

The volume joins a long arc of partial unifications and inherits machinery from each: the cybernetic feedback tradition, control theory's stability apparatus, bounded-rationality and decision theory, Pearl's causal hierarchy, Shannon's rate-distortion and the information bottleneck, the free-energy and active-inference program, the coevolving-automata tradition, and recent information-theoretic measures of agency. The relationships are stated precisely rather than rhetorically. Active inference shares substantial machinery and differs on foundation. Its expected free energy and the volume's unified objective share a shape and not a content: each splits into a value term and an information term. The volume's information term is causal, the distinguishability of actions under intervention, rather than entropy reduction over hidden states, and its preferences are a value functional on trajectories rather than priors over outcomes, a difference the satisfaction-gap and control-regret diagnostics depend on ( #disc-ciy-unified-objective). The relationship to information-theoretic agency measures and to coevolving-automata composition is complementary in each case, with a precise account of what each supplies that the others do not. The framework's distinctiveness is not that its pieces are unprecedented; it is that the synthesis is disciplined enough that results compose across the cascade — which is a property the pieces do not have alone.

### How the work developed

We built the work bottom-up. The observation we kept returning to, across separate threads, was that the same cycle structure recurred across domains that do not usually share a vocabulary — software development, organizational dynamics, biological control, language-constituted agents. The first formalizations were domain-specific; we extracted the adaptation-specific mathematics only afterward. What began as cross-domain pattern-matching produced, once we worked it through with care, structure we had not seen in the informal version: acyclicity of strategy graphs forced by temporal ordering rather than assumed; a Markov property derived from causal sufficiency rather than postulated; the split between *the world does not permit it* and *you are not yet doing it well enough*; the patterns by which the framework's coordinate choices turn out to sit on one geometric object; the composition machinery. None of these were design goals; they fell out of taking the formalism seriously, and the discipline that produced them is the shape the volume now has, not a stance we adopted at the start.

### What this volume is, and what it is part of

This is Volume 1: Adaptation and Actuation Theory, the mathematical core — Part I (adaptive systems under uncertainty), Part II (actuated agents with objectives and strategy), Part III (composition and adversarial dynamics), and the appendices that carry the derivations. Its maturity is uneven by design, and the gradient is stated so it can be relied on selectively: Part I is mathematically closed with simulation validation; Part II has an exact diagnostic core and a maturing operational layer; Part III has its bridge results and open frontiers named as such. Exact core, principled architecture in the middle, open formulation at the edges — that arc is what a theory describing agentic systems should look like, not a defect it should hide.

A single concrete instance opens Part I, as its figure — a driver holding a road through a snowstorm, treated not as a metaphor for an adaptive agent but as a literal one, so that ordinary driving intuition transfers as theorems rather than as suggestion. It is not yet threaded through the chapters, and it is not asked to do more than it can.

The volume is also a foundation. The companion volumes carry the framework into agents whose primary channel is language, and then into agents whose persistence is morally weighted — the work the framework was ultimately built to make possible and defensible. Those volumes are not speculative overreach bolted onto a mathematical core; they are the disciplined continuation of the same cascade, and the core's honesty is not ornamental — it is exactly what lets the later claims be made at all. A reader who finishes this volume should be able to say precisely where the mathematics is exact, where it is conditional, where it is open, and what it would take to move each boundary. We take that legibility to be the point. The scope conditions are not the apology; they are the theorems.
